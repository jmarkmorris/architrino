# Mathematical Terminology (Canonical Dialect)

This table lists the symbols and mathematical terms used across the Geometrical Model of Nature and explains their meaning in this project’s context.

Treat this as the legend for the equations. When a chapter uses a symbol such as $T$, $\mathbf X$, $g_{\mu\nu}^{\mathrm{eff}}$, $\xi$, or $\chi_{\text{sea}}$, this page records the intended layer and prevents nearby standard notation from taking over by habit.

| Symbol / Term | Meaning in this project | Extended explanation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\mathcal{M} = \mathbb{R} \times \mathbb{R}^3$ | Absolute timespace: formal product of absolute time and Euclidean 3-space; no fundamental non-degenerate 4D metric is prescribed. | Treat as a product manifold with a global time coordinate and a flat 3D Riemannian metric; differential operators split into absolute-time derivatives $d/dT$ and spatial operators on slices $\Sigma_T$. Worldlines are paths parameterized by $T$, governed by delayed history-dependent dynamics, and fields are distributions on $\Sigma_T$ parametrized by $T$. Use `absolute timespace` when the formal product structure matters; otherwise name the factors directly as `absolute time` and `Euclidean void`. |
| $T$ | Absolute time; increases globally and may be nondimensionalized by a declared reference duration. | Single global independent variable for native dynamics. If a reference duration is used to nondimensionalize $T$, state that convention separately from an epoch; derivatives with respect to $T$ define native velocity $\mathbf V$ and native acceleration $\mathbf A$. Monotonicity enforces causal order and precludes closed time loops. |
| $P$, $P_b$, $P_q$, $P_0$ | Cycle period, branch periods, and reference period. | Use $P$ for the duration of one declared cycle and retain the role subscript when needed: $P_b$ for branch $b$, $P_q$ for clock branch $q$, and $P_0$ for its declared reference period. State the clock or time coordinate in which the duration is measured. A period is not the absolute-time coordinate $T$, an epoch $T_0$, or an arbitrary normalization scale. Use $P_{\mathrm{cyc}}$ when another meaning of $P$ coexists; preserve independently defined probability, pressure, momentum, and map notation. |
| $P_{\mathrm{ret}}$, $P_{\mathbf k}$, $P_N$, $P_{N0}$ | Complete-history return, winding-indexed period, and local/reference Noether sea cadence periods. | These are role-qualified members of the period family. Specify what returns: one projected signal, a reduced shape, or the complete delayed history. Equal dimensions do not make those return conditions equivalent. The reference cadence obeys $P_{N0}=2\pi/\Omega_{N0}$ when $\Omega_{N0}$ is the corresponding angular frequency. |
| $\mathbb{U}_{\text{now}} \equiv S(T)$ | Complete ontic universe state on simultaneity slice $\Sigma_T$ at global time $T$. | Canonical symbol for the "state of the universe now": $S(T)=\{(\mathbf X_i(T),\mathbf V_i(T),q_i,\ldots)\}$, including required path-history/provenance bookkeeping and self-hit history for deterministic evolution. This is an ontological complete-state description, not an observer. A finite simulation supplies only its declared constituent and history domain. |
| $\Sigma_T$ | Simultaneity slice at time $T$: $\{T\} \times \mathbb{R}^3$. | A 3D hypersurface carrying $h_{ij}$ and volume element $dV$; gradients, divergences, and Laplacians act within $\Sigma_T$. Absolute-timespace integrals use $dT\,dV$. |
| $h_{ij} = \delta_{ij}$ | Euclidean spatial metric on $\Sigma_T$; implements dot products and norms. | Flat metric used for inner products and norms; in spherical coordinates it becomes $\mathrm{diag}(1,r^2,r^2\sin^2\theta)$. Raising/lowering uses $h^{ij}=\delta^{ij}$ only in Cartesian coordinates; in spherical coordinates the inverse metric has reciprocal diagonal entries away from coordinate singularities. |
| $\mathbf X,\ \mathbf X_i(T)$ | Native spatial position vector in the Euclidean void. | Spatial vectors valued in $\mathbb{R}^3$ on the absolute slice $\Sigma_T$. The worldline $\mathbf X_i(T)$ is assumed absolutely continuous so $\mathbf V_i=d\mathbf X_i/dT$ exists a.e.; do not assemble native positions into substrate 4-vectors. |
| $\mathbf V=d\mathbf X/dT,\ \mathbf A=d\mathbf V/dT$ | Native velocity and acceleration vectors. | Kinematic derivatives with respect to absolute time $T$. A locally finite acceleration measure gives velocity of locally bounded variation. Ordinary admitted simple-root contributions can be continuous even in the sharp limit; explicit impulse atoms can produce jumps. Mollification retains history dependence and requires regularity, separation/core control, and convergence assumptions for a well-posed delay evolution. |
| $t_{\mathrm{eff}},\ x_{\mathrm{eff}}^i$ | Effective observer-coordinate time and spatial chart coordinates. | Use these only after the derivation has moved into an observer-level or GR-comparison chart. They may have the same physical dimensions as $T$ and $X^i$, but they are not the same variables. Bare $t$ and $x^i$ may appear only in a labeled standard comparison form for recognition or source matching. They must not become working symbols in student-facing prose; translate them immediately into $t_{\mathrm{eff}},x_{\mathrm{eff}}^i$ or $T,\mathbf X$ before interpretation, diagramming, or term-by-term mapping. |
| $dt_{\mathrm{eff}},\ dx_{\mathrm{eff}}^i,\ \partial_{t_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ | Effective-coordinate differentials and time derivative. | Use these in effective metric, ADM, PPN, Shapiro, lensing, redshift, and observer-clock equations. Do not substitute them for $dT$, $dX^i$, or $\partial_T$ without an explicit coordinate-layer map. |
| $\tau,\ d\tau$ | Derived physical-clock time and its differential. | Proper time is a standard relativity bridge term for a clock readout extracted from physical clocks. In native prose, define it as `derived clock time`, `clock map`, or `extracted clock observable`, not as substrate time. Write $d\tau/dt_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for observer-coordinate clock-rate comparisons and $d\tau/dT$ only when a native clock-map derivation has been declared. |
| $T_W$ | Record window: the finite interval over which a measurement record is formed, evaluated, and required to persist for a declared channel. | The observation-interval symbol in the record packet $(\mathcal{K}_A,\mathcal{Q},W,T_W,\{R_k\},\mu_{*,T_W})$; it is the horizon of the finite-window basin measure $\mu_{*,T_W}$ and of the record residuals such as $\Delta_{\mathrm{Born}}(T_W)$, $\Delta E_{\mathrm{unrec}}(T_W;\theta)$, and $\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{grav}}(T_W;\theta)$. Keep it distinct from absolute time $T$ (the global substrate clock) and from the persistence time $T_{\mathrm{rec}}$ (how long a formed record must survive). Do not write a bare $T$ for the window; use $T_W$. |
| $T_{\mathrm{rec}}$ | Record persistence time: the minimum duration a formed record must remain stable to count as durable. | Sets the persistence window $[\tau_{\mathrm{meas}},\,\tau_{\mathrm{meas}}+T_{\mathrm{rec}}]$ used by the record-autonomy and restartability residuals. It is a duration measured inside a record window $T_W$, not the window itself and not absolute time $T$. |
| $\chi_{\mathrm{eff}}:(T,\mathbf X,\mathcal N_{\mathrm{sea}},\text{observer record})\mapsto(t_{\mathrm{eff}},x_{\mathrm{eff}}^i)$ | Coordinate-layer map from native absolute variables to effective observer-chart variables. | This map is a closure target unless the local document derives it. It prevents hidden substitution of $T,\mathbf X$ for $t_{\mathrm{eff}},x_{\mathrm{eff}}^i$ in equations that can otherwise pass dimensional checks while failing a layer check. |
| $\|\cdot\|$ | Euclidean norm (length) induced by $h_{ij}$. | The norm induced by $h$; used for separations $r$, speeds, and lengths; coordinate-independent. |
| hat (ˆ) | Unit vector; e.g., $\hat{\mathbf{r}} = \mathbf{r}/\|\mathbf{r}\|$. | Normalization to unit length. $\hat{\mathbf{r}}$ fixes the line of action. At $r=0$ the direction is undefined, and the coincident root is excluded by $H(0)=0$ rather than assigned a symmetry-cancellation contribution. |
| Group velocity; group speed | Whole-assembly translation velocity; its magnitude. | Use one declared center and reference frame. Preserve constant-motion and averaging assumptions explicitly. Distinguish internal constituent motion, Noether sea flow, and conventional wave-packet group velocity. See [Site and Group Velocity Usage](terminology-usage.md#site-and-group-velocity-usage). |
| $v$ | Assembly group speed in a declared channel comparison. | Use $v$ only as a group speed, normally through a channel-qualified ratio such as $\beta_f=v/c_f$. It is not the causal-wake propagation speed. |
| $\kappa > 0$ | Universal coupling constant (absorbs geometric normalizations). | A scale factor absorbing geometric constants (e.g., $4\pi$ and $1/c_f$ Jacobians). Varying $\kappa$ rescales all per-hit accelerations and shifts emergent scales such as $d_0$ and $P_0$. |
| $\epsilon > 0$ | Potential polarity-unit magnitude; assigned effective electric-bookkeeping label $\lvert e\rvert/6$. | Fundamental polarity magnitude for architrinos in observer-level charge bookkeeping. Identifying $\epsilon$ with the magnitude of $e$ divided by 6 makes observed quark charges become integers in units of $\epsilon$, simplifying inventory bookkeeping; this convention alone does not derive electric-charge conservation. |
| $\epsilon_+\equiv+\epsilon,\ \epsilon_-\equiv-\epsilon$ | Signed polarity-unit labels for positive and negative architrino polarity in inventory counts. | Use these labels for axial inventories, weak-coupling-triad inventories, and neutral Noether braid content, e.g. $5\epsilon_+ + 1\epsilon_-$. The full entity names `electrino` and `positrino` remain valid when naming architrinos; compact inventory notation must use the signed $\epsilon_\pm$ labels. |
| $o_{\mathrm{PA}}\in\{+1,-1\}$ | Retained pro/anti Noether braid orientation sign. | Tracks the orientation of the indexed three-dimensional branch frame when that orientation is deformation-stable. Derive it from the indexed path record with a declared nondegenerate polar frame, not from a high/middle/low radius order. It is $C$-even and $P$-odd on that frame. Three angular-momentum axial vectors are parity-even and do not define the same orientation. Do not use it as the matter/antimatter sign. |
| $c_{\mathrm{pol}}\in\{+1,-1\}$ | Polarity-assignment sign on a chart that also carries $o_{\mathrm{PA}}$ and $\chi_{\mathrm{pol}}$. | Defined by $c_{\mathrm{pol}}\equiv\chi_{\mathrm{pol}}o_{\mathrm{PA}}$. It is $C$-odd and $P$-even. At a planar limit where $o_{\mathrm{PA}}$ is undefined, use the relational notation $\mathfrak B$ and $C(\mathfrak B)$ rather than assigning this scalar. |
| $\chi_{\mathrm{pol}}\equiv\operatorname{sign}(\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf S)=o_{\mathrm{PA}}c_{\mathrm{pol}}$ | Polarity-weighted handedness sign on a chart with $\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf S\ne0$. | Here $\mathbf p$ must be the polarity dipole, a polar vector odd under $C$, and $\mathbf S$ the $C$-even axial quantity. Then the sign is reversed by $C$ and $P$ and preserved by $CP$. Ordinary momentum–spin helicity is $C$-even and cannot replace this definition. The factorization into $o_{\mathrm{PA}}c_{\mathrm{pol}}$ requires the same nondegenerate chart. |
| $q_t,\,q_r$ | Intrinsic polarities of the transmitter and receiver (each equals $\pm\epsilon$). | Intrinsic, fixed signs $\pm\epsilon$. They enter accelerations through $\lvert q_tq_r\rvert$ and $\sigma_{tr}=\operatorname{sign}(q_tq_r)$. |
| $\sigma_{tr}=\operatorname{sign}(q_tq_r)$ | Interaction sign: $+1$ like-on-like (repulsion), $-1$ unlike (attraction). | It selects repulsion versus attraction by multiplying the radial unit vector in the EOM. |
| $\mathbf r_t,\ r,\ \hat{\mathbf r}_t$ | Separation vector, distance, and direction from the transmitter's emission site to the receiver's reception site. | $\mathbf r_t=\mathbf X_r(T_r)-\mathbf X_t(T_t)$, $r=\|\mathbf r_t\|$, and $\hat{\mathbf r}_t=\mathbf r_t/r$. The transmitter's position at $T_r$ is not part of the arriving-hit geometry. |
| $D_t(T_r;T_t)$ | Transmitter-side wake-spacing and emission-time Jacobian factor. | $D_t=c_f-\hat{\mathbf r}_t\cdot\mathbf V_t(T_t)$. It controls root transversality and how transmitter motion compresses or dilates successive emitted wake surfaces. |
| $D_r(T_r;T_t)$ | Receiver-side wake-crossing and root-playback factor. | $D_r=c_f-\hat{\mathbf r}_t\cdot\mathbf V_r(T_r)$. It controls how the receiver crosses the emitted wake sequence and enters the exact root-playback derivative. |
| $W_{r\leftarrow t}^{\mathrm{acc}}(T_r;T_t)=c_f/\lvert D_t\rvert$ | Unsigned transmitter-side acceleration weight in the canonical EOM. | It is fixed by transmitter-side causal-surface density and is distinct from the signed root-playback derivative $D_r/D_t$. |
| $J_{r\leftarrow t}^{t}(T_r;T_t)=D_t/c_f$ | Dimensionless transmitter-side causal Jacobian. | It is useful for root ledgers, transversality floors, and transmitter-side diagnostics. It is not by itself the full current acceleration weight. |
| $T_t,\ T_r,\ \Delta_{r\leftarrow t}=T_r-T_t$ | Transmitter emission time, receiver reception time, and causal travel delay. | $T_t$ labels the past event that emitted the arriving wake. $T_r$ labels the receiver event at which acceleration is evaluated. Reserve bare $T$ for absolute time when no transmitter-receiver event pair is being distinguished. |
| $\mathcal C_{r\leftarrow t}(T_r)$ | Causal set of transmitter emission times that reach receiver $r$ at $T_r$. | The set of $T_t<T_r$ solving $\|\mathbf X_r(T_r)-\mathbf X_t(T_t)\|-c_f(T_r-T_t)=0$. At an existing simple root, the implicit function theorem gives a locally unique smooth branch. A strict sub-field-speed bound throughout the retained transmitter interval gives at most one root there; existence also requires the relevant endpoint/sign conditions. Multiple roots can occur outside that domain. |
| Root ledger / causal-root ledger | Integer bookkeeping of active causal-root branches on a fixed branch chart. | The ledger records which causal roots are active, not a new acceleration law. In the circular binary reduction, $(N_s(s),M_p(s))$ counts active self-hit branches and active partner-hit branches. Use transmitter identity (`self` vs. `partner`) separately from root count (`single-root` vs. `multi-root`). |
| candidate braid; certified braid | Certificate-status terms for Noether braid branches. | A candidate braid is a proposed branch or branch family before all required entries close. A certified braid is a retained branch record with the family-declared inventory: six worldlines for the base, orthogonal-axis three-binary, and coincident-axis three-binary cases, or twelve worldlines for two-component circular configurations. It returns to itself under the delayed dynamics up to declared neutral symmetries, carries positive non-symmetry stability margins, and closes the observer-export entries required of a physical branch. Its retained record includes only path-history, causal-root, wake-tail, energy/action, momentum, angular-momentum, phase, orientation, response-center, group-velocity, Noether sea, and observer-export data that can still affect the declared delayed update or certification. A certified braid must reproduce Lorentz clock/ruler deformation in the applicable homogeneous moving-branch regime, including $\xi\to1/\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $d\tau/dt_{\mathrm{eff}}\to1/\gamma_{\mathrm{eff}}$, and bounded preferred-frame leakage after an effective observer chart is declared. |
| eigen-braid spectrum | Theorem-target set of dynamically admissible discrete Noether braid modes. | Reserve this term for the spectrum construction in Braid Mathematics: candidate mode solutions indexed by cadence, scale, root topology, and winding data. Membership in the eigen-braid spectrum does not certify a braid. Use candidate braid or certified braid for certificate status; do not use eigen-braid as a certificate-status modifier or label. |
| Wake / Causal Wake | The causal wake-surface record left by architrino motion. | Preferred substrate-level term for the causal structures emitted by moving architrinos. | Wake equation; wake map; wake pattern; wake spectrum | Construction rule and its evaluated representations. | The wake equation superposes the causal wakes emitted by constituent architrinos from one declared source-history record. At fixed source record and analysis protocol, the evaluated scalar is the wake map; its spatial or temporal structure is the wake pattern; and its frequency or angular-mode decomposition is the wake spectrum. The Master Equation remains the received-hit acceleration law, while `wave equation` remains correct for a qualified continuum comparison or recovery equation. |
| $\delta(r - c_f\Delta),\ \delta_{S_r}$ | Surface delta on an expanding causal wake surface; surface measure on surface $S_r$. | $\delta(r-c_f\Delta)$ represents a razor-thin causal wake surface in radial coordinates, while $\delta_{S_r}$ is the surface delta on $S_r$. | $\delta_\eta(\cdot)$ | Mollified (Gaussian) causal surface with width $\eta > 0$ for smooth dynamics. | A normalized Gaussian of width $\eta$ on the full real argument line. Causal or radial domain restriction may require explicit normalization. Smooth acceleration and a weak sharp limit additionally require core, history, and integrability controls. |
| $H(\Delta)$ | Heaviside step; convention $H(0)=0$ (no coincident-time self-acceleration). | Enforces causality (no advanced effects). | $\Phi,\ \Phi_\eta$ | Potential and mollified potential reconstructed from superposed causal wakes. | Bookkeeping potentials reconstructed from the superposed causal-wake contributions; a pointwise $\nabla\Phi_\eta$ requires control of both surface and core singularities. | $c_f$ | Primitive causal-wake propagation speed. | Use $c_f$ in every substrate constraint, numerical instantiation, fixture, test, tolerance, and operator-facing numerical example. It is not automatically the photon-channel speed; $c_\gamma(\mathbf X,T)=c_{\text{eff}}(\mathbf X,T)$ is a photon Gate A closure target, not a definition. |
| $c_0$ | Asymptotic observer-sector speed calibration used in weak-field and PPN comparisons. | Use $c_0$ in observer-level benchmark formulas after the effective chart and asymptotic calibration have been declared.
