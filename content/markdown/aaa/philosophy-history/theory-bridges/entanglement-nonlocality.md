## Traditional Quantum Mechanical View

### Entangled States

In standard quantum mechanics, a pure state of two systems $A$ and $B$ is entangled when the composite state $|\Psi\rangle_{AB}$ cannot be written as a product of individual states:

$$
|\Psi\rangle_{AB} \neq |\phi\rangle_A \otimes |\chi\rangle_B
$$

[View →](../../../../../equation-mapping.html#corpus-equation-d3d60fad3debb4f7)

For mixed states, entanglement means that no convex mixture of product density operators represents $\rho_{AB}$: failure of a single product factorization is insufficient, because separable mixtures can be classically correlated.

The canonical example is the spin-singlet state of two spin-$\tfrac{1}{2}$ particles:

$$
|\Psi^-\rangle = \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(|\!\uparrow\rangle_A |\!\downarrow\rangle_B - |\!\downarrow\rangle_A |\!\uparrow\rangle_B\bigr)
$$

[View →](../../../../../equation-mapping.html#corpus-equation-6691ff13ef00d83f)

Each reduced spin state is maximally mixed. Conditional on an ideal projective result at $A$, the singlet predicts the opposite result at $B$ along the same axis. The conditional state assignment changes with the available record; averaging over the unread result leaves $B$'s reduced state unchanged. This is not a controllable instantaneous physical signal.

### The EPR Argument and Bell's Theorem

Einstein, Podolsky, and Rosen (1935) combined perfect correlations with a locality premise and a criterion of physical reality to argue that quantum mechanics is incomplete. Bell (1964) established an obstruction to local completions. The later CHSH form bounds normalized local response laws for two settings per wing and outcomes between minus and plus one, using a setting-independent common hidden-state measure:

$$
|S| \leq 2 \quad \text{(Bell-CHSH inequality for local hidden variables)}
$$

[View →](../../../../../equation-mapping.html#corpus-equation-6a0c0a5ebfd97a1c)

For an ideal singlet and optimal settings quantum mechanics reaches $|S| = 2\sqrt{2}$. Bell experiments test statistically significant violations of the local bound; their measured values need not attain that ideal maximum. The standard conclusion is that no theory satisfying **Bell locality** (the outcomes at $A$ depend only on settings and hidden variables at $A$, not on the distant setting at $B$) and **measurement independence** (the choice of measurement settings is uncorrelated with the hidden variables) can reproduce all quantum predictions.

### The No-Signaling Constraint

Despite the correlations, entanglement cannot transmit information faster than light. The marginal statistics at either detector, averaged over the distant partner's outcomes, are independent of the distant measurement choice. This is the **no-signaling theorem**, which follows for standard local trace-preserving operations when the remote result is unread. Postselection changes conditional distributions, but identifying the selected trials requires communication. Experiments test the relevant marginal independence within finite uncertainty.

## Ontic vs. Epistemic: The Two-Level Reading

The $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ framework supports a clean two-level interpretation of entanglement:

**Ontic level ($\mathbb{U}_{\text{now}}$ universe-state perspective).** The kinematic snapshot $\Gamma(T)$ and its admitted histories are definite. After a source event at $T_0$, the daughter microstates $\Gamma_A(T)$ and $\Gamma_B$ are determined on admitted well-posed branches by the complete initial histories and boundary conditions. For ordinary pair-provenance cases, the "entanglement" begins with the fact that $\Gamma_A$ and $\Gamma_B$ are jointly constrained; that bookkeeping record gates the Bell response but does not by itself create a Bell violation. Bell-level nonfactorizability requires the coupled apparatus-response law on the declared $c_f$ coordination channel. Special black-hole and horizon-interface cases may additionally admit effective connected-geometry descriptions, but those belong to the strong-field geometry program rather than to ordinary pair provenance by default.

**Epistemic level (Physical Observer).** The PO has access only to coarse-grained observables (effective fields, detector clicks). Unable to track the full microstate, the PO uses an effective density matrix $\rho_{AB}$; its entanglement must follow from the reconstructed state, not from ignorance alone. The PO interprets correlations as "entanglement" and the resolution of metastability as "collapse." These are accurate operational descriptions but do not require ontological indeterminacy. The selected Bell route nevertheless proposes live nonlocal response dependence relative to the effective photon cone.

The persistent philosophical puzzles of entanglement—how can a measurement "here" instantaneously affect a system "there"?—are relocated by this reading rather than solved by assertion. There is no instantaneous effect. A successful Bell closure must show that the pair-provenance domain and the coupled apparatus-response law on the $c_f$ coordination channel push forward to the tested joint law, with the comparison requiring ordinary observer communication.

## Observables and Falsifiability

**Working closure route:** Ordinary entanglement correlations should be derived from a deterministic pair-provenance record established at a shared source event, maintained through path-history structure, and resolved by apparatus-response kernels coupled through the live substrate-causal $c_f$ coordination channel gated by that provenance, with no faster-than-$c_f$ influence and no controllable observer signaling. Special black-hole entanglement can carry effective connected-geometry meaning at the horizon-interface level, but that is a separate strong-field case rather than a general rule for arbitrary entanglement.

**Assumptions:**
- The kinematic snapshot $\Gamma(T)$ and the required admitted histories are definite; future prediction also requires well-posed evolution and boundary data.
- Conservation constraints at the source event constrain the joint hidden-variable distribution; the Bell gate must derive the remaining measure structure rather than assume it.
- Measurement is a threshold crossing in the coupled substrate response; any distant substrate input is the declared $c_f$ coordination channel, with no faster-than-$c_f$ input and no controllable observer signaling.
- Measurement independence holds (no superdeterminism).

**Closure Targets and Constraints:**
- Bell gate: derive the pair-provenance ledger, the coupled apparatus-response law on the $c_f$ coordination channel, and the observer-level compression that reproduce the tested Bell correlations with no faster-than-$c_f$ influence and no observer-level signaling.
- Measurement-independence guardrail: report $\Delta_{\mathrm{MI}}^{\mathrm{prov}}$ for any pair-provenance simulation or analytic Bell packet, and do not count a correlation fit as successful if it requires setting-dependent hidden-variable preparation.
- Residual reporting: report $\Delta_{\mathrm{NS}}^{A}$, $\Delta_{\mathrm{NS}}^{B}$, and $\Delta_{\mathrm{Bell}}$ alongside any claimed Bell-pair recovery.
- Photon-polarization gate: for entangled photon tests, Gate B must recover the transverse analyzer statistics and no-signaling behavior before the note may claim operational equivalence with quantum mechanics.
- Decoherence timescales for entangled assemblies are expected to scale with the local Noether sea density and temperature, but the quantitative law remains a validation target.
- No signaling: local choice at one wing must not change the distant marginal outside an allowed observer communication channel, including the interval between the effective photon cone and the proposed $c_f$ cone.

**Failure Modes:**
- If a specified realization predicts a remote-setting dependence of an observable marginal contrary to the tested no-signaling constraint, that realization fails. A verified experimental signaling effect would instead challenge the retained no-signaling premise and require comparison with all candidate models.
- A statistically established CHSH value **exceeding** the Tsirelson bound ($|S| = 2\sqrt{2}$), for normalized dichotomic local quantum observables and a common trial ensemble without setting-dependent selection, would contradict that quantum model class. Calibration, selection, and statistical uncertainty must be included before assigning this conclusion.
