## Manuscript-to-source and analytical checks

### Phase return and minimal period

Subtracting constant phases at times separated by a positive period gives exactly the simultaneous integer-turn condition in §2.1. With frequencies equal to integer multiples of a positive base, their greatest common divisor gives the stated minimum period of the nonconstant phase vector. The sufficiency is immediate by divisibility. For necessity, any period makes every integer coefficient times the normalized period an integer; a Bézout combination of those coefficients makes their gcd times that normalized period an integer as well. Zero coefficients impose no extra constraint, and the manuscript excludes the all-zero vector from this minimum-period formula.

This is phase-vector return. The manuscript correctly allows an observation map to identify or erase phases and distinguishes return of the visible image from return of direction, rate and required delayed history. The 4:2:1 example has primitive winding vector 4,2,1 over the slow carrier cycle. The outside excerpt supports its branch-specific frequency indexing and denial of radius order or a generic doubling law.

### Helix and breathing maps

The §1.2 displacement follows by one full trigonometric turn and unchanged linear drift. Nonzero drift prevents absolute spatial closure; zero drift is a circle; subtracting translation yields a relative return. Neither trajectory is asserted to solve the master equation.

For the §2.2 breathing example, the positive-radius inequality avoids an origin crossing. Multiplying the breathing cosine by the rotation sine/cosine gives the carrier and sum/difference components by the product-to-sum identities. Hence it is a two-phase construction with sidebands rather than automatically the two-Cartesian-harmonic Lissajous form. Commensurate phases provide a common period, while minimal visible period still depends on the map. The source’s suggested breathing role is preserved without assuming an admitted physical mode.

### Irrational flow and image density

For two nonzero constant frequencies with irrational ratio, a common positive phase period would force a rational ratio, so none exists. The stated return-section argument reduces density to irrational circle rotation; continuous time supplies the remaining phase coordinate. With a continuous map from the compact phase torus into the Euclidean coordinate space, the trajectory image lies in a closed compact image, and density in the domain approximates every image point. This justifies the closure-of-image equality in §2.3.

The planar independent cosine/sine map has a rectangular image; a torus embedding has a toroidal one; a spherical map must declare its actual image. The manuscript does not infer a physical torus from phase-space density. It also avoids equating density with exact visits, thermal mixing, a probability law or return of a full delayed state.

### Finite detuning

Over q positive rotation cycles, the stated constant-ratio model advances the breathing phase by an integer p-turn contribution plus the displayed residual proportional to q times detuning. The manuscript identifies this as unwrapped mismatch, with visible phase compared modulo a turn. A nonzero rational detuning can have a later longer return and an irrational one can produce arbitrarily close finite-window returns; neither possibility is misidentified as an exact lock at the approximate candidate period.

Every positive finite tolerance around a rational ratio contains irrationals. The source’s bounded-denominator label therefore cannot establish exact rationality, permanent lock or a positive-width family of retained physical histories. The text preserves this limitation and does not rerun or certify the implementation.
