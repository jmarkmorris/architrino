The reviewer independently assessed the explanatory deductions without running a query engine. A content hash cannot supply missing text; snapshot-only read therefore needs the stored content view. Equal record counts do not imply byte identity, and repeated deterministic builds cannot certify the currency of their input. Unicode-character budgets and UTF-8 response-byte budgets constrain different objects, particularly when envelopes and metadata consume bytes. A public digest contains no server-held secret and therefore does not itself authenticate a cursor or publisher. These are elementary representation and logic observations, not new measurements of the repository implementation.

For a graph with equal-cost edges, processing nodes by breadth-first depth and suppressing later visits gives a shortest path within the declared visible traversal, with the stated deterministic expansion order choosing among ties. Hidden intermediate nodes and hidden evidence invalidate their paths; neither page continuation nor a 256-node cap supplies whole-graph or scientific completeness. The report does not independently execute the source-declared graph oracle or prove the code follows this rule. Likewise one-reference replacement of a validated pair explains the stated atomic observation contract, but the retained same-content rollback does not independently test changed-content restoration.

The empirical boundaries remain particularly important. Eight benchmark targets establish no universal recall rate. A zero-scoring-record fallback can admit new results, so unchanged successful fixture bytes do not establish unchanged rankings for every possible query. Safe telemetry deliberately omits arguments and bodies, precluding their independent reconstruction; discovery/listing does not establish calls. A source-reported health-positive/authentication-negative case explains why readiness alone is inadequate for connectivity, while a recent poll is still insufficient for two genuine remote-client transcripts. Source limits and resource ceilings are policy, not profiled cost or capacity. These distinctions are preserved after the narrow family-label correction.
