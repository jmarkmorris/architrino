## Weaker EOM-Level Question

A narrower question may eventually be worth asking without changing the primitive inventory or acceleration law:

> When the existing delayed, radial, per-hit acceleration contributions are summed over a moving binary, ordered loop, or other minimal assembly, can the aggregate response acquire a component transverse to the assembly-to-receiver direction or a circulation-like dependence on source history?

For one regular causal hit, the acceleration direction is radial along the delayed source-receiver line. The weaker inquiry would not ask that hit to carry a primitive magnetic field. It would inspect whether multiple radial hits, sampled at different source times and from moving assembly sites, produce an effective antisymmetric or transverse response only after aggregation.

Plainly: many individually radial contributions can sum to a non-radial assembly response because they arrive from different delayed directions. That is a possible precursor to magnetic behavior, not magnetism at the point level.

The claim level is speculation constrained by the existing EOM. A useful future check would be read-only: decompose one accepted evolved assembly's receiver-side acceleration record into longitudinal, transverse, even, and odd-under-motion-reversal parts without adding a new term. Failure to produce a stable transverse or circulation-like aggregate would reject that assembly and regime as a magnetic precursor; success would still be only an effective assembly result requiring independent electrodynamics comparison.

This is not queued. Reconsider it only when an accepted evolved assembly and a clear independent moving-source benchmark are available, or when another live proof lane produces a concrete transverse-response identity.

## Assembly-Level Medium Picture

Permittivity and permeability are naturally interpreted as bulk response coefficients rather than properties of the Euclidean void. Every laboratory material is an overlay of matter assemblies within the dense Noether sea. The overlay causally changes the nearby sea through its wakes, boundaries, motion, orientation, and polarization. Effective electromagnetic coefficients may therefore characterize the joint material and local-sea state:

$$
\left(
\epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}},
\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}
\right)
=
\mathcal C_\gamma
\left[
\Theta_{\mathrm{sea}},
\Theta_{\mathrm{overlay}},
\mathcal H_{\partial\Omega}
\right].
$$

Here $\Theta_{\mathrm{sea}}$ records local sea density, cadence, orientation, strain, and delay response; $\Theta_{\mathrm{overlay}}$ records embedded material assemblies; and $\mathcal H_{\partial\Omega}$ records incoming causal and boundary history. In a linear homogeneous observer regime the familiar comparison is

$$
c_\gamma^2
=
\frac{1}{\epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}}.
$$

Plainly: vacuum coefficients can describe the quiescent Noether sea, while water or glass coefficients describe that sea after a material population has loaded and reorganized it. Empty geometry need not possess either property.

The same hierarchy plausibly extends to gravitation. A central matter assembly is a persistent boundary condition that polarizes and loads the surrounding [Noether Sea](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/spacetime/noether-sea.md#refractive-gravity-and-effective-metric), changing its density, stress, cadence, and effective signal response. It is plausible—but not derived—that position-dependent $\epsilon_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm{eff}}$ reparameterize part of that photon-channel response. One shared constitutive law would have to recover delay, refraction, impedance, clock response, and material boundaries without per-observable retuning.

Physical acceleration raises a related but distinct question. An accelerating coordinate chart does not change the sea. A physically driven apparatus has support or propulsion histories that may impose a directional, time-dependent sea load, perhaps requiring tensor rather than scalar response coefficients. Whether the local accelerated-apparatus readout matches a gravitationally loaded readout is a recovery target; it does not imply identical sea microstates, and tidal gradients or response times may distinguish them.

Plainly: material loading, gravitational loading, and physical acceleration may be different states of one assembly medium. That is the stronger home for the permittivity/permeability idea, but it is not an active electrodynamics task in this parked lane.

## Parked Primitive-Dyon Counterfactual

### Core Idea And Objection

A hypothetical point magnetic charge shares a tempting source analogy with an electrino or positrino: point emission, spherical expansion, and inverse-square flux density. A point transceiver carrying both electric and magnetic charge is called a **dyon** in the standard comparison literature. A naive primitive inventory would contain four sign combinations:

1. positive-north;
2. positive-south;
3. negative-north; and
4. negative-south.

Here `north` and `south` are provisional magnetic-charge signs, not the ends of an assembled bar magnet. The central problem is unchanged: a moving spherical point source does not visibly select the sideways direction associated with an observer-level magnetic response. Adding a magnetic cross-product rule would insert the desired result rather than derive it.

Plainly: four flavors are easy to name, but they do not explain where the sideways acceleration comes from.

The counterfactual would also require a derived reason that free magnetic charge is absent, a finite causal acceleration law for all electric-electric, magnetic-magnetic, and cross sectors, a controlled linear superposition limit, and consistent reaction and exposure accounts. These burdens make the proposal look like an imported assembly-level theory expressed at the wrong level.

### Schwinger 1969: Relevant First-Page Signal

The available evidence is only the first page of Julian Schwinger's *A Magnetic Model of Matter*, *Science* 165 (August 22, 1969), p. 757. Schwinger explicitly presents a speculative hypothesis, not a derived particle model.

The relevant proposal is that electric-magnetic charge reciprocity makes the elementary magnetic charge large in the normalization fixed by the observed electric unit. Opposite magnetic charges would then be difficult to separate, allowing ordinary matter to remain magnetically neutral even if constituents carry both charges. Dual-charged constituents could also carry fractional electric charge while magnetically neutral composites expose integral electric charge.

Plainly: primitive magnetic charge would not necessarily imply observable free monopoles; it might be hidden in neutral assemblies. The supplied page does not derive the separation mechanism or any acceleration dynamics.

Schwinger also suggests that electric-to-magnetic charge ratios participate in CP violation, but the argument continues beyond the supplied page. The only usable prompt is that a dyon inventory may need a derived ratio, orientation, or conjugation structure rather than two freely assigned signs.

### Witten 1979: Relevant Charge Constraints

Edward Witten's *Dyons of Charge $e\theta/2\pi$* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(79)90838-4)) supplies two well-founded standard-theory comparison results. In the paper's conventions, two charge pairs obey the Dirac-Schwinger-Zwanziger relation

$$
q_1g_2-q_2g_1=2\pi n,
\qquad n\in\mathbb Z,
$$

and, in Witten's declared gauge-theory model with $\theta$ as the only source of CP violation, the monopole sector has

$$
q
=
e\left(n-\frac{\theta}{2\pi}\right),
\qquad n\in\mathbb Z.
$$

Plainly: electric and magnetic labels need not be independent. Pairwise compatibility can restrict which combinations coexist, and one shared phase parameter can shift the exposed electric charge of a magnetic sector.

Only the comparison questions transfer: whether a candidate inventory has a native pairwise compatibility rule; whether assembly phase or retained history can shift exposed charge; and whether electric and magnetic signs have distinct conjugation maps. Witten's gauge fields, Higgs mechanism, vacuum angle, and quantization machinery are not architrino-level premises. The paper supplies no transverse acceleration mechanism.

### dos Santos 2015: Radial/Transverse Comparison Structure

Renato P. dos Santos's pedagogical classical-mechanics paper [*Magnetic monopoles and dyons revisited*](https://arxiv.org/abs/1503.00499) packages each particle's standard electric and magnetic labels into two pairings:

$$
q=e_1e_2+\frac{g_1g_2}{c^2},
\qquad
\kappa=e_1g_2-g_1e_2.
$$

In the paper's assumed Maxwell-dual Lorentz dynamics, the symmetric pairing $q$ multiplies the radial term, while the antisymmetric pairing $\kappa$ multiplies the velocity-dependent transverse term. If the two electric-magnetic label pairs are proportional, $\kappa=0$ and the transverse term disappears. Thus four sign combinations by themselves do not explain magnetic response; the response depends on an oriented relation between two charge pairs.

Plainly: the useful feature is not the dyon ontology. It is the clean separation of an attraction-or-repulsion channel from a sideways channel, plus a precise null case in which the sideways channel must vanish.

The same assumed transverse term yields the conserved comparison quantity

$$
\mathbf J
=
\mathbf L+\frac{\mu_0}{4\pi}\kappa\widehat{\mathbf r},
$$

which confines the classical relative orbit to a Poincaré cone. The paper also shows that radial binding still requires the attractive $q$ sector; nonzero $\kappa$ redirects the orbit but does not supply the attraction. These are useful observer-level diagnostics: a future assembly derivation of a stable odd transverse response should be checked for an associated corrected angular-momentum-like invariant and nonplanar geometry, while binding and transverse behavior should remain separately accounted.

Plainly: if delayed assembly geometry ever produces a magnetic-like sideways response, it may leave both a trajectory signature and a conservation-account signature. Neither signature may be imposed on the Master Equation as a premise.

For the parked weaker EOM question, a possible comparison observable is

$$
\mathbf a_{\perp,\mathrm{odd}}
=
\frac{1}{2}
\left(\mathbf I-\widehat{\mathbf r}\widehat{\mathbf r}^{\mathsf T}\right)
\left(
\mathbf a_{\mathrm{eff}}[\mathcal H_+]
-
\mathbf a_{\mathrm{eff}}[\mathcal H_-]
\right),
$$

where $\mathcal H_+$ and $\mathcal H_-$ are consistently paired assembly histories related by motion reversal, not merely two instantaneous velocity labels. A persistent nonzero value would isolate an odd transverse aggregate for later comparison; zero would reject that assembly and regime as this kind of precursor. This diagnostic is not queued and imports none of the paper's dyon law.

The orbit-closure arithmetic, rainbow and glory scattering, relativistic extension, and semiclassical quantization do not currently map to a live $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ question and are not retained here.

### Revisit Conditions

Do not reactivate the primitive-dyon branch merely because another magnetic charge model is interesting. Reconsider it only if at least one of the following occurs:

- a native causal-history derivation produces a transverse point-level acceleration direction without importing a magnetic cross product;
- an assembly-level derivation fails for a specific, demonstrated reason that primitive magnetic exposure would resolve;
- a derived charge-accounting or symmetry result requires a second primitive sign; or
- a concrete observer-level constraint cannot be recovered from assembly and Noether sea response but is naturally satisfied by one fully specified dyonic law.

If reopened, the first artifact would be an impossibility-or-viability screen, not an implementation: it would test transverse geometry, magnetic neutralization, charge compatibility, superposition, and null monopole exposure together.

Plainly: the idea remains available, but evidence—not curiosity—must pull it back into active work.
