# Scattering Histories and Positive Geometry

## 1. A Geometric Comparison at the End of a Physical Explanation

### 1.1. Two different starting points

The amplituhedron comparison asks whether a recovered scattering description could possess a positive-geometric organization. It does not propose a material polytope, a new primitive law or an identification of geometry with an architrino assembly. In $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$, the starting objects are identities, polarity and delayed path histories in Euclidean space and absolute time; the master equation determines acceleration. An effective scattering amplitude, its on-shell variables and a canonical differential form are possible outputs whose construction remains a separate obligation.

The [preserved comparison](amplituhedron.md) describes the original amplituhedron as a formulation for planar $\mathcal N=4$ supersymmetric Yang–Mills scattering. The physical theory, the geometric object and the broader program of positive geometries are different concepts. The special theory is the declared comparison domain, not the observed particle theory or a substrate premise. The source's literature survey and research-status statements are historical support; this exposition does not certify their present external status.

Methodological resemblance is weaker than a mathematical bridge. Both a delayed-history program and a positive-geometric formulation can seek an explanation of familiar effective structure, but that shared aim proves no identification of their variables, measures or dynamics. Even exact agreement on a limited set of scattering observables would not uniquely determine the underlying ontology.

### 1.2. The chain that must remain intact

A candidate recovery begins with a finite interaction history containing incoming assemblies, transient channels, outgoing assemblies, recoil, remnants, wakes and Noether sea exchange. It then needs preparation and detector records, an outcome measure, and an effective amplitude with its conventions. Only after those objects exist can a positive-geometry representation be tested.

These dependencies are not an invertible sequence. An outcome distribution does not in general determine the complex amplitude that produced it. For example, replacing a single-channel amplitude by $e^{i\chi}\mathcal M$ leaves its squared magnitude unchanged. Relative phases require interference information, and an overall phase may remain conventional. A list of cross sections therefore cannot supply an amplitude merely by taking square roots. The source's recovery ladder is a sequence of obligations, not a theorem that each displayed output reconstructs its predecessor or successor uniquely.

The physical comparison is demanding precisely because one history must account for rates, angular and polarization dependence, thresholds, allowed and forbidden channels, form factors, resonance behavior and intermediate-channel factorization. Independent retuning for every observable would replace that history with unrelated fits. Positive geometry is an additional test of support and boundary organization, conditional on a regime where the target geometry is defined.

## 2. Positive Geometry and Its Boundaries

### 2.1. A positive image and a differential form

The source presents the tree construction schematically as

$$
Y=CZ,\qquad C\in G_+(k,n).
$$

Here the positive Grassmannian consists of appropriately oriented plane representatives with positive ordered maximal minors, and the external data $Z$ must also satisfy the declared positive-data conditions with dimensions fixed by the chosen construction. Positivity of the plane representative alone does not supply that external-data requirement. Neither condition follows from positive causal-root weights, positive probabilities or a partition of histories into strata. Those are different mathematical conditions.

The associated canonical form is a differential form with logarithmic boundary singularities and prescribed residues. Its coefficients depend on coordinates; the geometric form transforms with the corresponding Jacobian. The term “volume” in this setting is not ordinary Euclidean volume, and the form is not automatically a normalized probability density. The source describes its relation to a tree amplitude or loop integrand within the special comparison theory. Extracting physical conventions, integrating a loop integrand and constructing an infrared-safe observable remain further steps before an experimental comparison.

### 2.2. What a decomposition may change

Different valid triangulations can provide different intermediate expressions for the same form. Their artificial internal boundaries must cancel; genuine external boundaries retain the prescribed residues. This is a representation-level account of physical singularities and factorization. It does not remove causal histories from nature or establish universal locality and unitarity from an arbitrary stratified set.

An elementary one-dimensional example makes the cancellation concrete. For an oriented interval with endpoints $a<b$, define the comparison form

$$
\omega_{[a,b]}=\frac{(b-a)\,dx}{(x-a)(b-x)}.
$$

For an interior point $c$, direct partial fractions give

$$
\omega_{[a,c]}+\omega_{[c,b]}=\omega_{[a,b]}.
$$

The pole at the inserted point cancels. The result follows by writing the first coefficient as $1/(x-a)+1/(c-x)$ and the second as $1/(x-c)+1/(b-x)$. This is an algebraic illustration of internal-boundary cancellation, not an amplituhedron calculation or a retained scattering carrier. It demonstrates why reproducing each local piece is insufficient if their common artificial boundary survives in the sum.

### 2.3. Form, amplitude and probability are different types

For the interval from zero to one,

$$
\omega_{[0,1]}=\frac{dx}{x(1-x)},\qquad
\int_\delta^{1-\delta}\omega_{[0,1]}
=2\log\frac{1-\delta}{\delta},\qquad 0<\delta<\frac12.
$$

The integral diverges as the cutoff tends to zero. A finite normalized history probability cannot push forward to this unregulated density on the whole open interval: pushforward preserves total mass, while the displayed density has infinite mass. This explicit example does not exclude every relation between histories and canonical forms. It shows that a proposed equality must specify its measure type, domain, boundary prescription and normalization before it can be assessed.

An oriented or complex amplitude form carries information beyond an ordinary positive event probability. Taking its absolute value or normalizing a truncated density changes the object and requires justification. Conversely, squaring an amplitude and applying flux, phase-space and detector factors produces an observational object with different units and dependencies. A recovery must explain those connections, not suppress them in one equality sign.

## 3. From an Amplitude to an Experiment

### 3.1. Preparation and the observable

At the effective level an $S$-matrix maps asymptotic incoming states to outgoing states. Such states idealize a preparation and an observation outside the interaction region. A differential cross-section comparison has the schematic form

$$
d\sigma_{a\to b}
=\frac{1}{\mathcal F}|\mathcal M_{a\to b}|^2\,d\Pi_b,
$$

with declared incoming flux, phase-space measure, species and polarization conventions. Spin sums or averages, identical-particle factors, coherent alternatives and unresolved states belong to the same definition. A detector comparison additionally needs beam or parton distributions where applicable, event selection, acceptance, resolution and reconstruction. The displayed formula is an effective target; it does not postulate quantum probabilities at the architrino level.

Varying energy, angle, momentum transfer, polarization, incoming species and target state can constrain internal structure, interaction channels, selection rules and transient states. Resonance widths, branching fractions and form factors are complementary observables, rather than interchangeable descriptions of one measured number. Agreement across them restricts a microscopic explanation without proving it unique.

### 3.2. Prediction and alternative calculation methods

A predictive comparison fixes the theory, approximation, parameters and preparation; calculates the amplitude; constructs an observable; adds the experimental interface; and freezes the comparison and its fitting boundary before judging the signal data. Data collection or unblinding is followed by comparison under that protocol. A discrepancy can arise from calculation, effective-model, calibration or statistical assumptions; it is not automatically a new physical mechanism.

Within a common domain, diagrams, on-shell recursion, unitarity-based methods and positive geometry are alternative ways to obtain the same amplitude. They must agree at the same theory point, order and conventions. Changing representation alone cannot improve the truth of an incorrect underlying theory.

It can nevertheless make higher orders, larger final-state multiplicities, stable numerical evaluation or additional observables accessible. Symmetry, factorization and alternative representations can also expose mistakes. Such advantages require measured comparisons of time, memory, conditioning, accuracy or reachable calculation under declared conditions. An elegant geometry is not a performance measurement. Parton distributions, nonperturbative dynamics, hadronization, unknown parameters, detector calibration and experimental uncertainties remain separate limitations.

### 3.3. Broader mathematical aims

The preserved survey describes a program broader than faster calculation: classification of consistent interactions from masses, spins, symmetries and residues; direct use of on-shell data; and geometric or combinatorial explanations of cancellations and singularities. Extensions to finite color, nonplanar processes, less supersymmetry, massive particles, QCD and electroweak scattering, gravity and cosmological observables need their own domains and constructions.

The source's mass-and-spin, all-loop counting and cosmological-polytope examples are retained research references. They are not a newly verified universal formulation of realistic collider physics. An extension need not preserve one named geometry. Nor does “extension” mean improving an approximate fit of the original exact-domain expression: it means determining which organizing principles apply beyond that domain, through loop integration and into observable predictions.

## 4. A Conditional History-to-Geometry Bridge

### 4.1. Define the projection before its residual

The proposed map is

$$
\Psi:\mathcal H_{\mathrm{scat}}^{W,\mathrm{ret}}
\longrightarrow\mathcal K_{n,k},
$$

from retained finite-window histories to a declared effective kinematic chart. Its construction must preserve incoming, intermediate and outgoing identities, the common event account and the preparation/exposure/detector dependence. The chart cannot be selected after inspecting the desired minors merely to manufacture positivity.

The source writes a proposed pushforward-versus-canonical-form residual. Sections 2.3 and 1.2 explain why this is presently a typed comparison to construct, not a literal equality of an unspecified probability measure with an amplitude form. One possible probabilistic target would instead compare the pushed-forward event measure with an independently defined event measure built from the effective amplitude and the same observational conventions. If a form-level target is intended, it needs a separately derived amplitude-valued object and its orientation, chart and boundary rules. Neither option is constructed here, and choosing a convenient normalization does not derive it.

A quantitative residual also needs a common domain, norm or test-function class, units and error bound. Coordinate invariance means comparing the same form or measure under its correct transformation, not demanding unchanged component coefficients in every chart. Detector/exposure conventions and channel parameters must remain fixed during the comparison.

### 4.2. Factorization needs a physical intermediate channel

For a declared single effective channel and consistent normalization, the source's four-point target is

$$
\lim_{s\to0}sA_4=A_3^{\mathrm L}A_3^{\mathrm R}.
$$

Here $s$ is the local channel coordinate vanishing at the simple pole. A general physical channel may require a sum over admitted intermediate species or polarizations and their normalization factors. The compact product is therefore a restricted comparison, not a universal unsummed factorization formula or a statement about every loop singularity.

The retained-history side must supply the transient assembly or event channel responsible for that factorization, with recoil, remnants, wake and sea exchange on the same record. A spurious double pole, wrong residue or deliberately incorrect channel that passes the same test defeats the proposed bridge for that class. Copying a correct amplitude identity without the intermediate carrier satisfies no dynamical explanation.

### 4.3. What would constitute a discriminating comparison

A bounded mathematical comparison would combine one retained four-point history family, one source-derived projection, a physical simple-pole residue, a wrong-channel rejection and an alternative decomposition whose artificial boundary cancels. Ordered-minor positivity, properly transformed chart agreement and a predeclared residual tolerance would be additional requirements. The analytical residue reference must be independent of the implementation being tested; two wrappers around the same amplitude calculation establish implementation parity only.

Failure remains informative when it identifies a specific obstruction in the carrier, projection, positivity, factorization or effective measure. A failure of one bridge to a special planar theory does not by itself falsify $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$. Conversely, a successful alternative representation of an already known amplitude need not provide a new physical discriminator.

## 5. Scope of the Open Comparison

The retained assessment places positive geometry downstream of ordinary scattering recovery. Its proposed revisit conditions concern a retained four-point carrier, an accepted source-bound cross-section/form-factor account, an independently derived effective amplitude, a materially more applicable external construction, or a discriminator unavailable from the ordinary program. None is established by this manuscript. Historical statements about missing carriers are source-time assessments, not a fresh audit of every current owner.

The bridge would fail if positivity requires fitted target coordinates, physical boundaries lack admissible intermediate channels, residues or pole orders disagree, spurious boundaries survive, results change under legitimate chart/decomposition transformations, or detector/exposure/channel retuning is needed. Importing supersymmetry, planarity, momentum twistors, locality or unitarity as primitive substrate premises would also evade the intended question.

The comparison remains a conditional mathematical proposal. Its strongest present content is the separation of physical histories, observational measures, amplitudes and canonical forms, together with explicit tests connecting those levels. Original ownership, deferred research decisions, exact revisit conditions and the twelve historical discussion conclusions remain in the [preserved source](amplituhedron.md). Explanatory synthesis supplies no scientific execution or reactivation.
