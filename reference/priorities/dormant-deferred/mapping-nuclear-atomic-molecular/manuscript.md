# Nuclear, Atomic, and Molecular Assembly Records

## 1. A Hierarchy of Physical Assemblies

### 1.1 The shared explanatory form

Nuclei, atoms, bonds and biomolecular records introduce larger organizations of matter, not new primitive substances. In $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$, their proposed explanation begins with localized assemblies, polarity, Euclidean space, absolute time and delayed path-history interaction. The master equation supplies acceleration. Effective potentials, masses, orbitals, reaction coordinates and statistical readouts belong to higher-level descriptions that must be recovered from those histories.

Every example therefore needs more than a recognizable final shape. It combines an assembly state, a preparation and transition history, an account of exchanged quantities, a Noether sea or material environment, an observer benchmark and a falsifier. A nucleus must preserve hadronic structure while developing residual binding; an orbital must connect preparation to detector records; a catalyst must return to a usable state; and a repaired molecular record must retain the identities that make it the same record.

The [recovery synthesis](brainstorming.md) places these questions in one hierarchy. None of the developed molecular examples supplies a retained lower-level bond, turnover or repair history. Their completed contribution is a source-bound comparison design. The nuclear examples additionally contain reduced calculations and a proposed source-support chain, whose limited mathematical and physical scope remains explicit below.

### 1.2 State, transition and observation

A generic candidate contains participating assembly identities, causal-wake histories, exclusion or interface geometry, the local sea at a declared coarse-graining scale, a retained branch or basin, an event account, a finite window and detector response. The state and its observation are distinct. A measured line, crystal structure or reaction yield constrains an effective record; it does not directly reveal the primitive mechanism that produced it.

The comparison layer can use standard quantum, nuclear or biochemical mathematics under its declared assumptions. It cannot use those successful equations to define the substrate history and then count their reappearance as a derivation. Likewise, a reduced model swept over its own coefficients establishes properties of that model. Independent data, an independently authored reference or a mathematical proof must supply the second side of any correctness claim.

Shared environmental response is essential. Nuclear, atomic and molecular examples consume a common constitutive Noether sea response when their regimes overlap. They do not derive private versions to fit each line, molecule or residue. Numerical substrate instances use $c_f=1$; the historical MeV, femtometre, spectroscopic and biochemical values below are observer comparisons with their own unit dictionaries, not different numerical wake-speed assignments.

### 1.3 Accounting without duplication

For a declared event, a proposed assembly-level account has the form

$$
\sum_a\Delta\mathcal L_{E\mathbf p\mathbf J}^{(a)}=0,
$$

where the partition may include matter, radiation, recoil, sea, solvent and apparatus. The quantities must be derived or justified at that assembly level. Each contribution needs a source, destination, time convention and identity, and absent channels must be zero or below a stated bound. Recoil cannot be counted again if it is already included in matter momentum. Energy initially carried by a product and later deposited as heat must be tracked through successive states or fluxes rather than added twice as simultaneous final energy.

This is a conditional conservation requirement, not an independently supplied conserved functional for the delayed master equation. A bookkeeping identity cannot create a missing carrier or explain a reaction. It can, however, reject an explanation that loses a product, changes its source history or hides energy behind the word “binding.”

## 2. Nuclear Residuals and the Iron-Group Maximum

### 2.1 Binding above the nucleon scale

The effective nuclear energy decomposition is

$$
E_{\mathrm{nuc}}
=\sum_{a=1}^{A}M_a c_{\mathrm{eff}}^2
+E_{\mathrm{corr}}+E_{\mathrm{Coul}}+E_{\mathrm{excl}}
+E_{\mathrm{shell}}+E_{\mathrm{sea-pol}}.
$$

The terms distinguish nucleon reference energies, residual corridors, exposed electrical response, exclusion, shell/readout structure and sea polarization. They are not independent primitive forces. The proposed residual interaction must emerge from accepted proton and neutron envelopes and their common confinement/environmental response.

The [nuclear benchmark ladder](nuclear-binding-closure.md) requires a spin-indexed distinction before Coulomb is used: the proton–neutron triplet may bind while the singlet remains unbound; the identical-proton restrictions and Coulomb comparison then keep the ordinary diproton unbound. Further requirements are bounded binding per nucleon, favorable alpha-like organization of two protons and two neutrons, and beta stability with nucleon reference energies, electron/neutrino channels and medium changes accounted for together. A model that binds every pair equally does not satisfy this ladder.

The effective two-nucleon target separates

$$
V_{NN}^{(c)}(r)
=V_{\mathrm{excl}}^{(c)}(r)
+V_{\mathrm{Coul}}^{(c)}(r)
+V_{\mathrm{corr}}^{(c)}(r)
+V_{\mathrm{sea-pol}}^{(c)}(r),
$$

with channel label $c$. Signs, range, spin/orientation dependence and saturation require source support. Borrowing a familiar nuclear-potential shape and renaming its terms does not recover them.

### 2.2 Paired corridor relaxation

Let $\Gamma_{N_1}$ and $\Gamma_{N_2}(r)$ be closed color-corridor graphs of two color-singlet nucleon envelopes. The proposed residual is the difference between paired and isolated constrained energies:

$$
\Delta E_{\mathrm{corr}}^{NN}(r)
=\min_{\Theta_{12}\in\Pi_{\mathrm{singlet}}}
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}}[\Gamma_{N_1}\cup\Gamma_{N_2}(r),\Theta_{12}]
-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\min_{\Theta_i\in\Pi_{\mathrm{singlet}}}
\mathcal E_{\mathrm{conf}}[\Gamma_{N_i},\Theta_i].
$$

The minima require finite lower bounds, admissible configurations and attainment. Without attainment, an infimum can define a variational target but does not name a realized assembly. The same functional must also supply the open-corridor tension through the same axis, transverse-response and ambient sea data. The residual is permitted to feed the corridor and sea-polarization terms, not to replace exclusion, spin restrictions or the separately declared Coulomb comparison.

A finite residual must decay as the two accepted singlet envelopes separate. That decay and the absence of an open-color far field are related obligations, but not identical assertions. Section 3 gives the conditional bound that could connect them.

$$
W_c=\frac{N_{\mathrm{share},c}}{N_{\mathrm{ret},c}}P_c,

\qquad

\left(\widehat{\Delta\phi}^{\,2}

The counts must be defined with $N_{\mathrm{ret},c}>0$, and the hatted mismatches use declared positive comparison scales. The initial source rows use compatibility fractions $4/4$ and $1/4$ with zero mismatches, yielding $(W,M)=(1,0)$ for the proton–neutron row and $(1/4,3/4)$ for the proton–proton row. \qquad

The maximization requires a nonempty declared set. Bounded corridor coordination and sea reward compete with surface loss, Coulomb stress, asymmetry and packing costs. These standard nuclear scalings are comparison grammar. The graph version limits local degree and per-nucleon corridor capacity, rewards compatible near-neighbor interfaces, penalizes mismatch and excess degree, keeps proton electrical stress separate, and bounds sea polarization. A shared global coefficient set avoids one kind of element-by-element tuning but does not by itself derive its coefficients or provide an independent reference.

### 2.5 Fusion, fission and surviving constituents

For a symmetric split of a reduced Coulomb term,

$$
2a_C\frac{(Z/2)^2}{(A/2)^{1/3}}
=2^{-2/3}a_C\frac{Z^2}{A^{1/3}}.

The daughter pair thus carries about 63% of that parent term under the same radius scaling. ## 3. Finite Tails, Projection Bounds, and Evidence Scope

### 3.1 A conditional route to no open color

On one declared source domain, define

$$
\mathcal T_{NN}(R)
=\sup_{r\ge R}|\Delta E_{\mathrm{corr}}^{NN}(r)|.
$$

The proposed implication is

$$
0\le\mathcal N_{\mathrm{open}}(R)
\le K_{\mathrm{open}}\mathcal T_{NN}(R)^2,
\qquad
K_{\mathrm{open}}<\infty,
\qquad
\mathcal T_{NN}(R)\longrightarrow0
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathcal N_{\mathrm{open}}(R)\longrightarrow0.
$$

The implication is elementary once one uniform finite constant and the same-domain inequality are established. It does not establish either premise. A finite constant chosen separately for each radius is insufficient if it grows fast enough to offset the tail. The proposed factorization $K_{\mathrm{open}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{open}}\kappa_{\mathrm{open}}^2$ therefore needs a controlled projection norm and surface measure across the limit, not only at one compact normalized audit surface.

The source's exponential-tail route fixes a uniform large-distance envelope, a corridor weight and a decay-versus-weight-growth inequality. ### 3.2 Idempotence and norm control

An idempotent projection is not necessarily an orthogonal projection. On a Euclidean two-dimensional space,

$$
P=\begin{pmatrix}1&a\\0&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad P^2=P,
\qquad \|P\|_2=\sqrt{1+a^2}>1
\quad(a\ne0).
$$

The norm follows from $PP^{\mathsf T}=\operatorname{diag}(1+a^2,0)$. Thus idempotence alone does not justify the unit norm used in a normalized projection-support record. Orthogonality in the declared inner product, or an explicit independent operator-norm bound, would supply the missing justification. A finite-dimensional matrix is bounded, but its bound need not be one; a “finite domain” also needs its function space and norm specified.

The [retained projection-support record](../../../../scripts/nuclear-atomic/open-color-projection-surface-support-retained-evidence.v1.json) assigns a normalized finite audit surface and unit projection bound while explicitly withholding the full no-open-color and event conclusions. Its recorded acceptance is a support-level repository disposition. The counterexample above qualifies what follows mathematically from its idempotence wording; it does not rewrite that record or certify its unresolved physical operator.

### 3.3 Variational extraction and refinement

The proposed confinement-tension extraction is an infimum of a source-bound functional over admissible fields and geometric variables. A claimed minimizer needs suitable existence assumptions, such as a nonempty compact admissible class and a proper lower-semicontinuous functional, or an appropriate coercive weak-compactness argument. Lower bounds and stationarity alone do not prove attainment or global minimality.

Agreement at two resolutions is a bounded comparison. It does not prove convergence as resolution tends to zero, and replaying a retained value through the same producer is not an independent reference. The extraction must preserve the same transverse stiffness, excitation potential, density, delay, axis and response inputs while carrying its variational certificate and refinement domain. Accepted input rows cannot substitute for that extraction.

### 4.3 Heat is an ensemble and material readout

The familiar effective power relation is

under the constant-rate ensemble comparison and a declared deposition fraction. Ground-state nuclear gamma and conversion rows can be zero without asserting that subsequent material or atomic relaxation emits nothing.

### 4.4 Escape probability and rate

The proposed microscopic target has the approximate form

$$

$$

Here $\nu_{\mathrm{hit}}$ must be an attempt frequency and $P_{\mathrm{esc}}$ a suitably conditioned per-attempt escape probability, with the renewal, rare-event or hazard assumptions that justify their product. A fraction escaping during an arbitrary finite window is not automatically that probability. Under a constant-hazard model, for example, the window fraction is $1-e^{-\lambda_\alpha\Delta T}$, not generally $\lambda_\alpha$ or $P_{\mathrm{esc}}$. The half-life relation itself assumes the corresponding exponential-survival description.

The missing physical record must supply the parent basin, alpha-like separatrix, active causal-root branch, history measure, attempt process, daughter, recoil, de-excitation outcome, material cell and common sea update. It fails if rate and branch accounts need different parent rules, if heat hides an unmatched residual, or if a gamma line is treated as acceptance of its photon-source dynamics. ## 5. Atomic Labels and Detector Records

### 5.1 Effective orbital mathematics

The standard orbital address is

$$
(n,\ell,m_\ell,m_s),\qquad
n=1,2,\ldots,\quad
0\le\ell\le n-1,\quad
-\ell\le m_\ell\le\ell,\quad
m_s=\pm\frac12.
$$

The labels $n$, $\ell$ and $m_\ell$ are integers. The familiar principal-family convention and its radial interpretation belong to the declared atomic comparison regime. For a central effective envelope,

$$
\psi_{n\ell m_\ell}(r,\theta,\phi)
=R_{n\ell}(r)Y_\ell^{m_\ell}(\theta,\phi),
\qquad
\hat{\mathbf L}^{2}\psi
=\ell(\ell+1)\hbar^2\psi,
\qquad
\hat L_z\psi=m_\ell\hbar\psi.
$$

These are effective-model equations, not electron trajectories or primitive probability-cloud ontology. Central symmetry supports the angular separation; Coulomb-specific energy degeneracy or node formulas require their additional regime rather than being inferred for every central potential.

The letters $s,p,d,f$ correspond to $\ell=0,1,2,3$. A subshell has $2\ell+1$ spatial orbitals and the familiar spin-resolved capacity

$$
N_{\mathrm{subshell}}=2(2\ell+1),
$$

giving 2, 6, 10 and 14. Standard configurations and noble-gas shorthand organize the effective occupancy record. These capacities remain an exclusion/spin recovery target; repeating them in an encoding is not their primitive derivation. ### 8.3 Integrity, reset and functional discrimination

For preserved identity and duplex-facing fields $K$, define normalized component discrepancies with predeclared tolerances and take

$$
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{integrity}}

d_k(\mathcal I^{\mathrm{out}},\mathcal I^{\mathrm{target}}),
\qquad
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{integrity}}\le1,

Categorical identity fields require their stated equality or failure rule; geometric distances must be normalized before sharing the unit threshold.
