## Reverse audit and direct mathematical consistency

The manuscript's additions are explicitly conceptual distinctions or conditional definitions. The waveform/symbol/meaning distinction, calibration/correction limits, latency/payload separation and provenance condition sharpen the source's preservation requirement without asserting measured performance. Exact symbol recovery does not establish truth, authorship, energy balance or recovery of arbitrary discarded information. These qualifications correctly prevent a stronger claim than the selected record supports.

For manuscript 50–87, fix the common metric space, admitted states, decoders, calibration/alignment and intended source record before observing the output. For a finite route, the triangle inequality gives

$$
d(r_n,r_\star)\le d(r_n,r_{n-1})+\cdots+d(r_1,r_0)+d(r_0,r_\star)\le\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\varepsilon_i+d(r_0,r_\star).
$$

This direct derivation independently checks the candidate's formula, index ranges and source-error term. It requires all compared records to belong to the same metric space and the assumed local bounds to hold on the realized route. No stochastic independence is needed for this deterministic inequality. A measured or validated right-hand side no larger than the separate endpoint tolerance is sufficient to certify that tolerance through this bound; a larger bound alone does not prove endpoint failure. The inequality does not assert either outcome in an actual phone call. Finite nonnegative errors and tolerance allow exact equality as a special case. Merely changing representation does not permit pressure, symbol error, delay and semantic scores in unlike units to be added.

The transition/decoder notation is schematic rather than an asserted autonomous apparatus law. A noisy realization can be represented by expanded state, while probability of success requires a separately declared distribution or validated joint bounds. Changing projections across stages requires actual comparison/reconstruction maps; deleting an endpoint-required feature fails preservation unless another declared source restores it. The manuscript names these conditions rather than assuming a universal decoder.

Manuscript 68 fixes the source reference independently and warns against a decoder chosen after seeing the target. A held-out input needs a separately retained reference record; a code-consistency pass tests its declared error class and may not identify the intended message. These are appropriately stronger evidentiary requirements than self-agreement. Manuscript 89–93 separately requires delivery/persistence windows, correct lineage and other categorical/order conditions. High metric fidelity cannot compensate for late arrival or the wrong source. The argument is therefore conditional record comparison, not a general physical preservation law or an empirical verification.
