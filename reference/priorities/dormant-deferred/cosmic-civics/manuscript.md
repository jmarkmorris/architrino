# Cosmic Civics: Passage, Assertions, and the Limits of Maps

## 1. From Physical Description to Civic Questions

### 1.1. The subject of the inquiry

Cosmic Civics is an exploratory way to compare descriptions of activity with assertions about that activity. Its starting point is familiar: a map can show a place, an actor, a route, a use, or a boundary, while different sources attach different meanings to what it depicts. Occupancy, access, jurisdiction, responsibility, and exclusion are distinct questions even when they concern the same region. The inquiry extends that separation toward moving systems, changing operational locations, and hypothetical future claims about passage.

The [concept and scope account](concept-and-scope.md) supplies the framework developed here. The [exploratory account](brainstorming.md) supplies alternative record structures, map layers, examples, and unresolved questions. These are conceptual proposals, not a legal theory, policy program, implemented registry, or set of recommendations for a real dispute. The central issue is how to make an assertion and its limits visible without converting its representation into an entitlement.

A geographic information system provides the source's organizing analogy: separate thematic layers can depict different aspects of a region or activity. Cosmic Civics asks what additional distinctions a claims-oriented record and display might need across time, scale, domain, and declared reference frame. This analogy is not an assessment of the capabilities or limits of existing GIS software. It introduces an information problem: the description of a movement, the assertion attached to it, and the authority claimed for that assertion must remain distinguishable.

### 1.2. The working chain and its discontinuity

The source organizes its questions through a four-link chain:

> Absolute Timespace → Assembly Architecture → Passage → Claim

The links have different standing. In the local account, absolute timespace retains the established corpus meaning of the formal product of absolute time and the Euclidean void. That vocabulary supplies a physical background when the full product matters. It does not identify a legal geography, ownership substrate, curved relativistic spacetime, or rule selecting an operational frame.

Assembly Architecture is a proposed bridge label for the physical and organizational structures through which actors, vehicles, habitats, instruments, institutions, and records are represented. It adds no canonical tier to Architrino Assembly Architecture and supplies no theorem that assigns rights to an actor. Passage is a proposed description of movement or occupancy in a declared region and interval, with chart, scale, resolution, and provenance. Claim is a source-bound assertion concerning a region, activity, passage, actor, or record, accompanied by its declared authority.

The arrows organize questions rather than derive conclusions. In particular, the transition from Passage to Claim contains an explicit break: a path does not establish permission, trespass, title, priority, liability, or a toll. Describing occupancy or physical capability cannot, by itself, answer the legal or moral question. The method would fail its own purpose if the chain encouraged the reader to infer entitlement from motion.

### 1.3. Three kinds of statement

The sources distinguish established corpus language, proposed conceptual framing, and open legal or philosophical questions. Established vocabulary remains at its existing scope; the proposed chain, thematic layers, and record ideas remain proposals; authority, recognition, enforceability, duties, and moral justification remain questions. Nothing in the framework changes the ontology, dynamics, recovery obligations, or acceptance status of $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$.

This classification also separates a statement's subject from its evidence. An observed crossing, a reconstruction of an earlier route, a predicted encounter, a proposed journey, and a deliberately hypothetical passage do not have the same status. Likewise, an assertion recorded by a registry is different from recognition by a named institution. Neither a coordinate nor a status label can supply the missing justification.

## 2. The Registry and the Atlas

### 2.1. What a registry would record

The proposed claims registry would preserve a designated source record for an assertion. Its authority would be internal to that proposed information system: it would identify what was recorded and how it was sourced. This meaning of “authoritative record” does not give the registry legal authority over the asserted object, actor, route, activity, or region.

The candidate entry would retain eight information families: the claimant or asserting source; the object, region, route, activity, or passage asserted about; the declared reference frame or chart and its provenance; duration or applicable interval; asserted jurisdiction or rule source; evidence and provenance; conditions, qualifications, objections, or dependencies; and status. Candidate statuses include hypothetical, asserted, disputed, recognized by a named authority, rejected, withdrawn, and unknown.

These fields are discussion material rather than a database schema. They describe information that an eventual comparison might need, without deciding a legal definition of claim or passage. Even “recognized by a named authority” would record a specified institution's act. It would not certify universal recognition or resolve whether that institution's authority is contested.

### 2.2. What an atlas would show

The proposed claims atlas would display selected registry records together with relevant physical or operational context. Its candidate layers include passage, jurisdiction, access, exclusion, liability, and use. A display could change scale, time interval, uncertainty representation, or declared reference frame while retaining the sources of the assertions it shows.

One registry entry could support several views: a land-parcel view, a water-route view, an altitude-and-time airspace view, or an outer-space operational-chart view. Conversely, one displayed region could contain several conflicting entries. A view is therefore a selection and presentation of records, rather than a replacement for their provenance or a decision between them.

The distinction matters when visual prominence is mistaken for authority. A brighter color, larger label, earlier layer, sharper boundary, or visible route intersection does not create, rank, recognize, validate, or enforce a right. The proposed atlas would expose disagreement and uncertainty for comparison; it would not adjudicate them. Keeping the record separate from its view makes it possible to ask what was asserted before asking how its presentation influences interpretation.

### 2.3. Completeness without validity

The registry proposal raises a limited but important question: what makes an assertion complete enough to compare? A record may need the asserted subject, source, interval, coordinate description, and qualifications before its relationship to another record can be understood. Supplying those descriptions still does not make the assertion valid.

Several associated questions remain open. How could one assertion retain multiple coordinate descriptions without declaring a universal legal frame? Which status labels merely describe a record, and which require a named authority? How could a display show expired duration, changing frames, unresolved jurisdiction, or conflicting evidence without silently ranking claims by graphical emphasis? These are tests of the proposed representational method, not an accepted implementation specification.

## 3. Layers That Keep Questions Separate

### 3.1. The original thematic stack

The original map analogy has seven themes. A rights layer asks what is asserted, by whom, and from what source. A jurisdiction layer asks which institution or rule set is asserted to apply. Proximity concerns nearness under a declared metric and time window, with coordinates, scale, uncertainty, and operational purpose stated. Liability concerns alleged responsibility or exposure, which remains dependent on governing sources, facts, causation, and competent review.

Use records an activity without automatically creating title or priority. Access records an asserted permission for entry, transit, observation, or resource interaction without granting it. Exclusion records a boundary or restriction without establishing its authority or enforceability. The layers can overlap while disagreeing: a described route can coexist with disputed access, competing jurisdictional assertions, and operational risk whose legal significance is unresolved.

### 3.2. An expanded analytic stack

The exploratory account expands those themes into eleven candidate layers. The table preserves their different questions; it does not define a legal ontology, database contract, application requirement, or governance system.

| Candidate layer | Information proposed for comparison | Question retained |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical domain | Land, water, air, outer-space region, route, or volume. | What region or movement is described? |
| Time | Instant, interval, recurrence, prediction window, or historical record. | When does it apply? |
| Operational location | Position or region relative to a declared chart, instrument, observer, or mission frame. | Relative to what, at what scale, and with what uncertainty? |
| Passage | Route, occupancy, crossing, or proximity event tied to a source. | What movement or presence is recorded or proposed? |
| Use | Activity, resource interaction, observation, communication, residence, transit, or service. | What is happening or proposed? |
| Rights or access | Asserted permission, entitlement, license, custom, agreement, or restriction. | Who asserts it, from what source, and in which domain? |
| Jurisdiction | Asserted institution, rule source, venue, or governing relation. | Which authority is claimed, and who contests it? |
| Exclusion | Boundary, safety or privacy zone, access condition, or asserted prohibition. | What is excluded, by whom, and on what asserted basis? |
| Proximity and risk | Distance, encounter window, collision envelope, environmental effect, or interference. | Which operational relation matters, and why? |
| Liability | Alleged duty, causation, damage, exposure, defense, or remedy. | Which facts and governing sources would be needed for a conclusion? |
| Claim state | Asserted, disputed, withdrawn, adjudicated, hypothetical, or unknown. | What is the status of the record, rather than the truth of the claim? |

The expanded stack makes the proposal's separation more explicit. A proximity event can be represented in physical and temporal terms while the liability layer remains an allegation. A claim-state label can record adjudication without replacing the source and scope of that adjudication. Neither overlap between layers nor completeness of the stack supplies a legal inference rule.

## 4. Moving Systems, Time, and Record Candidates

### 4.1. A location requires provenance

A bare point or boundary is insufficient for the source's moving-system inquiry. The candidate description includes the mapped object or actor as identified by its source, the chart or operational frame and its provenance, and a declared interval. Absolute-time and observer-time descriptions remain explicitly distinguished. Position, region or route must retain uncertainty, scale, and resolution, together with the activity being described, the source of associated assertions, and observed, reconstructed, predicted, proposed, or hypothetical status.

An assembly-anchored or mission-selected frame can serve an operational purpose without becoming a universal frame-selection law. The question of who or what selected the chart remains part of its provenance. A change of chart does not by itself establish a change of legal authority, and the formal background of absolute timespace does not determine which operational coordinates a legal or civic comparison must use.

This leaves several representational questions open: how to expose chart changes, delayed observations, latency, uncertainty, and prediction horizons; when observer-level coordinates are needed; and how moving boundaries can be compared without assuming one legally meaningful coordinate system across all domains. The manuscript supplies no new coordinate transformation, physical solution, or proof of feasible motion.

### 4.2. Passage and claim as separate discussion objects

The candidate passage record would retain ten families of information: source-declared actor identity; object or assembly description; domain and region definition; chart or frame provenance; time variable and interval with substrate and observer levels separated; path, occupancy, crossing, or proximity relation; spatial and temporal uncertainty; source, evidence grade, and reconstruction method; observation or proposal status; and linked assertions or objections, each carrying its own source and authority label.

A candidate claim record asks a different set of questions. Who is the claimant or source? Which subject, activity, passage, or region is referenced? What kind of assertion is made—access, use, exclusion, jurisdiction, liability, ownership, duty, fee, or toll? What basis and domain are asserted, over what interval and geographic or operational scope? Which evidence, notice, objections, conflicts, adjudication, or outcomes are relevant, and what status is attributed to the record?

The two candidates need not become formal records merely because their fields can be described. Passage could instead remain a map feature, event record, path-history summary, or family of domain-specific descriptions. Claim could remain an assertion label or later distinguish evidence, authority, objection, adjudication, and outcome more sharply. The choice between these alternatives is unresolved. No status is inferred from geometry alone, and a geometric comparison does not select the institution entitled to decide a dispute.

## 5. Domains and Illustrative Hypotheticals

### 5.1. Beginning with ordinary domains

The inquiry begins with questions about land, water, and air before extending to outer space. For land, the proposed separation distinguishes physical occupation from title, permission, zoning, access, boundaries, use, and liability. For water, it asks how moving conditions, shared routes, resources, environmental effects, boundaries, and multiple authorities interact. For airspace, it makes altitude, time, route, proximity, safety, access, and jurisdiction explicit instead of relying on a flat parcel image.

The outer-space extension asks which aspects of this mapping discipline remain useful when objects and regions move, operational positions depend on declared charts or observers, and asserted authority remains domain-specific or unsettled. These are framing questions. They summarize no jurisdiction's law and do not make one domain's institutions, rights, or restrictions applicable to another.

### 5.2. Five hypothetical families

The sources retain five overlapping families of thought experiment. Each is illustrative only: the described institutions, permissions, physical feasibility, and enforceability are not established.

First, a hypothetical organization publishes a time-dependent corridor and asserts a passage fee or toll. The proposed record could show its source, region, interval, route intersection, notice, objection, and status. Displaying the assertion would neither create the fee nor demonstrate that the organization exists or has authority to collect it.

Second, two moving systems or habitats publish overlapping exclusion or safety regions in different operational charts. Comparison raises questions of time, coordinates, uncertainty, source identity, notice, and risk. Even a perfect geometric comparison would leave the lawfulness and ethical justification of either restriction unresolved.

Third, a journey crosses land, water, airspace, and an outer-space segment. The map could expose changes in domain, terminology, source, and asserted authority. Continuity of the route would not establish continuity of a rights regime, merge laws, or settle jurisdictional boundaries.

Fourth, a resource-use layer overlaps a passage layer. Their intersection identifies a possible conflict for study, without establishing ownership, damage, liability, priority, or a duty to pay. An intersection describes a relation between records; it does not determine what legal consequence, if any, follows.

Fifth, a system repeatedly passes near a resource, habitat, route, or observation target. Nearness and repeated use can be recorded as facts or allegations at their declared evidence grade. They do not automatically establish ownership, priority, exclusion, liability, or a toll right. Whether repeated use could ground a claim under a particular legal or moral account remains a separate question.

These families illustrate the distinction between representation and justification. They are not a completed comparison of current land, maritime, aviation, orbital, or interstellar regimes, and they establish no feasibility of an outer-space journey.

## 6. Civic and Ethical Questions Without a Prescribed Regime

### 6.1. Concepts that must not collapse into one another

The civic inquiry asks what distinguishes occupancy, passage, use, access, control, exclusion, stewardship, ownership, and jurisdiction. The concepts can concern the same activity while posing different questions. Recording that a source uses one term does not decide its legal meaning or moral justification, and translating it into a common display vocabulary must not erase those differences.

The sources leave several substantive questions open. Could repeated passage or use support a claim, and under which legal or moral account? What could justify a fee or toll, and how would service, stewardship, congestion management, coercion, and enclosure be distinguished? Which features of land, water, and air law must remain domain-specific? What would public, commons, open access, or shared use mean when regions and participants move? None of these questions selects a desirable future regime.

### 6.2. Plural reasoning and representational limits

Another question concerns whether a civic framework can remain plural about law and ethics while using a clear record format. Shared fields might make assertions comparable, but comparability would not make authorities interchangeable or resolve their conflicts. The atlas proposal must therefore confront the possibility that its visual choices privilege one assertion even when the record itself preserves objections.

The proposed method has identifiable failure conditions. It fails its organizing purpose if the working chain suggests that physics generates entitlement; if a representative passage cannot be described without conflating physical and legal claims; if a common category erases a domain-specific distinction; or if a display turns uncertainty, expiry, conflicting authority, or visual prominence into an unacknowledged ranking. These are conceptual rejection criteria drawn from the sources, not outcomes of a completed user study or formal validation.

The boundary between physical description and civic judgment is thus part of the subject, rather than an inconvenience to remove. Source, evidence, authority, and objection remain separate even when a common map makes their objects appear adjacent. A registry can preserve the record of a disagreement while the grounds for deciding it remain outside the registry.

## 7. Unresolved Development of the Framework

The sources retain six directions at different provisional grades. A terminology and claim-level crosswalk is an unresolved clarification target: it would decide whether Passage, Claim, and Assembly Architecture remain local labels and state rejection criteria. A cross-domain comparison matrix is a guessed direction requiring bounded, source-labeled comparison. A frame-explicit passage record is an inferred candidate whose falsifier is a representative case it cannot express without confusing physical and legal claims.

A claim, objection, and status model is a guessed direction requiring jurisdiction-specific legal sourcing under separate authority. Claims-atlas wireframes are another guessed direction, concerned with whether uncertainty, conflict, expiry, moving frames, and source authority can remain visible without graphical ranking. A civic philosophy paper is a guessed direction about movement, commons, exclusion, stewardship, and passage fees, dependent on a selected philosophical scope and expert sources. These are retained alternatives and open questions, not commissioned deliverables or an implementation plan.

The accepted umbrella name and usefulness of an organizing chain would not themselves establish a legal or physical theory. Any later development would need an explicit domain, source authority, claim classification, and failure test. The present exposition preserves the conceptual separation among background, activity, assertion, and display while leaving formal record choices, legal recognition, civic justification, and implementation unresolved.
