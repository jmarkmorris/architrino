# Dark-Sector Candidate Assemblies and Their Observable Boundaries

## 1. What a Dark Candidate Must Explain

### 1.1. Darkness is a channel relation

A dark-sector candidate in $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ is a proposed assembly, release channel, transport mode or conversion history whose ordinary visible exposure is limited. That description does not establish a new species. The physical questions are what persists, which interaction channels remain exposed, how the object is prepared and transported, and what can terminate its darkness.

The [candidate source packet](dark-sector-photon-like-mode.md) explores assemblies adjacent to the ordinary photon channel that could emerge from a strong-field interface while initially lacking ordinary visible-photon exposure. Shielded, folded, misregistered or nontransverse internal ordering are proposed explanations of that limited exposure. None is a derived release mechanism. The terms dark photon, hidden gauge boson and Hawking-radiation correction would introduce additional claims that the packet does not establish.

Hawking radiation supplies an observer-level comparison target, not an architrino-level premise. Likewise, a gravitational redshift description does not itself determine the evolution of internal binary radii or speeds. The candidate must acquire those relations from admitted assembly histories, delayed interactions and the relevant material or Noether sea response. An effective astronomical description may constrain that derivation without replacing it.

### 1.2. A complete candidate record

The [strategy](priorities.md) retains topological and analogue-medium comparisons only within a fuller record,

$$
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{dark}}
=\left(A_{\mathrm{int}},\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{dark}},
\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{top}},\Delta_{\mathrm{dark}},
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{reentry}}\right).
$$

Here $A_{\mathrm{int}}$ is the internal action or assembly record, $\mathcal{B}_{\mathrm{dark}}$ the candidate branch, $\mathcal{I}_{\mathrm{top}}$ an effective invariant or defect label when one exists, $\Delta_{\mathrm{dark}}$ a specified stability or exposure gap, and $\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{reentry}}$ the visible-channel re-entry residual. Stability and weak exposure are different properties; a single unspecified gap cannot establish both. A stability claim first requires an actual retained configuration and its appropriate balance or return condition.

Analogue horizons, protected edge modes, vortices and topological defects demonstrate possibilities in their own systems. Their names alone supply none of the remaining candidate entries. A retained invariant must specify its domain and which admissible transformations preserve it; a defect label without internal variables, an exposed channel and a release or re-entry route remains an analogy.

The candidate registry therefore needs explicit internal ordering, exposure, medium response and a rejection condition for each entry. It is a proposed comparison framework, not a populated catalog of accepted assemblies.

## 2. Photon-Adjacent Release and Internal Evolution

### 2.1. Five distinct candidate families

The source proposes five families. They address different missing mechanisms and should not be merged merely because each can be called photon-like.

| Candidate | Proposed physical distinction | Unresolved obligation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Middle-nested packet | Inner and outer photon-related structure is enclosed, folded or hidden within a middle channel. | Specify internal ordering, the exposed photon quotient and the shielding relation. |
| Horizon-locked tri-planar packet | Three binaries share approximately one radius and synchronized, interleaved motion with $v_I\approx v_M\approx v_O\approx c_f$. | Establish whether this is an admissible release boundary, propagation endpoint or unstable transition. |
| High-side photon cousin | Observer propagation resembles a photon while internal evolution approaches visible exposure from a proposed high side. | Distinguish $v_b>c_f$, an effective medium speed and an internal return-map branch; these are different meanings. |
| Dense neutral release cluster | Several seed assemblies form a neutral bundle with suppressed far-channel exposure. | Derive cancellation or shielding, close recoil and angular accounting, and describe later visible unpacking. |
| Redshift-triggered transition packet | The candidate is an evolving internal state rather than a fixed species. | Derive the propagation map and a physically meaningful transition threshold. |

Compactness, large retained internal action and high reactivity are source hypotheses. Escape from a steep effective well alone proves none of them. Charge neutrality also does not imply zero radiative or higher-order exposure. Each proposed cancellation must be evaluated in its actual channel on the same assembly record.

### 2.2. An internal state is more than an observed frequency

The proposed record is

$$
\Gamma_{\mathrm{dark}\gamma}
=\left(R_I,R_M,R_O,\omega_I,\omega_M,\omega_O,
v_I,v_M,v_O,E,\mathbf p,\mathbf J,\zeta_\gamma\right).
$$

The binary radii, cadences and speeds describe candidate internal motion. The energy, momentum and angular-momentum entries are assembly-level accounting quantities requiring their established definitions; they do not assign mass or a primitive force law to an architrino. The exposure coordinate $\zeta_\gamma$ also needs a declared observable projection and normalization before it becomes measurable. This finite list summarizes a record; it does not replace the complete delayed history needed to evolve it.

The proposed propagation map

$$
\Gamma_{\mathrm{dark}\gamma}(r)
\longmapsto\Gamma_{\mathrm{dark}\gamma}(r+\Delta r)
$$

is meaningful as written only on a route for which the radial parameter distinguishes the successive states. Turning points or history dependence require the path and absolute-time information as well. No propagation law is supplied by writing the arrow. In particular, an observed change in frequency cannot be substituted for a demonstrated change in every internal cadence, speed or radius.

The five source thresholds are synchronized binary speeds; equal, phase-locked interleaved radii; a cadence-ratio change such as $4:2:1$ toward degeneracy; a photon-exposure crossing; and a medium-response threshold that routes the packet into radiation, transport, reaction or another branch. Their approximate equalities require independently chosen tolerances and a persistence window. Three equal instantaneous speeds do not establish equal radii, phase locking or complete-history return.

### 2.3. A threshold needs an evolution law

As a conditional description, let a declared branch diagnostic be $b[H_T]$, a function of the admitted history $H_T$, and let $b_\star$ specify a candidate boundary. Define

$$
F(T)=b[H_T]-b_\star.
$$

If $F$ is continuous and changes sign between two retained times, the intermediate value theorem gives at least one boundary contact. It gives neither a unique contact nor a transition into a visible channel. A tangential contact can occur without a sign change, and a sign change of an arbitrary diagnostic need not represent a physical branch boundary. The diagnostic, continuity domain and relation to an actual assembly transition must therefore be supplied independently.

This conditional argument clarifies why redshift-triggered re-entry remains open: the source has neither an admitted evolution of the chosen internal diagnostic nor a demonstrated visible descendant. The synchronized, same-radius case remains a candidate transition surface to investigate. It is not proved singular or a separatrix merely by its equality conditions, and no stable configuration follows from those conditions.

## 3. Re-Entry Requires a Complete Event

### 3.1. What must leave the incoming record

The source's event map is

$$
\mathcal{L}_{E\mathbf p\mathbf J}:
\Gamma_{\mathrm{in}}
\longrightarrow
\left(\Gamma_{\mathrm{out}},\gamma_{\mathrm{vis}},
\Delta\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{sea}},\Gamma_{\mathrm{remnant}}\right).
$$

It requires outgoing ordinary photons or other radiation, medium heating or response, recoil and angular-momentum accounting, and any surviving dark remnant. Untracked loss is a failed account. The output entries need disjoint meanings so the same descendant is not counted once in a general outgoing total and again as a photon or remnant.

Calling the event an assembly shower is only descriptive until its identities and channels are recorded. Reaction, thermalization, ordinary-photon shedding and visible re-entry are distinct routes. A valid account must identify which occurs and how its photon exposure arises. The existing radiation Gate C and shared residual-routing requirements remain the relevant source-defined acceptance boundaries; this manuscript does not certify their satisfaction.

The useful observational prediction comes from one common release and event model. Independently adjusting X-rays, radio emission, spectral lines and background production would erase that constraint. A candidate that fits a visible output only by omitting medium update, remnant state or another required channel has not closed the event.

### 3.2. A background requires a population and a transfer map

The source's most speculative extension is a cosmic population of early strong-field releases whose later conversion and thermalization contribute to a CMB-like background. A possible source class is not yet a population history, and production of ordinary photons does not establish a thermal spectrum.

The required description includes a source function, a thermalization rule and a redshift transfer map. It must account for temperature and anisotropy rather than arbitrary injected radiation. Near-blackbody character, nucleosynthesis, recombination, reionization and structure-growth constraints are observer-level targets; they are not thermal laws imposed on a single architrino. Foregrounds, dust and ordinary reprocessing remain competing explanations of any apparent contribution.

A proposed source spectrum can fail after propagation even if its local release account closes. Conversely, fitting one background spectrum cannot establish the claimed microscopic release channel. The source therefore keeps the CMB connection speculative until a concrete transfer description survives the joint constraints.

### 4.2. A joint pattern and its alternatives

The proposed comparison combines eight classes of observations: continuum compactness, luminosity and abundance; Balmer emission or asymmetry; X-ray weakness relative to inferred power; radio behavior; hot dust or a dense cocoon; clustering or halo occupation; redshift distribution and lower-redshift analogues; and any cumulative CMB, reionization or nucleosynthesis effect.

Radio quietness is conditional, because a visible re-entry outflow, jet or remnant could expose a radio channel. Low-redshift analogues would favor an environmental trigger over a strictly primordial-only interpretation only after their physical comparability is established. Neither suggestion supplies a release law.

The same model must predict a repeatable relation across independent samples. Ordinary active nuclei, dense gas, direct-collapse black holes, low-spin halos, starbursts, dust-obscured star formation and stellar-population modeling may already explain the class. The source also proposes a supermassive-star shell in its future discriminant table. These alternatives cannot be discarded merely because a speculative assembly has a descriptive resemblance.

Failure has several separate forms: no residual survives conventional comparison; the predicted multi-channel relation fails to repeat; the release violates the required event, no-signaling, effective thermodynamic or cosmological constraints; or the cumulative visible production spoils the background spectrum, anisotropy, reionization or nucleosynthesis. No numerical discriminator has yet been supplied here.

## 5. WIMP, Supersymmetry and Axion-Like Comparisons

### 5.1. A WIMP vector tied to one candidate

The [WIMP packet](wimp-susy-benchmark.md) draws on the exposed Jungman, Kamionkowski and Griest arXiv record for *Supersymmetric dark matter*, `hep-ph/9506380`. It explicitly reports access limited to abstract, metadata, contents and a source note saying the full review was not submitted there. That is the retained packet's source boundary; this manuscript does not claim a fresh full-paper review.

Its comparison vector is

$$
\mathcal B_A^{\mathrm{WIMP}}
=\left(m_A,\Omega_A h^2,\langle\sigma v\rangle_A,
\sigma_A^{\mathrm{scalar}},\sigma_A^{\mathrm{axial}},
\Gamma_\nu^{\odot/\oplus},\Phi_{\bar p},\Phi_{e^+},\Phi_\gamma\right).
$$

The entries organize candidate mass, relic abundance, annihilation, scalar and axial scattering, neutrino signals associated with the Sun or Earth, and antiproton, positron and photon fluxes. Here $h$ is the cosmological reduced-Hubble normalization in the abundance comparison, not an internal action constant. These are effective benchmark quantities, not primitive assembly laws.

One candidate must declare which quantities it predicts, which it only bounds and which null results it must satisfy. A fit to abundance alone does not identify a WIMP or neutralino, and a benchmark vector with unknown entries is not a completed detection test. No current numerical abundance or detection limit is newly acquired in this synthesis.

### 5.2. A supersymmetry analogy needs an actual transformation

The source preserves the conjecture that a transformation of inner and middle binary variables might admit a supersymmetry-like comparison. Its proposed theorem target is

$$
\Pi_{\mathrm{gauge}}(A')=\Pi_{\mathrm{gauge}}(A),
\qquad s_{\mathrm{eff}}(A')=s_{\mathrm{eff}}(A)\pm\frac12,
\qquad\Delta\mathcal L_{E\mathbf p\mathbf J}=0.
$$

The effective gauge projection would remain fixed while the recovered spin comparison changes by one half and the full event account closes. The source also permits a declared bounded exposed change compatible with null results, but that is a weaker approximate comparison, not the exact first equality. Free adjustment of exposed records defeats the proposed analogy.

These conditions have not been established by a transformation on admitted assemblies. Even satisfying the displayed necessary targets would not by itself recover the full algebra, dynamics or phenomenology of supersymmetry. Identifying middle and inner binaries with supersymmetric partners therefore remains a conjecture, not ontology.

### 5.3. Axion-like residuals and their domain

The axion-like comparison retains

$$
\mathcal R_{a\text{-}\mathrm{like}}
=\left(R_m,R_{\gamma\leftrightarrow a},R_{\mathrm{cool}},
R_{\mathrm{SN}},R_\Omega,R_{J^{PC}},R_{\mathrm{null}}\right).
$$

Its rows concern a mass window, photon conversion, stellar cooling, supernova constraints, abundance, spin/parity comparison and null results. Neutral weak exposure, small mass and scalar or pseudoscalar response are possible comparison features, not an axion field assigned to the Euclidean void or Noether sea. The spin/parity labels require the relevant effective symmetry and projection; they are not primitive labels supplied by the notation.

The residual components can have different units, uncertainties and acceptance rules. Agreement in one component cannot compensate for failure in another without a separately justified comparison procedure. A candidate that requires a primitive axion field to match the vector fails the proposed assembly interpretation, even if that field is useful in an external effective model.

## 6. The Present Explanatory Boundary

The source retains six ways to make the photon-adjacent proposal discriminating: an admitted interface release condition; an explicit internal assembly record; a derived propagation map; a ledger-complete visible re-entry threshold; a population source function; and a repeated observable relation that distinguishes conventional alternatives. Any one can supply a concrete starting constraint. A complete release-to-observation explanation needs the relevant links together.

No family is selected by this manuscript. The middle-nested, synchronized tri-planar and redshift-triggered alternatives remain candidates for initial study, alongside the other two recorded families. The proposed state record and event account organize their differences without asserting that any exists. The comparative source table preserves conventional alternatives, while the CMB possibility remains conditional on a successful transfer and thermalization account.

The result of this synthesis is an explicit set of physical questions, conditional mathematical distinctions and observer-level comparison boundaries. It establishes neither a dark-sector assembly nor a transport law, a supersymmetry transformation, an axion interpretation or an astronomical explanation. Those claims require admitted histories and independent evidence connecting preparation, persistence, exposure and re-entry on the same physical record.
