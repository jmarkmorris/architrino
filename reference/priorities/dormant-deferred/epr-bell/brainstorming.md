## Trial Records and Correlations

One Bell trial records two selected settings and two binary outcomes:

$$
(x_i,y_i,a_i,b_i),
\qquad a_i,b_i\in\{-1,+1\}.
$$

Plainly: each row identifies which local test each station selected and which of two outcomes each detector recorded.

For a fixed setting pair, the recorded correlation is

$$
E_{xy}
=
\frac{N_{++}+N_{--}-N_{+-}-N_{-+}}
{N_{++}+N_{--}+N_{+-}+N_{-+}}.
$$

Plainly: matching outcomes contribute positively, unlike outcomes contribute negatively, and the average lies between $-1$ and $+1$.

The CHSH statistic is

$$
S=E_{00}+E_{01}+E_{10}-E_{11}.
$$

Plainly: CHSH combines the four correlations obtained from the four setting pairs. The arithmetic is simple once a valid trial table exists; identifying trials, settings, detections, closure times, and accepted records is a physical and statistical inference.

## Bell Factorization and Measurement Independence

For a complete declared past state $\lambda$, Bell factorization is

$$
P(a,b\mid x,y,\lambda)
=
P(a\mid x,\lambda)P(b\mid y,\lambda).
$$

Plainly: after the complete relevant past is fixed, each response uses only its local setting and that state.

Measurement independence is

$$
\rho(\lambda\mid x,y)=\rho(\lambda).
$$

Plainly: choosing later settings does not select a different distribution of complete past states.

For deterministic local responses $A_x,B_y\in\{-1,+1\}$,

$$
s(\lambda)
=A_0B_0+A_0B_1+A_1B_0-A_1B_1
=A_0(B_0+B_1)+A_1(B_0-B_1).
$$

One bracket vanishes and the other equals $+2$ or $-2$, so $|s(\lambda)|=2$. Measurement independence permits the same distribution to average all four setting pairs:

$$
S=\int d\lambda\,\rho(\lambda)s(\lambda),
\qquad |S|\le2.
$$

Plainly: a more detailed passive source record does not evade the bound. Stochastic local models also obey it because their local random variables can be included in a larger complete state.

## Parameter Independence, Outcome Independence, and No-Signaling

Parameter independence requires

$$
P(a\mid x,y,\lambda)=P(a\mid x,\lambda),
\qquad
P(b\mid x,y,\lambda)=P(b\mid y,\lambda).
$$

Outcome independence requires

$$
P(a,b\mid x,y,\lambda)
=
P(a\mid x,y,\lambda)P(b\mid x,y,\lambda).
$$

Together they yield Bell factorization. A complete deterministic state satisfies outcome independence automatically, so a deterministic, measurement-independent $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ route that recovers Bell violation must introduce remote-setting dependence somewhere in its response law or change another declared premise.

Plainly: an irreducible correlated stochastic law is not merely another description of the current deterministic ontology. It would change the law or concede that the declared state was incomplete.

Operational no-signaling is weaker than factorization:

$$
\sum_b P(a,b\mid x,y)=P(a\mid x),
$$

with the corresponding condition on Bob's marginal. Conditional remote-setting dependence can average out of local observed frequencies.

Plainly: Bell-factorization failure, an observer-visible marginal change, and controllable message transmission are three different verdicts.

## The Passive-Record Negative Control

A complete passive record formed when the pair is prepared cannot reproduce Bell violations if local response, measurement independence, and valid sampling remain in force. This is the precise boundary of the phrase `decided at entanglement` when it means that later detectors merely reveal a fixed shared answer record.

One-particle predecision does not establish this two-wing claim. Definite substrate states can explain why one detector creates one real record, and a source conservation ledger can constrain the pair, but neither supplies a response law across independently selected setting pairs.

Plainly: preparation history, outcome formation, source conservation, local analyzer response, live coordination, and trial selection are separate obligations.

## Forward-Causal Route Map

Within the current absolute-time and finite-causal-speed ontology, a complete shared Noether sea state can enter as a passive common-past record, a live cross-wing channel during measurement, or a source of statistical dependence between the setting pair and $\lambda$. These roles respectively preserve Bell factorization, change Bell factorization, or change measurement independence.

Other logical routes carry additional ontology burdens. Retrocausal dependence conflicts with current forward-only causal support. A global or instantaneous nonseparable law changes the primitive inventory. An irreducible unscreened stochastic joint law changes the deterministic ontology or leaves $\lambda$ incomplete. Apparatus or trial effects challenge the experiment-to-table map and require a concrete surviving mechanism.

Plainly: no route is established. Each surviving option changes a different mathematical or physical assumption and therefore carries a different proof and falsification burden.

## Finite-Speed Reach and the Bancal Boundary

A live channel from Alice's setting event to Bob's outcome-closure event can contribute only when

$$
T_B^{\mathrm{out}}-T_A^{\mathrm{set}}
\ge\frac{L_{AB}}{c_f}.
$$

Plainly: the relevant setting-dependent state must cross the baseline before the remote result becomes irreversible.

If a setting-independent fraction $r$ is reachable and the reachable and unreachable laws are $P_1$ and $P_0$, then

$$
P=rP_1+(1-r)P_0,
\qquad S=rS_1+(1-r)S_0.
$$

Plainly: this mixture follows only under the declared reach model. The unreachable law must be specified; failure of reach does not imply $S_0=2$.

Published finite-influence experiments provide conditional lower bounds inside preferred-frame models. Identifying the hypothetical influence with primitive wakes and therefore with $c_f$ is a separate model-dependent inference, not a measurement of the wake speed. Frequency-dependent photon behavior would require an additional constitutive law rather than following from a speed hierarchy alone.

The Bancal construction tests a specified class of finite-speed hidden-influence models. When two later parties are outside each other's finite-speed influence cones, the model requires conditional local screening after earlier data are fixed. Combined with measurement independence, specified quantum marginals, and exact operational no-signaling, this produces an inequality violated by the comparison distribution.

Plainly: the theorem does not exclude every conceivable medium, but a shielding proposal must state which screening, independence, marginal, or no-signaling premise changes and must derive the replacement multipartite distribution.
