## A. All-Rail Terminal Barrel

Let the three nested layers share the common spindle cadence $\omega$, with cylindrical radii

$$
\rho_a=R_a\cos\alpha_a,
\qquad
a\in\{I,M,O\}.
$$

Within this declared family, simultaneous rail residence is

$$
\omega\rho_I
=\omega\rho_M
=\omega\rho_O
=c_f.
$$

For finite common $\omega$, it follows immediately that

$$
\rho_I=\rho_M=\rho_O=\frac{c_f}{\omega}.
$$

This is the **conditional barrel lemma**: all three layers occupy one cylindrical radius. It does not force equal spherical radii, equal heights, equal tilts, or equal phase offsets; those variables can remain distinct through $R_a\cos\alpha_a=c_f/\omega$.

The lemma is kinematic. It does not prove that the barrel is dynamically realized, stable, retained, entropy-carrying, or horizon-forming. A theorem-grade physical barrel still requires same-record force, action, wake, angular-momentum, stability, and boundary rows.

The barrel is also not a proof of a Planck-frequency ceiling. The primitive statement is about the field-speed rail $c_f$ inside the declared spindle family.

## B. Horizon-Interface Alignment

Horizon-interface alignment remains a separate strong-field boundary problem. Its current residual target is

$$
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{align,H}}
=
\max\left(
\|F_H\|,
\left|\frac{s_M}{c_f}-1\right|,
\left|\frac{s_O}{c_f}-1\right|,
\left|\frac{v_{\mathrm{trans}}}{c_{\mathrm{eff}}}-1\right|,
\left|\frac{c_{\mathrm{eff}}}{c_f}-1\right|,
d_{\mathrm{align}},
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{retune}}
\right).
$$

The symbols must be bound to one accepted horizon-interface carrier: $F_H$ for the boundary equation, $s_M$ and $s_O$ for layer-speed rows, $v_{\mathrm{trans}}$ for whole-carrier translation, $c_{\mathrm{eff}}$ for the local dressed comparison speed, $d_{\mathrm{align}}$ for the declared alignment distance, and $\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{retune}}$ for hidden branch retuning.

A passing alignment row is necessary evidence for this candidate mechanism, not a horizon definition by itself. The same retained record must also close:

- embedded Noether sea boundary conditions and exterior/interior continuation;
- mass, spin, area, surface-gravity, entropy, and conserved-ledger deposition rows;
- release or absorption routing;
- observer-level GR comparisons.

### Horizon And Light Ring Stay Distinct

The horizon radius $r_H$ and photon-sphere or light-ring radius $r_{\mathrm{ph}}$ / $r_{\mathrm{LR}}$ remain separate observables. The Schwarzschild comparison already requires

$$
r_s=\frac{2GM}{c_0^2},
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{ph}}=\frac{3GM}{c_0^2},
\qquad
r_{\mathrm{ISCO}}=\frac{6GM}{c_0^2}.
$$

Translation or infall approaching a local effective signal limit may inform the horizon-interface row. Orbital or null propagation informs the light-ring row. Coincidence is allowed only when a declared rotating branch derives it; it is never the default identification. Photon planar-pair recovery remains a consumer of the light-ring record, not a replacement for the horizon carrier.

## C. `4:2:1` Watchlist

The `4:2:1` pattern remains a hypothesis, not a foundation or theorem target. It may advance only if an existing proof route independently produces it from at least one of:

- a reduced return map or frequency-lock equation;
- an accepted retained branch ledger;
- a Planck-alignment calculation with its scale map already closed.

Searching only candidate integer triples and selecting `4:2:1` because it is familiar does not count as derivation. The watch item should be rejected if the accepted map selects another family or if `4:2:1` has no invariant consequence that distinguishes it from neighboring ratios. Until then it remains sequestered in the hypothesis registry and cannot enter reader-facing prose as a physical law.

## D. Dark-Sector Photon-Like Release Watchlist

Dark-sector photon-like release remains a speculative release-channel class, not a new particle or settled dark-photon ontology. It stays behind the existing `release_channel_selection`, radiation Gate C, cosmology transport, and discriminating-observable burdens.

Advancement requires one same-event route with:

1. a horizon-interface source and conserved input ledger;
2. a defined outgoing carrier or mode record distinct from the visible photon channel;
3. energy, momentum, angular momentum, polarity, wake, recoil, and remnant closure;
4. a redshift, thermalization, conversion, or visible re-entry law;
5. at least one observable that distinguishes the route from ordinary jets, AGN emission, dust, dense gas, star formation, or visible photon transport.

Without those rows, high-redshift compact red sources and CMB-adjacent ideas remain comparison prompts only. They cannot be used as confirming evidence for the release hypothesis.
