### First choice: nonrigid two-member histories

The [exploration](../../master-equation-closure/binary-research/analysis/two-member-subwake-periodicity-exploration-2026-10-06.md) and its [separate second reading](../../master-equation-closure/binary-research/analysis/two-member-subwake-periodicity-second-reading-2026-10-06.md) already supply exact delayed angular identities, conditional exclusions and failed candidate monotone quantities. The remaining problem is not another uniform circle. It permits varying separation, speed, phase and common motion while requiring the full equation throughout the complete history. The second reading distinguishes prescribed-window algebra, released trajectories and genuinely self-consistent complete pasts; the new work must retain that distinction.

The first bounded milestone is to reconstruct the corrected identities and identify exactly which sign hypotheses a possible counterexample must violate. The next is a new period-integrated identity, a proved history-dependent monotone quantity, or a fully declared boundary-value construction outside the established exclusions. A finite collocation residual is discovery evidence; accepting a solution requires the complete root census, all acceleration components, a separation/speed domain and independent enclosure or proof. A failure to find a solution in a finite family gives only a bounded negative. Stability is a later question after a solution exists.

An exclusion would constrain recurring two-member configurations in its proved class. It would not exclude every bounded aperiodic motion, prove that three or more members cannot bind, or establish a globally accepted minimum assembly size. A positive periodic solution would supply an exact dynamical reference; persistence under perturbation, attraction, stability and physical identity would still require separate evidence. A counterexample in the theorem's declared class overturns a negative; a missing root or nonzero full-vector residual defeats a positive.
