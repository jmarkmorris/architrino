## 3. What the owners would need to supply first

Before any Saturday claim could rely on solver trajectories, the following would have to be in place for the geometry class of that claim. The first two items and the outcome gate are obligations the App Solver owners already record in their contracts and closeouts. The last two are downstream scientific proof obligations inferred here from the Saturday results and the comparison; they belong to whoever makes the scientific claim, not to the solver owners.

- **True-history inclusion on the claim's domain.** A validated whole-step enclosure of the kind accepted on October 12, extended to the geometry actually used. The closeout lists joint histories, self-hits and overlapping, near or above-wake-speed geometry among the obligations outside the accepted result; what a given claim needs is a proved enclosure covering the correlations it uses, for example above-wake-speed sources and ordinary own-path rows for the ring, or long horizons for repeating binaries, not any one representation such as joint histories. On the unsupported domains the accepted route refuses, so it currently gives no evidence there.
- **Independent agreement on that domain.** Agreement with the independent oracle or another independently authored reference, together with the convergence and negative controls of the evolution contract's validation ladder. Deterministic replay or parity with the solver's own outputs does not count, as the repository's evidence rules require.
- **The binary outcome gate.** In abbreviated form, for any binary fate the contract's [complete gate](../../app-solver/contracts/evolution-contract-v1.md#binary-outcome-gate) requires both complete initial retained histories, both partner and both same-transmitter root families, the branch transitions met, all acceleration weights and regulator transitions, adaptive many-orbit evolution with the listed diagnostic histories, and timestep, history-depth, precision, regulator, restart and worker-count convergence appropriate to the claim. A terminal circular state additionally requires the three limiting conditions on radial velocity, radial acceleration and tangential acceleration and a stable return map under declared small perturbations; a repeating noncircular state requires finite-period return of the complete retained history and continuation state. Even then the result is a numerical claim. A *rigorously admitted* repeating solution, which is what handover item 2 asks for, needs an existence argument for the delay equation; a trajectory alone does not supply one.
- **An infinite-population tail theorem for lattice use.** The solver's contract is posed for $N$ retained histories, and the lattice notes state that a finite population or a fixed exterior is a different preparation. Using a finite run as evidence about the lattice would need a theorem bounding the omitted infinite tail along the true solution. Today's [field theorem](../../master-equation-closure/analysis/saturday-cross-geometry-continuation-2026-10-20.md#6-explicit-tails) gives the form such a bound takes (light-cone sums with explicit tails), and its [sharpness companion](../../master-equation-closure/analysis/saturday-cross-geometry-continuation-2026-10-20-sharpness.md) shows that uniform smallness of the far members' motion is not enough for the prescribed sum to exist at all.
- **Sharp-law fold passage.** The evolution contract routes folds and caustics through a finite-width causal-surface calculation with a recorded finite impulse. A solver passage through a fold under that routing is a statement about the routed computation; it would not by itself be the unchanged sharp-law passage that the cross-geometry comparison records as unconstructed.

> Claim grade: measured, by reading, for the obligations attributed to the owners; inferred for the two downstream scientific obligations, from the cited Saturday results. Falsifier: an owner passage showing that one of these items is already accepted on the relevant domain, or a Saturday claim whose rigorous scope is met by a solver trajectory without one of them.

## 4. Risks and inconsistencies flagged for the owners

These are observations for the App Solver owners and the coordinator. Nothing was changed.

1. **A possibly stale blocker in EOM-006.** The row says it "depends on EOM-003 through EOM-005". The work log records EOM-003, EOM-004 and EOM-005 as accepted and removed from the live queue on 2026-09-02 (its entries at lines 19 to 57, by `grep -n` on `EOM-00[345]` in `work-log.md`), and priorities lists their closing evidence. The row does not state what currently blocks it. The likely blocker is the open true-history and binary-gate work, but the row should say so.
2. **No single owner row for generic history qualification.** The queue rules refer to "The remaining true-history, consumer and scientific acceptance obligations below", but no ranked or deferred row owns true-history qualification by name. Downstream lines, including this note and the handover, therefore cannot cite one owner entry. A named row, or an explicit statement of which row owns it, would let dependent claims point to one place.
3. **Two uses of "generated-history inclusion".** The October 12 closeout accepts generated-history inclusion for the bounded validated route, while the fixture text says generic generated-history inclusion is not qualified. Both are accurate at their scopes, but a reader can conflate them. Keeping the qualifier ("bounded validated route" against "generic") wherever the phrase appears would prevent that.
4. **Finite-population runs presented as lattice evidence.** Because the contract is finite and today's sharpness result shows that the lattice prescription can fail under uniform bounds, any consumer that displays a lattice-patch run should label it as a finite preparation, not as the lattice history.
5. **Finite-width fold routing against the sharp law.** The same distinction as in Section 3, flagged here because a fold-transit record in solver output is the most likely place for a sharp-law passage claim to be inferred by mistake.
