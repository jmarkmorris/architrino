## Two-Framework Unification Versus Full Theory Closure

**Claim level: effective and historical synthesis.** The familiar statement that foundational physics must unify general relativity with quantum theory compresses a many-domain theory ecology into two high-level poles. In practice, physics uses the Standard Model and its gauge sectors, quantum mechanics, quantum field theories, general relativity, statistical mechanics, thermodynamics, hydrodynamics, nuclear and atomic models, cosmological parameter theories, material constitutive models, and numerous controlled or phenomenological approximations. Many of these are not independent candidate ontologies: some are limits, coarse-grainings, or specializations of broader frameworks. Their bridges nevertheless vary in strength, from theorem-controlled limits and renormalization-group matching to fitted constitutive relations and domain-specific approximations.

**Claim level: ontology and closure target.** Unifying the quantum-field and relativistic formalisms would be a major framework unification, but it would not by itself establish one unified theory of nature. Full closure requires a single retained constituent history to generate the effective variables, validity domains, constants, state spaces, and bridge maps across the entire theory ecology. A proposed foundational theory has not met that stronger standard if it merely places general relativity and quantum fields inside one formal package while leaving emergence into chemistry, thermodynamics, nuclear structure, measurement records, and cosmology to separately supplied models.

Plainly: the slogan presents two continents to be joined by one bridge. Actual physics is an archipelago whose islands already have roads, ferries, tunnels, and local traffic rules of very different kinds. Joining the two largest continents does not prove that every island and every connection has been derived from one geology.

- **Assumptions:** domain theories are classified by actual dependence rather than counted as equally fundamental; successful effective autonomy is preserved; a bridge counts as derived only when its source variables, target variables, regime, residual, and failure condition are explicit.
- **Proof burden:** inventory the empirically indispensable theory domains, classify each inter-domain bridge as exact, asymptotic, renormalization-group, statistical, constitutive, phenomenological, or absent, and identify whether one retained substrate record supplies both sides of every required handoff.
- **Falsifier:** the criticism is overstated if the supposedly independent domains can already be derived through explicit controlled maps from one common physical state space with no private constants, carrier substitutions, or phenomenological insertions.
