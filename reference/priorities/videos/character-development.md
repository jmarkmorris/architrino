# Character Development

## Status

- Status: `draft-ready`
- Scope: first YouTube 4K HD landscape prototype
- Approval state: pending operator review
- Source role: character bible seed for scripts, prompts, voices, continuity checks, and generated media trials

These characters are fictional STEAM undergraduates. Their identities are design constraints for consistency and representation, not the source of their authority. Their authority in videos must come from what they explain, demonstrate, ask, or clarify.

## Ensemble Concept

The first prototype ensemble is a three-person peer-learning group. Each character has a recurring job:

- make one hard idea concrete;
- ask one useful viewer-facing question;
- translate one visual, app action, chart, or formula into plain language;
- and keep the explanation moving without becoming a full theory lecture.

The prototype should use the three undergraduates only. Add an `Educator` later only if the script needs an instructor role that the peer ensemble cannot naturally carry.

## Shared Continuity Rules

- Presentation style: lifelike animated characters first, with enough realism for emotional presence but enough stylization to preserve continuity across generated shots.
- Camera style: waist-up or chest-up presenter shots, medium close-ups for dialogue, and over-shoulder or picture-in-picture framing for app and concept visuals.
- Wardrobe: modern STEAM-studio clothing, clean lines, practical materials, no logos, no celebrity-coded outfits.
- Lighting: bright studio key light, soft fill, neutral background, subtle Architrino accent color only when it supports continuity.
- Framing: leave open space on one side for formula, chart, caption, or app overlay.
- Motion: restrained gestures, no exaggerated influencer mannerisms, no distracting idle animation.
- Identity handling: never use ethnicity, orientation, or appearance as a joke, proof of expertise, or substitute for personality.
- Likeness handling: do not imitate, reference, or converge toward any real person, celebrity, public figure, influencer, actor, professor, or existing fictional character.

## Character Sheets

### Mira Halenko

#### Prototype Role

Mira is the physics-and-engineering explainer. She turns the abstract mechanism into a concrete diagram, names the moving parts, and gives the viewer a stable mental model before a visual demonstration.

#### STEAM Emphasis

- Science: physical intuition and mechanism.
- Engineering: system constraints, cause-and-effect pathways, and model assumptions.
- Mathematics: only when a compact expression helps the viewer hold the mechanism.

#### Public-Safe Biography

Mira is an undergraduate who likes turning abstract physics into buildable models. In videos, she is the person who stops the group long enough to define the mechanism, sketch the diagram, and explain what the viewer should watch for next.

#### Appearance Direction

- Auburn-haired straight woman with optional Ukrainian design influence.
- Straight or gently layered hair, medium length, controlled and repeatable.
- Light or medium-light skin tone, natural makeup, clear eyes, calm facial expression.
- Technical wardrobe: fitted jacket, knit top, or clean lab-studio layers in slate, white, deep green, or muted copper.
- No flag motifs, folk-costume shorthand, celebrity styling, or accent-based caricature.

#### Visual Prompt Sheet

Use:

> Fictional STEAM undergraduate woman, auburn straight hair, calm physics-and-engineering presenter, modern technical studio wardrobe, clean jacket and knit top, warm precise expression, waist-up presenter framing, bright soft studio light, neutral background, space beside her for diagrams, lifelike animated style, consistent character model, no logos.

Avoid:

> real person likeness, celebrity resemblance, public figure resemblance, influencer glamour pose, national costume, exaggerated accent cues, lab-coat stereotype, harsh shadows, ornate background, changing hair color, changing facial structure.

#### Voice Prompt Sheet

- Voice range: warm alto.
- Pace: calm, moderate, deliberate.
- Diction: precise technical wording without stiffness.
- Emotional color: steady, grounded, quietly curious.
- Best use: definitions, mechanism setup, diagram narration, careful claims.
- Avoid: exaggerated accent, breathy drama, hype, sarcasm, condescension, celebrity imitation.

#### Dialogue Rules

- Give Mira the first precise definition in a scene.
- Let her name assumptions and limits.
- Keep her lines compact and visual: "Watch the delay path here" works better than long abstract claims.
- Do not make her the only technically competent character.

### Lena Vale

#### Prototype Role

Lena is the arts-and-mathematics bridge. She makes visuals, diagrams, formulas, and captions readable, and she notices when the viewer needs a cleaner representation.

#### STEAM Emphasis

- Arts: visual design, metaphor discipline, viewer attention.
- Mathematics: formula readability, symbolic economy, diagram-to-expression mapping.
- Science: conceptual accuracy in visual explanation.

#### Public-Safe Biography

Lena is an undergraduate who treats good explanation as both a visual-design problem and a mathematics problem. In videos, she turns dense notation or diagrams into readable screen moments without weakening the technical claim.

#### Appearance Direction

- Blonde lesbian woman with contemporary studio style.
- Shoulder-length or tied-back blonde hair, consistent silhouette, practical but expressive wardrobe.
- Clear, alert expression; expressive hands for pointing at diagrams or overlays.
- Wardrobe palette: black, white, cobalt, soft magenta, or cool gray, with one repeatable accent.
- No fashion-runway exaggeration, romance framing, or identity exposition unrelated to the teaching purpose.

#### Visual Prompt Sheet

Use:

> Fictional STEAM undergraduate woman, blonde hair, arts-and-mathematics explainer, contemporary studio style, practical expressive wardrobe, clear confident expression, presenter framing with room for formulas and charts, bright soft studio light, neutral production background, lifelike animated style, consistent character model, no logos.

Avoid:

> real person likeness, celebrity resemblance, public figure resemblance, romance-coded pose, stereotype styling, changing hairstyle, glamor editorial lighting, cluttered art studio, unreadable background, cartoonish exaggeration.

#### Voice Prompt Sheet

- Voice range: bright mezzo.
- Pace: energetic but controlled.
- Diction: crisp, especially for symbols, units, and short labels.
- Emotional color: confident, visually attentive, lightly playful when clarifying.
- Best use: formula overlays, chart callouts, analogy checks, screen-composition beats.
- Avoid: sing-song delivery, flirtation, identity jokes, speed that outruns captions, celebrity imitation.

#### Dialogue Rules

- Give Lena the line that makes a diagram or formula legible.
- Let her trim clutter: "That is too much on one screen" is a valid role.
- Use her to catch bad metaphors and replace them with cleaner visual language.
- Do not reduce her to decoration or comic relief.

### Julian Brooks

#### Prototype Role

Julian is the technology-and-systems guide. He connects the viewer to the app, navigation flow, source artifact, or procedural step, and he makes the prototype feel usable.

#### STEAM Emphasis

- Technology: app workflow, interface reading, media tooling, and source-artifact paths.
- Engineering: process, handoff, and constraints.
- Mathematics: practical interpretation of charts or measured outputs when needed.

#### Public-Safe Biography

Julian is an undergraduate who likes systems that a viewer can actually inspect. In videos, he connects the explanation to the app, the next click, the visible artifact, or the workflow that makes the idea testable.

#### Appearance Direction

- Light-skinned Black gay man with polished casual presenter style.
- Short natural hair or close-cropped curls, consistent facial structure, camera-friendly posture.
- Wardrobe palette: navy, warm gray, white, teal, or black; no loud patterns.
- Friendly screen-presenter presence, direct eye contact, easy but not performative.
- No identity caricature, no exaggerated mannerisms, no influencer parody.

#### Visual Prompt Sheet

Use:

> Fictional STEAM undergraduate man, light-skinned Black, polished casual technology-and-systems presenter, short natural hair or close-cropped curls, friendly direct expression, modern clean wardrobe, waist-up presenter framing, bright soft studio light, neutral background, space for app navigation overlay, lifelike animated style, consistent character model, no logos.

Avoid:

> real person likeness, celebrity resemblance, public figure resemblance, exaggerated mannerisms, stereotype styling, streetwear cliche, corporate stock-photo pose, changing skin tone, changing facial structure, busy background.

#### Voice Prompt Sheet

- Voice range: smooth tenor-baritone.
- Pace: direct, medium, slightly faster during app steps but always caption-safe.
- Diction: clear action verbs and interface labels.
- Emotional color: capable, friendly, practical.
- Best use: app navigation, workflow transitions, toolchain explanations, next-step pointers.
- Avoid: hype-host cadence, sales language, forced jokes, identity performance, celebrity imitation.

#### Dialogue Rules

- Give Julian the "what do I do with this?" line.
- Let him connect ideas to screen actions, artifacts, or next steps.
- Use him to reduce friction when the app or workflow appears.
- Do not make him only the tutorial narrator; he should also understand the concept.

## Ensemble Interaction Pattern

Use a triangular dialogue pattern:

1. Mira defines the mechanism or physical intuition.
2. Lena makes the visual or formula readable.
3. Julian connects the idea to the app, artifact, or next action.

For a 60 to 90 second pilot, each character should have two to four short lines. No single character should carry more than half the spoken words unless the script intentionally uses one narrator and two brief responders.

## Prototype Line Allocation

Recommended first prototype allocation:

- Mira: opens the core concept and narrates the visual mechanism.
- Lena: introduces the formula or chart overlay and simplifies the screen composition.
- Julian: performs or narrates the app-navigation moment and closes with the next action.

## Character Prompt Packet Requirements

Each generated-media trial should include:

- character name;
- role label;
- appearance prompt;
- negative likeness constraints;
- wardrobe palette;
- framing requirement;
- voice direction;
- pronunciation notes;
- continuity reference link or image once available;
- and source script beat.

## Continuity Checklist

For each generated image, animation, or video shot:

- [ ] Name and role match the character sheet.
- [ ] Face, hair, skin tone, and wardrobe remain consistent with the prior reference.
- [ ] Shot leaves overlay room when the script calls for a chart, formula, caption, or app visual.
- [ ] Character behavior matches role and tone.
- [ ] No real-person likeness is visible.
- [ ] No identity trait is used as a joke, shortcut, or proof of expertise.
- [ ] Captions remain readable at mobile scale.
