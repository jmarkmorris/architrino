## BT28-01 — Low: planar containment is weaker than affine dimension two

**Location:** lines 25–36, component-braid dimensionality. The source first defines D as affine dimension, then says a component is 2D when one fixed plane contains all its paths. A line or point also lies in a fixed plane, so that condition alone does not establish affine dimension two. The displayed cases omit what happens when complete membership and paths are present but their affine span has dimension below two.

**Independent counterexample:** take a source-declared component whose members remain at distinct fixed positions `(a_i,0,0)`, with at least two distinct values of a. Its complete paths have affine dimension one: every difference is a multiple of `(1,0,0)`, and a nonzero difference exists. All paths lie in the fixed plane z=0. Thus the written plane-containment test labels it 2D while the preceding affine-dimension definition does not. This is a prescribed coordinate counterexample, not a claimed bound or physically admitted Noether braid. The chapter defines component membership through source declaration and does not add a nondegeneracy condition that excludes such data; its own all-axial coordinate boundary makes degenerate geometry relevant.

**Current consumer:** contextual source reading of `src/apps/borg/library/BorgLibraryDescriptors.mjs`, lines 28–70, finds `describeBounds` using affine rank and `describeBraidDimensionality` returning unavailable when a declared component has rank below two. It expressly calls that component degenerate. `BorgLibraryComposition.mjs`, lines 1–19, requires a complete disjoint nonempty member partition but supplies no geometric nondegeneracy condition. This is evidence of the consumer's explicit handling, not proof that its numerical rank tolerances are exact or that the runtime was exercised. The corpus and consumer descriptions therefore differ at this boundary.

**Smallest proposal:** define 2D as paths lying in one fixed plane but not all in one affine line; preserve 3D as not lying in any fixed plane. Extend the `Not assigned` case to include a component of affine dimension below two, consistently with the live consumer. Do not introduce another braid class, silently classify line/point boundaries as physical braids, or change any EOM law. If the scientific owner instead intends 2D to mean planar including degeneracies, it must explicitly distinguish that planar category from exact affine dimension and adjudicate the consumer boundary.

**Falsifier:** an applicable source condition that excludes every rank-zero/rank-one component from the taxonomy's domain, or an explicit definition making D a planar/spatial category rather than affine dimension, would remove this ambiguity. Neither was found in the complete chapter or the inspected composition contract. ## Independent no-change assessments

1. **Component dimension versus assembly span, lines 27–41.** Two nondegenerate circles in parallel planes z=0 and z=1 each have affine span two. Their union has affine span three because planar independent directions together with a between-plane displacement span R3. The chapter correctly retains 2D component classification and a separate 3D assembly span. This remains true independently of whether such paths satisfy the Master Equation.
2. **Circle occupancy, lines 43–69.** Equality of complete geometric carriers is an equivalence relation; cardinality of each equivalence class gives occupancy. Classes of sizes 1 and 2 give Mixed, two classes each of size 2 give Multiple per circle without one global circle. Changing phase, travel direction or polarity on a fixed circle changes none of those geometric sets. Equal radii with different centers do not imply equal circles. The text makes these distinctions explicitly. Missing complete carriers are not silently counted as singleton classes.
3. **Co-sphericity, lines 71–83.** The criterion concerns member positions, not orbit centers. A circle of radius rho in a plane at axial height h about a common sphere center obeys squared distance h²+rho², so circles with different center heights can lie on the same sphere when that sum is common. Conversely, coincident circle centers do not make unequal-radius circles co-spherical about that declared center. This supports retaining sphere and circle classifications as separately evidenced characteristics.
4. **Coordinate relation, lines 111–121.** For an axial unit vector n and transverse unit vector e with n·e=0, an endpoint displacement h n+rho e has squared norm h²+rho². The added definitions of R, h and rho make the displayed relation reconstructible. The vanishing-h and vanishing-rho boundaries are coordinate loci, not proofs of dynamical occupancy.
5. **Identity and evidence boundaries, lines 3–21 and 123–132.** The accepted [Borg identity-relation contract](../../app-borg/contracts/assembly-identity-relation-contract.md) keeps exact configuration identity separate from display bytes, taxonomy co-membership, occurrence, and source-declared discovery-entry grouping. The chapter's flat scientific records do not forbid grouping several distinct exact configurations for discovery; no parent-template inheritance is claimed by that contract. Therefore the current grouping contract is not a reason to rewrite the chapter's flat scientific identity statement. No complete registry/hash or live UI audit was performed.
