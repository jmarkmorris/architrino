## Proposed corrections

### CAS19-01 — Medium: a shared seed is identified with shared worldlines

**Passage:** lines 17–35, especially line 33: “The same prescribed worldlines admit a phase-compensated orthogonal-axis description and a coincident-axis description”. The geometric body-diagonal projection is correct, but the path identity is not established. [Spatial Braid Assemblies](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/noether-braid/3d-braid-assemblies.md), lines 70–72, expressly distinguishes the static own-axis prescription at transverse radius zero from the body-diagonal rotating prescription of the same six initial positions.

**Derived independent reference:** put the positive seed positions at $R\mathbf e_a$ and let $U(T)$ rotate about $\hat{\mathbf n}=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3$ with nonzero angular rate $\omega$. Projection gives $\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot R\mathbf e_a=R/\sqrt3$ and transverse squared radius $R^2-R^2/3=2R^2/3$, preserving both displayed geometric formulas. But the body-diagonal paths have velocity $\omega R\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf e_a\ne0$ at the seed. The own-axis prescription at $\rho=0$ has zero internal velocity for every binary. A fixed coordinate rotation cannot convert stationary paths into moving paths. This is a kinematic counterexample, independent of any solver or acceptance result.

**Consequence and smallest repair:** retain the exact seed projection and the prescribed rotating two-ring channel; replace the worldline identity with a shared instantaneous-inventory statement and explicitly distinguish the two motion prescriptions. Retain the no-retention qualification. Falsifier: an explicit orthogonal-axis path prescription satisfying its own fixed-axis relations and agreeing with these body-diagonal rotating worldlines for all absolute times, rather than only at the seed.

### CAS19-02 — Medium: the two-plane exclusion is extended to the general height chart

**Passage:** line 35: “The all-equatorial boundary remains the only fixed-coordinate coincident-axis three-binary locus not excluded by that axial argument”. The lemma at lines 39–59 assumes exactly two fixed planes at heights $+h$ and $-h$, with one polarity per plane. The owning coordinate chart instead permits independent $h_1,h_2,h_3$ and mixed boundary choices; see Spatial Braid Assemblies, lines 204–207 and 242–254.

**Derived independent reference:** for a receiver and transmitter at signed heights $z_i,z_j$, the axial acceleration of one simple causal hit is $\kappa\operatorname{sign}(q_iq_j)|q_iq_j|(z_i-z_j)W^{\mathrm{acc}}_{ij}/r_{ij}^3$. If two same-polarity sites lie at unequal positive heights, their contribution to the higher receiver is outward. The lemma's decisive same-plane-zero step then fails. This does not construct an acceleration-balanced unequal-height branch; it demonstrates that the proved sign argument does not exclude that chart.

**Consequence and smallest repair:** restrict the all-equatorial exception to the equal-height, polarity-segregated two-ring family actually covered by the lemma; leave unequal-height and mixed-polarity charts unadjudicated by this proof. Preserve the lemma itself. Falsifier: a separate complete argument excluding every admitted non-equatorial height/polarity chart, including the nonzero same-polarity axial contributions. Merely reproducing the two-plane result does not supply it.

### CAS19-03 — Medium: an axial frame is offered for a parity-odd orientation

[Mathematical Terminology](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/archie/mathematics-terminology.md), line 31, and [Terminology Usage](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/archie/terminology-usage.md), line 161, explicitly require a declared nondegenerate indexed polar frame and prohibit substituting three angular-momentum axial vectors.

**Derived independent reference:** inversion sends every polar vector $\mathbf d_a$ to $-\mathbf d_a$, so the sign of a nonzero three-column determinant reverses. An axial vector formed from a cross product obeys $(-\mathbf d)\times(-\dot{\mathbf d})=\mathbf d\times\dot{\mathbf d}$, so a determinant of three such axial vectors is parity-even. In a common-axis angular-momentum chart those vectors can also be collinear, leaving no determinant sign at all. The local transformation table for the polarity dipole and one declared axial spin remains correct; it cannot cure this separate orientation definition.

**Consequence and smallest repair:** require the nondegenerate indexed polar frame at line 85, remove the axial-frame alternative, and explicitly condition the factorization and crossing-order interpretation on a declared nondegenerate chart. Preserve the correct $C/P/CP$ transformation table and the distinction between orientation and polarity assignment. Falsifier: an explicit extraction from the proposed angular-momentum record that supplies an independently declared polar frame and proves a nonzero parity-odd orientation throughout the stated domain. Three axial vectors alone cannot do so.

The exact delayed chord gives $c_f\Delta=2\rho|\cos(\delta/2)|$, hence $\delta=2(s/c_f)|\cos(\delta/2)|$. Replacing that cosine by one is a small-sweep estimate, not an exact finite-angle relation; an order-one speed ratio does not provide that approximation's control. No numerical trajectory was run.

**Consequence and smallest repair:** use explicitly defined internal speed and keep it separate from group translation. Calling its diameter-based estimate a rough scale would make the approximation's limited role clearer; deriving a new transit solution is optional and outside the needed repair. This preserves the useful insight that causal delay matters without importing Kepler dynamics. Falsifier of the notation finding: a local definition of $v$ compatible with its reserved group-speed meaning that yields the asserted sweep for a nonzero-frequency stationary-center binary.
