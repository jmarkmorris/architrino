## EOS02-01 — medium: a change in basin weight does not establish branch opening

**Exact passage:** lines 154–166 give `mu_c(B_k^W(c))=0` and `mu_c'(B_k^W(c'))>0`, then translate this as “a branch with no admissible histories before, and some after.” Lines 78–80 associate opening an impossible branch with crossing a singular chart surface.

**Independent counterexamples.** A nonempty singleton basin `{1/2}` inside `[0,1]` has zero Lebesgue measure. More robustly, keep the admissible set, flow, branch charts and basin `B=[0,1/2]` unchanged. Let the first normalized preparation have density 2 on `(1/2,1]`, and let the second be uniform on `[0,1]`. The displayed weights change from zero to `1/2`, although the branch was admissible in both cases. Both measures are normalized; the basin boundary has measure zero under both, and a second basin can complete the partition. Thus the chapter's existing normalization and clean-boundary conditions do not remove the counterexample. This is an abstract measure/flow countermodel, not an asserted Architrino solution.

**Consequence.** The test confuses zero preparation probability with dynamical impossibility and can declare a chart opening when only the preparation changed. This matters to the chapter's separation of ontology, admissibility and inference.

**Smallest proposed repair:** retain the useful zero-to-positive equation as a preparation-weight opening criterion. Say explicitly that it does not prove a newly admissible branch. A claim of dynamical branch opening separately requires an empty-to-nonempty basin/admitted-chart change, or a theorem linking the declared measure support to branch existence and controlling changes in preparation. Preserve the same-measure requirements for subsequent probability/interference/thermodynamic comparisons.

**Owner and falsifier:** route the bounded prose clarification to the existing corpus owner, with the branch-selection/measure owner if stronger equivalence is desired. A proved local equivalence between zero measure and absent admissible histories under explicit hypotheses would overturn the finding; neither normalization nor boundary-nullness supplies it. No Born-rule failure is inferred.

**History:** the inspected pre-integration diff already described a zero-to-positive measure change as a wall crossing. The later wording makes emptiness explicit, but the underlying inference predates that wording. ## EOS02-02 — low: separate fold onset from admission of simple-root contributions

Line 354 says, “The onset condition is root existence plus transversality,” while line 358 correctly identifies generic same-transmitter onset as a fold. At an ordinary fold the new root is degenerate: the transmitter derivative vanishes at onset. The normal form `g(s,lambda)=(s-s_*)^2-lambda`, with `s_*` chosen inside the past-time domain, has no roots for negative lambda, one double root with zero derivative at zero, and two simple roots thereafter. Hence transversality belongs to ordinary simple-root evaluation after onset; it is not a necessary property of the first fold event.

The displayed same-transmitter set and sub-wake-speed chord inequality are correct and require no change. The current Master Equation already distinguishes regular positive-separation roots from certified fold/caustic treatment. Smallest proposed correction: state that onset is root existence, with a singular-event account where the transversality floor fails; admitted ordinary contributions require the applicable simple-root condition and transmitter weight. Route within the existing root-chart wording owner, without changing equations, assigning a new continuation law or reopening a physical onset theorem. Falsifier: an explicitly defined alternative use of “onset” excluding the first fold event and applying only to the later admitted simple-root chart would resolve the wording; that is not the present sentence's stated scope.
