The present chapter instead calls these necessary and gives a higher-order counterexample. Independent calculation: both $f(z)=z^4$ and $g(z)=-z^4$ have first and second derivative zero at zero, whereas their signs away from zero establish a strict minimum and maximum respectively. Positive definite internal Hessian suffices locally by the second-order Taylor estimate; semidefiniteness does not. The repaired mathematical distinction, meaning and explanatory example survive. A new unconditional stability assertion or an invalid sign calculation would reopen the issue. No delayed dynamical stability follows without the explicitly required conservative reduction.

**MG-05, mass weighting and physical modes:** the earlier review identifies missing symmetric-positive response assumptions and identification of Hessian eigenvectors with vibration modes. The present chapter states those assumptions and the correct transformation. Independently take the effective conservative linear equation $M\ddot x+Hx=0$ with constant symmetric positive definite $M$ and symmetric $H$. Substitution of $x=u e^{i\omega t_{\mathrm{eff}}}$ gives $Hu=\omega^2Mu$. Multiplying by $M^{-1/2}$ and setting $w=M^{1/2}u$ gives $(M^{-1/2}HM^{-1/2})w=\omega^2w$. This derives both similarity and the transformed eigenvector without relying on a solver. It is an effective comparison law conditional on the chapter's recovery premises, not an imported primitive law. The retained conditions and explanation survive. Frequency-dependent response or significant memory/dissipation defeats this reduction and is already excluded by the chapter's stated domain.

Additional independent checks: the infinitesimal rigid displacement map $\delta R_A=a+\theta\times R_A$ has a one-dimensional rotational kernel for collinear distinct nuclei, giving two rotational displacement modes; a nonlinear configuration has three. Adding three translations gives the stated internal mode counts only under actual rigid-motion symmetries. A fixed orienting environment can remove those symmetries, as the chapter says. Two torsional potentials differing by a fourth-order term near the minimum can have identical Hessians but different finite-angle barriers, supporting its separation of local frequency from barrier height.
