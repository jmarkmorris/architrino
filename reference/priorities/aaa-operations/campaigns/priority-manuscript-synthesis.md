## Coverage before compression

Each lane writer first inventories the whole lane recursively: Markdown, mathematical appendices, code, structured data, retained measurements, images, attachments, archives and nested folders. Identify nested priority owners separately and assign each exactly once. Inventory is not reading: record read, partly read, unread, inaccessible and nontext material requiring another instrument. Trace external-to-lane references only as needed to establish the actual law, evidence or dependency; do not quietly claim a repository-wide source search.

Keep a source-to-chapter coverage map in the lane's existing work log, using a focused supporting coverage file only when size warrants it. Record each distinct substantive result or insight with its precise source section, assumptions, grade, current disposition and destination. Preserve negative findings, counterexamples, failed approaches with reusable reasons, unresolved conflicts and qualifications. Map a finding either into a manuscript passage or to an explicit supporting reference with the reason it remains supporting. Duplicate accounts can share a destination, but their provenance must remain traceable. Unread or unavailable evidence remains an explicit coverage gap.

Preserve original source and evidence bytes during first-pass synthesis. No deletion, move, archiving, hash repinning or rewrite of frozen proofs. The manuscript does not need to repeat every receipt or every line of derivation; it must retain the scientific substance and route readers to the detailed justification. No claim that nothing was lost based only on file counts, headings or a summarizer's assurance. Completion requires a source-to-manuscript omission audit, including the qualifications and negative results.

## Lane workflow and acceptance

### Argument structure for drafting and maintenance

Organize the manuscript by scientific questions and explanatory dependencies, using chapters, sections and subsections where the argument requires them. Use a single document title, then coherent major chapters with numbered subordinate headings such as 3.1 and 3.1.1 when useful. Heading depth is not a ranking of certainty or importance: a central unresolved question can deserve a whole chapter, while an established supporting calculation may belong in a subsection. State evidentiary status explicitly in prose. Do not create miscellaneous chapters or force every chapter to have the same depth.

Before drafting or revising, test whether each major heading names a coherent subject and whether its subordinate sections supply the definitions, assumptions, derivation, examples and limitations needed to explain it. Add explanatory prose where needed; merely relabeling the existing flat sections does not meet this requirement. Keep supporting technical detail accessible without burying the main argument. When an accepted result arrives, integrate it at its logical destination and reconsider the surrounding outline rather than append another top-level section. Update the coverage map and affected internal links when headings or destinations change.

Prepare this hierarchy for eventual corpus integration. Check existing corpus ownership: a lane manuscript can supply sections to several existing corpus documents and need not become one new page. Complete the scientific exposition before promotion; preserve its hierarchy wherever appropriate to the destination. This campaign does not authorize promotion.

1. Establish the lane boundary, live file inventory, current owners, active writers and source hashes. Inventory digest binders before any edits to existing files. The coordinator owns global policy and dispatch records.
2. Read and map the sources in manageable batches. Large lanes use several read-only extraction assignments divided by source families; one writer integrates their returned findings. Extractors report unread portions explicitly. Never infer scientific independence from two readers of one argument.
3. Draft the chapter outline around the lane's actual research questions. Begin with purpose, primitives or system contract, and the central closure problem. Develop definitions, arguments, evidence, counterexamples, consequences and unresolved alternatives in connected prose. Follow live canon without importing effective physics as substrate premises.
4. Write manuscript.md. Existing manuscripts are updated incrementally rather than replaced. Every substantial conclusion states its assumptions and evidence grade. Link to focused supporting sources; keep task identifiers, receipts and coordination history outside the scientific narrative. Resolve cross-lane overlap by naming the subject owner and explaining the local consequence, not by inventing competing canonical claims.
5. Have a separate reviewer audit the manuscript against the source inventory and coverage map. Review both directions: every manuscript claim needs support, and every substantive source finding needs a disposition. Distinguish an editorial fidelity audit from independent mathematical proof verification. A contradiction becomes a named unresolved question, never a silent choice.
6. Repair supported omissions and wording issues, then verify file links, math delimiters, KaTeX parsing and a real browser-rendered excerpt. Check for missed mathematics as well as parse errors: a malformed delimiter can leave an expression outside the renderer's count. Preserve the source formula. Run scoped content/whitespace checks and applicable ranking checks only when corresponding metadata changes.
7. Coordinator accepts a lane draft only when coverage gaps and review limitations are explicit, the scientific story is readable, and the source evidence remains preserved. Record handoff and link the manuscript from the lane tracker. This does not finish the lane's scientific obligations or authorize promotion or publication.
