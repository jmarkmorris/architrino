## App Solver: mathematical computation with a distinct explanatory role

App Solver differs from the other two because its central subject includes the mathematics of generating a trajectory under the delayed interaction law. The fully read [master-equation binding](../../app-solver/contracts/master-eom-binding-v1.md), [evolution contract](../../app-solver/contracts/evolution-contract-v1.md) and [error-budget ledger](../../app-solver/contracts/certified-error-budget-ledger.md) specify more than application controls. They connect continuous retained histories, causal roots, acceleration, coupled evolution, numerical enclosures and accepted output. This is a suitable subject for a technical manuscript even when the underlying physical law remains owned by Master-Equation Closure.

The distinction between initial data and a displayed future path supplies the natural starting point. A delayed equation needs a retained history over an interval, because a receiver at the current time depends on earlier transmitter states. Position and velocity at one instant do not encode that history. The evolution contract requires each accepted extension to be computed from the causal roots of histories already accepted by that run. It also requires all paths in a candidate step to use the same accepted coupled state. Otherwise the computation can accidentally use one path's proposed future state to advance another.

Root geometry gives another substantial mathematical chapter. A simple root has a transmitter-side factor that excludes zero, allowing a certified enclosure and a signed continuation rate. The signed root playback ratio and the acceleration weight serve different purposes: a receiver-side zero can stop local emission-time playback without removing the acceleration contribution. Root continuation alone cannot certify that no additional roots exist. The contract separately requires recovery scans, root-free complements and explicit treatment of unresolved boundaries. A fold changes the numerical route; it does not authorize evaluating a divergent sharp expression or silently discarding the contribution.

The error ledger shows why this is numerical analysis rather than only workflow. For a step of width $h$, an acceleration uncertainty bounded componentwise by $\delta a$ contributes at most $h\delta a$ to the velocity increment and $h^2\delta a/2$ to the position increment. These bounds follow by integrating the uncertainty once and twice. Root-time uncertainty must first be propagated into an acceleration enclosure; it cannot be added directly to a position allowance. The ledger also distinguishes per-step increments from inherited history uncertainty and allocates finite-width event uncertainty through velocity impulse and position moment. A resource limit remains a limit on attempted computation and supplies no permission to enlarge a mathematical error allowance.
