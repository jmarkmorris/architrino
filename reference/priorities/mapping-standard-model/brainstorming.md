The LEP bottom asymmetry is compatible at the observer level with a small rotation of effective left- and right-channel couplings that approximately preserves their norm:

$$
\begin{pmatrix}g_L^b\\g_R^b\end{pmatrix}_{\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\phi_b&-\sin\phi_b\\
\sin\phi_b&\cos\phi_b
\end{pmatrix}
\begin{pmatrix}g_L^b\\g_R^b\end{pmatrix}_{\mathrm{SM}}.

This preserves $(g_L^b)^2+(g_R^b)^2$ while changing

$$
A_b=\frac{(g_L^b)^2-(g_R^b)^2}{(g_L^b)^2+(g_R^b)^2}.

Plainly: the comparison separates a rate-like norm from a left–right asymmetry, but it does not derive the rotation or identify its assembly carrier.

$$
\mathcal M_{D^{(*)},\ell}^{\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}}(q^2)

#### Closure Question

Can one branch-predicted rotation of the effective bottom-quark left/right neutral-current exposure improve the correlated $(R_b,A_b,A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b)$ comparison while preserving the total $Z\to b\bar b$ rate?

This packet answers only the observer-level covariance question. It does not derive the rotation from a retained bottom branch and therefore does not explain the LEP anomaly.

#### Source And Measurement Layers

The current-value screening row uses the [2025 Particle Data Group electroweak review](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2025/reviews/rpp2025-rev-standard-model.pdf) values and predictions. The experimental correlation coefficients are inherited from Tables 5.10 and 5.11 of the final LEP/SLD report, [*Precision Electroweak Measurements on the Z Resonance*](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ex/0509008). The final heavy-flavor fit gives

$$
\rho(R_b,A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b)=-0.10,
\qquad
\rho(R_b,A_b)=-0.08,
\qquad
\rho(A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b,A_b)=+0.06.
$$

The row called $A_b$ below is the direct SLD left-right-forward-backward measurement. The value of $A_b$ inferred from $A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b=(3/4)A_eA_b$ is not an independent datum and must not be included beside $A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b$ in the same covariance vector.

Because the latest PDG theory update does not republish a new three-row experimental covariance matrix, combining the current PDG central values with the final LEP/SLD correlations is a screening fit rather than an official electroweak refit.

#### Leading Pseudo-Observable Map

Let

$$
\mathbf g_b^{(0)}=
\begin{pmatrix}g_L^{b,0}\\g_R^{b,0}\end{pmatrix}
$$

be the Standard Model effective coupling vector. The candidate exposure rotation is

$$
\mathbf g_b(\phi_b)
=
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\phi_b&-\sin\phi_b\\
\sin\phi_b&\cos\phi_b
\end{pmatrix}
\mathbf g_b^{(0)}.
$$

Define

$$
S_b=(g_L^b)^2+(g_R^b)^2,
\qquad
D_b=(g_L^b)^2-(g_R^b)^2,
\qquad
B_b=\frac{2g_L^bg_R^b}{S_b}.
$$

At leading pseudo-observable order,

$$
\Gamma_b\propto S_b,
\qquad
R_b=\frac{\Gamma_b}{\Gamma_{\mathrm{had}}},
\qquad
A_b=\frac{D_b}{S_b},
\qquad
A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b=\frac{3}{4}A_eA_b.
$$

The rotation preserves $S_b$ exactly. If the other hadronic widths and radiative rows are held fixed,

$$
R_b(\phi_b)=R_b^{(0)}.
$$

Writing $A_b^{(0)}=D_b^{(0)}/S_b$ and $B_b^{(0)}=2g_L^{b,0}g_R^{b,0}/S_b$, the exact asymmetry response is

$$
A_b(\phi_b)
=
A_b^{(0)}\cos(2\phi_b)
-
B_b^{(0)}\sin(2\phi_b).
$$

For the bottom branch $g_L^{b,0}g_R^{b,0}<0$, so

$$
B_b^{(0)}=-\sqrt{1-(A_b^{(0)})^2}.
$$

Holding the electron coupling row fixed gives

$$
A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b(\phi_b)
=
A_{\mathrm{FB}}^{b,0}
\frac{A_b(\phi_b)}{A_b^{(0)}}.
$$

The first-order response vector in the ordered basis

$$
\mathbf y=(R_b,A_b,A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b)^T
$$

is therefore

$$
\mathbf J_b
=
\left.\frac{\partial\mathbf y}{\partial\phi_b}\right|_{\phi_b=0}
=
\begin{pmatrix}
0\\
-2B_b^{(0)}\\
-2B_b^{(0)}A_{\mathrm{FB}}^{b,0}/A_b^{(0)}
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The zero first component is the central prediction: a pure exposure rotation changes the angular/chiral readout but not the leading total bottom width.

#### Covariance Residual

For observed vector $\mathbf y_{\mathrm{obs}}$, baseline prediction $\mathbf y_0$, and covariance $C$, define

$$
\chi^2(\phi_b)
=
\left[\mathbf y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbf y(\phi_b)\right]^T
C^{-1}
\left[\mathbf y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbf y(\phi_b)\right].
$$

In the linear neighborhood,

$$
\widehat\phi_b
=
\frac{
\mathbf J_b^TC^{-1}(\mathbf y_{\mathrm{obs}}-\mathbf y_0)
}{
\mathbf J_b^TC^{-1}\mathbf J_b
},
\qquad
\sigma_{\phi_b}
=
\left(\mathbf J_b^TC^{-1}\mathbf J_b\right)^{-1/2}.
$$

The numerical result below minimizes the exact trigonometric map rather than truncating at first order.

#### Interpretation And Failure Conditions

The covariance calculation establishes three limited results:

1. A norm-preserving left/right rotation can lower both $A_b$ and $A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b$ while leaving $R_b$ unchanged at leading order.
2. The direct SLD $A_b$ and LEP $A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b$ rows prefer the same sign of rotation, but not the same magnitude. The one-parameter fit compromises between them.
3. Current inputs reduce the local preference to about $2\sigma$; the covariance shape is interesting, but the anomaly is not strong.

The candidate fails as an $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ mechanism if any of the following occurs:

- the retained bottom branch does not predict $\phi_b<0$ with magnitude of order $10^{-2}\,\mathrm{rad}$;
- the same construction produces excluded rotations for the down, strange, charged-lepton, or electron-coupling rows;
- radiative or Noether sea corrections change $S_b$ enough to spoil the successful $R_b$ row;
- the rotation introduces tree-level flavor-changing neutral currents;
- the fit improves only after $\phi_b$ is selected from $A_b$ or $A_{\mathrm{FB}}^b$ rather than computed from the branch;
- or the full electroweak covariance block worsens after the remaining $Z$-pole observables are restored.

#### Branch-Derivation Burden

The next native object is not another anomaly fit. Let the normalized coupling directions obtained after observer projection be

$$
\widehat{\mathbf g}_b^{(0)}
=
\frac{(g_L^{b,0},g_R^{b,0})}{\sqrt{(g_L^{b,0})^2+(g_R^{b,0})^2}},
\qquad
\widehat{\mathbf g}_b^{\mathrm{branch}}
=
\frac{(g_L^{b,\mathrm{branch}},g_R^{b,\mathrm{branch}})}
{\sqrt{(g_L^{b,\mathrm{branch}})^2+(g_R^{b,\mathrm{branch}})^2}}.
$$

The branch-predicted angle is then the ordinary oriented angle

$$
\phi_b^{\mathrm{branch}}
=
\operatorname{atan2}\!\left(
g_L^{b,0}g_R^{b,\mathrm{branch}}-g_R^{b,0}g_L^{b,\mathrm{branch}},
g_L^{b,0}g_L^{b,\mathrm{branch}}+g_R^{b,0}g_R^{b,\mathrm{branch}}
\right).
$$

The effective branch couplings in this equation must be projected from the same Generation-III bottom record that supplies mass response, color triplet structure, weak exposure, and neutral-current provenance. Their allowed native inputs are the existing $R_{\mathrm{rel}}^b$, $\mathsf s_{\mathrm{sh}}(b)$, $\Sigma_{\mathrm{WCT}}^b$, $\theta_{\mathrm{sea}}$, and $\mathcal W_{\mathrm{causal}}^b$ records; the fit angle itself is not an input.

The first useful native calculation is to project one retained or explicitly candidate bottom branch onto the already established left/right weak-exposure channels, compute the resulting normalized two-component exposure vector, and compare its angle with the corresponding Standard Model reference direction without using the LEP asymmetry as input.
