## Mathematical Targets

### Event-ledger row

For each weak reaction $e$, the candidate ledger row is

$$
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{weak}}(e)
=
\left(
X_{\mathrm{in}},
X_{\mathrm{meas}},
X_{\mathrm{invis}},
\mathcal{E}_W,
\mathcal{C}_{W/Z},
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{CKM}},
\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{PMNS}},
Q_{E\mathbf{p}\mathbf{J}},
Q_{\mathrm{pol}},
Q_{\mathrm{path}},
Q_{\mathrm{sea}},
D_{\mathrm{obs}}
\right).
$$

$X_{\mathrm{meas}}$ contains directly reconstructed charged or hadronic products. $X_{\mathrm{invis}}$ contains neutrino, flavor, or invisible rows inferred from the balance, spectrum, detector, and weak-bookkeeping constraints. The row fails if $X_{\mathrm{invis}}$ is declared without $D_{\mathrm{obs}}$ and $Q_{E\mathbf{p}\mathbf{J}}$.

### Exposure-domain rows

The weak-exposure domain must return one domain object

$$
\mathcal{E}_W
\longrightarrow
\left(
V\!-\!A,
\mathcal{C}_{W/Z},
\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{CKM}},
\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{PMNS}},
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{weak}}
\right),
$$

not separate tuned objects for chirality, weak-corridor provenance, quark mixing, lepton mixing, and reaction ledgers.

### Flavor-overlap residuals

The quark and lepton overlap residual can be staged as

$$
\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{flavor}}
=
d_{\mathrm{unit}}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{CKM}}^\dagger\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{CKM}},I)
+d_{\mathrm{CKM}}(\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{CKM}}^{\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}},\mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{CKM}}^{\mathrm{obs}})
+d_{\mathrm{CP}}(J^{\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}},J^{\mathrm{obs}})
+d_{\mathrm{PMNS}}(\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{PMNS}}^{\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}},\mathbf{U}_{\mathrm{PMNS}}^{\mathrm{obs}})
+R_{\mathrm{domain}}.
$$

$R_{\mathrm{domain}}$ is zero only when the same weak-exposure domain supplies chirality, corridor provenance, CKM rows, PMNS rows, and source/detector event ledgers.

### Decay-clock constraints

For a parent channel $c$, the priority decay-clock residual is

$$
\mathcal{R}_{\tau}(c)
=
\left(
\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{obs}}(c)-\tau_{\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}}(c)}{\sigma_\tau(c)},
\frac{B_{\mathrm{obs}}(c)-B_{\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}}(c)}{\sigma_B(c)},
\frac{L_{\mathrm{decay}}(c)-\beta\gamma c\,\tau_{\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}}(c)}{\sigma_L(c)}
\right).
$$

This is a finite-window observer target. It is not evidence that architrino primitives have physical mass, and it cannot replace the retained parent-branch carrier.

### Missing-transverse-momentum provenance

For collider invisible rows, the balance row is

$$
\Delta_T(e)
=
\mathbf{p}_T^{\mathrm{miss}}
+\sum_{o\in O_{\mathrm{hard}}}\mathbf{p}_{T,o}
+\mathbf{p}_T^{\mathrm{soft}}.
$$

The invisible row is admissible only when $O_{\mathrm{hard}}$, the soft term, pileup convention, recoil convention, uncertainties, and object calibration are recorded in $D_{\mathrm{obs}}$.

## Mass-Map Recovery Links

- Lifetimes and branching ratios are observer finite-window benchmarks for parent-assembly decay-clock rows; they are not primitive mass assignments.
- CKM and PMNS entries are flavor-overlap benchmark surfaces. Their native consumer is the weak-exposure domain, not a detached parameter table.
- Neutrino $\Delta m^2$ rows are common-clock phase residuals. Absolute neutrino mass limits remain separate observer bounds until a retained neutral-lepton branch supplies the carrier.
- Muon, pion, kaon, and neutron lifetime rows connect to the mass map only through retained parent-assembly branch geometry, weak exposure, and finite-window decay probability.
- Missing transverse momentum is a detector-balance product. It can support neutrino or invisible weak rows only after the measured-object ledger closes.
