These characters are fictional STEAM-aligned presenters. Their visual identities are design constraints for consistency and representation, not the source of their authority. Their authority in videos must come from what they explain, demonstrate, ask, clarify, challenge, or verify.

## Ensemble Concept

The recurring ensemble is a flexible cast. Any character may appear alone, with one other character, or with any subset of the ensemble. Each character has a recurring job:

- make one hard idea concrete;
- ask one useful viewer-facing question;
- translate one visual, app action, chart, or formula into plain language;
- challenge one assumption, definition, or evidence gap when a claim needs pressure;
- and keep the explanation moving without becoming a full theory lecture.

Cast selection should follow the instructional need of the video. Add an `Educator` later only if the script needs an instructor role that the peer ensemble cannot naturally carry.

## Shared Character Register

All four characters should present as sophisticated, articulate, highly educated, and well spoken. In practice, this means:

- they speak in complete, carefully shaped sentences;
- they use precise vocabulary without becoming obscure;
- they sound academically serious but still conversational;
- they show discipline, taste, and composure in how they dress, move, and explain;
- they can simplify difficult material without sounding casual, shallow, or gimmicky;
- and they avoid filler-heavy speech, influencer cadence, slang-driven humor, and exaggerated reactions.

Their level of education should read as academically advanced for STEAM undergraduates: well-read, technically capable, culturally literate, and comfortable with precise explanations.

## Shared Continuity Rules

- Presentation style: lifelike animated characters first, with enough realism for emotional presence but enough stylization to preserve continuity across generated shots.
- Camera style: waist-up or chest-up presenter shots, medium close-ups for dialogue, and over-shoulder or picture-in-picture framing for app and concept visuals.
- Wardrobe: modern STEAM-studio clothing, clean lines, practical materials, no logos, no celebrity-coded outfits.
- Build and fitness: thin, fit, athletic builds that read as regular gym habits rather than extreme bodybuilding.
- Fit and silhouette: clothing should fit well and accentuate each character's athletic body while staying sophisticated, tasteful, and camera-appropriate.
- Lighting: bright studio key light, soft fill, neutral background, subtle Architrino accent color only when it supports continuity.
- Framing: leave open space on one side for formula, chart, caption, or app overlay.
- Motion: restrained gestures, no exaggerated influencer mannerisms, no distracting idle animation.
- Identity handling: never use demographic traits or appearance as a joke, proof of expertise, or substitute for personality.
- Personal-life boundary: scripts should not discuss the characters' dating, relationships, family, private biography, or other personal-life details unless explicitly approved for a specific story reason.
- Likeness handling: do not imitate, reference, or converge toward any real person, celebrity, public figure, influencer, actor, professor, or existing fictional character.

## Character Sheets

### Mia Quinn

#### Prototype Role

Mia is the conceptual physics lead. She defines the key idea, names the moving parts, and gives the viewer a clean mental model before a visual demonstration.

#### STEAM Emphasis

- Science: physical intuition and mechanism.
- Engineering: only where system constraints clarify the physical idea.
- Mathematics: only when a compact expression helps the viewer hold the mechanism.

#### Public-Safe Biography

Mia is an undergraduate who likes turning abstract physics into conceptual scaffolding viewers can hold. In videos, she is the person who stops the group long enough to define the mechanism, sketch the idea, and explain what the viewer should watch for next.

#### Appearance Direction

- Auburn-haired woman with optional Ukrainian design influence.
- Thin, fit, athletic build with a regular gym habit.
- Straight or gently layered hair, medium length, controlled and repeatable.
- Light or medium-light skin tone, natural makeup, clear eyes, calm facial expression.
- Technical wardrobe: fitted jacket, knit top, or clean lab-studio layers in slate, white, deep green, or muted copper; clothes fit well and accentuate her athletic body.
- No flag motifs, folk-costume shorthand, celebrity styling, or accent-based caricature.

#### Visual Prompt Sheet

Use:

> Fictional STEAM undergraduate woman, auburn hair, thin fit athletic build, calm conceptual physics presenter, modern technical studio wardrobe, well-fitted jacket and knit top that accentuate her athletic body tastefully, warm precise expression, waist-up presenter framing, bright soft studio light, neutral background, space beside her for diagrams, lifelike animated style, consistent character model, no logos.

Avoid:

> real person likeness, celebrity resemblance, public figure resemblance, influencer glamour pose, national costume, exaggerated accent cues, lab-coat stereotype, harsh shadows, ornate background, changing hair color, changing facial structure.

#### Voice Prompt Sheet

- Voice range: warm alto.
- Pace: calm, moderate, deliberate.
- Diction: polished, well-spoken technical wording without stiffness.
- Emotional color: steady, grounded, quietly curious.
- Best use: definitions, mechanism setup, diagram narration, careful claims.
- Avoid: exaggerated accent, breathy drama, hype, sarcasm, condescension, celebrity imitation, filler-heavy speech.

#### Dialogue Rules

- Give Mia the first precise definition in a scene.
- Let her name assumptions and limits.
- Keep her lines compact and visual: "Watch the delay path here" works better than long abstract claims.
- Do not make her the only technically competent character.

### Sophia Calder

#### Prototype Role

Sophia is the visual mathematics lead. She specializes in mathematical intuition and visual reasoning: translating equations into geometric pictures, identifying patterns hidden in data, and helping viewers see why a mathematical relationship matters before calculating it.

#### STEAM Emphasis

- Mathematics: intuition, structure, symbolic economy, and geometric meaning.
- Arts: visual reasoning, metaphor discipline, and viewer attention.
- Science: conceptual accuracy in visual explanation.

#### Public-Safe Biography

Sophia is an undergraduate who treats good explanation as a visual mathematics problem. In videos, she asks what an equation is really doing, turns dense notation into geometric pictures, and makes patterns visible without weakening the technical claim.

#### Appearance Direction

- Blonde woman with contemporary studio style.
- Thin, fit, athletic build with a regular gym habit.
- Shoulder-length or tied-back blonde hair, consistent silhouette, practical but expressive wardrobe.
- Clear, alert expression; expressive hands for pointing at diagrams or overlays.
- Wardrobe palette: black, white, cobalt, soft magenta, or cool gray, with one repeatable accent; clothes fit well and accentuate her athletic body.
- No fashion-runway exaggeration, romance framing, or identity exposition unrelated to the teaching purpose.

#### Visual Prompt Sheet

Use:

> Fictional STEAM undergraduate woman, blonde hair, thin fit athletic build, visual mathematics presenter, contemporary studio style, practical expressive well-fitted wardrobe that accentuates her athletic body tastefully, clear confident expression, presenter framing with room for geometric diagrams, formulas, and charts, bright soft studio light, neutral production background, lifelike animated style, consistent character model, no logos.

Avoid:

> real person likeness, celebrity resemblance, public figure resemblance, romance-coded pose, stereotype styling, changing hairstyle, glamor editorial lighting, cluttered art studio, unreadable background, cartoonish exaggeration.

#### Voice Prompt Sheet

- Voice range: bright mezzo.
- Pace: energetic but controlled.
- Diction: articulate and crisp, especially for symbols, units, and short labels.
- Emotional color: confident, visually attentive, lightly playful when clarifying.
- Best use: mathematical intuition, geometric interpretation, formula overlays, pattern recognition, chart callouts, analogy checks.
- Avoid: sing-song delivery, flirtation, identity jokes, speed that outruns captions, celebrity imitation, casual throwaway phrasing.

#### Dialogue Rules

- Give Sophia the line that asks what the equation is really doing.
- Let her turn equations into geometric pictures and patterns in data.
- Use her to catch weak visual metaphors and replace them with mathematically cleaner visual language.
- Do not reduce her to decoration or comic relief.

#### STEAM Emphasis

- Engineering: process, handoff, and constraints.
- Mathematics: practical interpretation of charts or measured outputs when needed.

#### Public-Safe Biography

Julian is an undergraduate who likes systems that a viewer can actually inspect. In videos, he connects the explanation to the app, the next click, the visible artifact, or the workflow that makes the idea testable.

- Thin, fit, athletic build with a regular gym habit.
- Short natural hair or close-cropped curls, consistent facial structure, camera-friendly posture.
- Wardrobe palette: navy, warm gray, white, teal, or black; clothes fit well and accentuate his athletic body; no loud patterns.
- Friendly screen-presenter presence, direct eye contact, easy but not performative.
- No identity caricature, no exaggerated mannerisms, no influencer parody.

#### Voice Prompt Sheet

### Rachel Stone

#### Prototype Role

Rachel is the experimentalist skeptic. She asks hard questions, requests evidence, challenges assumptions, and forces definitions when the explanation starts to outrun what has been shown.

#### STEAM Emphasis

- Science: experimental design, evidence, observables, and measurement discipline.
- Engineering: testability, failure modes, and constraints.
- Mathematics: uncertainty, comparison, and residual checks when a claim needs numerical discipline.

#### Public-Safe Biography

Rachel is an undergraduate experimentalist who wants claims to earn their place. In videos, she asks the question a careful viewer is already thinking: why should I believe this, what would count as evidence, and which assumption is doing the work?

#### Appearance Direction

- Blonde woman with a composed experimentalist presence.
- Thin, fit, athletic build with a regular gym habit.
- Shoulder-length or tied-back hair, controlled and repeatable.
- Focused expression, direct eye contact, precise posture.
- Wardrobe palette: charcoal, white, deep red, forest green, or graphite; clothes fit well and accentuate her athletic body.
- No detective cliches, courtroom styling, hostile demeanor, or adversarial caricature.

#### Visual Prompt Sheet

Use:

> Fictional STEAM undergraduate woman, blonde hair, thin fit athletic build, experimentalist skeptic, composed evidence-focused presenter, modern clean well-fitted wardrobe that accentuates her athletic body tastefully, focused direct expression, waist-up presenter framing, bright soft studio light, neutral background, space beside her for evidence notes or residual checks, lifelike animated style, consistent character model, no logos.

Avoid:

> real person likeness, celebrity resemblance, public figure resemblance, hostile prosecutor pose, detective costume, exaggerated suspicion, sarcastic expression, cluttered lab background, changing facial structure, changing hair color.

#### Voice Prompt Sheet

- Voice range: clear mezzo-alto.
- Pace: measured, exact, slightly clipped when asking a hard question.
- Diction: precise, evidence-centered, and well spoken.
- Emotional color: composed, skeptical, intellectually fair.
- Best use: evidence requests, assumption checks, definition pressure, experimental questions, residual or validation framing.
- Avoid: cynicism, sarcasm, gotcha cadence, hostile interrogation, celebrity imitation, filler-heavy speech.

#### Dialogue Rules

- Give Rachel the "why should I believe that?" line.
- Let her ask for the observable, test, residual, or definition.
- Use her to make theory claims more credible by voicing the viewer's objection.
- Do not make her a generic contrarian; her skepticism should improve the explanation.

## Ensemble Interaction Pattern

Use the four-role dialogue pattern when a video needs the full ensemble:

1. Mia defines the mechanism or physical intuition.
2. Sophia explains what the mathematics means visually.
3. Rachel asks what evidence or definition supports the claim.
4. Julian connects the idea to the app, artifact, or next action.

For a 60 to 90 second pilot, cast only the characters needed for the topic. A single-character video is valid when one role can carry the explanation cleanly; two-, three-, and four-character videos are valid when dialogue improves the teaching.

## Prototype Line Allocation

Recommended first prototype allocation if all four appear:

- Mia: opens the core concept and narrates the visual mechanism.
- Sophia: explains what the formula, geometry, or data pattern means.
- Rachel: asks for evidence, definitions, or a validation check.
- Julian: performs or narrates the app-navigation moment and closes with the next action.

## Continuity Checklist

For each generated image, animation, or video shot:

- [ ] Name and role match the character sheet.
- [ ] Face, hair, skin tone, and wardrobe remain consistent with the prior reference.
- [ ] Shot leaves overlay room when the script calls for a chart, formula, caption, or app visual.
- [ ] Character behavior matches role and tone.
- [ ] No real-person likeness is visible.
- [ ] No demographic or appearance trait is used as a joke, shortcut, or proof of expertise.
- [ ] No personal-life detail is introduced unless explicitly approved for the script.
- [ ] Captions remain readable at mobile scale.
