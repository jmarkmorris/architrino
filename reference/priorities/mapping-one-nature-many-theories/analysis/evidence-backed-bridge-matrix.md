# Evidence-Backed Bridge Matrix

**Status:** Completed first-pass evidence object for ONMT-001. This is a priority-stage audit, not reader-facing canon. Standard-physics results below are recovery targets or comparison results, never substrate premises for $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$.

## Purpose

The theory-island metaphor becomes useful only when every bridge can be read in both directions. From a more resolved description toward an effective description, the audit asks what map is controlled and what residual bounds it. From the effective description back toward the more resolved one, the audit asks what can actually be reconstructed and how large the class of compatible antecedents remains. These are different questions because coarse-graining, effective matching, statistical inference, and constitutive closure are usually many-to-one.

The first pass contains eight representative certificates. They span controlled low-energy matching, asymptotic reduction, statistical and constitutive closure, inverse inference, and unresolved handoffs. They establish that the network contains bridges of materially different authority. They do not establish that every physics domain is an independent ontology, that every bridge is incomplete, or that $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ has recovered any row.

Plainly: each island can be approached from either shore. The established direction may carry reliable predictions outward, while the return journey may recover only a family of possible source states. The matrix records both facts.

## Bidirectional Certificate

For a bridge from description $i$ to description $j$, let

$$
M_{j\leftarrow i}:\mathcal D_i\longrightarrow\mathcal D_j
$$

denote the controlled forward map over a declared regime $\mathcal R_{ij}$. Given a target record $y\in\mathcal D_j$, the reverse object is generally not an inverse function. It is the compatibility fiber

$$
\mathfrak F_{i\mid y}^{(\epsilon)}
=
\left\{
x\in\mathcal D_i:
d_j\!\left(M_{j\leftarrow i}(x),y\right)\leq\epsilon_{ij}
\right\}.
$$

Plainly: a coarse record can rule out many fine histories while still failing to select one. Writing the reverse side as a set prevents a successful reduction from being mistaken for a unique reconstruction.

For a proposed common physical history $H\in\mathcal H$, the row-level closure test is

$$
\Delta_{j\leftarrow i}(H)
=
d_j\!\left(
M_{j\leftarrow i}(P_i(H)),
P_j(H)
\right)
\leq
\epsilon_{ij},
$$

where $P_i$ and $P_j$ are independently specified projections into the two observer-level descriptions. A bridge certificate therefore records

$$
\widehat{\mathcal B}_{j\leftarrow i}
=
\left(
\mathcal D_i,
\mathcal D_j,
M_{j\leftarrow i},
\mathcal R_{ij},
\epsilon_{ij},
\mathcal I_{ij},
\mathcal L_{ij},
\Pi_{ij},
\mathfrak F_{i\mid y}^{(\epsilon)},
\mathcal S_{ij},
\mathcal C_{ij},
\mathcal F_{ij}
\right).
$$

Here $\mathcal I_{ij}$ is preserved information, $\mathcal L_{ij}$ is discarded or non-identifiable information, $\Pi_{ij}$ records parameter and consumed-data provenance, $\mathcal S_{ij}$ is an independently checkable source, $\mathcal C_{ij}$ is the $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ common-history target, and $\mathcal F_{ij}$ is the falsifier.

Plainly: the added fields expose two common disguises. A fitted parameter cannot pass as a derived constant, and a citation to a working approximation cannot pass as evidence that a common constituent history has been retained.

## B1 — Electroweak Theory to the Fermi Interaction

- **Claim grade:** Derived within the Standard Model as a controlled low-energy effective match; measured for the numerical value of $G_F$; not derived within $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$.
- **Source and target descriptions:** The source is the charged-current electroweak sector with resolved $W$ propagation. The target is the local current-current Fermi interaction used for low-energy processes such as muon decay.
- **Forward bridge map:** Expanding the $W$ propagator for $q^2\ll M_W^2$ contracts the resolved exchange into a four-fermion operator. At tree level, $G_F/\sqrt{2}=g^2/(8M_W^2)$; radiative corrections alter the parameter relation through the electroweak correction ledger.
- **Validity regime and residual:** The expansion is controlled by powers of $q^2/M_W^2$, with QED and electroweak radiative corrections declared separately. The Particle Data Group review notes both the muon-lifetime extraction of $G_F$ and the small propagator correction proportional to $m_\mu^2/M_W^2$.
- **Preserved information:** Low-energy charged-current amplitudes, chirality structure, reaction-channel selection, and the matched coupling normalization to the computed order.
- **Discarded or non-identifiable information:** Resolved $W$ propagation, polarization and off-shell structure, high-energy behavior, and any unique microscopic reconstruction from $G_F$ alone.
- **Parameter and consumed-data provenance:** $G_F$ is extracted from the measured muon lifetime after declared corrections; $g$, $M_W$, and the radiative correction scheme belong to the electroweak description. The low-energy datum is therefore not an independent derivation of the high-energy ontology.
- **Reverse fiber:** All high-resolution theories whose low-energy charged-current amplitudes match the same operator coefficients within tolerance remain compatible until additional electroweak observables constrain the lift.
- **Independent source:** [Particle Data Group, “Electroweak Model and Constraints on New Physics”](https://pdg.lbl.gov/2020/mobile/reviews/pdf/rpp2020-rev-standard-model-m.pdf), especially Sec. 10.2.1.
- **Common-history projection target:** One retained substrate reaction history must project both to the resolved weak-corridor record and to the same low-energy four-fermion event ledger without refitting $G_F$. The current scientific owner is [Electroweak Bosons](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/assemblies/bosons/electroweak-bosons.md#low-energy-four-fermi-limit).
- **Falsifier:** A candidate common history fails this row if its resolved weak record and contracted low-energy record disagree beyond the declared propagator and radiative residual, or if matching requires a separately fitted low-energy coupling.

## B2 — QED to NRQED and Nonrelativistic Bound States

- **Claim grade:** Derived within standard effective-field-theory practice to a declared truncation order; not a $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ derivation of atomic structure.
- **Source and target descriptions:** The source is relativistic QED. The target is NRQED and its nonrelativistic bound-state Hamiltonian or Lagrangian with Wilson coefficients.
- **Forward bridge map:** Relativistic amplitudes are matched at a scale near the particle mass to an operator expansion of the form $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{NRQED}}=\sum_n c_n(\mu)\mathcal O_n/m^{d_n-4}$. Bound-state calculations then expand in the characteristic velocity, coupling, and inverse mass while retaining the matched short-distance coefficients.
- **Validity regime and residual:** The regime requires nonrelativistic constituents and declared separation among the mass, momentum $mv$, and energy $mv^2$ scales. The residual is the first omitted order in the chosen velocity, coupling, recoil, and operator expansions rather than one universal number.
- **Preserved information:** Low-energy on-shell amplitudes, gauge-compatible operator relations, spin and recoil effects to the retained order, and short-distance influence encoded in Wilson coefficients.
- **Discarded or non-identifiable information:** Explicit antiparticle and hard relativistic modes, details above the matching scale, and any unique ultraviolet history beyond what the retained coefficients constrain.
- **Parameter and consumed-data provenance:** Wilson coefficients are calculated by matching to QED or, where additional structure enters, supplied by measurement or a separate higher-resolution calculation. A precision atomic result inherits those coefficients and its nuclear-structure inputs.
- **Reverse fiber:** A finite set of low-energy coefficients identifies an equivalence class of ultraviolet theories, not a unique relativistic state or interaction history.
- **Independent sources:** [Caswell and Lepage, “Effective Lagrangians for Bound State Problems in QED, QCD, and Other Field Theories”](https://doi.org/10.1016/0370-2693(86)91297-9) and [Labelle, “NRQED in Bound States”](https://arxiv.org/abs/hep-ph/9209266).
- **Common-history projection target:** One retained substrate record must project to both the relativistic electromagnetic comparison amplitudes and the effective atomic envelope, including the same spin, recoil, radiative, and nuclear-structure inputs. The current domain owner is [Atomic Structure](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/nuclear-atomic/atomic-structure.md).
- **Falsifier:** The row fails if the low-energy atomic projection cannot reproduce independently fixed QED coefficients and spectra within the declared truncation error, or if separate substrate parameter fits are required for relativistic and atomic records.

## B3 — QCD to Chiral Nuclear Effective Theory

- **Claim grade:** Derived as a symmetry-constrained and power-counted effective framework; measured or lattice-informed for many low-energy constants and boundary inputs; not a complete first-principles derivation of nuclear structure from one quark-gluon history.
- **Source and target descriptions:** The source is QCD with quark and gluon variables. The target is chiral EFT with nucleons, pions, contact operators, and organized two-, three-, and higher-body interactions.
- **Forward bridge map:** Low-momentum operators are ordered by powers of $Q/\Lambda_\chi$ subject to QCD's approximate chiral symmetry and its breaking pattern. Long-range pion terms and short-range contact terms are combined, while low-energy constants carry unresolved short-distance QCD information.
- **Validity regime and residual:** The regime is $Q$ below the chiral breakdown scale and within a declared regulator and many-body truncation. Residual authority comes from order-by-order power counting, regulator checks, and uncertainty estimates; it is not uniform across all nuclear observables.
- **Preserved information:** QCD symmetry constraints, pion-range structure, quantum numbers, unitarity requirements, and observables to the computed EFT order.
- **Discarded or non-identifiable information:** Explicit quark-gluon histories inside nucleons, high-momentum modes, and short-range structure compressed into low-energy constants and regulator-dependent intermediate representations.
- **Parameter and consumed-data provenance:** Low-energy constants may be constrained by pion-nucleon or nucleon-nucleon data and increasingly by lattice QCD. When fitted to nuclear data, they are not independent predictions of QCD.
- **Reverse fiber:** The same low-energy nuclear observables can be compatible with multiple short-distance QCD realizations and regulator representations until sufficient matched data constrain the lift.
- **Independent source:** [Epelbaum, Hammer, and Meißner, “Modern Theory of Nuclear Forces”](https://arxiv.org/abs/0811.1338).
- **Common-history projection target:** The same retained substrate inventory must yield both quark/hadron records and the nuclear binding, channel, and reaction records without replacing quarks by ontologically unrelated nucleons at the handoff. The current owner is [Nuclear Binding](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/nuclear-atomic/nuclear-binding.md).
- **Falsifier:** The row fails if a proposed substrate map reproduces isolated hadron records but cannot generate nuclear interactions with the correct channel structure and controlled residuals, or if nuclear matching requires private constituent or coupling inputs absent from the hadron projection.

## B4 — Boltzmann Kinetics to Hydrodynamics

- **Claim grade:** Derived in formal and, for specified scalings and solution classes, rigorous hydrodynamic limits; constitutive and phenomenological outside those controlled regimes; not a universal derivation of continuum behavior from arbitrary microscopic matter.
- **Source and target descriptions:** The source is a one-particle distribution $f(x,v,t)$ obeying a Boltzmann kinetic equation. The targets are Euler or Navier-Stokes-Fourier fields such as density, bulk velocity, temperature, stress, and heat flux.
- **Forward bridge map:** The primary fields are velocity moments of $f$, while a small-Knudsen scaling and approach to local equilibrium close the moment hierarchy. Euler behavior appears at leading order and viscous or heat-conducting terms enter at subsequent order under the declared asymptotic program.
- **Validity regime and residual:** The bridge depends on dilute-gas assumptions, collision-kernel properties, scaling, regularity, boundary conditions, and proximity to local equilibrium. Residuals are expressed in the relevant Knudsen/Mach scaling and solution norm; no single global $O(\mathrm{Kn})$ claim applies to every flow.
- **Preserved information:** Conserved collision invariants, local density, momentum and energy moments, and—where derived from the collision operator—transport coefficients and their controlled corrections.
- **Discarded or non-identifiable information:** The detailed velocity distribution beyond retained moments, molecular correlations excluded by the kinetic closure, and the unique molecular history.
- **Parameter and consumed-data provenance:** For controlled dilute gases, viscosity and thermal conductivity can be related to the collision kernel. For real fluids and broader continuum models, equations of state and transport laws may require empirical constitutive input.
- **Reverse fiber:** A hydrodynamic field history admits many compatible distributions $f$ and vastly more compatible molecular histories; a closure ansatz selects a tractable representative family rather than inverting the coarse-graining.
- **Independent sources:** [Bardos, Golse, and Levermore, “Fluid Dynamic Limits of Kinetic Equations I: Formal Derivations”](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01026608) and [Golse, “The Boltzmann Equation and Its Hydrodynamic Limits”](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1874-5717(06)80006-X).
- **Common-history projection target:** One retained substrate ensemble and coarse-graining rule must supply the effective thermodynamic and transport fields while preserving the same conservation and boundary ledger. This lane records the cross-domain burden; the detailed thermodynamic and material derivations remain with their scientific owners.
- **Falsifier:** The row fails for a proposed common-history map if its direct hydrodynamic projection disagrees with the moment reduction of its kinetic projection beyond the declared limit, or if the two routes require different transport or boundary parameters.

## B5 — Quantum Dynamics to Decohered Apparatus Records

- **Claim grade:** Derived for the reduced-state map and measured in many decoherence regimes; inferred for the emergence of stable classical-looking correlations; unresolved for the selection and persistence of one definite outcome under unitary dynamics alone.
- **Source and target descriptions:** The source is a quantum state of system, apparatus, and environment evolved jointly. The intermediate target is the apparatus reduced state and a dynamically selected stable basis. The claimed endpoint is a single persistent apparatus record.
- **Forward bridge map:** For joint evolution $U(t)$, the reduced state is $\rho_A(t)=\operatorname{Tr}_{S,E}[U(t)\rho_{SAE}(0)U^\dagger(t)]$. Environment-induced decoherence suppresses selected off-diagonal terms and stabilizes pointer correlations under model-specific interaction conditions.
- **Validity regime and residual:** The reduction by partial trace is exact after the system split is declared. Decoherence rates and preferred structures depend on the interaction Hamiltonian, initial correlations, environmental spectrum, and approximation used. A useful model residual is the norm of off-diagonal terms in the declared pointer basis over the record window.
- **Preserved information:** Outcome weights in the reduced description, stable correlations, and the observables redundantly or robustly recorded in the environment under the selected model.
- **Discarded or non-identifiable information:** Global phase relations and correlations hidden by the trace, the unique global state given the reduced state, and—without additional interpretive or dynamical structure—the fact of one selected outcome.
- **Parameter and consumed-data provenance:** The system-environment split, interaction Hamiltonian, bath state, pointer observable, and record criterion must be fixed independently of the desired classical outcome.
- **Reverse fiber:** Many entangled global states share the same reduced apparatus state. Even an effectively diagonal reduced density matrix does not uniquely specify the global branch or, by itself, convert the ensemble description into one selected event.
- **Independent source:** [Schlosshauer, “Decoherence, the Measurement Problem, and Interpretations of Quantum Mechanics”](https://arxiv.org/abs/quant-ph/0312059).
- **Common-history projection target:** One retained substrate event history must produce the quantum probability record, apparatus transition, environmental handoff, conservation ledger, and one persistent outcome from the same measure. The current owners are [Measurement Ontology](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/quantum/measurement-ontology.md) and [Measurement Problem and Collapse](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/philosophy-history/theory-bridges/measurement-problem-and-collapse.md).
- **Falsifier:** The proposed common-history bridge fails if outcome weights, apparatus dynamics, environmental exchange, or record persistence require different ensembles, or if the substrate event rule cannot reproduce interference when isolation is restored and stable records when decoherence is strong.

## B6 — General Relativity to Newtonian and Post-Newtonian Gravity

- **Claim grade:** Derived within general relativity as a controlled weak-field and slow-motion expansion for declared systems; not a substrate derivation of metric behavior.
- **Source and target descriptions:** The source is a relativistic metric and matter system satisfying Einstein's field equations. The targets are Newtonian gravity and successive post-Newtonian equations for motion and radiation.
- **Forward bridge map:** Expand the metric and equations in small parameters such as $v/c$ and $GM/(rc^2)$, solve the constraint and field hierarchy in a specified gauge and source class, and project the result onto effective potentials, trajectories, multipoles, and waveforms.
- **Validity regime and residual:** The expansion applies in weak-field, slow-motion, suitably isolated regimes, with strong-field internal structure handled only under additional assumptions or matching. At $N$PN order the residual begins at the first omitted PN order, but regularization, radiation reaction, spin, eccentricity, and finite-size effects must be declared separately.
- **Preserved information:** Newtonian attraction, relativistic orbital corrections, conserved quantities within the approximation, radiation reaction and wave observables to the retained order, and source multipoles carried by the chosen formalism.
- **Discarded or non-identifiable information:** Exact strong-field geometry, gauge-dependent metric detail, higher multipoles and radiation terms beyond truncation, and any unique relativistic spacetime inferred from Newtonian trajectories alone.
- **Parameter and consumed-data provenance:** Mass, spin, multipole, equation-of-state, and initial/boundary inputs remain source-model data unless independently derived. Agreement after fitting them does not derive their origin.
- **Reverse fiber:** A Newtonian or finite-PN record is compatible with multiple relativistic metrics, coordinate choices, higher-order completions, and source interiors.
- **Independent sources:** [Einstein, Infeld, and Hoffmann, “The Gravitational Equations and the Problem of Motion”](https://edition-open-sources.org/sources/10/17/index.html) and [Blanchet, “Post-Newtonian Theory for Gravitational Waves”](https://arxiv.org/abs/1310.1528).
- **Common-history projection target:** The same substrate and Noether sea record must project to the effective metric, Newtonian limit, post-Newtonian corrections, clock behavior, light propagation, and radiation without separate fits. The current owner is [General Relativity](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/spacetime/general-relativity.md).
- **Falsifier:** The row fails if a common substrate record that matches the Newtonian sector cannot reproduce the required post-Newtonian and signal observables within one fixed parameter and nuisance ledger.

## B7 — Relativistic Cosmology to CMB Parameter Inference

- **Claim grade:** Derived for the selected relativistic background, perturbation, and Boltzmann model; measured for the sky data; inferred for parameter posteriors conditional on likelihood, nuisance model, priors, and model family; not a unique reconstruction of cosmic ontology.
- **Source and target descriptions:** The source is a relativistic cosmological model with matter content, initial perturbations, and effective background variables. The forward target is the set of transfer functions and angular spectra. The inverse target is a posterior over cosmological and nuisance parameters given the observed sky.
- **Forward bridge map:** A Boltzmann hierarchy evolves the declared species and perturbations to predicted spectra $C_\ell(\theta)$. An observational likelihood then supplies $p(\theta\mid D)\propto p(D\mid\theta)p(\theta)$ after instrument, beam, calibration, foreground, mask, and other nuisance choices are declared.
- **Validity regime and residual:** The bridge is conditional on the cosmological model family, perturbative treatment, recombination and species microphysics, numerical tolerances, foreground model, likelihood construction, and data combination. Cosmic variance and parameter degeneracy are properties of the inference, not evidence of a unique source history.
- **Preserved information:** Model-predicted TT/TE/EE and lensing spectra, cross-channel correlations, and parameter combinations identifiable within the adopted likelihood.
- **Discarded or non-identifiable information:** Most phase and microhistory information compressed into spectra, degenerate parameter directions, and any ontology not distinguished by the selected observables and priors.
- **Parameter and consumed-data provenance:** The background parameterization, primordial spectrum, recombination inputs, neutrino sector, foreground parameters, calibration data, masks, priors, and external datasets must remain visible. The effective scale factor $a(t)$ is an observer-level comparison variable here, not substrate ontology.
- **Reverse fiber:** The posterior is explicitly a distribution over compatible parameter rows, and distinct model families or nuisance prescriptions may fit overlapping data products. It is not an inverse map from the CMB to one physical history.
- **Independent sources:** [Lewis, Challinor, and Lasenby, “Efficient Computation of Cosmic Microwave Background Anisotropies in Closed FRW Models”](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/9911177) and [Planck Collaboration, “Planck 2018 Results VI: Cosmological Parameters”](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201833910).
- **Common-history projection target:** One substrate source-and-transport record must reproduce the CMB spectrum, TT/TE/EE/lensing structure, frame correction, baryon and neutrino handoffs, distance indicators, and structure growth without changing the hidden state between channels. The current owners are [CMB](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/cosmology/CMB.md) and [Cosmology Reconstruction](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/cosmology/cosmology-reconstruction.md).
- **Falsifier:** The row fails if the direct cosmological projection and the independently specified observation pipeline cannot reproduce the same multi-channel record within declared residuals, or if each observable requires a separately retuned substrate history.

## B8 — Quantum Fields on Curved Spacetime to the Quantum-Gravity Interface

- **Claim grade:** Derived as a semiclassical or stochastic approximation under declared assumptions; inferred as a guide to quantum-gravity consistency; unresolved as a complete theory in which matter and geometry share one quantum or substrate history.
- **Source and target descriptions:** The joint source consists of quantum matter fields on a declared classical curved background together with classical gravitational dynamics. The intermediate targets are semiclassical gravity and its stochastic extension. The unresolved destination is a common theory of matter and geometry.
- **Forward bridge map:** Semiclassical gravity replaces the classical matter source by a renormalized expectation value, schematically $G_{\mu\nu}+\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}+\text{local curvature terms}=8\pi G\langle \hat T_{\mu\nu}\rangle_{\mathrm{ren}}$. Stochastic gravity supplements the mean response with stress-tensor fluctuations encoded through a noise kernel and an Einstein-Langevin equation.
- **Validity regime and residual:** The approximation requires curvature below the effective cutoff, a controlled quantum state, renormalization conditions, and metric fluctuations small enough for the classical or stochastic geometry description to remain valid. There is no single universal scalar residual; fluctuation criteria, state dependence, backreaction stability, and higher-curvature terms must be checked case by case.
- **Preserved information:** Renormalized mean stress response, particle-creation and vacuum-polarization effects in the declared background, and selected stress fluctuations in the stochastic extension.
- **Discarded or non-identifiable information:** Full quantum geometry, matter-geometry entanglement beyond the approximation, ultraviolet completion, and a unique microscopic state behind the mean or noise record.
- **Parameter and consumed-data provenance:** The background class, quantum state, renormalization prescription, gravitational couplings, boundary conditions, and truncation scale are external inputs to the semiclassical problem unless a deeper theory derives them.
- **Reverse fiber:** A semiclassical metric and stress expectation are compatible with many quantum states and with multiple candidate ultraviolet theories. The stochastic two-point record narrows that set but does not uniquely reconstruct quantum geometry.
- **Independent source:** [Hu and Verdaguer, “Stochastic Gravity: Theory and Applications”](https://link.springer.com/article/10.12942/lrr-2008-3).
- **Common-history projection target:** One substrate history must generate matter records, effective metric response, stress fluctuations, backreaction, and the classical limit without importing a separately prescribed background. The current gravity-side owner is [General Relativity](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/spacetime/general-relativity.md); quantum and effective-metric closure remain separate scientific workstreams.
- **Falsifier:** The row fails if the candidate substrate requires one state to generate quantum matter and a separately imposed geometry, or if the same history cannot reproduce the controlled semiclassical data products while remaining consistent with its weak-field and quantum-record projections.

## Cross-Bridge Findings

### 1. Forward Control Does Not Imply Reverse Reconstruction

B1, B2, B4, and B6 show why a strong forward bridge can coexist with a broad reverse fiber. Their reductions may be quantitatively controlled while the target record remains insufficient to reconstruct eliminated modes, fine correlations, source interiors, or ultraviolet completion. This is information loss or non-identifiability in the map, not evidence that the forward theory is defective.

### 2. A Bridge Can Mix Derivation and Calibration

B3 and B7 show that one edge can combine exact equations, controlled expansions, numerical transport, fitted constants, nuisance models, and empirical calibration. The authority attaches field by field, not to the edge as a single adjective. “Derived” is therefore too coarse unless parameter provenance and consumed data are named.

### 3. Some Handoffs Do Not Reach Their Claimed Endpoint

B5 reaches a decohered reduced state under declared assumptions but not one uniquely selected outcome by that map alone. B8 reaches semiclassical or stochastic gravity in controlled regimes but not a common microscopic account of matter and geometry. These are not failures of the intermediate theories; they are limits on what those theories establish.

### 4. Scale Is Only One Network Coordinate

B1 through B4 contain familiar logarithmic ladders, thresholds, matching scales, and coarse-graining. B5 is primarily a state-reduction and record problem, B6 is an asymptotic field-strength and speed limit, B7 includes a forward model followed by statistical inversion, and B8 is a mixed classical-quantum interface. The network therefore cannot be faithfully drawn as one vertical scale ladder.

Plainly: “zooming” explains part of the map, but not all of it. Some connections change resolution, some change variables, some average over inaccessible detail, and some infer hidden parameters from data. The full board has several kinds of motion.

## Path-Consistency Test

When two routes connect the same islands, the common-history requirement is not merely that both routes fit their own target data. For routes $\gamma_1$ and $\gamma_2$ from $i$ to $k$, define

$$
\Delta_{\gamma_1,\gamma_2}(H)
=
d_k\!\left(
M_{\gamma_1}(P_i(H)),
M_{\gamma_2}(P_i(H))
\right).
$$

A common-source account requires $\Delta_{\gamma_1,\gamma_2}(H)$ to remain within the combined declared tolerance without route-specific retuning. The first high-value test case is the atomic/nuclear chain, where electroweak and QED inputs, QCD-to-nuclear matching, atomic structure, and measured spectra meet in one matter record.

Plainly: if two legitimate routes across the map reach different descriptions of the same observable event, the discrepancy cannot be hidden by saying that each theory works on its own island. The mismatch locates either a missing bridge term, inconsistent parameter provenance, or a false common-history claim.
