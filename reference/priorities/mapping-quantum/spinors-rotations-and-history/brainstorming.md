## The Central Question

What returns after one full rotation, what requires two rotations, and what could make that distinction observable?

For a spin-one-half spinor, following transformation through one full rotation changes its sign; a second full rotation restores it. Unit quaternions provide a compact rotation description in which two opposite representatives describe the same ordinary spatial rotation. This is the double-cover relationship $SU(2)\to SO(3)$, where $SO(3)$ describes ordinary spatial rotations and $SU(2)$ supplies their spinor lift.

Plainly: the same visible orientation can have two mathematical representatives. Following the rotation continuously keeps track of which representative is reached. The sign of an isolated quantum state is not observable by itself, so an interpretation needs a relative comparison or another explicitly defined physical record.

## Learning Path

1. **Ordinary rotations and quaternions.** Build a geometric understanding of rotation axes, rotation order, and quaternions as rotation coordinates. Use a belt or plate demonstration as an illustration, not as a particle model.
2. **The double cover.** Follow one rotation continuously through one turn and two turns, comparing the ordinary orientation with the lifted spinor representative.
3. **Phase and physical comparison.** Separate a coordinate sign, a phase convention, a full-history distinction, and an experimentally accessible relative phase. Connect this to the sibling [geometric-phase exploration](../../mapping-standard-model/geometric-phase-and-holonomy/brainstorming.md).
4. **Weyl and Dirac spinors.** Only after the rotation picture is clear, study these as effective relativistic descriptions and ask what additional transformation structure they require. Do not import relativistic spacetime or a wave equation as the architrino substrate.

Plainly: begin with geometry that can be followed by hand, then ask what the mathematical labels mean physically, and leave the larger relativistic formalism until those distinctions are comfortable.
