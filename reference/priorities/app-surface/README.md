# Surface — Closed-Surface Path Research

**Surface** is the operator-selected application name; `app-surface` is its priority-folder identity. The app studies distributions of architrinos whose future paths are calculated from their supplied histories and delayed interactions while an explicit artificial constraint keeps them on a fixed closed surface. The first case is three electrinos and three positrinos on a sphere; selected comparisons are an oblate spheroid and a ring torus. The selected Master Equation variant and speed regime determine tangent interaction acceleration; discarded normal interaction vectors are recorded only after superposition at each member.

This folder owns the Surface app workstream. The full application brief and research explanation are recorded in [manuscript.md](manuscript.md). EOM supplies [Stage B constrained geometry/evolution](../app-solver/analysis/equation-provider-stage-b-2026-10-10.md) and [Stage C transport, domains and restart](../app-solver/analysis/equation-provider-stage-c-2026-10-11.md); [Stage D](../app-solver/analysis/equation-provider-stage-d-2026-10-11.md) independently qualifies one bounded spherical 3:3 calculation through time 0.25. [Stage E](../app-solver/analysis/equation-provider-stage-e-2026-10-11.md) delivers the dedicated [Surface page](../../../surface.html), with bounded record consumption, accepted-only playback and separately reviewed engineering verification. Run the local EOM service with `npm run dev`; static use supports preparation/imported replay.

Numerical preparations offer separate **Master equation variant** and **Speed domain** selectors. Choose Canonical (inverse-square) or experimental Logarithmic potential (inverse-distance), then independently choose unrestricted, inclusive or strict speed. Switching one preserves the other; direct surface changes preserve both current selections. Both interactions support None/Strict checking and the existing surface geometries. The [logarithmic composition definition](../master-equation-closure/equation-variants/surface-constraint/logarithmic-definition.md) and [implementation contract](../app-solver/contracts/evolution-contract-v2.md#independent-surface-interaction-and-speed-selections) state its scope and coupling distinction. The optional analytical replay remains fixed to its retained canonical equation.

Animation duration defaults to 60 viewing seconds, with 30-second, two-minute and five-minute alternatives. Both support plots and their window controls use the same animation-second axis; recorded simulation times and support averages retain their original units. The opening view prepares a moving six-member sphere at speed 0.05, canonical interaction, unrestricted speed and checking None, with a 60-unit simulation horizon. Play automatically inspects the request, starts one buffered calculation and animates available paths. Pause holds animation while calculation continues; Stop calculation requests a durable boundary; Reset returns to the beginning without discarding calculated paths. Surface selection directly creates compatible circular histories, including transitions between a torus and sphere/spheroid. Cross-topology coherent-circle changes use the equator and preserve member phases and speeds; prepared past depth expands when the geometry requires it; a new torus uses major radius three times its tube radius. Coupling, geometry dimensions, latitude, horizon, step and checking are in Advanced settings. Configuration presets and Apply/Inspect/Start steps have been removed. Reviewed histories remain optional under Examples; New experiment exits replay into a fresh numerical experiment. Analytical replay remains explicitly labelled illustrative interpolation of retained observations, with no new evolution or recurrence claim.

New numerical preparations default to **Error bound checking: None**; **Strict** retains the checked evolution route. None calculates exploratory motion and sampled support without trajectory/acceleration error bounds, with initial and resumed horizons up to 60 simulation-time units subject to history and resource limits. Changing the selection creates a new experiment; retained records keep their original grade. The choice does not recalculate the optional analytical replay. The [implementation and focused measurements](analysis/sustained-motion-investigation-2026-11-01.md#optional-error-bound-checking-implementation) distinguish this exploratory route from the unresolved strict-certification reach and future streaming work.

Numerical runs now use [buffered path delivery](analysis/sustained-motion-investigation-2026-11-01.md#buffered-delivery-implementation). Surface starts after an initial buffer while one EOM evolution worker continues, shows the available lead, and waits at the computed boundary if needed. Terminal delivery preserves playback position and supplies the durable record for export/resume. The six-member None sphere has completed a full requested interval through 60 with live animation already active before calculation finished. This finite-run delivery does not establish indefinite throughput or improve Strict numerical reach.


### Initial configurations and two-ring exploration

Surface lists 13 named studied response forms, counting the canonical baseline, separate instantaneous/delayed Weber forms and the multiplier-free linear side comparison. This is an inventory convention, not a count of qualified providers: only canonical and logarithmic have executable Surface EOM providers. The selector lists other forms as unavailable, and its expandable inventory links their research accounts and historical/withdrawn statuses. Transported wake state remains an additional proposal; ceiling responses and speed-domain choices are separate specifications.

The initial equatorial circle is a moving demonstration preparation. Initial arrangement now also offers six coordinate poles on a sphere (three positive-axis positrinos and three negative-axis electrinos). Initial velocity independently offers coherent tangent motion, rest or a random tangent velocity per member. Random speed is uniform from zero to the supplied maximum; direction is uniform in the tangent plane, or either sign on a ring. The visible string seed is hashed with FNV-1a and sampled with Mulberry32 under `surface_initial_conditions/v1`. Re-entering the same seed with the same preparation reproduces the histories; changing geometry, arrangement or population changes the preparation. Initial velocity vectors are inspectable. Request exports wrap the request and seed/settings in `surface_preparation/v1`; playback records retain the same settings, while legacy raw request imports remain supported and do not invent a seed.

Random, resting, polar and two-ring preparations supply prescribed cubic pasts and currently require None checking. Their small stored interpolation/rounding allowances do not certify the future trajectory. Exact latitude-circle preparations retain the existing Strict route. Prepared past depth depends on geometry extent, covering its diameter with a margin. This fixes the default torus admission failure from a past ending too soon for the earliest partner roots; the new torus has tube radius 0.1, major radius 0.3 and prepared past starting at -1.

The Two separated vertical rings choice uses circles in parallel YZ planes, radius 0.1 and separation 0.02 by default, with adjustable separation. EOM keeps each member assigned to its original plane and circle and tangent at projected endpoints. The full added acceleration combines cancellation of normal interaction and the curvature requirement; graphs show its magnitude, and direction arrows/diagnostics retain its three-dimensional vector. This is an artificial kinematic constraint, not evidence of natural confinement. None-only cubics between endpoints have no calculated constraint or trajectory error bound.
