## Causal memory and generated-history eligibility

**Derived, conditional:** if all positions belong to a fixed shape inside an ambient ball of radius $B$, any admitted hit has delay at most $2B/c_f$: the emission and reception points are at most $2B$ apart. This is an ambient chord bound, not a geodesic distance. Under the separately checked coherent speed bound $u<c_f$, the causal residual is strictly monotone in source time; positive simultaneous partner separation then gives at most one strictly past partner root, and the coherent self path has no positive-delay self root. These conclusions require the actual root, separation and chart premises, not simply a requested surface label.

Unknown emissions older than supplied memory are excluded only with the explicit assumption that the unprovided older past obeyed the same ambient bound. Without that assumption the scope is declared retained memory; a compact future surface does not constrain an unknown earlier trajectory.

## What counts as a return

Let $P>0$ be a proposed lag, $a$ an anchor, $\pi$ a fixed polarity-preserving permutation of the six member identities, and $Q$ a fixed proper rigid rotation preserving the actual surface. Both $\pi$ and $Q$ must be the same for every member and every time throughout a compared window. Coordinates are relative to the fixed surface center. Compare $H_{a+P}$ with the transformed $H_a$:

$$
D_X(a,P,Q,\pi)=\max_i\sup_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\frac{\|\mathbf X_i(a+P+s)-Q\mathbf X_{\pi(i)}(a+s)\|}{\ell},
$$

$$
D_V(a,P,Q,\pi)=\max_i\sup_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\frac{\|\mathbf V_i(a+P+s)-Q\mathbf V_{\pi(i)}(a+s)\|}{c_f}.
$$

Here $\ell=L+r=0.15$ for the torus. The velocity normalization is $c_f=1$, not the instantaneous speed, so zero or changing speed does not make the metric singular. All six histories enter the maximum. A fit to one path, positions alone, independently rephased members or a different rotation at each sample cannot satisfy this definition.

Use three clearly distinguished readouts:

| Readout | Allowed identification | Meaning of a successful comparison |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Labeled fixed-coordinate return | $Q=I$, $\pi=\mathrm{id}$ | The six identified recent paths return in the fixed frame. |
| Polarity-preserving return | $Q=I$ and one of $3!3!=36$ fixed permutations | The unlabeled polarity distribution returns; labeled paths may exchange roles. |
| Relative return | One fixed $Q$ in the selected surface's rotation group and one fixed polarity-preserving $\pi$ | The recent paths return modulo a common surface symmetry. |

For centered sphere geometry, use $SO(3)$, the proper rotations about its center. For the centered oblate spheroid or ring torus, start with the identity-connected axial subgroup $Q=R_z(\alpha)$; it preserves the fixed surface. Other discrete shape symmetries can be separately listed later. Do not use arbitrary sphere rotations on a torus. Reflections, translations, time reversal, time rescaling and polarity reversal are excluded from this selected readout. The axial restriction is deliberate and means absence under this subgroup is not absence under every possible surface symmetry.

**Derived:** constant proper rotation and fixed relabeling preserve ambient distances, dot products, polarity products, the Euclidean root equation and the normal-only support equation when the rotation preserves the shape. Thus exact all-history recurrence under such a transformation is consistent with the selected delayed law on the same regular chart. For a finite-memory state, continuation equivalence additionally needs the stated older-tail exclusion and local uniqueness/existence premises. An approximate finite return does not inherit an exact theorem of future periodicity. A rotation changing the shape, a time-dependent rotation or an omitted history invalidates the equivalence.

### Why the chart bounds still matter

**Derived, conditional:** suppose aligned reception positions and every aligned source position in the relevant common memory differ by at most $\delta_X$, both roots are interior to that memory, and the coherent source speed is at most $u<1$. At the same source time, the two causal residuals differ by at most $2\delta_X$, by the reverse triangle inequality. Each residual changes with source time with magnitude at least $1-u$. Their roots therefore differ by at most

$$
|\Delta S|\le\frac{2\delta_X}{1-u}.
$$

For example, the proposed position threshold $\delta_X=0.00015$ and a separately certified $u\le0.02$ would give $|\Delta S|\le0.0003/0.98<0.000307$ at $c_f=1$. The speed bound is a conditional example, not a premise imported from the short run into a future target. If the source acceleration magnitude is bounded by $M_A$ on the connecting emission interval, comparing velocities at their different roots adds at most $M_A|\Delta S|$ to the same-time velocity discrepancy. This explains why the original certificates and acceleration carriers remain relevant to the readout.

Small history discrepancy alone does not bound acceleration near zero separation or a vanishing transmitter factor. Preserve positive delayed-separation and factor floors, root-interior margins and dense source derivative bounds for every proposed candidate. If any fail, a comparison of retained polynomial arrays remains a geometric record observation at its actual provenance, rather than a qualified true-history delayed-equation return. The root bound is falsified by losing an interior root, the uniform subwake premise or the claimed position bound; it is not a global error-propagation or stability theorem.

## Tolerances, uncertainty and numerical decisions

The first declared research thresholds are $\epsilon_X=10^{-3}$ and $\epsilon_V=10^{-4}$ in the normalized metrics. They correspond to $1.5\times10^{-4}$ position and $10^{-4}$ velocity for this torus. These are design choices, not physical constants, inferred energy scales or measured recurrence levels. A candidate must retain its raw discrepancy and numerical uncertainty so a later threshold cannot silently manufacture it.

For any fixed candidate $(a,P,Q,\pi)$, enclose the entire continuous-window discrepancies using the retained polynomial centerlines and their component error radii. Let $[L_X,U_X]$ and $[L_V,U_V]$ contain the true maxima. Return-qualified means $U_X\le\epsilon_X$ and $U_V\le\epsilon_V$, with each maximum's numerical uncertainty width at most ten percent of its threshold. A lower bound above either threshold rejects that fixed candidate. Any remaining threshold overlap, missing enclosure or excessive uncertainty is **unresolved**, never pass or fail by midpoint.

The ten-percent rule also prevents a large interval box from being presented as a meaningful match. Repeat proposed matches with half-step and tighter tolerances, keeping preparation/model and declared anchors/lags/transforms fixed. The scientific comparison must retain independent root/kernel/support checks on the newly reached trajectory, not transfer an old archive's qualification to a new horizon or binary. Agreement between two refinements alone is implementation consistency. A failed chart, resource limit or exhausted subdivision budget yields a bounded inconclusive outcome and its actual accepted prefix.

For future implementation, partition the comparison window at the union of both paths' shifted segment boundaries. On each cell, subtract the transformed polynomials in one common time coordinate. The squared position difference has degree at most six and squared velocity difference degree at most four. Exact rational Bernstein bounds and subdivision can enclose those nominal maxima without sampling gaps. Add the two transformed component-error budgets by the triangle inequality; rotation can mix coordinate errors, so raw componentwise maxima cannot simply be reused. A rational proper rotation admits exact coefficient arithmetic; a numerically fitted rotation needs a certified rotation/coefficient enclosure or an explicit bounded rotation error. Retained decimal coefficients are exact data for the nominal polynomial; they are not the exact trajectory.

If a fitted matrix $\widehat Q$ is within operator-norm error $\eta_Q$ of the claimed proper rotation and source positions/speeds have bounds $B_X,B_V$, add $\eta_QB_X$ to position discrepancy and $\eta_QB_V$ to velocity discrepancy. A small least-squares residual or near-orthogonal matrix alone is not that error certificate. Candidate discovery may use floating-point least squares; the final pass uses a single certified transform on all continuous cells.

## Avoiding trivial and manufactured periods

Slow movement makes very short lags look recurrent. Search only $P\ge1$, and record the full lag-discrepancy curve rather than the best number alone. Use no future interpolation outside accepted coverage. The angular preparation period is $2\pi/0.1\approx62.83$; it is a preparation timescale, not a predicted return period. A uniformly rotating pattern can match modulo an axial rotation at every lag, so a relative return alone need not identify a period. Keep fixed-frame and relative readouts together. A broad or nearly flat low-discrepancy lag band is reported as persistent organization or an unresolved lag, not assigned a fundamental period. Recovering the smallest period needs a separately certified exclusion of shorter eligible lags and treatment of permutation/symmetry degeneracy. A snapshot fit cannot do this.

Alongside return discrepancies, report each window's maximum speed and excursion from its endpoint, $E_X(t)=\max_i\sup_{s\in[-\tau,0]}\|\mathbf X_i(t+s)-\mathbf X_i(t)\|/\ell$, with continuous enclosures. If both excursion and speed are within the declared position/velocity thresholds, label the observation low activity at those thresholds; do not turn an apparent return into an oscillation frequency. A slowly drifting path can pass a finite-tolerance return test without being periodic. Keep any fitted transform ambiguities and the set of tested lags visible. These activity quantities describe motion and do not diagnose an equilibrium or supply a dynamical residual.

For a candidate lag $P$, require at least three **disjoint** generated comparison windows at times $a,a+P,a+2P$ and one common per-return transformation $g=(Q,\pi)$. Check all three relations: $H_{a+P}$ against $gH_a$, $H_{a+2P}$ against $g^2H_a$, and $H_{a+2P}$ against $gH_{a+P}$, each at the same declared thresholds. The third comparison is necessary: two errors of opposite sign relative to a common anchor can each be at threshold while their adjacent difference is twice that threshold. Because $P\ge1>\tau$, the three windows are disjoint, but they remain dependent parts of the same deterministic trajectory, not statistically independent repetitions. This protects against one accidental return while remaining a finite observation. Overlapping sliding windows may help discovery. A decreasing discrepancy over any of these windows is recorded as a finite trend, not asymptotic attraction.

Apply the group powers consistently: $(gH)_i=QH_{\pi(i)}$ implies $(g^2H)_i=Q^2H_{\pi^2(i)}$. Refitting an unrelated rotation or permutation at each return changes the question and cannot satisfy this repeated-return criterion.
