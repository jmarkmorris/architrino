# Sustained six-member Surface evolution

November 1, 2026. The operator requested an investigation of what prevents newly calculated six-architrino motion from keeping ahead of continuous animation. This is a bounded engineering investigation, not a new scientific qualification or authorization to change the equation, relax tolerances, or substitute prescribed future paths. The [earlier cost investigation](display-speed-and-solver-cost-2026-10-20.md) remains historical evidence; the measurements below use a newly rebuilt executable.

## Result

**Measured:** the current numerical ring preparation completes its first 0.25 simulation-time units in approximately 7.5 wall seconds, but the faster ring's continuation stops at approximately 0.33375. Reducing the minimum future step by a factor of 125 or refining the same circular past's segment spacing by a factor of four does not materially extend that boundary. The rejected attempts name `surface_acceleration_budget_exceeded`, beneath the controller's generic `minimum_step_exhausted` halt. None of these runs reaches the service deadline.

**Inferred engineering conclusion:** sustained motion is blocked first by numerical reach, separately by expensive serial certification, and independently by an application/transport design that returns playable paths only after a whole operation completes. Faster arithmetic alone would reach the same error-bound barrier sooner. A buffered viewer need not calculate a new certified state on every rendered frame, but the solver must continue producing accepted history beyond the buffer's consumption frontier.

The radius-ten six-pole opening animation remains the separately reviewed analytical replay. These numerical benchmarks use the existing finite circular-past ring, not the stationary six-pole preparation. Direct inspection of `ProviderTransport.cpp::decode_history` finds only cubic and uniform-circular v2 histories, while `SurfaceEquationProvider.cpp::admit` and `SurfaceConstraint.cpp::admit_surface_preparation` require the exact circular preparation certificate. The complete stationary past used by the analytical six-pole replay is not a delivered numerical Surface input capability. No numerical reproduction of that replay is established here.

## What actually stops continued motion

**Measured by the response ledgers:** both the resumed default-floor run and the extended smaller-floor run publish through approximately 0.33375. The latter then rejects candidate widths descending from 0.00125 to 0.00001; every rejection names the same surface acceleration-width failure. Its maximum accepted position error is approximately 0.000002758 and velocity error approximately 0.00004524. Refining the past changes those values slightly but leaves the accepted boundary unchanged at the shown precision. The smaller step is not a demonstrated remedy for this case.

**Measured by source inspection:** `SurfaceEquationProvider.cpp::evaluate` obtains the canonical interval acceleration, evaluates the constrained acceleration using position and velocity hulls, and rejects a Cartesian component wider than the requested acceleration tolerance. `SurfacePublication.cpp::validated_segment` propagates inherited endpoint errors, bounds the discrepancy between constrained acceleration and the cubic's acceleration over 64 reception cells, and inflates position/velocity tubes until inclusion is proved. The subsequent endpoint recertification in `CoupledEvolution.cpp` evaluates the resulting widened histories again. The response does not expose which internal snapshot first failed for each rejected attempt; attributing each rejection to a specific snapshot would require additional instrumentation.

**Inferred:** inherited uncertainty and conservative Cartesian interval propagation are the useful next numerical target. Smaller future intervals cannot in general remove uncertainty already present in the accepted history. This investigation does not establish that the actual paths become unstable, that the constraint fails physically, or that a looser tolerance would remain adequate. A tighter independently justified enclosure that advances well past this frontier would falsify the claim that this boundary is intrinsic to the selected motion; this investigation makes no such intrinsic-motion claim.

## Where the CPU goes

**Measured by `/usr/bin/sample`, five-second sample at 1 ms requested spacing:** 3714 of 4135 main-thread stack observations are under `certify_surface_publication`, approximately 89.8%. Two disjoint root-residual branches alone account for 2506 observations, approximately 60.6% of the main-thread total. The persistent exact-pair worker spends 3913 of its 4135 observations waiting on a condition variable. These are sampled-stack shares, not an exhaustive instrumented CPU-time decomposition or proof that a particular optimization achieves a given speed-up.

**Measured by source inspection:** the expensive Surface publication loop processes members sequentially. Each accepted step publishes two half-segments per member. For every segment and tube-inclusion iteration, the code visits 64 reception cells, checks the five other members, and performs two threshold bisections of up to 60 iterations from the retained-history start to the step boundary. Derived from these loop bounds, one pass across all twelve half-segments permits up to 460800 root-residual evaluations before additional tube-inclusion iterations. The endpoint corrector and point-root work are separate.

The earlier binary-search history lookup and request-revalidation bypass are present in the inspected `History.cpp` and `CoupledEvolution.cpp`; repeating those earlier recommendations would not identify new work. The newer validated ordinary bridge rejects a Surface geometry in `ValidatedOrdinaryBridge.cpp`, so selecting that route is not a supported switch for these constrained runs.

## Why short repeated requests do not give live animation

**Measured by source inspection:** `main.js::run` stops playback, awaits `evolveRecord` or `resumeRecord`, and only then calls `showRecord`. Progress callbacks update counters, not trajectory segments. `EomV2HttpClient.js` polls those counters while awaiting a terminal JSON response. `ProviderTransport.cpp` accumulates the run before producing its response; `EomV2ProcessClient.mjs` starts a fresh CLI worker per operation, retains/parses its complete result, and returns a bounded projection afterwards. There is no accepted-path streaming channel in this Surface execution path.

Every Surface checkpoint resume reconstructs the publication chronology and invokes `certify_surface_publication` on each prior boundary before advancing (`ProviderTransport.cpp::resume_provider_checkpoint`). The measured first new accepted step arrives at 3.280 seconds in the resume versus 0.229 seconds in the fresh watchable run. That difference includes revalidation and the first new step, so it is not an isolated revalidation benchmark. The source establishes why repeated small resume calls incur growing repeated proof work.

The first watchable run's raw response is 53011715 bytes; the extended smaller-step response is 110257363 bytes. Complete proof ledgers remain appropriate retained evidence, but a live viewer needs a small accepted-path feed rather than repeatedly parsing that whole response. Existing limits remain: initial UI duration at most 0.25, UI resume end at most 1, 80 cumulative attempts in the default request, 120-second service deadline plus cancellation grace, bounded response/record storage and finite retained history. These were not the first blocking conditions in the measured runs. A persistent execution session must retain explicit resource and cancellation limits rather than erase them to obtain apparent continuity.

## Recommended implementation order

1. **Numerical reach first.** Develop and independently check a tighter Surface error representation and constrained-acceleration enclosure, retaining correlations that Cartesian boxes lose. Evaluate centered or intrinsic-surface representations at their declared mathematical scope. Neither resetting accumulated error nor loosening the current tolerances is justified by these results. The first concrete acceptance objective should be accepted extension beyond the measured 0.33375 frontier with unchanged equation and stated accuracy.
2. **Reduce measured proof cost.** Reuse certified root brackets across nearby cells/steps, with independently checked bracket/sign coverage, instead of restarting both searches from the full history each time. Investigate parallel receiver publication against a frozen input history; history access, diagnostics and deterministic failure/reduction behavior need review before any speed-up claim. Neither proposal has a measured implementation benefit yet.
3. **Provide buffered accepted-path delivery.** Keep a watched EOM session and its valid in-memory carriers alive while it advances, emit only atomically accepted extensions to a small playback buffer, and write durable checkpoints without re-solving all previous steps on every in-session chunk. Preserve the present full-revalidation boundary for untrusted imported/restarted state. Define the target simulation-time-per-viewing-second mapping and prove measured production stays ahead of consumption over a declared horizon; rendering can interpolate accepted cubics between solver updates.
4. **If the intended live scenario is the displayed six-pole release, add its actual preparation capability.** Admit the stationary past with the required older-history/root coverage and independent numerical checks in the EOM solver. The current analytical replay cannot be passed off as that implementation.

These are engineering recommendations, not an implementation selection or a reactivation of the deferred scientific campaign. Increasing the service timeout, changing the animation duration or enabling more point-root threads alone does not address all three blockers.
