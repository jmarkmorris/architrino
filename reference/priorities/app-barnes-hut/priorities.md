## Objective

Design a hierarchical acceleration algorithm for large populations of architrinos, the equal and opposite point transceivers of $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$, that reduces the cost of evaluating the Master Equation's delayed causal-root sum below the exhaustive ordered-pair count while preserving the repository's certification discipline: no contribution is dropped, every approximation carries a certified remainder, and the result gains authority only through independent references. The design begins from Barnes-Hut and its rivals and departs from them wherever the architrino law differs from instantaneous gravity or electrostatics.

## Current

The operator opened the lane on 2026-10-10 and asked for a primer on Barnes-Hut, a survey of rival many-body methods, and a section of candidate innovations for equal and opposite point transceivers. The [manuscript](manuscript.md) holds all three. The primer and rival survey are factual background drawn from web sources recorded in the manuscript; they are comparison material, not architrino-level premises. The innovation section is design reasoning graded `guessed` or `inferred` throughout; nothing in it is implemented, measured, or independently reviewed.

Five differences between the architrino problem and the classical treecode problem organize the design and are developed in the manuscript's [departure analysis](manuscript.md#where-the-architrino-problem-departs-from-the-treecode-problem): signed polarity of exactly two equal magnitudes, so neutral clusters have zero monopole and the far field is dipole-led; delayed interaction through causal roots on retained histories, so a source cell must be aggregated on the receiver's past cone rather than at one instant; a transmitter-velocity weight on every hit, so cell moments are moments of a weighted emission measure; the absence of a mutual-interaction symmetry, because the reverse hit occurs at a different time; and a self-hit term that no cell may absorb. The candidate algorithm, a [certified space-time multipole route](manuscript.md#a-candidate-algorithm-certified-space-time-multipoles-on-the-past-cone), extends the EOM solver's existing enclosed-pair route from zero-centered enclosure to moment-expanded value plus certified remainder.
