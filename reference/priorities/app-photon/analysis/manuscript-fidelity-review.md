## Independent algebra and evidence assessment

The following checks are derived directly from the displayed prescribed geometry and algebra, without evaluating code, tests or scientific experiments. They confirm the explanatory steps at their declared scope, not the acceptance of the underlying model.

For delay u and positive longitudinal gap d, the translating displacement has x component d + c_gamma u. At equal positive signal and translation speeds, its norm is at least d + c_gamma u, strictly greater than the required c_sig u. Thus this particular positive-gap geometry has no positive-delay root, independently of a bounded scan label. With equal offsets and fixed nonzero transverse magnitude rho, squaring the distance equation gives (c_sig squared minus c_gamma squared) times u squared = rho squared. A positive explicit root requires positive speed margin; when rho depends on source phase the written expression is instead implicit. The manuscript states both restrictions. The approximate behind-receiver delay additionally assumes small transverse separation.

Differentiating the smooth residual F = R minus c_sig times (t minus tau) gives partial derivatives F_t = minus D_r and F_tau = D_t. On a simple branch, d tau/dt = D_r/D_t. That signed derivative is algebraically distinct from the nonnegative source factor c_sig/absolute D_t. The 0.08 display floor changes only the inverse-square magnitude denominator; replacing the actual distance in the direction normalization would change the vector again. Nothing in this reasoning supplies an unexamined Master Equation proof or licenses missing-record reconstruction.

The layer sum and DC/cosine/sine projections are linear when the samples, window and phase grid agree. Therefore coefficient sums close for every retained sample set, regardless of whether root coverage is complete. Magnitude formation is nonlinear: opposite equal coefficient pairs cancel even though their separate amplitudes are nonzero. Expanding A cos(phase + phi) gives C = A cos(phi), S = minus A sin(phi), so atan2(minus S, C) has the manuscript's correct sign. At zero amplitude phase has no direction. These identities alone do not prove harmonic-fit accuracy, adequate sampling or physical field completeness.

An average of ratios is generally different from a ratio of averages. As a paper check on the source's H4 example without a new numerical fixture, an ideal ellipse with transverse component amplitudes 1 and one-half and quarter-cycle phase offset has unregularized first-component projected-energy fraction four-fifths, but averaged instantaneous first-component fraction two-thirds; their difference is minus two-fifteenths. The orthogonal transverse direction reverses that offset. Thus the historical approximately 0.133 mismatch can exist with exact arithmetic. The manuscript correctly preserves the positive regularizer and requires matching estimator/window rather than interpreting this offset as root error. Its plane-wave cross-product component signs follow the declared x,y,z orientation and remain an effective comparison only.

The stationary facade example reduces to emission time one, delay/distance two, factors one and unit-coupling vector (one-quarter, zero, zero). Its transverse projection is zero; the manuscript explicitly avoids calling it a nonzero photon transverse-field test. The period-one source clock at emission one has phase zero/cycle one; the period-two receiver clock at reception three has phase 180 degrees/cycle one. These are independent analytic expectations, while the source's test agreement and bounded no-root fixture implementation remain unread historical test claims. Orthogonal translation and orbital velocity also justify the stated speed-budget identity without proving that any candidate root exists.
