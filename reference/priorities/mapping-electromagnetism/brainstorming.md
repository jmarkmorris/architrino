## Session 4 — What Maxwell's Equations Say The Magnetic Field Is (2026-08-19)

### Direct Answer

In the modern Maxwell system, $\mathbf B(\mathbf x,t)$ is the magnetic flux density: a spatial axial-vector field whose divergence, curl, time evolution, boundary behavior, and coupling to $\mathbf E$, charge, and current satisfy a declared system of partial differential equations. With constitutive relations and initial plus boundary data, those equations determine the allowed $\mathbf B$ configurations.

Maxwell's equations do **not** specify what physical substance or microscopic assembly $\mathbf B$ is. They define what it does mathematically and how it is related to other effective variables. The Lorentz response law, probe torque, and material constitutive laws add operational meaning, but they are not all contained in the four Maxwell equations themselves.

Plainly: Maxwell gives an extraordinarily precise job description for $\mathbf B$, not a microscopic biography.

### Angle 1 — The Four-Equation Role

In the microscopic vacuum-form SI comparison, the modern equations are

$$
\nabla\cdot\mathbf E
=\frac{\rho}{\epsilon_0},
\qquad
\nabla\cdot\mathbf B=0,
$$

$$
\nabla\times\mathbf E
=-\frac{\partial\mathbf B}{\partial t},
\qquad
\nabla\times\mathbf B
=\mu_0\mathbf J
+\mu_0\epsilon_0\frac{\partial\mathbf E}{\partial t}.
$$

The two equations containing $\mathbf B$ directly say:

- $\mathbf B$ has no local scalar source in the classical no-monopole regime;
- spatial circulation of $\mathbf B$ is tied to current and changing electric flux; and
- changing $\mathbf B$ is tied to circulating $\mathbf E$.

These relations constrain $\mathbf B$ but do not determine a unique solution without the source history, material response where applicable, and initial or boundary conditions.

Plainly: Maxwell says magnetic structure does not begin or end at isolated points, curls around current and changing electric structure, and participates in coupled electromagnetic change.

### Angle 2 — Flux Through Surfaces

The integral form of the magnetic Gauss equation is

$$
\oint_{\partial V}
\mathbf B\cdot d\mathbf A=0.
$$

Thus $\mathbf B$ is the density whose oriented flux is integrated through a surface. The total magnetic flux through every closed surface vanishes in the classical regime. Magnetic field-line drawings therefore have no isolated beginnings or endings; they close, continue through matter, extend to the boundary, or occupy nontrivial global topology. The drawn lines are visualization aids, not objects required by the equations.

The equation does not require $\mathbf B=0$ inside a source-free region. A uniform field, a wave, or a topologically sustained flux can have zero divergence while remaining nonzero.

Plainly: zero divergence means no unbalanced magnetic source inside a closed surface. It does not mean no magnetism in empty-looking space.
