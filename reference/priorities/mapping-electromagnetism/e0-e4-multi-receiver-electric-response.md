# $E0$--$E4$ Multi-Receiver Electric Response

## Status And Ownership

- Owner: `EMAP-004`, the Noether sea electromagnetic constitutive map.
- Status: predeclared diagnostic and live-evolution protocol; execution blocked on retained receiver and Noether sea branches.
- Claim grade: proposed discriminator. A prescribed-path result is `candidate_only`; it cannot establish an electric field, a retained assembly, or a physical Noether sea response.
- Numerical convention: every future numerical instantiation uses $c_f=1$.

This packet extends the original $E0$--$E4$ direct-wake versus sea-response experiment across four receiver classes while keeping group translation and internal deformation as separate outputs. It does not introduce an electric field into the Master Equation. The source, sea, receiver, and boundary records remain collections of architrino histories and causal-wake events; any $\mathbf E_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is an observer-level projection reconstructed only after the native rows are populated.

Plainly: the experiment asks how four different assemblies respond to one prepared electric-like environment. It does not tell their architrinos to obey an electric-force law.

## Closure Question

Can one source and Noether sea record simultaneously produce:

1. negligible leading group translation but bounded internal deformation of a neutral sea braid in the declared uniform-source window;
2. opposite group translation of complete electron and positron conjugate branches, with their internal responses recorded rather than assumed rigid;
3. negligible leading group translation but nonzero composite deformation of a neutral nucleon surrogate in that window; and
4. one direct-wake, sea-essential, or mixed classification that survives the receiver comparison without changing the source, projection, coefficients, or tolerance definitions?

The electron and positron in this packet are matched conjugate receiver cases. They are not an electron--positron pair-production event and are not placed together where their mutual interaction would contaminate the comparison. The primary window must be approximately uniform across each receiver according to a predeclared source-hit nonuniformity tolerance. A controlled gradient is a separate extension; neutral gradient translation must not be misclassified as failure of uniform-field cancellation.

## Common-History Rule

The experiment has two stages because an exactly common evolving sea and receiver backreaction cannot both be maintained across different receivers.

### Stage A: Common-Event Diagnostic

All four receivers are evaluated as passive diagnostic probes at the same predeclared reception events on one fixed source and sea history. Receiver paths do not update the source or sea in this stage. This makes the direct and sea acceleration partitions exactly comparable but cannot certify a physical retained evolution.

### Stage B: Matched Live Evolutions

At fork time $T_0$, make four evolution records with one receiver per fork. Each fork shares the same source, sea, boundary, and causal-memory prefix,

$$
\mathcal K_{\mathrm{common}}
=
\left(
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{src}}|_{[T_0-h,T_0]},
\mathcal H_{\mathrm{sea}}|_{[T_0-h,T_0]},
\mathcal H_{\partial\Omega}|_{[T_0-h,T_0]},
\mathcal C_{\mathrm{active}},
\eta,
\epsilon_c,
\mathcal P,
\mathcal T
\right),
$$

where $\mathcal P$ is the fixed projection definition and $\mathcal T$ is the fixed tolerance set. After $T_0$, each receiver, source, and sea evolves under the unchanged Master Equation. Later histories are allowed to differ because receiver backreaction is part of the physical question.

Plainly: Stage A compares the receivers against literally identical incoming histories. Stage B gives each receiver an identical starting history and then allows its real feedback to change what happens next.

The common-history key fails if any fork changes the source preparation, retained memory window, root-selection convention, regulator, boundary record, projection, coefficients, or tolerances. A receiver-specific Noether sea state at $T_0$ is hidden retuning rather than a comparison.

## Separate Group And Internal Coordinates

Architrinos have no primitive mass, so the first group coordinate is the identity-preserving arithmetic centroid of receiver $R$,

$$
\mathbf X_{R,k}^{\mathrm{grp}}(T)
=
\frac{1}{N_R}
\sum_{a\in R}
\mathbf X_{a,k}(T),
\qquad
\mathbf A_{R,k}^{\mathrm{grp}}(T)
=
\frac{1}{N_R}
\sum_{a\in R}
\mathbf A_{a,k}(T),
$$

for run $k\in\{E0,E1,E2,E3,E4\}$. If a later retained assembly supplies a different derived physical center, both centers must be reported rather than silently replacing the arithmetic diagnostic.

The source-induced group displacement removes baseline inertial motion,

$$
\Delta\mathbf X_{R,k}^{\mathrm{grp}}(T)
=
\mathbf X_{R,k}^{\mathrm{grp}}(T)
-
\mathbf X_{R,E0}^{\mathrm{grp}}(T),
$$

on phase-matched records.

Plainly: group translation records where the assembly as a whole goes. It does not say whether the architrinos inside it moved closer together, farther apart, or changed phase.

Define identity-matched internal coordinates

$$
\mathbf u_{a,R,k}(T)
=
\mathbf X_{a,k}(T)
-
\mathbf X_{R,k}^{\mathrm{grp}}(T).
$$

At each declared return section, let $Q_{R,k}^{*}(T)\in SO(3)$ be the best identity-preserving rigid alignment of the $E0$ internal coordinates to run $k$. Record $Q_{R,k}^{*}$ as the assembly-orientation response and define the dimensionless non-rigid deformation

$$
\epsilon_{R,k}^{\mathrm{int}}(T)
=
\left[
\frac{
\sum_{a\in R}
\left\|
\mathbf u_{a,R,k}(T)
-
Q_{R,k}^{*}(T)\mathbf u_{a,R,E0}(T)
\right\|^2
}{
\sum_{a\in R}
\left\|\mathbf u_{a,R,E0}(T)\right\|^2
+\varepsilon
}
\right]^{1/2}.
$$

Plainly: the best rigid alignment prevents ordinary translation and rotation from being mislabeled as stretching. Orientation is still saved as its own response instead of being discarded.

The internal electric-polarity moment is recorded independently,

$$
\mathbf p_{R,k}^{\mathrm{int}}(T)
=
\sum_{a\in R}
q_a\mathbf u_{a,R,k}(T),
$$

together with positive- and negative-sector centroids whenever both sectors are present. This moment is a native signed geometry diagnostic, not yet an observer-calibrated electric dipole moment.

Plainly: two assemblies can have the same small deformation norm while moving opposite polarity sectors in different directions. The signed internal moment keeps that distinction visible.

## Transmitter-Tagged Source And Sea Effects

For both group and internal rows, retain the source-tagged and sea-tagged acceleration contributions before summation. The responsive-sea increments are

$$
\Delta\mathbf A_{R,\mathrm{grp}}^{\mathrm{sea}}
=
\mathbf A_{R,E3}^{\mathrm{grp}}
-
\mathbf A_{R,E2}^{\mathrm{grp}},
$$

$$
\Delta\epsilon_{R,\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{sea}}
=
\epsilon_{R,E3}^{\mathrm{int}}
-
\epsilon_{R,E2}^{\mathrm{int}}.
$$

The first asks whether sea response changes group translation. The second asks whether it changes internal deformation. Neither difference alone proves that the sea is physically essential; the live $E3$ branch must also pass the retention, requirement, and no-hidden-retune checks.

Plainly: the sea may contribute very little to whole-assembly acceleration but substantially change the assembly's internal strain, or the reverse. One scalar "sea effect" would hide that result.

## Conjugation And Neutrality Controls

The source polarity reversal $S\mapsto\mathcal C S$, receiver conjugation $R_-\mapsto R_+$, and combined conjugation must be stored as separate controls. Complete simultaneous conjugation leaves all Master Equation pair products unchanged, so the combined-control histories should agree after identity mapping within numerical tolerance. At fixed source polarity, the leading electric-odd electron and positron group responses are expected to reverse in the weak electric regime. First isolate the electric-odd displacement with the source-conjugation pair,

$$
\Delta\mathbf X_{R,k}^{\mathrm{grp,odd}}
=
\frac12
\left(
\Delta\mathbf X_{R,k}^{\mathrm{grp}}[S]
-
\Delta\mathbf X_{R,k}^{\mathrm{grp}}[\mathcal C S]
\right).
$$

Plainly: subtracting the source-conjugate run removes polarity-even drift before asking whether the electron and positron electric responses reverse.

Then define the group conjugation residual

$$
\mathcal R_{\mathrm{conj}}^{\mathrm{grp}}
=
\frac{
\left\|
\Delta\mathbf X_{R_+,k}^{\mathrm{grp,odd}}
+
\Delta\mathbf X_{R_-,k}^{\mathrm{grp,odd}}
\right\|
}{
\left\|\Delta\mathbf X_{R_+,k}^{\mathrm{grp,odd}}\right\|
+
\left\|\Delta\mathbf X_{R_-,k}^{\mathrm{grp,odd}}\right\|
+\varepsilon
}.
$$

Plainly: this residual is small only when the electric-odd whole-assembly displacements of the conjugate receivers are equal and opposite.

Internal configurations are compared after the declared polarity-conjugate identity map; equality of deformation norms alone is not sufficient if the underlying signed displacement pattern is wrong.

For $R_N$ and $R_0$, total neutrality is checked directly while group and internal response are kept separate. A valid neutral result may have

$$
\Delta\mathbf X_{R,k}^{\mathrm{grp}}\approx0,
\qquad
\epsilon_{R,k}^{\mathrm{int}}>0,
$$

within the declared weak-response window. Exact neutrality does not by itself prove the first relation; it remains a measured cancellation requirement.

Plainly: the charged conjugates should move oppositely as wholes, while neutral receivers may remain centered and still deform internally. All three statements must be measured from the constituent histories.

## $E4$ Removal And Relaxation Rule

Source removal does not require a previously accelerated charged receiver to return to its original position or velocity. After the last direct source wake clears the receiver, the group test is return of acceleration to the appropriate baseline plus explicit accounting for any residual sea or boundary contribution. The internal test is whether deformation, orientation, phase, cadence, and branch residuals return to the $E0$ family or settle onto a declared new branch.

Define a late-window group-acceleration residual and internal-return residual,

$$
\mathcal R_{R,E4}^{\mathrm{grp}}
=
\frac{
\left\|
\mathbf A_{R,E4}^{\mathrm{grp}}
-
\mathbf A_{R,E0}^{\mathrm{grp}}
\right\|
}{
\left\|\mathbf A_{R,E0}^{\mathrm{grp}}\right\|
+a_{\mathrm{scale}}
},
$$

$$
\mathcal R_{R,E4}^{\mathrm{int}}
=
\epsilon_{R,E4}^{\mathrm{int}}
$$

on matched late return sections. A nonzero internal residual must be classified as slow relaxation, persistent polarization, excitation, radiation-coupled change, or loss of the original branch rather than averaged away.

Plainly: removing the source should stop the continuing electric-like acceleration after all delayed wakes clear. It need not erase the velocity the assembly already gained. Internal stretching has a separate return test.

## Acceptance And Rejection

The extended experiment is protocol-complete only when:

1. within each Stage-A run, every receiver carries the same declared source and sea record; $E1$--$E3$ preserve their specified shared source identity, and every Stage-B receiver fork carries the same common prefix;
2. group translation, rigid orientation, non-rigid deformation, and signed internal polarity response are all reported separately;
3. $R_+$ is a complete conjugate of $R_-$ rather than a separately tuned model;
4. $R_N$ and $R_0$ pass explicit neutrality and $E0$ baseline checks;
5. $E1$, $E2$, and $E3$ retain source- and sea-transmitter attribution without changing coefficients or field definitions;
6. $E3$ reports whether each receiver remains on a retained branch;
7. $E4$ separates cessation of group acceleration from internal return and records any persistent sea or branch change; and
8. all nonzero energy, momentum, angular momentum, wake, recoil, medium, radiation, remnant, and boundary rows remain visible.

Reject or retain as unresolved any result that uses different source or sea states for different receivers, places mutually interacting receivers into one comparison cell without accounting for cross-talk, calls a frozen-sea control a physical evolution, mass-weights primitive architrinos, treats rigid rotation as deformation, infers zero internal response from neutrality, or interprets a prescribed surrogate as a neutron or retained electron.
