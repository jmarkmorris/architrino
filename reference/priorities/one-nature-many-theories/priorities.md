## Objective

Develop an evidence-backed account of physics as one natural world represented by many domain theories, effective models, and bridges of unequal authority, then determine which parts belong in a reader-facing philosophy-history chapter.

## Current

The [working draft](one-nature-many-theories.md) establishes the central thesis, domain inventory, bridge taxonomy, common-history projection criterion, gauge and scale examples, fairness boundaries, and a relocation plan. The first-pass inventory is illustrative rather than source-complete. No reader-facing corpus promotion, global priority rank, or new $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ recovery claim follows from the lane's creation.

## Scope

This lane owns the cross-domain assessment: the distinction between framework unification and full theory closure, the theory-domain bridge matrix, the logarithmic chutes-and-ladders metaphor, historical source synthesis, and the editorial boundary between the Weyl near-miss episode and the wider issue.

It does not own the mechanisms or acceptance gates of the domain theories it audits. Gauge recovery remains with Standard Model Closure and the assembly gauge chapters; quantum, thermodynamic, nuclear, cosmological, and gravitational derivations remain with their scientific owners. This lane records the authority of those bridges and routes missing work to the correct owner rather than duplicating their queues.

## Claim Boundary

- **Effective summary:** physics uses many domain theories and bridge types, some rigorously derived and some partly phenomenological.
- **Inferred historical assessment:** the familiar general-relativity-plus-quantum-theory slogan understates the full emergence and bridge burden of unification.
- **Derivation / closure target:** one common physical history should project into every mandatory domain record and reproduce declared inter-domain bridges within tolerance.
- **Speculation:** constituent continuity will yield predictive or explanatory compression beyond independently matched effective descriptions.
