## Contract Rule

An architecture-ready chapter remains at that level only when its existing `Resolution tests` field supplies all four of these elements:

1. a declared recovery target or native result;
2. an identifiable source or evidence family;
3. an observer-facing record, simulation variable, or mathematical object to compare;
4. a failure condition that prevents post hoc reinterpretation.

The contract must be executed by the primary owner named in the claim-level audit. A source family is an evidence route, not a permission to import its standard-physics ontology into the substrate derivation.

## Audit Result

All 16 architecture-ready chapters have a defensible, explicitly bounded test contract. No architecture-ready chapter required demotion during this pass. The contracts are test definitions only; none is marked executed or passed by this audit.

The 24 `direction-ready`, 4 `appendix-watch`, and 2 `exclude-for-now` chapters remain outside this contract set. Their classification does not rise merely because a generic observable can be named; promotion first requires the sharper native mechanism specified in the claim-level audit.

Plainly: the architecture-ready set survived because every chapter says what it must recover, where the comparison data come from, what quantity is compared, and what would count as failure.
