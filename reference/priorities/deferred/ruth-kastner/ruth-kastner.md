### $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ Discussion From The 2022 Reference

The strongest usable insight is not the transaction vocabulary itself. It is the insistence that the measurement boundary has to be physically earned. In $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ terms, a detector branch should not become a measurement outcome merely because it appears in a compressed state description. It should count only when a declared apparatus channel has closed the relevant local couplings, conservation ledger, environmental persistence, and repeatable-frequency conditions.

A useful native target is to split the record predicate into named factors rather than hiding everything inside one indicator:

$$
\mathsf R_\theta(\gamma_i,T)
=
\mathsf C_\theta(\gamma_i,T)
\mathsf L_\theta(\gamma_i,T)
\mathsf P_\theta(\gamma_i,T)
\mathsf N_\theta(\gamma_i,T),
$$

where $\mathsf C_\theta$ is the detector-coupling condition, $\mathsf L_\theta$ is the energy/momentum ledger condition, $\mathsf P_\theta$ is the persistence or record-durability condition, and $\mathsf N_\theta$ is the no-signaling/context-consistency condition for the declared channel. Then the record-normalized outcome weights remain:

$$
p_i^{\mathrm{rec}}(T;\theta)
=
\frac{
\mu_{*,T}\!\left(B_i^{(d)}\cap \mathsf R_\theta^{-1}(1)\right)
}{
\sum_j \mu_{*,T}\!\left(B_j^{(d)}\cap \mathsf R_\theta^{-1}(1)\right)
}.
$$

That formula is not a PTI import. It is a way to convert Ruth's "hard part" into a native $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ work item: define record eligibility precisely enough that Born-like weights, conservation laws, no-signaling, and repeatable frequencies are recovered by the dynamics rather than by interpretive naming.

The second useful insight is the virtual/real interaction distinction. RTI treats virtual exchange and radiative energy transfer as different physical regimes. The native comparison is that $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ should keep reversible perturbation, correlation, photon-like packet formation, detector absorption, and durable record formation as separate states in the apparatus ledger. This is especially relevant to photon Gate B and any future quantum-closure simulation that tries to infer detector clicks from field propagation.

The third useful insight is spacetime emergence as an event graph. RTI's quantum substratum should remain external, but its insistence that spacetime events are established by actualized physical processes is a useful comparison pressure for $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ emergent metric prose. The safe translation is: do not say spacetime structure is "selected" unless the record/event ledger says which architrino assemblies, causal wakes, and conservation balances make that event available to the observer-level description.

## What She Is Known For

Kastner argues that quantum events involve a transaction between:

1. an offer wave emitted by a source;
2. a confirmation wave returned by an absorber;
3. a completed source-absorber handshake that yields the observed quantum event.

The transactional picture is time-symmetric. It uses both backward-in-time and causal forward-in-time solutions of the wave equations to describe the exchange. In $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ terms, this should be treated as an external comparison framework for event selection and record formation, not as native causal-wake ontology.

## Main Contribution

Kastner's main contribution is extending the original Transactional Interpretation into PTI.

In PTI:

- quantum possibilities are treated as physically real;
- transactions occur in a realm of possibilities before spacetime events;
- spacetime may emerge from underlying quantum processes.

For this repo, those claims should stay comparison-scoped unless a later source-mining pass identifies a concrete theorem target, equation, or measurement-record criterion that improves an existing $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ proof route.

## Why Track This

PTI is relevant because it attempts to address:

- the measurement problem;
- wavefunction collapse;
- quantum nonlocality;
- the Born probability rule.

It attempts this without adding hidden variables or modifying the standard quantum equations.
