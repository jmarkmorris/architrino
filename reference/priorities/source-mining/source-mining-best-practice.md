# Source Mining Best Practice

## Purpose

This is the current best-practice guide for source mining. It is priority-only process material, not reader-facing corpus prose.

The goal is high-quality extraction of insights, linkages, mappings, and theory advancements from source material. The process should sharpen the active $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ theory stack without importing another framework as doctrine, inventing new terminology, or adding more gates, certificates, residual inventories, live-ledger checklists, or similar hurdle infrastructure to the corpus.

## Core Operating Rules

- Mine for concrete value: definitions, equations, mechanisms, examples, analogy limits, benchmark values, source leads, closure targets, proof routes, simulation targets, and corpus-destination mappings.
- Separate observable or data product from interpretation. Preserve validated constraints and challenge interpretations only when the corpus already has a clear basis.
- Use established project terminology. Translate external vocabulary into canonical terms instead of adding new names.
- Keep speculative claims marked as speculation. Do not turn source-language metaphysics, worldview, or model-specific terminology into $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ ontology.
- Prefer theory advancement over audit infrastructure. If a source suggests another gate, certificate, residual inventory, solver-test doc, or live-ledger checklist, keep it priority-only unless the operator/developer explicitly selects it as a current need.
- Do not paste full transcripts, papers, articles, or copyrighted source text into reports. Use paraphrase, short compliant excerpts only when necessary, and exact timestamps, pages, slide numbers, or URLs for traceability.

## Relationship To Other AAA Processes

This guide is a source-intake and source-processing practice. It is not another gate system, not a certificate system, and not a competing corpus-advancement protocol.

- The [corpus advancement pass](../../entourage/cody/prompts/corpus-advancement-pass.md) remains the authority for deciding whether mined material should be promoted into `content/markdown/aaa`, deferred with a blocker, staged as a discussion prompt, or rejected.
- The Archie canon remains the authority for terminology, notation, and reader-facing style. Source mining translates external vocabulary into established project terminology; it does not update canon by itself.
- Priority workstreams remain the staging area for unresolved source material, backlog rows, report packets, and follow-up prompts. A source-mining report is working memory and traceability, not reader-facing doctrine.
- Existing validation, gate, certificate, residual, and ledger processes remain separate. Source mining may identify benchmark pressure or an existing recovery target, but it should not create new hurdle artifacts by default.
- Authored corpus pages should receive only the safe current substance: definitions, equations, proof routes, worked examples, claim-level corrections, or source-backed bridge prose. They should not link back to priority-only source-mining process material.

## Corpus Deferral Rules

The source-mining process should not add more gate or audit infrastructure to the reader-facing corpus at this point. These artifacts can remain useful as priority-only working material, but they should not be promoted into `content/markdown/aaa` unless the operator/developer explicitly selects one as a current need.

Do not add these artifact types to the corpus for now:

- Gate: a pass/fail decision rule for whether a claim may advance.
- Certificate: the full evidence packet proving a gate passed or explaining why it failed.
- Solver-test doc: how a numerical or finite-mode search must be run and interpreted.
- Residual inventory: the list of equations or errors that must be small or explicitly open.
- Live-ledger checklist: the "same data, same branch, same root convention" audit surface.
- Similar gate, certificate, residual, solver-test, ledger, checklist, fixture, or requirement artifacts that would add more hurdle infrastructure instead of extracting insights, linkages, mappings, or theory advancements.

## Creative Reframing And Theory Mapping

Source mining needs disciplined creativity. A source claim often does not map directly into $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ at the same theoretical level where the source states it. The useful work is not only extracting what the source says, but also holding the source claim in mind long enough to ask what it becomes after the ontology, level of description, and mathematical variables are changed.

When a source gives a familiar concept, formula, or explanatory frame, test several mapping postures before recommending a corpus update:

- Direct map: the source quantity or equation appears to describe the same observable or recovery target under different terminology.
- Reframed map: the source observable is preserved, but the explanation changes. For example, a redshift observation may remain a redshift data product while the corpus asks whether the underlying description belongs to Noether sea evolution, transport, clock-rate comparison, source evolution, or another effective mechanism rather than literal expansion of the Euclidean void.
- Inverse or backward map: the source formula is treated as the endpoint to recover, then mined backward for the variables and assumptions an $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ derivation would have to reproduce or replace.
- Middle-out map: the source and $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ descriptions meet at an intermediate invariant, conservation law, response coefficient, scaling relation, benchmark value, or data product rather than at ontology.
- Higher-level duality or analogy: the source mathematics may be aggregate, effective, thermodynamic, information-theoretic, or statistical rather than assembly-level dynamics. Keep the analogy only after naming the level difference.
- No clean map: the source may still provide a source lead, contrast case, or rejected interpretation without producing a corpus edit.

For formulas, preserve the source equation as an exemplar only after identifying its theoretical level. Ask whether it is a microscopic equation, an assembly-level model, an effective field equation, a statistical population law, a data-reduction formula, or a fitted phenomenological relation. Then ask whether $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ should recover the formula directly, recover a limit of it, reinterpret its variables, replace its assumptions, or use it only as a comparison benchmark.

Cosmology is the standard stress test for this discipline. A source may discuss redshift, the scale factor $a(t)$, the Hubble parameter $H(t)$, the CMB, BBN, structure formation, or the Big Bang as one connected Lambda-CDM or GR narrative. Source mining should split that package into layers: the observed data products, the inference pipeline, the fitted parameters, the effective equations, the interpretation, and the ontological claim. Only after that split should the miner ask which pieces map into $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ as recovery targets, which become effective summaries, which are comparison frameworks, and which are rejected or reframed.

The same discipline applies to GR, QM/QFT, thermodynamics, and particle phenomenology. A formula from those frameworks may be the thing to recover, the aggregate limit of a deeper mechanism, the wrong ontology with the right benchmark, or a useful bridge that should never be mistaken for assembly-level dynamics. The report should say which case it is, what evidence supports that classification, and what first calculation, proof route, or simulation target would decide whether the mapping is real.

## Standard Output Shape

Every source-mining pass should produce a compact report with these parts when applicable:

1. Source summary: title, author or channel, date, duration or page count, URL or file path, and local artifact paths.
2. Source map: the exact transcript, slides, Notes pages, PDF sections, web hierarchy, figures, tables, or references inspected.
3. High-signal map: timestamped, page-based, slide-based, or URL-based bullets for the strongest source signals.
4. Classification: observable constraint, standard-theory result, interpretation, comparison framework, speculative claim, not useful.
5. Corpus value assessment: what helps $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$, what should not be imported, where the corpus already covers it, and where the corpus could be strengthened.
6. Claim map: ontology, derivation or closure target, effective summary, speculation.
7. Theory-advancement candidates: candidate definitions, closure equations, invariants, proof scaffolds, validation criteria, simulation targets, or disciplined discussion prompts.
8. Affected-document table: path, issue or opportunity, source signal, recommended update, priority, risk, claim bucket, and approval requirement.
9. Priority Action Menu: paste-ready follow-up prompts for work outside the current authority.

## Source-Family Intake

Develop practical source-mining processes for each major source type. These processes are extraction aids, not new gates, hurdles, or scorecard surfaces. Their purpose is to recover high-quality insights, linkages, mappings, and theory advancements from each source family and route useful material into the active $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ theory stack.

Required source families:

- WordPress, legacy social material, and operational notes.
- PowerPoint and slide decks.
- arXiv-style papers, books, reports, and other research PDFs.
- YouTube videos, seminars, interviews, and course playlists.
- Wikipedia and encyclopedia-style sources.
- Official data, review, and reference portals such as PDG, CODATA, NIST, GWOSC, NASA LAMBDA, CERN, LVK, and EHT.

Each source-type process should produce:

- a source map with the exact files, URLs, hierarchy, transcripts, slides, Notes pages, figures, equations, or references that were inspected;
- an insight inventory that separates definitions, equations, mechanisms, examples, analogies, benchmark values, and source leads;
- linkage rows that connect mined material to existing priority workstreams and corpus destinations;
- mapping rows that translate external terminology into established project terminology without inventing new names;
- theory-advancement candidates stated with claim level, assumptions, proof burden, and intended destination;
- and a short discard note for material that is interesting but does not improve definitions, equations, closure targets, proof routes, worked examples, mappings, or source leads.

Use source backlogs as triage surfaces, not requirement factories. A source earns mining time only when it can yield at least one concrete artifact: a numerical benchmark, derivation route, worked example, source lead, canonical table or figure, visual explanation, or reader-facing bridge for the active theory stack.

### Legacy Social, WordPress, And Operational Material

Mine legacy material for original intuition and source leads while keeping it subordinate to theory closure.

1. Record platform, date range, author identity when known, thread or post hierarchy, and outbound links.
2. Extract original intuitions, naming decisions, reader-facing bridges, analogy seeds, and references to older source material.
3. Preserve productization, big-picture outlook writing, and future-technology packaging as source material, but do not let them outrank the derivation spine.
4. Mark abandoned language and historical process as non-corpus unless it can be rewritten as current doctrine with a clear claim level.
5. Route product or outlook material through priority-only planning unless it directly improves a current definition, equation, proof route, or reader-facing bridge.

- broad future-technology narrative material;
