At a fixed receiver event $(T_r,\mathbf X_r)$, a source hit contributes through the canonical sharp kernel

$$
K(\mathbf x,\mathbf v)=\frac{\mathbf n}{r^2D},\qquad
r=\|\mathbf X_r-\mathbf x\|,\qquad
\mathbf n=\frac{\mathbf X_r-\mathbf x}{r},\qquad
D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v.
$$

Here $\mathbf x$ and $\mathbf v$ are evaluated at the source's causal emission time, and all numerical instances use $c_f=1$. Let the source charges be $+\epsilon$ and $-\epsilon$, with $\epsilon>0$, receiver charge $q_r$, coupling $\kappa$, and $C=|\kappa q_r|\epsilon$. On a common emission slab, certify a receiver-distance floor $r^->0$ on a convex source-position domain, each source's speed upper bound $v^+<1$, equal-time source separation $\ell^+$, relative speed $w^+$, and each source's acceleration bound $a^+$. Define $\nu=1-v^+>0$.

When both members have a certified interior root in that slab, the complete two-hit acceleration has each component in $[-B,B]$, with

$$
B=C\left[
\frac{(2\nu+v^+)\ell^+}{(r^-)^3\nu^3}
+\frac{w^++a^+\ell^+/\nu}{(r^-)^2\nu^2}
\right].
$$

**Derived, under the linked theorem's hypotheses:** the first term accounts for delayed position separation; the second accounts for unequal source velocities and their change between the two emission times. Equal charge magnitudes cancel the common unweighted kernel, but do not remove the velocity term. Consequently the complete pair need not have cubic-distance falloff. A falsifier is an independently evaluated pair satisfying every bound and membership condition whose exact acceleration lies outside the emitted interval.

Membership is a separate admission obligation. For each source,

$$
g_\pm(S)=\|\mathbf X_r-\mathbf X_\pm(S)\|-(T_r-S),
\qquad g_\pm'(S)\ge\nu.
$$

Strict endpoint signs $g_\pm(S_0)<0<g_\pm(S_1)$ certify one interior root. Strictly positive lower-endpoint residual or strictly negative upper-endpoint residual certifies absence. Both absent means zero contribution; mixed membership, endpoint equality and unresolved signs fall back. Monotonicity alone proves at most one root and cannot certify presence.

The theorem supplies outward whole-slab bounds from cubic Bernstein control vectors, including derivative controls for speed and acceleration. Production admission must use those bounds or another proved construction. Time samples, binary64 coefficient estimates and nominal trajectory distances cannot supply certificates.

## First increment: canonical snapshots

### Admitted domain

All of the following are required; failure of one condition declines the grouped optimization and preserves an existing certified route.

1. The selected equation is the canonical sharp interaction with identity receiver response and no Surface constraint. The grouped proof does not establish alternative-provider or finite-width-chart behavior. Field speed is exactly admitted as one.
2. The receiver event is one exact decimal reception token, evaluated with an outward arithmetic enclosure. The inspected `Interval::decimal_token` pads even representable values, so demanding a represented point interval would decline every candidate; this corrects the original scope wording. An actual reception-time uncertainty interval requires a separately qualified extension and falls back in this increment. The receiver-position box extension is derived and independently accepted in the [Stage 0 review](../analysis/matched-pair-review-stage-0-2026-10-11.md). A nominal midpoint is insufficient.
3. Sources are distinct from each other and from the receiver, and their charge tokens prove exact opposite equal magnitudes. Self-history remains on its existing route. Each source belongs to at most one group for this receiver event.
4. One common cubic slab covers both sources' entire declared root-search interval. An independently certified prefix exclusion may shorten that interval, but the grouped certificate must reference the excluded prefix. A selected slab containing two roots does not cover unexamined earlier or later source history. Different segment domains may be restricted to a common slab using a certified polynomial transformation; coverage still has to be complete.
5. Both source histories are exactly the supplied cubics, with zero position and velocity reconstruction-error tokens. Outward conversion of exact coefficient tokens is permitted; physical history uncertainty is not coefficient-rounding uncertainty. An analytic factory or computed-solution carrier is not silently relabeled an exact cubic.
6. Whole-slab bounds certify $r^->0$, $v^+<1$, finite $\ell^+,w^+,a^+$, membership of both roots or absence of both, and sufficient width budget. Endpoint equality, arithmetic uncertainty and nonfinite bounds decline the optimization.

The sub-wake-speed condition applies only to this optimization. It imposes no speed cap or clamp on the EOM solver's unrestricted domain; unsupported pairs continue through the existing exact or event-aware routes.

**Derived mathematical bridge, independently reviewed at Stage 0:** for a receiver-position box, use one convex source-position domain and a common distance floor valid for every receiver point in that box. Certify the same strict membership signs uniformly over the box. For each fixed point the existing proof then applies with common bounds, so taking the union of its enclosed accelerations preserves $[-B,B]^3$. Stage 1 must implement that outward construction and its controls; it must never certify an interval receiver by testing its midpoint. When the complete slab ends exactly at the reception token, the distance floor supplies the strict upper residual sign. Other slab endpoints still require their own membership proof.

### Certificate and numerical ownership

Use a separate typed grouped certificate rather than overloading a one-transmitter certificate. The implementation proposal is `NativeMatchedPairEnclosureCertificate`; its final schema is chosen when implementation is selected. It must bind:

- Receiver identity and history fingerprint; both ordered source identities, exact charge evidence and history fingerprints; selected canonical plan and execution-policy identity.
- Reception event, covered emission window, segment indices, any certified excluded prefix, coefficient representation and arithmetic precision.
- Convex geometry, $r^-,v^+,\nu,\ell^+,w^+,a^+$ and their outward construction, each member's endpoint residual evidence and membership disposition.
- Outward radius, emitted component intervals, actual emitted component widths, the two source slices, total debit and failure or fallback reason.
- One numerical reduction owner and two logical membership references, with deterministic leader/follower association and invalidation conditions.

Each logical source marker points to that group; it must not assert that roots were enumerated. The receiver acceleration uses the grouped interval exactly once. A stable leader slot plus an additive identity in the follower slot can preserve the existing reduction tree, provided the follower is explicitly a reduction identity referring to the group, **not** a claim that the second source's physical acceleration is zero. A genuinely group-aware reduction is also acceptable if its order and disabled-route compatibility are proved. The chosen representation must remain reconstructible by diagnostic consumers.

Maintain the original source count $N$ in the slice denominator. For fraction $f_{\mathrm{FF}}$ and receiver component-width tolerance $\tau_A$, each source owns $b_t=f_{\mathrm{FF}}\tau_A/N$. The group's actual outward width must satisfy

$$
\operatorname{width}(\mathcal A_{+-})\le b_++b_-.
$$

Count both enclosed logical relationships, one numerical group and its width once. Sum receiver widths upward and compare with the receiver allocation's lower endpoint. A local two-slice pass does not replace receiver-total certification; exact rows, other enclosures, rounding and reduction still consume their existing budgets. Both-absent groups consume zero width. No slice is reused, and $N$ is not replaced with the number of groups.

An enclosure certificate is valid only for its bound event, histories, membership, precision and policy. Revalidate rather than reuse after a receiver-event change, history append, source reassignment or policy change. Warm-root or topology records for the two members must not masquerade as complete isolated-root evidence while a group owns them.
