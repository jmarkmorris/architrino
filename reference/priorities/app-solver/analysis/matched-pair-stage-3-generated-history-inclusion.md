# Generated-History Inclusion: an Exact Obstruction and the Smallest Proof Route

Stage 3 of the [matched-pair integration scope](../contracts/matched-pair-enclosure-integration-scope.md) would use the grouped enclosure during evolution. Evolution's source histories are generated by the solver, and the grouped theorem needs each source's true path to be continuously differentiable with its position and velocity inside the published tubes. This record answers one question: do the tubes the solver publishes enclose the true solution of the canonical equation and its derivative? It names the carrier, shows by an exact argument that the carrier's radii are not such an enclosure, and states the smallest route that would produce one. It proposes no production change and Stage 3 is not released. All numerical instances use $c_f=1$.

## 1. The carrier, as built

**Claim grade: measured**, by reading `append_candidate_segments`, `endpoint_local_errors` and `inflate_fine_histories` in `src/eom/src/CoupledEvolution.cpp` at the live inventory of 2026-10-11T22:24:04Z. Falsifier: different arithmetic in those symbols at that inventory.

One accepted step from $T$ to $T+h$ builds each path's segment as follows.

1. The acceleration is certified at the two step endpoints only, $\mathbf a_0$ at $T$ and $\mathbf a_1$ at $T+h$, the latter by the corrector iteration on the candidate history.
2. The endpoint state is $\mathbf v_1=\mathbf v_0+\tfrac h2(\mathbf a_0+\mathbf a_1)$ and $\mathbf x_1=\mathbf x_0+h\mathbf v_0+\tfrac{h^2}{6}(2\mathbf a_0+\mathbf a_1)$. These are the exact integrals of an acceleration that varies linearly in time between its endpoint values.
3. The nominal segment is the cubic Hermite curve through the midpoints of $(\mathbf x_0,\mathbf v_0)$ and $(\mathbf x_1,\mathbf v_1)$.
4. Its base radii are sums of the interval radii of those endpoint states: for position, the radii of $\mathbf x_0$ and $\mathbf x_1$ plus $h$ times those of $\mathbf v_0$ and $\mathbf v_1$; for velocity, $1.5/h$ times the position radii plus twice the velocity radii. They bound how far a Hermite curve moves when its endpoint data move within their intervals.
5. The step is taken once as a full step and once as two half steps. The published segment is the half-step one, with each radius increased by the absolute difference between the full-step and half-step endpoint midpoints, per axis. The step is accepted when that difference is within the declared position and velocity tolerances.

So a published radius has two ingredients: the width of the interval arithmetic that produced the endpoint states, and the step-doubling discrepancy at the step endpoint. Inherited uncertainty enters through the first: the endpoint state intervals carry the previous segment's radii forward.

## 2. Exact obstruction

Neither ingredient bounds the error of replacing the true acceleration between the endpoints by a straight line in time. The interval widths enclose rounding and input uncertainty of the formula in step 2; they say nothing about whether that formula is the right integral. The discrepancy compares two applications of the same rule, and it can vanish while the rule is wrong.

**Proposition.** Consider the scalar problem $\ddot x=a(t)$ with a prescribed continuous $a$, integrated by the rule of step 2. The full-step and two-half-step endpoint states coincide exactly whenever $a(T+h/2)=\tfrac12\bigl(a(T)+a(T+h)\bigr)$, whatever $a$ does elsewhere in the step.

*Proof.* The full step gives $v_1=v_0+\tfrac h2(a_0+a_1)$. Two half steps give $v_0+\tfrac h4(a_0+a_m)+\tfrac h4(a_m+a_1)=v_0+\tfrac h4(a_0+2a_m+a_1)$. These are equal exactly when $a_m=\tfrac12(a_0+a_1)$. For position, the full step gives $x_0+hv_0+\tfrac{h^2}{6}(2a_0+a_1)$; the two half steps give $x_0+hv_0+\tfrac{h^2}{24}(5a_0+6a_m+a_1)$, by substituting the half-step velocity into the second half step. With $a_m=\tfrac12(a_0+a_1)$ that is $x_0+hv_0+\tfrac{h^2}{24}(8a_0+4a_1)$, the same value. ∎

**Consequence.** Take $a(t)=A\sin^2\bigl(2\pi(t-T)/h\bigr)$ with $A\neq0$. It vanishes at $T$, $T+h/2$ and $T+h$, so the rule computes no change of velocity in either the full step or the half steps, and the discrepancy is exactly zero. The true velocity change over the step is $\int_T^{T+h}a=Ah/2$, and the true position change beyond $hv_0$ is $Ah^2/4$. With exact arithmetic the published radii would be zero and the true state would lie outside the tube by $Ah/2$ in velocity and $Ah^2/4$ in position. A worked instance with $h=1$ and $A=1$: published change zero, true velocity change $0.5$, true displacement $0.25$.

**Claim grade: derived**, for the rule of step 2 applied to a prescribed acceleration. Falsifier: an error in the algebra above, or a reading of the code under which the published radii include a term that bounds the acceleration between the step endpoints.

**What the proposition does and does not show.** It shows that the published radii are not, by their construction, an enclosure of the true solution: an estimator that can read zero while the error is of order one is not a certificate. That is an obstruction to citing the carrier as satisfying the theorem's tube hypothesis. It does not show that any particular solver run has a true solution outside its tubes. In the coupled canonical problem the acceleration is not prescribed; it is determined by the receiver's own position during the step and by the sources' histories, and for smooth slow motion the discrepancy is typically a good estimate. The statement that an actual canonical run can leave its tubes is **inferred**, not demonstrated here. A demonstration would need a run compared with a separately authored high-accuracy reference; a prescribed source whose induced acceleration at a receiver repeats at the three nodes of a step while varying between them is the natural construction. Agreement of repeated runs or of step-refinement ladders would not settle it in either direction.

Regularity is not the problem. Where the canonical acceleration is finite and continuous, the true solution is twice continuously differentiable, so the continuously differentiable path the grouped theorem requires exists. The missing property is inclusion alone.

## 3. The smallest proof route

What is needed is a statement of the form: for every time in the step, the true position and velocity lie in computable sets. The least machinery that yields one is a validated step that uses only an enclosure of the acceleration over the whole step, with no claim about its derivatives.

**Setting.** Restrict to steps in which every causal root of every receiver, for every reception time in $[T,T+h]$, has its emission time before $T$. Then during the step each receiver's acceleration depends only on already accepted histories and on that receiver's own current position, so each receiver obeys an ordinary differential equation $\ddot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf F(t,\mathbf x)$ on the step. A sufficient geometric condition is that every source stays farther than $h$ from every receiver throughout the step, since a wake emitted during the step cannot cross more than $h$ within it; self-hits and close pairs fall outside the restriction and keep their existing treatment.

**Route.** Let $\mathcal X_0$ and $\mathcal V_0$ be the inherited boxes for a receiver's position and velocity at $T$.

1. *A priori enclosure.* Find a box $\mathcal A$ and boxes $\mathcal X\supseteq\mathcal X_0+[0,h]\mathcal V_0+[0,h^2/2]\mathcal A$ and $\mathcal V\supseteq\mathcal V_0+[0,h]\mathcal A$ such that $\mathbf F(t,\mathbf x)\in\mathcal A$ for all $t\in[T,T+h]$ and all $\mathbf x\in\mathcal X$. By the standard fixed-point argument for integral equations, a solution starting in $\mathcal X_0\times\mathcal V_0$ then exists on the step and stays in $\mathcal X\times\mathcal V$.
2. *Tube.* For that solution and every $\tau\in[0,h]$, $\mathbf v(T+\tau)\in\mathcal V_0+\tau\mathcal A$ and $\mathbf x(T+\tau)\in\mathcal X_0+\tau\mathcal V_0+\tfrac{\tau^2}{2}\mathcal A$, by integrating $\ddot{\mathbf x}\in\mathcal A$ twice. These sets are the tube. Around the midpoint quadratic they have half-widths that grow from the inherited radii by $\tau\,\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal A)$ in velocity and $\tfrac{\tau^2}{2}\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal A)$ in position, so inherited uncertainty is carried and never reset.
3. *Publication.* Publish any cubic together with radii large enough that its tube contains the sets of item 2 at every time in the step, checked with a Bernstein bound of the difference between the cubic and the midpoint quadratic. The velocity tube encloses the true velocity, which is the derivative of the true position because both belong to the same solution.

A segment published this way satisfies the grouped theorem's hypotheses by proof. The tube is first order in $h$ times the spread of the acceleration over the step, coarser than the fourth-order behaviour of the present corrector; tightening it needs bounds on the time derivative of $\mathbf F$ along the motion, which the existing first-order root and acceleration sensitivities could supply in a later refinement.

**Claim grade: derived** for items 1 to 3 as mathematics, given the enclosure $\mathcal A$; falsifier: a solution of $\ddot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf F$ with $\mathbf F\in\mathcal A$ on $\mathcal X$ that leaves the stated sets. That the solver can produce $\mathcal A$ is **not established**, and is the content of the next paragraph.

**What the solver has and lacks for it.** It has outward acceleration certificates at one reception time with an interval receiver position; the individual far-field bound and the grouped bound, which are already valid over a whole emission window and a receiver box; block certificates over a reception-time interval, for exclusion only; and the corrector's endpoint intervals. It lacks an acceleration enclosure valid over a whole reception-time interval for sources that are not far, which is the uniform-time extension that the snapshot stages deliberately excluded; the a priori fixed-point test; and publication of radii derived from $\mathcal A$ in place of the discrepancy. For a population in which every pair is far, the first gap closes with the far-field and grouped bounds alone, because those bounds hold uniformly once the receiver box is taken to be $\mathcal X$ and the emission window is extended to the step. That special case is the smallest instance in which the whole route could be implemented and independently checked.

## 4. Disposition

The carrier's radii are a step-doubling discrepancy plus arithmetic widths. They are not an enclosure of the true solution, by the proposition of section 2. Solver-generated histories therefore do not satisfy the grouped theorem's hypotheses by anything now in the solver, and admitting them would make every grouped interval on evolved histories conditional on an unproved inclusion.

The route of section 3 would remove the condition. It is a new validated-step capability with its own proof obligations and independent controls, and it changes what evolution publishes. It was not selected by the scope's Stage 3, which assumed an existing uncertainty certificate to propagate. Within the present window it can be stated and reviewed, not implemented and qualified.

Stage 3 is therefore not entered. Exact fallback remains in force for every evolution evaluation: the evolution entry points refuse the grouped policy, and that refusal is the correct behaviour until an inclusion result exists.
