## 2. The ordinary carrier, corrected

**Claim grade: measured**, by reading `append_candidate_segments`, `append_event_aware_candidate_segments`, `endpoint_local_errors` and `inflate_fine_histories` in `src/eom/src/CoupledEvolution.cpp`; `shasum -a 256 -c` of the 82-file live inventory, run from `src/eom` at 2026-10-11T22:45:15Z, reports every file unchanged from the inventory taken at 22:24:04Z. Falsifier: different arithmetic in those symbols at that inventory.

For an ordinary step from $T$ to $T+h$, `append_candidate_segments` builds each path's segment from the state at $T$ and two certified accelerations, $\mathbf a_0$ at $T$ and $\mathbf a_1$ at $T+h$.

1. The cubic's coefficients about $T$ are the midpoint of the inherited position interval, the midpoint of the inherited velocity interval, $\mathbf a_0/2$ and $(\mathbf a_1-\mathbf a_0)/(6h)$, using acceleration midpoints. Its acceleration is the straight line in time from $\mathbf a_0$ to $\mathbf a_1$. A path flagged for right-endpoint acceleration uses $\mathbf a_1$ for both, a constant acceleration.
2. Its endpoint state is therefore $\mathbf v_1=\mathbf v_0+\tfrac h2(\mathbf a_0+\mathbf a_1)$ and $\mathbf x_1=\mathbf x_0+h\mathbf v_0+\tfrac{h^2}{6}(2\mathbf a_0+\mathbf a_1)$, as the earlier record stated.
3. Its base radii are $r_x=r_{x0}+h\,r_{v0}+\tfrac{h^2}{2}\max(r_{a0},r_{a1})$ and $r_v=r_{v0}+h\max(r_{a0},r_{a1})$ per axis, where $r_{x0}$ and $r_{v0}$ are the radii of the inherited state intervals and $r_{a0}$, $r_{a1}$ those of the two acceleration intervals. The source comment states their meaning: they carry the interval inputs through the step, by $\int_0^h a_{\rm err}\,dt\le h\,r_a$ and $\int_0^h(h-t)\,a_{\rm err}\,dt\le h^2r_a/2$.
4. The summed endpoint-Hermite radii that the earlier record named belong to `append_event_aware_candidate_segments` only.
5. The step is computed once as a full step and once as two half steps. `endpoint_local_errors` takes, per axis, the absolute difference of the full-step and half-step endpoint midpoints; the step is accepted when these are within the declared position and velocity tolerances; `inflate_fine_histories` adds them to the half-step segments' radii, and those segments are published.

The corrected radii in item 3 bound how far the published cubic moves when the inherited state and the two endpoint accelerations move inside their intervals. They are an input-uncertainty propagation for a straight-line acceleration. Nothing in them, and nothing in item 5, bounds the difference between the true acceleration and that straight line between the step's nodes. The earlier record's conclusion about the two ingredients therefore stands with the corrected formulas. Its proposition used only the rule of item 2 and is unaffected.

## 3. Local error of the rule, replacing the withdrawn order statement

**Claim grade: derived**, for a prescribed acceleration $a(t)$ that is twice continuously differentiable on the step. Falsifier: an error in the integrals below.

Write $a_{\rm lin}$ for the straight line through $a(T)$ and $a(T+h)$. Then $a(T+\tau)-a_{\rm lin}(T+\tau)=\tfrac12a''(\xi_\tau)\,\tau(\tau-h)$ for some $\xi_\tau$ in the step. Integrating once, the velocity error of the rule at the step end is $-\tfrac{h^3}{12}a''(\xi)$; integrating against $(h-\tau)$, the position error is $-\tfrac{h^4}{24}a''(\xi')$. The velocity rule is the trapezoid rule, with local error of third order in $h$, as the review said. Neither statement applies when $a$ is less regular, and neither is a bound available to the solver, which does not compute $a''$. The validated tube of section 5 is first order in $h$ times the spread of the acceleration over the step; it is coarser than these local errors for smooth slow motion and is the only one of the three that is a proved enclosure.

## 4. A canonical control: an accepted step whose tube excludes the true motion

The earlier record said a demonstration "would need a run compared with a separately authored high-accuracy reference", using "a prescribed source whose induced acceleration at a receiver repeats at the three nodes of a step while varying between them". That construction was built and run. Inputs, commands, outputs, identities and retained failures are in the [control receipt](../evidence/matched-pair-stage-3-aliasing-control-receipt-2026-10-11.md).

### 4.1 Construction

Everything lies on the $z$ axis. A receiver of charge $1$ rests at the origin up to $T=0$. One source of charge $1$ has the prescribed past $z_s(S)=R+A\,p(S)$ with $R=10$, where $p$ has period $1$, rises from $0$ to $1$ on $[k,k+\tfrac12]$ as $p(u)=12u^2-16u^3$ and falls symmetrically on $[k+\tfrac12,k+1]$. It is an exact piecewise cubic with continuous velocity, speed at most $3A$ and acceleration at most $24A$ in magnitude. The coupling is $\kappa=10^{-3}$ and the amplitude $A=0.2$, so the source speed stays at or below $0.6$. With $A=0$ the source is static, which is the known case.

For a receiver at $z=\zeta\le0$ and reception time $t$, the causal root $S$ solves $t-S=z_s(S)-\zeta$, the transmitter factor is $D=1+\dot z_s(S)$, and the canonical acceleration of the receiver is $a(t,\zeta)=-\kappa/\bigl((z_s(S)-\zeta)^2D\bigr)$. At a fixed receiver the reception time $t=S+z_s(S)$ advances by exactly $1$ when $S$ does, so $a(t,0)$ has period $1$ in $t$; and at integer $t$ the root is at $S=t-R$, an integer, where the source is at $R$ with zero velocity, so $a=-\kappa/R^2$ at every integer reception time. A step of length $2$ from $T=0$ has its three nodes at $t=0,1,2$.

### 4.2 Three tiers, kept separate

**Tier A — fixed-receiver acceleration diagnostic. Claim grade: measured**, by `certify_native_acceleration_snapshot` of the live library on histories prescribed through $t=2$ with the receiver held at the origin. Known case first: with $A=0$ every reported interval contains $-\kappa/R^2=-10^{-5}$. Target: the reported acceleration is $-1.0000\times10^{-5}$ at $t=0$, $1$ and $2$, and the values sampled between them range from $-6.12\times10^{-6}$ to $-2.45\times10^{-5}$. At $t=0.35$ and $t=0.85$ the roots fall at quarter periods, where the closed form gives $-\kappa/(10.1^2\cdot1.6)$ and $-\kappa/(10.1^2\cdot0.4)$; both reported intervals contain those values. This tier shows only that the node-repeating acceleration profile exists for the canonical law.

**Tier B — evolving receiver under a prescribed source past. Claim grade: measured** for what the solver published, **derived** for the bracket of the true motion. The run is `evolve_native_coupled_histories` of the live library over one pinned step of length $2$, position and velocity tolerances $10^{-8}$. The step was accepted. Its step-doubling discrepancy for the receiver was $2.30\times10^{-10}$ in position and in velocity, and the published end intervals have that half-width. The exact-rational bracket of section 4.3 places the true end state elsewhere:

The entry point is the fully coupled evolver with two evolving paths, and the source's own motion after $T=0$ is solver-generated. That does not affect the claim. The receiver's true motion over the step is determined by the prescribed past alone, and the claim concerns the receiver's tube only.

**Tier C — fully coupled evolution in which the aliasing motion is itself solver-generated. Not attempted and not established.** The oscillating source past is prescribed initial data. It is an admissible request, because initial retained histories are inputs, but it is not a solution of the canonical law and nothing here shows that evolution from physically generated histories produces such a profile at its own step nodes.

### 4.3 The bracket of the true motion

**Claim grade: derived.** Evaluated in exact rational arithmetic by a [separately written instrument](../evidence/matched-pair-stage-3-aliasing-control-bound.py) that imports no solver code and reads only the published end intervals. Falsifier: an error in either bound below, or an exact evaluation of them that intersects a published interval.

*Fixed receiver.* With $\zeta=0$, $dt/dS=1+\dot z_s(S)=D>0$, so changing variable from reception time to emission time cancels the transmitter factor:

$$
\int_0^h a(t,0)\,dt=-\kappa\int_{-R}^{h-R}\frac{dS}{z_s(S)^2}=-\kappa\,h\,I,\qquad I=\int_0^1\frac{du}{(R+A\,p(u))^2},
$$

for integer $h$. Because $p$ is monotone on each half period, left and right Riemann sums bracket $I$; with $4000$ cells per half period and outward rounding at forty decimal digits, $I\in[9.804312\times10^{-3},\,9.804410\times10^{-3}]$. The rule of section 2 instead uses $1/R^2=10^{-2}$, the value at the nodes. The end position uses the same change of variable with weight $(h-t)$.

*Receiver motion.* The canonical per-hit acceleration depends on the receiver's position and not on its velocity, the transmitter-only finding of Stage 0. The acceleration is never positive, so $\zeta(t)\le0$, the distance is at least $R$ and $D\ge1-3A$. Then $|a|\le\kappa/(R^2(1-3A))$ and $|\zeta(t)|\le\kappa t^2/(2R^2(1-3A))$. From $\partial_\zeta\bigl[1/(r^2D)\bigr]=2/(r^3D^2)-\ddot z_s/(r^2D^3)$, the map $\zeta\mapsto a(t,\zeta)$ is Lipschitz with constant $\kappa L$, $L=2/(R^3(1-3A)^2)+24A/(R^2(1-3A)^3)=0.7625$. Hence the true end velocity differs from the fixed-receiver value by at most $\kappa^2Lh^3/(6R^2(1-3A))=2.54\times10^{-8}$ and the true end position by at most $\kappa^2Lh^4/(24R^2(1-3A))=1.27\times10^{-8}$ for $h=2$. These corrections are second order in $\kappa$; the gap between the rule and the truth is first order.

*Known case.* With $A=0$ the instrument returns $I=1/R^2$ exactly and a bracket that intersects the solver's published static interval. A second-order expansion of the static case gives the true end velocity $-\kappa h/R^2+\kappa^2h^3/(3R^5)=-1.99999733\times10^{-5}$, which lies inside the published $[-1.99999800\times10^{-5},\,-1.99999600\times10^{-5}]$. The instrument also checks its Riemann bracket against a closed-form integral before any target.

### 4.4 What the control does and does not establish

It establishes, for one constructed instance at Tier B, that an accepted ordinary canonical step can publish position and velocity intervals that exclude the true state by three to four orders of magnitude of their own width. That refutes the proposal to cite the published radii as the theorem's tube hypothesis; the derived proposition already blocked that citation, and the control removes the remaining doubt about whether the coupled canonical setting could differ.

It is not a defect report against the solver's own claims. Canonical steps are published with evidence status `executable_architecture_evidence`, not as solution enclosures, and the step-doubling discrepancy is documented as an error estimate. The instance is adversarial: the step is pinned, the source period divides the step, and the prescribed past is not dynamically generated. It says nothing about how often ordinary runs alias, nothing about Tier C, and nothing about the Master Equation itself; the bracket and the solver evaluate the same canonical law, so their disagreement is about integration in time, and their agreement at Tier A is two evaluations of one formula. The control and the bracket were written by the same author; the independent check is the reviewer's.

One failure is retained. With root tolerance $10^{-10}$ the same step halted with `root_completeness_not_certified`: the recertification at the step end returned `numeric_precision_limit_exhausted` for the receiver's root on the source. The receiver's published half-width, $2.3\times10^{-10}$, exceeds that tolerance, which is the **inferred** cause; with root and acceleration tolerances $10^{-7}$ the step is accepted. The known static case is accepted under both settings.

## 5. The validated far step, with the hypotheses stated

The earlier record sketched this route. The review asked for a validated initial past and induction, a complete uniform root domain with a regular chart, existence and uniqueness, inherited position and velocity widths, and an actual uniform reception-time argument. This section supplies them. **Claim grade: derived** as mathematics, subject to review; **not implemented**, and no instrument has checked it. Falsifier: a configuration satisfying H0 to H5 whose true solution leaves the stated sets.

Notation. Paths are indexed $i,j$. The step is $[T,T+h]$. A tube for path $j$ assigns to each retained time $S$ a position box and a velocity box.

**H0 — validated past.** Each path has a true trajectory $\mathbf X_j$ on the retained window $[S_{\min},T]$ that is continuously differentiable with Lipschitz velocity, and for every $S$ in that window $\mathbf X_j(S)$ lies in the position tube and $\dot{\mathbf X}_j(S)$ in the velocity tube. This holds for exact prescribed initial histories, which are piecewise cubic with exact joins and zero radii, and it is the conclusion of the theorem for every segment the theorem itself publishes. It is the induction hypothesis; segments published by the present carrier do not satisfy it by anything proved.

**H1 — sub-wake speed.** There is $v^+<1$ bounding every velocity tube on the retained window and every step velocity box $\mathcal V_i$ below.

**H2 — isolation of the step.** There are step position boxes $\mathcal X_i$ with $\inf\{|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_j|:\mathbf x_i\in\mathcal X_i,\ \mathbf x_j\in\mathcal X_j\}>h$ for all $i\neq j$.

**H3 — uniform lower signs.** For each ordered pair $i\neq j$, with $\mathcal P_j$ the position tube of $j$ at $S_{\min}$, either $\sup\{|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|:\mathbf x\in\mathcal X_i,\ \mathbf y\in\mathcal P_j\}<T-S_{\min}$, called present, or $\inf\{|\mathbf x-\mathbf y|:\mathbf x\in\mathcal X_i,\ \mathbf y\in\mathcal P_j\}>T+h-S_{\min}$, called absent. A pair satisfying neither is outside the theorem.

**H4 — acceleration enclosure.** There are boxes $\mathcal A_i$ such that, for every true past consistent with H0, every $t\in[T,T+h]$ and every $\mathbf x\in\mathcal X_i$, the canonical acceleration $\mathbf F_i(t,\mathbf x)$ of a receiver of path $i$'s charge at $(t,\mathbf x)$ lies in $\mathcal A_i$.

**H5 — a priori enclosure.** With $\mathcal X_{i,0}$ and $\mathcal V_{i,0}$ the tube sections of path $i$ at $T$, both with their inherited widths, $\mathcal X_i\supseteq\mathcal X_{i,0}+[0,h]\mathcal V_{i,0}+[0,h^2/2]\mathcal A_i$ and $\mathcal V_i\supseteq\mathcal V_{i,0}+[0,h]\mathcal A_i$.

**Lemma 1 — the root domain is complete, uniform and regular.** Fix $i\neq j$, $t\in[T,T+h]$ and $\mathbf x\in\mathcal X_i$, and let $g(S)=|\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(S)|-(t-S)$ be the causal residual, where for $S>T$ the trajectory is a continuation that stays in $\mathcal X_j$ with speed at most $v^+$. Parts (i) and (iii) use only the validated past; parts (ii) and (iv) use the continuation. The retained window is taken as the complete emission domain, as in the snapshot contract, and nothing is claimed about emissions before $S_{\min}$. (i) $g$ is strictly increasing with $g'\ge1-v^+>0$ wherever the distance is positive, by H1. (ii) For $S\in[T,t]$, H2 gives $|\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(S)|>h\ge t-S$, so $g>0$ there: this is the upper sign, and it holds for every reception time in the step because the source window ends at $T$ and the distance exceeds $h$. (iii) At $S_{\min}$, a present pair has $g(S_{\min})<0$ for every $(t,\mathbf x)$, because $g(S_{\min})$ is largest at the earliest reception time $t=T$; an absent pair has $g(S_{\min})>0$ for every $(t,\mathbf x)$, because it is smallest at the latest reception time $t=T+h$. Since $\mathbf X_j(T)\in\mathcal X_{j,0}\subseteq\mathcal X_j$, H2 gives $g(T)>0$ from the past alone. Therefore a present pair has exactly one root for every $(t,\mathbf x)$, in the open interval $(S_{\min},T)$, and an absent pair has none in $[S_{\min},t]$. Every root lies strictly inside the validated past and is independent of what any path does after $T$. At each root $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\dot{\mathbf X}_j\ge1-v^+>0$, so the root is simple and the sharp chart is regular. (iv) A path has no root on its own past: for $S<t$, $|\mathbf X_i(t)-\mathbf X_i(S)|\le v^+(t-S)<t-S$.

**Lemma 2 — existence and uniqueness.** Define $\mathbf F_i(t,\mathbf x)$ on $[T,T+h]\times\mathcal X_i$ from the validated past alone, as the sum over present pairs of the pair acceleration $\kappa q_iq_j\,\mathbf n/(r^2D)$ at the unique root in $(S_{\min},T)$ given by Lemma 1 (i) and (iii). The root is a continuously differentiable function of $(t,\mathbf x)$ by the implicit function theorem with $\partial_Sg\ge1-v^+$. At a root, $r=t-S\ge T-S$ and $r\ge|\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(T)|-v^+(T-S)>h-v^+(T-S)$, so $r>h/(1+v^+)>0$. Because $\dot{\mathbf X}_j$ is Lipschitz, $\mathbf F_i$ is continuous and Lipschitz in $\mathbf x$. Consider continuous curves $(\mathbf x,\mathbf v)$ on $[T,T+h]$ with values in $\mathcal X_i\times\mathcal V_i$ and initial state in $\mathcal X_{i,0}\times\mathcal V_{i,0}$. By H4 and H5 the integral operator of $\ddot{\mathbf x}=\mathbf F_i(t,\mathbf x)$ maps this closed convex set into itself; it has a fixed point, unique by the Lipschitz property. Take these curves as the continuations of all paths. They stay in the boxes with speed at most $v^+$, so Lemma 1 (ii) and (iv) apply to them: no emission after $T$ and no self-emission produces a root, the full canonical acceleration of each path equals $\mathbf F_i$ along its curve, and the curves solve the coupled canonical law on the step. Each path's equation is separate from the others during the step. Any other solution with the same past coincides with them: up to the first time it could differ the same argument applies, and the strict margin in H2 extends the absence of new roots slightly beyond that time, where uniqueness for the separate equations forces agreement.

**Theorem.** Under H0 to H5, for every path and every $\tau\in[0,h]$ the true state satisfies $\dot{\mathbf X}_i(T+\tau)\in\mathcal V_{i,0}+\tau\mathcal A_i$ and $\mathbf X_i(T+\tau)\in\mathcal X_{i,0}+\tau\mathcal V_{i,0}+\tfrac{\tau^2}{2}\mathcal A_i$. Any cubic published with radii whose tube contains these sets for every $\tau$ satisfies H0 on $[S_{\min},T+h]$: the true trajectory is continuously differentiable across $T$ because its state is continuous there, its velocity is Lipschitz because $\mathbf F_i$ is bounded, and the published velocity tube encloses the derivative of the trajectory the position tube encloses. The half-widths about the midpoint quadratic grow from the inherited position and velocity radii by $\tau\,\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal V_{i,0})+\tfrac{\tau^2}{2}\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal A_i)$ and $\tau\,\mathrm{rad}(\mathcal A_i)$; inherited widths are carried and never reset. *Proof.* Integrate $\ddot{\mathbf X}_i\in\mathcal A_i$ once and twice from the inherited sections, using Lemma 2 for existence and containment. ∎

By induction from exact initial histories, every segment published this way is validated, and such segments satisfy the tube hypothesis of the grouped theorem with a true path of the required regularity and with acceleration bound $\sup|\mathcal A_i|$.

**What the solver has and lacks for this.** All-far geometry alone does not implement the theorem; H3 and H4 are separate obligations. The existing snapshot certificate fixes one reception time: its root rows, its prefix exclusion and its acceleration intervals are point-event statements with an interval receiver position, and they are not the uniform-time extension that H3 and H4 require. The individual far-field bound and the grouped bound are stated over an emission window and a receiver box, so once H1 to H3 are checked over the step they supply a zero-centred $\mathcal A_i$ whose radius is the sum of the pair bounds. That enclosure is valid and coarse. For the control of section 4 it gives $\mathcal A=[-2.5\times10^{-5},2.5\times10^{-5}]$ and a velocity tube of half-width $5\times10^{-5}$ at $h=2$, which contains the true value where the published half-width of $2.3\times10^{-10}$ does not. A tighter $\mathcal A_i$ needs an acceleration evaluator valid over a reception-time interval, which does not exist. Also missing are the checks H3 and H5 themselves and publication of radii from $\mathcal A_i$ in place of the discrepancy. Steps with a near pair, a self-hit, a speed bound at or above $1$, or a pair that is neither present nor absent fall outside the theorem and keep their present treatment, without a validated tube.

## 6. Disposition

The published radii of the ordinary carrier are an input-uncertainty propagation for a straight-line acceleration plus a step-doubling discrepancy. They are not an enclosure of the true solution: derived by the proposition of the earlier record, and measured at Tier B by an accepted canonical step whose intervals exclude the derived true state. Whether dynamically generated histories alias in practice is open and is not needed for the disposition, because the grouped theorem needs a proof of inclusion and not a low frequency of failure.

Solver-generated histories are therefore not admitted to the grouped enclosure. Evolution entry points continue to refuse the grouped policy, and exact fallback remains in force for every evolution evaluation. The validated far step of section 5 is the smallest stated route to an inclusion result. It is a new capability that changes what evolution publishes, it is unimplemented, and it lies outside the Stage 3 that the scope selected. Stage 3 is not entered.
