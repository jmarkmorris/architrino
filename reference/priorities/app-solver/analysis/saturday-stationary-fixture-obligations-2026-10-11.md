# Stationary frontier fixture: a minimum reach and a derived rejection control in place of an exact failure time (2026-10-11)

## Result

**In words.** The native test `eom_stationary_joint_frontier_fixture` used to pass only if the EOM solver certified fixed replay steps exactly to the time $1.395$ and then failed exactly at $1.40$. The [accepted diagnosis](saturday-stationary-frontier-diagnosis-2026-10-11.md) showed that this pinned time depends on the sequence of step sizes the controller happens to accept, so the test failed after two unrelated source changes although nothing it was meant to protect had broken. The fixture now tests two stable properties instead. The EOM solver is unchanged.

1. **A minimum reach (implemented, measured).** The adaptive prefix must be accepted to the existing endpoint `1.2399999999999993`, with all four ordered pairs accounted for (both self-pairs certified root free, each cross pair with exactly one certified root and a root-free complement), the endpoint acceleration evaluated by the accepted corrector and the final recertification on the published history both certified, both retained histories (ordinary and joint) ending at that time, and a second pair-selection route giving the same result on the same endpoint.
2. **A rejection control chosen by a derived rule, not tuned (implemented; three grades, kept separate).** From the accepted endpoint the fixture builds the request for the next step with one change of numerical policy: the root tolerance is set to one quarter of the receiver's retained position remainder radius $R$. *Derived:* the joint root-time budget's computed width exceeds $R/2$, so that budget cannot certify a root within $R/4$ (Section 3). *Measured:* the whole step is refused, with `root_completeness_not_certified`. *Inferred:* that the solver's other route, the ordinary enclosure it tries first, cannot certify the root either. The fixture requires the rejection and then compares the returned ordinary histories and the returned joint histories, field by field, with copies taken before the call: 26,726 ordinary values and 203,102 joint values in the retained run, all equal.
3. **The natural frontier is reported and no longer required (implemented).** The fixture still replays fixed steps of $0.005$ and reports where they stop being certified (today: ten steps, to `1.2899999999999983`, as the diagnosis found). That location no longer decides the outcome. Any rejection met there must still leave both carriers unchanged.
4. **Outcome (measured).** Built afresh from the live tree, the no-argument fixture exits $0$ in 47 seconds, twice with byte-identical output, and passes as the registered test. A separately written reader of the record, frozen before the fixture was edited, checks the record's structure and re-evaluates two relations from printed enclosures, and passes it; twelve single-defect mutations of that record are each detected. What that reader can and cannot establish is in [Section 6](#6-the-checker-and-its-controls). The fixture's diagnostic mode, run with the arguments of the earlier retained run, reproduces that run's record and rejected-candidate file exactly.

5. **Corrected after review (implemented, measured).** The first version used its two carrier-comparison functions on evolved histories before testing them on a known case. The fixture now first compares small hand-written carriers, an equal copy and copies differing in one coefficient, one error radius and one radius by a single binary step, and refuses to continue if any control fails; the one-step test on a real carrier must now be detected, where before a constructor exception would also have passed. The corrected fixture was rebuilt and rerun: exit $0$, and apart from the added fields its record is identical to the first version's. The first version's source, binary, build inputs and outputs are retained unchanged; its order of events is stated in Section 5.

**What this is not.** It is a repair of one test's expectations. It does not qualify the solver, the trajectory, or the enclosure of the true solution by the generated history. The second route in item 1 is the same solver's other pair-selection route, not an independent verification. The rejection in item 2 is a refusal by this implementation's sufficient certificates; it is not a theorem that no sound certificate could exist, and a future tighter certificate that passed the control, or an accepted input for which the rule's hypotheses fail, would call for maintenance of this fixture and would not by itself be a solver regression. The record of item 1 holds one endpoint evaluation and one recertification; it is not a trace of every acceleration used to construct the accepted step. The fixture no longer protects the old reach of $1.395$; the minimum reach is the one selected in the [handover](../../../op/agent-chats/2026-10-11-saturday-quantitative-followups/chat.md).

> Claim grades. Measured: every run outcome, including the refusal of the whole control step, bound to the source and binary identities in [Section 5](#5-build-and-run-receipts). Derived: the lower bound $W>R/2$ on the joint root-time budget's width under the three hypotheses of Section 3, from the source of `RootTimeBudget.cpp` and `JointState.cpp`. Inferred: that the ordinary (non-joint) enclosure route cannot certify the control either; this is argued in Section 3 and is consistent with the reported certificate, but it is not derived. The preservation of the positive numerical policy rests on inspection of the patch, not on the record reader. Nothing here is acceptance of the solver or of a physical result.

## 3. Why the rejection control must be refused

**The obligation.** A root record may be accepted only when its outward width is within the unchanged root tolerance (evolution contract). In the joint route the solver certifies a root only if `certify_root_time_budget` returns `certified`, and that function sets `certified` exactly when its `root_time_width_upper` is at most the request's root-time tolerance.

**The width (read from `RootTimeBudget.cpp`).** With outward rounding throughout,

$$
W=\frac{2\,(P_{\rm affine}+P_{\rm rem}+N)}{m},\qquad P_{\rm rem}=\sum_{\rm axis}\lvert\hat d_{\rm axis}\rvert\,\rho_{\rm axis},
$$

where $\hat d$ is the unit nominal displacement, $\rho_{\rm axis}$ the independent remainder radius of the displacement on each axis, $m$ the smaller endpoint magnitude of the transmitter-factor enclosure, and $P_{\rm affine},N\ge0$. `JointState.cpp` forms $\rho_{\rm axis}$ as the receiver's independent remainder radius plus the transmitter's, plus a rounding term. Hence $\rho_x\ge R$, the receiver's radius on the $x$ axis at the reception time.

**The bound.** The configuration lies on the $x$ axis, so $\lvert\hat d_x\rvert\ge\tfrac12$ (it is $1$ up to rounding). The transmitter factor is $1-\hat n\cdot v$ with the transmitter below wake speed, so its enclosure's smaller magnitude is below $2$. Therefore

$$
W\ \ge\ \frac{2\,\lvert\hat d_x\rvert\,R}{m}\ >\ \frac{2\cdot\tfrac12\cdot R}{2}=\frac R2\ >\ \frac R4 .
$$

So $W>R/2$, and a request whose root tolerance is $R/4$ cannot have its cross roots certified by the joint budget at the accepted endpoint. The bound proved is $R/2$, not $R$; on an exactly collinear configuration with $m\le1$ it would be $2R$, but that stronger statement is not needed and is not claimed. This holds at the first thing the atomic step does, the acceleration snapshot at its start time, where the receiver is exactly the accepted endpoint state. It does not depend on where the natural frontier is, on the current width, or on any tuning.

**The hypotheses, and how the fixture checks them.** (i) $R>0$: read from the accepted joint carrier; (ii) $\lvert\hat d_x\rvert\ge\tfrac12$: for each cross pair the fixture takes the receiver's endpoint enclosure and the transmitter's position hull over its whole retained history and requires the axial separation to be positive and the squared transverse separation to be at most $2.99$ times its square; (iii) $m<2$: the transmitter's velocity hull over its whole retained history must have squared speed below $0.99$. If any fails, the fixture stops with an error that names the condition. Such a stop, like a future certificate tight enough to pass the control, means the control no longer applies to that input; it is a reason to revisit the fixture and is not by itself evidence of a solver regression.

**The rule and its values in the retained run.** $R$ is read from the accepted joint carrier by the same evaluation call the solver uses. Both members give $R=2.9590809023271205\times10^{-6}$, and the control's tolerance is $7.3977022558178011\times10^{-7}$, against the positive request's $10^{-5}$. The reported certificate has joint width $8.4331053275713993\times10^{-6}$ with remainder $3.5711601510616909\times10^{-6}$, consistent with the bound ($2\times3.571\times10^{-6}/8.433\times10^{-6}=0.847$ for $m$, below $2$; $8.433\times10^{-6}>R/2=1.4795\times10^{-6}>R/4=7.398\times10^{-7}$).

**The other route (inferred), and what is measured.** By the Codex review's reading of the consumer, the solver attempts an ordinary enclosure of each root first and falls back to the joint route only when that fails. The derivation above concerns the joint route alone, so it does not by itself prove that the whole root search must fail. What is measured is that it does: the solver reports the row as `interior_root_not_surrounded/joint_root/root_time_budget_exceeded`, so the ordinary isolation did not surround the root and the joint budget then refused it. That is expected: the ordinary position radii dominate the joint ones by the solver's fallback-dominance rule, so an interval residual built from them is not sign-definite within a distance of order $R/m$ of the root, and no bracket narrower than that can be certified. This step is argued, not derived from the source line by line.

**What kind of failure this is.** A root-enclosure refusal under a deliberately tightened, explicitly labelled tolerance. The row's code is `numeric_precision_limit_exhausted` at 512 bits, which is how the solver reports the row after its precision attempts; the step's code is `root_completeness_not_certified`. It is not a resource exhaustion in the sense of the ledger's resource table, and it is not a statement about the physical motion. If the receiver radius at the accepted endpoint were ever zero the control could not be built; the fixture then stops with an error and exit status $1$, not with a pass.

## 7. Limits, and what would overturn this

- **Reach.** The fixture now fails if the prefix does not reach `1.2399999999999993`, and no longer fails if fixed replay steps stop earlier or later than before. A solver change that shortened reach between $1.24$ and $1.395$ would no longer be caught by this test. Whether a larger required reach is wanted is the fixture owner's decision.
- **Same-library data.** The hulls and enclosures the checker uses are printed by the solver's history layer. The checker's arithmetic is independent; its inputs are not.
- **No oracle run.** The independent Python oracle was not run against the endpoint; the diagnosis's single comparison at $T=1.24$ stands as it was.
- **One host, one build.** Toolchain as stated. The record's byte stability was checked across two runs of one binary only.
- **Section 3's second leg is inferred**, and the derived bound is $R/2$ under three checked hypotheses, not a statement about every possible certificate.
- **The comparison functions' first use was target-first.** The corrected version puts known cases first; the first version's results for them are retained as they are.
- **The endpoint snapshot is one evaluation.** The fixture does not reconstruct the accelerations used to build the accepted step.
- **Consumers.** Searched as stated in Section 4; documentation that describes the v4 behaviour, including the accepted diagnosis, is historical and was not edited.

Falsifiers. A rebuild of the edited file from the recorded source state whose no-argument record differs from the retained one, or does not exit $0$. A request with root tolerance $R/4$ built by the stated rule that the solver certifies: that would refute Section 3. A rejected control after which any value of either carrier differs from its preimage while the fixture reports equality: that would show the comparison is defective. A record with a defect of one of the kinds the reader tests (the seventeen synthetic and twelve real-record mutations) that the reader passes; defects outside those kinds are beyond its reach and are not claimed to be caught. A hand-written comparator control that passes while the two carriers differ, or fails while they are equal. A consumer of the v4 record fields that now fails. Evidence that the diagnostic mode's output changed for identical arguments.
