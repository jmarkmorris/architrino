## Domain certificate and independent controls

**Derived.** A selected speed domain changes admission and publication, with no acceleration response or velocity clamp. With normalized wake speed one, unrestricted imposes no speed ceiling, inclusive requires $\|V(T)\|\le1$, and strict requires $\|V(T)\|<1$. A complete dense certificate encloses the speed squared for every retained time, including velocity reconstruction errors. An upper bound below one proves strict admission; an upper bound at most one proves inclusive admission. An interval straddling the boundary proves neither a violation nor admissibility. A point/slab lower bound above one proves violation; exact equality is outside the strict domain. Subdivision exhaustion must preserve these distinctions.

Exact analytical circular speed is the token-defined tangential speed because rigid rotations preserve norm. That identity can certify an inclusive equality preparation even when independent Cartesian reconstruction boxes straddle one. It applies only to the factory's original proven analytical domain; subsequently appended computed history requires its own dense certificate. Surface's ordinary method-of-steps proof independently requires coherent subwake paths. Therefore inclusive equality may be domain-admissible but unavailable for forward Surface evolution under its current proof chart. A halted equality case supplies no capped-response capability.

The following independent polynomial family isolates between-endpoint speed behavior. For $0\le t\le1$, take exact Cartesian position

$$
X_b(t)=(bt+1.5t^2-t^3,0,0),\qquad V_b(t)=(b+3t-3t^2,0,0).
$$

Completing the square gives $V_{b,1}(t)=b+0.75-3(t-0.5)^2$. For the positive $b$ values below, speed is this scalar velocity and has its maximum at $t=0.5$.

| Exact $b$ | Endpoint speed | Maximum speed | Independent conclusion |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 0.2 | 0.2 | 0.95 | Strict and inclusive admissible with zero reconstruction error; an additional componentwise first-axis velocity radius 0.01 remains below one. |
| 0.25 | 0.25 | 1 | Strict inadmissible; inclusive mathematically admissible, but a generic interval evaluator can honestly remain uncertified unless it proves the exact polynomial maximum. |
| 0.5 | 0.5 | 1.25 | Both restricted domains fail between otherwise safe endpoints. First contact is $t=(3-\sqrt3)/6$, and the second is $(3+\sqrt3)/6$. |

These are speed-certificate controls, not claims that arbitrary cubics are Surface preparations or solutions of the Master Equation. Exact constant Cartesian velocities supply additional equality controls. A left-first interval search can encounter an uncertified interval near first contact before visiting an interior slab with proved violation. That outcome is an uncertainty result, not an actual crossing measurement. An attempted interval can enclose a possible boundary; failure of certification alone does not establish that a contact exists.
