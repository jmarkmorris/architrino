# Why the stationary frontier fixture fails closed: a traced diagnosis (2026-10-11)

## Result

**In words.** The native test `eom_stationary_joint_frontier_fixture` fails today because the solver refuses one step: at that step its own bound on the timing uncertainty of a delayed signal is $1.3$ percent larger than its fixed tolerance allows. The refusal is the conservative, all-or-nothing kind the contracts ask for. The fixture, however, does not only check that the solver refuses in that way. It also pins the exact time at which the refusal must happen, and that time depends on the sequence of step sizes the controller happened to accept. Two later source changes altered that sequence, so the refusal now comes earlier than the pinned time.

1. **Reproduced (measured).** Built from a frozen copy of the current source, the fixture with no arguments exits with status $2$ and `result: fail_closed`. The committed source `3fa846aa9`, the live working tree with its uncommitted Stage C edits, and yesterday's commit `6f869aaae` give byte-identical output. The prefix is accepted to $T=1.24$ in 234 steps; ten replay steps of $0.005$ are certified to $1.29$; the eleventh, to $1.295$, is rejected with `root_completeness_not_certified`, atomically, with the published histories unchanged.
2. **The controlling bound (measured; rebuilt from retained coefficients).** Both cross roots at reception $1.295$ fail the root-time budget: reported width $1.0133177818824738\times10^{-5}$ against the unchanged tolerance $10^{-5}$. Two session instruments check this. The first recombines the solver's reported components in exact rational arithmetic and obtains $1.0133177818824728\times10^{-5}$. The second rebuilds the components themselves from the coefficients and remainders of the rejected candidate: the projected displacement uncertainty is $4.13659913\times10^{-6}$, agreeing with the solver's total to $5\times10^{-10}$ relative, and the ordinary-box width is reproduced to $8\times10^{-11}$ relative. One input is not reproduced: the solver's lower bound on the transmitter factor is more conservative than the one rebuilt here, by two velocity radii. The excess over tolerance persists with the rebuilt bound and even with the nominal factor. The uncertainty in the budget is the published uncertainty of the two members' paths. The step would pass if the projected sum were $5.4\times10^{-8}$ smaller. This shows the solver's bound exceeds the tolerance; it does not show that every sound bound must.
3. **Sampled comparison with numerical references (measured).** At $T=1.24$ the solver's nominal position differs from the separately authored oracle record by $9.75\times10^{-7}$, and its published radius there is $3.00\times10^{-6}$. At the sampled times the radius is about three times the difference from a session reference integration. These samples are consistent with radii of the right order. They do not establish that the true path is enclosed throughout, and they do not establish that the radii are tight: a factor of three leaves far more room than the $1.3$ percent at issue.
4. **Why the frontier moved (measured by controlled variants).** Two source changes each move it. With the bounded corrector recentering of commit `2859fedfa` the prefix opens with steps of $0.02$ where without it the steps are $0.01$; the early published radii are then four times larger, and the refusal moves from $1.40$ to $1.295$. Independently, the certified-acceleration change of commit `9f988c914` shifts the accepted sequence slightly (239 to 234 steps with the recentering, 179 to 176 without), enough that even without the recentering the refusal now comes one step early, at $1.395$ instead of $1.40$.
5. **The pin is two-sided and has no margin (measured).** With the fixture's prefix limited to steps of $0.01$ and the solver unchanged, all 31 replay steps to $1.395$ certify, but the probe at $1.40$, which the fixture requires to fail, certifies as well, and the fixture still exits $2$.

**Diagnosis.** The observed rejection is consistent with the contract clauses cited in Section 7: the reported root-time width exceeds the unchanged tolerance, the root is reported uncertified and not omitted, and the step is rejected atomically with the published histories unchanged. What fails is the fixture's expectation that the frontier sits between $1.395$ and $1.40$. That expectation encoded the step sequence of 14 September and has no tolerance for a change of that sequence. **This diagnosis by itself demonstrates no need for a production repair.** It does not exclude a production improvement: a sound tightening of the enclosure by little more than one percent would remove this particular rejection. The acceleration, correction, local-error and dense-history rows of the accepted steps were not audited. The smallest owned repair is in the fixture and is a decision for its owner; options are in [Section 8](#8-repair-options-for-the-owner-none-applied).

> Claim grades: measured for items 1, 3, 4 and 5, by the builds and runs listed under [Reproduction](#2-reproduction), each bound to a byte-identified source; for item 2, measured for the reported width, derived for the theorem, and measured agreement of the two rebuilds with the solver's components, with one input not reproduced; the comparisons with the oracle record and the session reference are sampled numerical-reference evidence and not a continuous certificate; inferred for the mechanism by which the recentering changes the early step size, which is read from the code and the accepted step sizes and was not instrumented. Nothing here is acceptance of the solver, of the fixture or of any model.

## 4. The budget, rebuilt

The retained [theorem](../evidence/borg-current-root-time-budget-theorem-2026-07-20.md) bounds the width of the root-time enclosure by

$$
W_S\le\frac{2\,(P+N)}{m},\qquad N=\frac{B^2}{2(r_0-B)},
$$

where $P$ is the admitted uncertainty of the receiver-to-emission displacement projected on the line of sight (shared-symbol part plus independent remainder), $B$ its Euclidean radius, $r_0$ the nominal separation and $m$ a lower bound of the transmitter factor. It is a statement about an admitted displacement set; that the set contains the true displacement is a separate obligation, not examined here. `RootTimeBudget.cpp` implements the formula with outward rounding, and `JointState.cpp` supplies it twice: once with the joint data (differences of shared-symbol coefficients plus the sum of independent remainders) and once with the sum of the ordinary position radii.

**First instrument: arithmetic from the reported components.** Known case first: the theorem document's own tabulated row (residual widths $2.3358335487121392\times10^{-4}$ and $5.8451014205730445\times10^{-7}$ over the factor $1.0416561786956829$) gives $2.2480341383515447\times10^{-4}$ against the tabulated $2.2480341383515460\times10^{-4}$. Target: with the reported $P=4.13659913087331\times10^{-6}$, $N=1.2625\times10^{-11}$ and $m=0.8164490606621689$, exact rational arithmetic gives $W_S=1.0133177818824728\times10^{-5}$ against the reported $1.0133177818824738\times10^{-5}$. This checks the solver's final arithmetic and nothing else.

## 5. What the inherited radius is made of

**How a step is accepted and its radius published (read from the frozen source).** A step is advanced once as a full step and once as two half steps. The atomic step is rejected with `numeric_step_budget_exceeded` when the difference of the two results at the step's end exceeds the full position or velocity tolerance, here $2\times10^{-6}$ each. A separate test, `step_has_growth_headroom`, compares the same difference with $0.125$ of the tolerance; it decides only whether the controller may enlarge the next step, not whether this one is accepted. The published segment's error radius is the half-step segment's own radius plus that end-point difference. The half-step segment's radius already carries the inherited radius and whatever else the corrector charges to it; the error-budget ledger notes that acceleration widths are carried into the published radii. So the difference between the radii of successive published segments is not the local-error record of the step, and the outputs used here do not expose that record. The first submitted revision described $0.125$ of the tolerance as the acceptance limit and read radius increments as local errors; both statements were wrong and are withdrawn.

**Radius against time (measured from the fixture's trajectory frames).** W is the frozen current source; V is the same without the recentering block. The last two columns compare W's nominal position with a session reference integration of the same pair.

## 6. Why the frontier is where it is

The refusal comes at the first step where the accumulated radii exceed $m\tau/2$. Its time is therefore a function of the whole accepted step sequence, and the fixture's pin holds only for one sequence.

**The recentering (measured; mechanism inferred).** With the block, the prefix opens with steps of $0.02$, the declared initial and maximum step. Without it, the prefix opens with steps of $0.01$. The inference, from the code, is that at $0.02$ the joint endpoint contraction first reports `krawczyk_image_not_strictly_interior`; without the block that rejects the step and the controller halves it, and with the block the corrector recentres within its existing iteration bound and the step passes every certificate. The prefix rejections were not logged, so this reading of why is inferred; that the early steps differ by a factor of two and the early published radii by four is measured. The effect on the fixture is measured by removing the block and nothing else: the frontier moves from $1.29$ to $1.39$ on today's source (V), and from $1.29$ to the pinned $1.395$ on the source of `2859fedfa` (the rerun binary).

**The certified-acceleration change (measured).** Between `2859fedfa` and `6f869aaae` only two files under `src/eom` changed, an unrelated tool and `CertifiedAcceleration.cpp` (commit `9f988c914`). Putting the older version of that file into today's export reproduces yesterday's retained output for `2859fedfa` byte for byte (YC). So this change accounts for 239 becoming 234 prefix steps with the recentering, and, without it, for 179 becoming 176 and the frontier moving from $1.395$ to $1.39$. What in that change alters the accepted sequence was not traced.

**The live Stage C edits and the Stage B merge (measured).** Neither changes this fixture's output.

**No margin on either side (measured).** At the pinned frontier of 14 September the probe at $1.40$ failed with a width of $1.0205\times10^{-5}$, two percent over. Today's rejection is $1.3$ percent over. With a finer prefix (P) the excess disappears at $1.40$ as well. The pinned frontier sits between two steps whose margins are a few percent of a quantity that any change of step sequence moves by tens of percent.

## 7. The observed rejection against the cited clauses

All five contracts were reread in full for this revision. What follows is limited to the behaviour observed: the root-budget rejection and the preservation of the published state. It is not a qualification of the solver against the contracts. The acceleration, correction, local-error and dense-history rows of the 244 accepted steps, the reconstruction of accelerations from emitted records, and the validation ladder were not audited.

- **Unchanged root tolerance; no silent omission.** The evolution contract allows a root record to be accepted "when its outward width is within the unchanged root tolerance" with the transmitter factor excluding zero, and requires that a contribution whose completeness cannot be certified is "never silently omitted"; `root_completeness_not_certified` is among its required failure codes, and "no failure may publish a rejected candidate state as accepted history". Observed: the reported width exceeds $10^{-5}$, the root is reported uncertified, the step fails with that code, and no run changed the tolerance.
- **Atomic acceptance or rejection.** The precision contract requires that "the step is accepted atomically or rejected atomically", and lists `numeric_precision_limit_exhausted` among its failure codes, which is the code on the root row. Observed: the fixture's own checks report `atomic_fail_closed: true`, the diagnostic mode reports `rejection_atomic: true`, and the ten accepted steps remain the published state.
- **Inherited radii.** The error-budget ledger describes an accepted step by inherited position and velocity radii plus step, acceleration and correction allowances, and states that the root-time ceiling "is not added to a state allowance". Its presets are Borg's, with a root-time ceiling of $10^{-3}$; this fixture is not a Borg preset and declares its own $10^{-5}$. The ledger is cited here only for the structure of a published radius; whether this fixture's steps satisfy the ledger's inequalities was not checked.
- **Binding.** `field_speed = 1`, sharp chart; the reported cross-root factors are those of a simple chart, with no own-path root. None of the binding's singular-event rows is in play in the observed rejection.
- **Independent oracle.** That contract's oracle "reports `reference` evidence" and says of its own step doubling that it "is not asserted to be an analytic proof of the exact delayed-system solution". The comparison in Section 5 uses a different, separately authored stationary-mirror oracle record, read only, at one checkpoint. It is numerical reference evidence.
- **Boundary with the v2 transport and the providers.** The fixture's request sets no equation selection, so by the solver's README it keeps the legacy canonical semantics, and it supplies typed joint histories through the existing route. The v2 contract says its new entry points "reject typed joint preparation" and that "the existing v7 owner retains its supported joint route"; this fixture does not use the v2 transport, a speed-domain selector or the surface composition. Consistently, the committed and live sources give byte-identical output for it. No model was selected or changed here.

No contract requirement is removed or weakened by anything here, and reproducing the fixture's outcome is not acceptance of anything.

## 8. Repair options for the owner; none applied

The owner is the App Solver lane together with whoever owns this fixture as a stationary-pair control. Nothing below was edited into any live file.

1. **Make the fixture test the property, not the place.** Assert that every accepted replay step has both cross roots certified within the declared tolerance; that the first uncertified step fails closed, atomically, with the root-time budget diagnostic and unchanged histories; that at a declared checkpoint the oracle's position lies inside the published enclosure; and that the certified reach is at least a declared bound. Report the frontier's location without requiring equality. This removes the two-sided pin. It also removes the fixture's present exact-reach regression protection, so the required reach is a number the owner has to choose; it cannot be described as keeping every existing protection. **Recommended**, because the present pin will break again at the next change of step sequence.
2. **Re-pin to today's behaviour.** Change the three time tokens to the frontier at $1.29$ and its neighbours. Smallest edit, but it records without comment that certified reach has fallen from $1.395$ to $1.29$, and it keeps the pin.
