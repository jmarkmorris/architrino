## Accepted Umbrella Name

**Cosmic Civics** is the accepted umbrella name.

It is intentionally broader than a legal map, a space-policy proposal, or a physics chapter. It holds a family of questions about how activity and assertions are mapped, compared, contested, and reasoned about across domains and scales.

## Original Impetus

Begin with a GIS-like stack of thematic maps for:

- rights;
- jurisdiction;
- proximity;
- liability;
- use;
- access;
- exclusion.

Apply the question first to ordinary land, water, and airspace. Ask how civilization maps human activity and asserted rights over nature, what each layer records, which source produced it, and where layers conflict.

Then expand cautiously toward outer space and larger scales:

- moving vehicles, habitats, observers, and regions;
- operational locations that are relative to a declared chart, observer, instrument, or mission purpose;
- time-dependent corridors, zones, and proximity relations;
- hypothetical future claims over use, access, exclusion, or passage;
- hypothetical passage fees or tolls asserted for transit through a defined region.

The expanded questions are exploratory. No bullet asserts that such a claim is legally recognized, physically realizable, administratively possible, enforceable, or authorized.

## Why A Registry And An Atlas Are Different

Today, a geospatial system, usually implemented with GIS, can organize Earth-referenced features and layers. The Cosmic Civics idea becomes more demanding when a route, region, claimant, jurisdictional assertion, or operational location changes with time, scale, domain, or declared reference frame.

> Current GIS maps geography; Cosmic Civics would require a reference-frame-aware claims registry and a multiscale claims atlas.

This is proposed future framing, not an implementation statement. No actual global or cosmic claims registry is asserted to exist.

The distinction matters because a record and a view have different jobs:

- A **claims registry** would preserve the designated source record for an assertion: claimant, asserted object, region or route, reference frame, duration, jurisdiction, evidence or provenance, conditions, and status.
- A **claims atlas** would present human-facing map layers for passage, jurisdiction, access, exclusion, liability, and use across relevant scales.

Calling the registry `authoritative` would mean only that it is the proposed system of record for what was asserted. It would not mean that the claimant has legal authority, that the assertion is recognized, or that the registry creates a right. The atlas would be a projection of selected records and context; its colors, boundaries, labels, overlays, and route intersections could not validate or enforce the claims they display.

One registry entry might support many atlas views: a land parcel view, a water-route view, an altitude-and-time airspace view, or an outer-space operational-chart view. Conversely, one atlas region might display several conflicting registry entries. Keeping the two components separate would make conflict, provenance, scale, frame, and status visible without letting display order silently become legal priority.

Questions to retain:

- What makes a registry entry complete enough to compare without implying that it is valid?
- How should one assertion retain multiple coordinate descriptions without selecting a universal legal frame?
- Which status labels describe records neutrally, and which would require a named legal authority?
- How should an atlas show conflicts, uncertainty, expired durations, changing frames, and unresolved jurisdiction without ranking claims by graphical prominence?

## Working Chain

> Absolute Timespace → Assembly Architecture → Passage → Claim

Possible reading of the chain:

1. **Absolute Timespace:** use the established formal background only at its canonical scope.
2. **Assembly Architecture:** ask what physical, organizational, observational, or institutional structures produce the relevant movement and records. This is a proposed bridge label, not a new canonical tier.
3. **Passage:** describe movement or occupancy through a declared region and interval without presuming permission or violation.
4. **Claim:** attach a source-bound assertion without presuming validity, recognition, priority, or enforceability.

The chain could fail as a useful organizing method if it encourages a false derivation from physical description to civic or legal entitlement. Any later formalization should therefore preserve an explicit break between `Passage` and `Claim`.

## Candidate Map Stack

A future conceptual map or prototype might keep separate layers for:

| Layer | Candidate record | Central question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Physical domain | Land, water, air, outer-space region, route, or volume | What region or movement is being described? |
| Time | Instant, interval, recurrence, prediction window, or historical record | When does the description apply? |
| Operational location | Position or region relative to a declared chart, instrument, observer, or mission frame | Relative to what, at what scale, and with what uncertainty? |
| Passage | Source-bound route, occupancy, crossing, or proximity event | What movement or presence is recorded or proposed? |
| Use | Activity, resource interaction, observation, communication, residence, transit, or service | What is happening or proposed? |
| Rights or access | Asserted permission, entitlement, license, custom, agreement, or restriction | Who asserts it, from what source, and in which domain? |
| Jurisdiction | Asserted institution, rule source, venue, or governing relation | Which authority is claimed, and who contests it? |
| Exclusion | Boundary, safety zone, privacy zone, access condition, or asserted prohibition | What is excluded, by whom, and with what claimed basis? |
| Proximity and risk | Distance, encounter window, collision envelope, environmental effect, or interference | What operational relation matters and why? |
| Liability | Alleged duty, causation, damage, exposure, defense, or remedy | Which facts and governing sources would be required before any conclusion? |
| Claim state | Asserted, disputed, withdrawn, adjudicated, hypothetical, or unknown | What is the record status rather than the truth of the claim? |

This is a candidate analytic stack, not a database schema, legal ontology, app requirement, or governance design.

## Ordinary-Domain Bridge

The ordinary bridge should remain visible in every expansion:

- A land map can separate physical occupation from title, permission, zoning, access, and liability questions.
- A water map can ask how routes, moving conditions, resources, boundaries, and multiple authorities interact.
- An airspace map can make altitude, timing, path, proximity, and safety visible instead of treating territory as a flat parcel.
- An outer-space map can reuse the discipline of separate layers while leaving law, authority, moving frames, and enforceability open.

These statements identify mapping questions. They are not legal summaries and should not be used to decide a real dispute.

## Illustrative Hypothetical Families

### Passage Through A Declared Region

A hypothetical future organization publishes a time-dependent region and asserts that transit through it creates a fee or toll. A Cosmic Civics record might display the region, source, interval, route intersection, notice record, objection, and status. It must also display that the authority and validity are hypothetical or disputed. The visualization cannot create the fee.

### Conflicting Moving Exclusion Zones

Two moving systems publish exclusion or safety regions using different operational charts. The conceptual problem is to reconcile time, coordinates, uncertainty, and source identity before comparing the regions. Even a perfect geometric comparison would not decide which restriction is lawful or ethically justified.

### Cross-Domain Journey

A single journey crosses land, water, airspace, and an outer-space segment. The map stack could show where domain, source, terminology, and asserted authority change. The stack must not imply that one continuous route produces one continuous rights regime.

### Proximity Without Ownership

A system repeatedly passes near a resource, habitat, route, or observation target. Proximity and repeated use may be mapped as facts or allegations. They do not automatically create ownership, priority, exclusion, liability, or a toll right.

## Candidate Passage Record

A possible future discussion object is a frame-explicit passage record with fields such as:

- subject or actor identifier as declared by the source;
- object or assembly description;
- domain and region definition;
- coordinate chart or operational frame provenance;
- time variable and interval, with substrate and observer levels kept distinct;
- path, occupancy, crossing, or proximity relation;
- spatial and temporal uncertainty;
- source, evidence grade, and reconstruction method;
- observed, reconstructed, predicted, proposed, or hypothetical status;
- linked assertions and objections, each with its own source and authority label.

This is a conceptual candidate only. It neither selects a physical frame nor defines legal passage.

## Candidate Claim Record

A separate claim record could ask for:

- claimant or source;
- subject, activity, passage, or region referenced;
- type of assertion, such as access, use, exclusion, jurisdiction, liability, ownership, duty, fee, or toll;
- asserted basis and domain;
- applicable time and geographic or operational scope;
- evidence, notice, objection, conflict, adjudication, and outcome fields where relevant;
- status label distinguishing hypothetical, asserted, disputed, recognized by a named authority, rejected, withdrawn, or unknown.

No status label should be inferred from geometry alone. Even `recognized by a named authority` would record that authority's act, not certify universal validity.

## Open Legal And Philosophical Questions

- What distinguishes occupancy, passage, use, access, control, exclusion, stewardship, ownership, and jurisdiction?
- Can repeated passage or use ever ground a claim, and if so under which legal or moral theory?
- What could justify a passage fee or toll, and what would distinguish service, stewardship, congestion management, coercion, or enclosure?
- How should overlapping claims be represented without silently ranking authorities?
- Which features of land, water, and air law are domain-specific and should not be universalized?
- What does `public`, `commons`, `open access`, or `shared use` mean when regions and participants move?
- Can a civic framework remain plural about law and ethics while maintaining one clear record format?

These questions are not answers and do not imply that any listed regime should exist.

## Open Mapping And Physics-Boundary Questions

- Which operational location records require observer-level coordinates rather than the canonical absolute-time/Euclidean-void variables?
- How should maps expose chart changes, uncertainty, prediction horizons, and delayed observations?
- Can the chain remain useful without making `Assembly Architecture` a new canonical term?
- Where must the map stop at physical description before legal, moral, or institutional reasoning begins?
- What counterexample would show that `Passage` is too broad to support a coherent cross-domain comparison?
