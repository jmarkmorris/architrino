## Current

`Cosmic Civics` is the accepted umbrella name for an exploratory priority area connecting established $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ ontology language to questions about movement, mapped human activity, and asserted claims. Its working chain is:

> Absolute Timespace → Assembly Architecture → Passage → Claim

Only `absolute timespace` enters that chain as established corpus terminology, at its canonical scope as the formal product of absolute time and the Euclidean void. `Assembly Architecture`, `Passage`, `Claim`, and the chain as a whole are priority-stage conceptual framing until their meanings and boundaries are separately accepted.

## Objective

Preserve and discipline a GIS-like, multi-layer inquiry into how civilizations describe activity, rights, jurisdiction, proximity, liability, use, access, and exclusion across land, water, airspace, and outer space, then ask how those mapping questions change for moving systems, relative operational locations, and hypothetical future passage claims.

The packet keeps those questions visible without treating a map as proof of a right, a proposed claim as legally valid, a hypothetical toll as accepted, or an operational coordinate choice as a new reference-frame-selection law.

## Scope Boundary

Cosmic Civics is:

- a priority-only exploratory umbrella;
- a bridge from ordinary land, water, and air questions toward broader spatial and movement questions;
- a place to separate established corpus language, proposed conceptual framing, and open legal or philosophical questions;
- a staging area for illustrative hypotheticals whose non-real, non-authoritative status is explicit.

Cosmic Civics is not:

- a legal opinion, rights regime, jurisdictional authority, policy proposal, or implementation plan;
- a claim that ownership, exclusion, liability, access, passage fees, or tolls extend into any new domain;
- a claim that a future institution, map, coordinate system, or observer can create or validate such rights;
- a new theory layer, physical law, reference-frame-selection rule, or change in $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ status.
