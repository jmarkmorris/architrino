## Hinge Diagnostics

Hinge analysis is diagnostic-only. For continuous search coordinates $\lambda_k$ and objective components $G_j$, estimate the local sensitivity matrix

$$
J_{jk}
=
\frac{\partial G_j}{\partial\lambda_k}
$$

with coordinate-scaled finite differences and a separately declared refinement ladder. Report the full matrix, singular values, coordinate scaling, and uncertainty. Do not collapse it to one weighted hinge score.

A coordinate may be reported as a candidate hinge only when:

- its influence is uniquely separated from the other coordinates beyond uncertainty;
- the index remains the same under refinement, held-out samples, and an admissible reparameterization;
- the conclusion survives removal of tuned objective weights; and
- an independently authored full adjudication reproduces the local ordering.

Failure of any condition removes the hinge label. No hinge diagnostic implies a binding mechanism, stability, quantization, or an energy channel.

## Finite-Width Basin Tests

Here `basin` means only a finite-width pass neighborhood in the declared prescribed-coordinate measure. It is not a dynamical basin of attraction.

For a candidate coordinate vector $\lambda_\star$, predeclare a positive-width neighborhood

$$
\mathcal N_\epsilon(\lambda_\star)
=
\left\{
\lambda:
\left|
\lambda_k-\lambda_{\star,k}
\right|
\le
\epsilon_k
\text{ for every opened coordinate }k
\right\}
$$

with every $\epsilon_k>0$. A finite-width basin test requires:

1. a space-filling sample inside $\mathcal N_\epsilon$;
2. boundary and corner samples;
3. at least one independently seeded held-out sample;
4. primary/refined numerical agreement;
5. a second admissible sampling measure or coordinate parameterization; and
6. the exact matched controls appropriate to the current phase.

One passing point is never a finite-width result. A negative conclusion closes only the sampled bounded domain, measure, tolerances, and coordinate strata. It does not close Family B, Family C, or an unsampled pairing sector.

## Independent Full-Adjudication Triggers

Compact diagnostic rows may trigger, but never substitute for, full adjudication. Trigger a frozen full-adjudication packet only when all of the following hold:

1. every hard diagnostic is evaluated and no acceptance-bearing event is unresolved;
2. the candidate passes a finite-width basin test under held-out seeds and refinement;
3. it is Pareto-nondominated or shows a declared matched-control benefit without an offsetting objective degradation;
4. its source, protocol, coordinate measure, software identity, and rerun command are frozen and hashed;
5. its exact phase-specific null and adverse controls are frozen in the same adjudication set; and
6. any hinge claim is explicitly marked diagnostic pending independent reproduction.

Full adjudication then requires:

- a complete result packet with raw causal-root and reduction ledgers;
- independent acceptance performed by a separately authored instrument;
- an independent causal-root or analytically known reference on every newly exercised root class;
- exact source/protocol identity checks;
- primary/refined and held-out re-evaluation; and
- a final report that preserves the vector objectives and all failed gates.

The independent instrument must not be changed in the same change as the evaluator or mathematical rule it checks. If both implementations must change, their agreement is implementation parity rather than independent evidence and the rule needs a separate derivation or oracle.

Any full-adjudication failure returns the candidate to diagnostic status. It does not license threshold relaxation, selective gate removal, or a claim about stability or physical realization.

## Phase Falsifiers

- **B1 embedding:** stop if $(\beta_1,\beta_2)=(0,0)$ does not reproduce the exact B1 source relations and quotient invariances.
- **Midpoint extension:** close only the sampled $(\beta_1,\beta_2)$ domain if no finite-width region survives held-out seeds and refinement.
- **Radius envelope:** reject a claimed central-envelope advantage if an equal-radius or reflection-broken matched control dominates it.
- **Phase or frequency organization:** reject if the pattern depends on global phase, persistent-index relabeling, incomplete return periods, or one sampling grid.
- **Pairing effect:** reject any semantic-only pairing difference; reject a geometry-active effect that disappears under its matched source reconstruction.
- **Family-C composition:** reject a claimed two-component advantage if it is absent against matched C1/C2, C3/C4, C5/C6, equal-radius, and circulation controls.
- **Accessory benefit:** reject if a matched Accessory Configuration improves only exterior objectives while degrading an internal or hard-gate row.
- **Hinge structure:** remove the label if the coordinate index is nonunique, refinement-dependent, reparameterization-dependent, or absent under independent full adjudication.
