**Date:** 2026-09-02 **Status:** operator selected Option A; exact-mirror continuation retained as a multivalued relation at current FSC authority; no selector or new law adopted. **Owner:** FSC-006b/FSC-005. **Decision:** Option A.

## Decision in one sentence

The operator selected Option A: the proposed exact-mirror model retains the straight trace and one delayed-braking branch for every prescribed positive onset as a multivalued continuation relation, because no admitted state functional derives one onset and no new selection law is authorized.

Plainly: the mathematics can describe every allowed future and can rule out pathological flickering at the event. It still has no alarm clock that chooses one future. Option A records that fact; Option B would add the missing clock as a new law.

## 2. Option A — retain a multivalued relation

### Prediction

For one complete quotient state $[\mathfrak h_E]$ at the isolated exact-mirror event, the proposed continuation is the set

$$
\mathcal F([\mathfrak h_E])
\supset
\left\{
\text{straight trace}
\right\}
\cup
\left\{
\text{delayed-braking trace with onset }u_*:u_*>0
\right\}.
$$

For $K\ge7u_*/2$, a prescribed delayed onset continues through a complete returning lobe. Repeating the same prescribed onset gives a genuine two-cycle of the quotient event map. The relation assigns neither a probability nor a preferred member.

Plainly: Option A predicts a set of permitted motions. It says exactly what happens after any supplied onset, but it does not pretend the present theory predicts which onset occurs.

### New law added

None. Option A retains the proposed constrained response, swept-source disposition, exact-mirror event update, ordinary ledger, and current solution class at their existing proposed authority. It records nonuniqueness as an unresolved foundational boundary.

### What remains unproved

Option A does not supply autonomous determinism, a stochastic transition kernel, physical realization, probability weights, a selected breather, stability, conservation, capture, general event semantics, or canonical adoption. The quotient cycle remains controlled by an externally prescribed onset.

### Falsifier

Option A's current-necessity rationale is falsified by an already admitted, quotient-compatible FSC equation that maps the live exact-mirror state to one continuation without new foundational data, or by a failure of either the straight trace or the proved positive-waiting branches.

Plainly: if the missing alarm is already present in the written model and has been overlooked, Option A would no longer be necessary. No such functional was found in the complete wake, event, response, or conditional action interfaces.

## 3. Option B — add an explicit selection law

Any Option B proposal must name a functional on the declared state rather than state only a desired outcome. Before consideration for adoption it must supply: its precise domain and formula; time-translation, reflection, polarity-label, and scale behavior; its event guard and reset; its received-clock, wake, event-family, and ownership accounting; existence of the selected continuation; quotient compatibility or an explicit enlargement of the live state; an operator-checkable falsifier; and evidence that the rule is derived from admitted state data rather than fitted to a waiting time or desired breather.

Plainly: “choose one” is not a law. The selector must say what information it reads, how it transforms under the symmetries, when it fires, what records it creates, and how it can be proved wrong.

### B1. Straight-passage selector

The narrow candidate already identified is a no-bootstrap admission clause: a margin-zero inactive channel may activate only after the channel-omitted admitted ledger independently creates a strict receiver-side crossing. In the isolated zero-ledger mirror chart this selects straight passage.

- **Prediction:** straight separation in the isolated chart; later activation remains possible if another admitted wake first creates the crossing.
- **New law:** a first-admission rule for margin-zero inactive channels.
- **Still unproved:** a general well-posed channel-omitted evolution and compatibility across many-channel simultaneous guards.
- **Falsifier:** a derived weaker rule selecting the same trace, or a chart in its declared domain where the clause suppresses an independently produced ordinary crossing.

Plainly: this choice adds no waiting-time scale, but it still changes the model. It forbids a channel from using the acceleration that would exist only after that same channel had already activated.

### B2. Positive-onset selector

A positive selector must be a quotient-state functional

$$
u_*=\tau([\mathfrak h_E])>0.
$$

If it uses only the current coupling and cap duration, scale covariance permits

$$
u_*=K\Phi(L/K),
$$

but supplies no equation for the dimensionless function $\Phi$. A repeated autonomous cycle additionally requires a positive solution of

$$
L=G(K,u),
\qquad
u=K\Phi(L/K).
$$

The direct choice $u=L$ has no positive fixed cycle in the proved regime $K/u\ge6$.

Plainly: units and symmetry narrow the shape of the alarm rule but do not choose it. A periodic alarm must also agree with the cap length produced by the motion it triggers; the simplest reuse-the-last-cap proposal fails that self-consistency test.

- **Prediction:** one delayed-braking branch and, only if the reset equation has a positive solution, a possible autonomous quotient cycle.
- **New law:** a deterministic maturity functional plus reset semantics.
- **Still unproved:** any admitted wake or action derivation of $\Phi$, positive fixed onset, stability, or physical realization.
- **Falsifier:** failure of the functional to select an existing branch, failure of its reset equation, dependence on a retired record without state enlargement, or loss of its declared symmetry and scale behavior.

### B3. Approximation-limit selector

This option must declare one physical approximation family, its parameter, event and ownership semantics at every parameter value, a topology of convergence, and a theorem that every admissible approximation sequence has the same limit. Current diagnostics point both to straight limits and to arbitrarily selected positive-onset limits under different smooth repairs.

- **Prediction:** whatever unique limit the declared approximation theorem proves.
- **New law:** the approximation family and the instruction that its limit defines the sharp event.
- **Still unproved:** parameterization independence, uniqueness of the limit, and derivation of the approximation from FSC primitives.
- **Falsifier:** two admitted approximation sequences with different continuation limits.

Plainly: “take a smooth limit” is not specific enough. The family being smoothed is part of the law, and two plausible smoothing families already lead to different futures.

### B4. Future wake/action-derived selector

This is the only route that could turn a positive onset into a derived FSC result rather than a convention. It would need an independently evolving wake or action account whose state changes on the zero-ledger plateau, reaches a declared maturity guard, transfers or preserves all owned records consistently, and yields a positive onset and reset fixed point without a fitted scale.

- **Prediction:** one onset determined by the new wake/action state.
- **New law:** the derived account's emission, propagation, maturity, reception, event, reset, and ownership evolution.
- **Still unproved:** the account itself, reduction to the existing ordinary row, quotient compatibility, positive fixed point, and independent evidence.
- **Falsifier:** no positive maturity crossing, disagreement with the regular ordinary row, lost or duplicated wake ownership, regulator dependence, or a selector that merely encodes a desired waiting time.

Plainly: this route could eventually provide a real alarm clock, but no such clock has been derived. Naming a future action or wake balance does not supply its equations.
