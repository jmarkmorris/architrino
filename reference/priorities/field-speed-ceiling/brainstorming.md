# Field-Speed Ceiling Brainstorming

## Proposal under examination

Treat $c_f$ as both causal-wake propagation speed and a primitive upper speed
for every architrino. A history can reach the boundary but has no admissible
super-field-speed segment.

This is a proposal, not a derived consequence of the current canonical law.

## Excess-wake transverse-redirection candidate

The minimal cap simply removes a forward speed-increasing contribution at the
field-speed boundary. A stronger candidate redirects the otherwise ineffective
part into transverse acceleration. In a planar geometry with a constant
redirected magnitude and one fixed turning center, the resulting cap-state path
is circular with radius $R=c_f^2/a_{\mathrm{turn}}$.

Open requirement: the causal-wake geometry must select the transverse direction
and magnitude. A speed ceiling alone cannot choose a left/right turning sense
or a circle center. This candidate is therefore a geometry target, not an
adopted cap law.

## Capped Braid self-reinforcement question

Can a candidate Braid have a self-consistent capped architecture in which every
constituent remains at $\|\mathbf V\|=c_f$ while the mutual causal-wake ledger
contains more speed-increasing wake than the ceiling permits? Under the
transverse-redirection candidate, the otherwise ineffective excess would have
to supply the geometrically selected turning components that maintain the
common Braid geometry.

The question is not whether a single constituent can be held at field speed.
It is whether one complete persistent-label Braid ledger can simultaneously:

- keep every constituent on the field-speed sphere;
- select compatible transverse directions and magnitudes for all labels;
- close the chosen radii, phases, and any common-radius or unequal-radius
  arrangement; and
- remain finite and provenance-complete without a special per-constituent
  rule.

The transverse response controls turning geometry; the speed ceiling controls
speed. Any self-reinforcing capped Braid remains a candidate to calculate, not
an established retained branch, stability result, angular-momentum result, or
physical realization.

### Fixed-speed common-radius family

For a planar constituent held at field speed on a circular path of radius $R$,
the required transverse acceleration is

$$
a_\perp=\frac{c_f^2}{R}.
$$

Thus a common-radius capped Braid can, kinematically, move to a lower overall
radius while staying at field speed only if its mutual wake geometry supplies
the larger compatible transverse response. The possible degree of freedom is a
family of radius-and-phase configurations satisfying the complete capped
ledger, not an arbitrary radius knob.

If the eventual redirection law has a maximum transverse response
$a_{\mathrm{turn,max}}$, the family has the geometric lower bound

$$
R\ge\frac{c_f^2}{a_{\mathrm{turn,max}}}.
$$

Whether the wake supplies a growing transverse response as the common radius
shrinks, whether it instead loses roots or changes phase topology, and whether
unequal-radius branches connect to this family are central calculation
questions. No radius family, minimum radius, or Braid contraction is yet
established.

### Capped-Braid (H)-transition hypothesis

Possible mechanism: an incoming wake contribution that would otherwise
increase a cap-state constituent's speed is redirected into the transverse
geometry needed for a smaller common Braid radius. A sustained compatible
input could therefore drive a collective contraction while every constituent
remains at field speed.

The candidate quantity denoted $H$ would not be assumed as an imported unit.
It would have to be derived as the complete causal-ledger transfer required to
move one globally phase-closed Braid geometry to its next admissible geometry.
The proposed collective acceptance condition is that the relevant binaries
reach the next radius/phase closure together, rather than leaving a permanent
partially shifted configuration.

The cap alone does not make this transfer discrete. If common-radius Braid
solutions form a continuous family, arbitrarily small compatible input could
produce arbitrarily small radius change. A genuine $H$-like step requires a
separate derived closure, topology, phase-lock, or root-ledger condition that
selects discrete neighboring configurations and gives their transfer measure.

This is a high-value mechanism hypothesis only. It does not establish
quantization, an angular-momentum law, a Braid transition, or a physical
realization.

### Planck-frequency / minimum-radius endpoint question

The endpoint question is whether a capped Braid has a smallest admissible
radius, whether that appears equivalently as a largest internal frequency, and
whether the resulting frequency can recover the observer-level Planck-frequency
comparison target. The Planck scale is not an input to this primitive model;
it is an external recovery target that a derived endpoint may or may not meet.

On a circular fixed-speed chart,

$$
\omega=\frac{c_f}{R}.
$$

Thus, on that chart only, a smallest admissible radius $R_{\min}$ and a
largest admissible frequency $\omega_{\max}=c_f/R_{\min}$ are the same bound
expressed in two coordinates. The mathematical question is not which label is
more fundamental, but what causal-root, phase-closure, transverse-response, or
ledger condition prevents $R$ from decreasing further.

Required derivation questions:

- Does the cap-state transverse response reach a finite maximum, forcing a
  lower radius through $R\ge c_f^2/a_{\mathrm{turn,max}}$?
- Does root topology, phase closure, or retained-history geometry terminate a
  common-radius branch first?
- Is the terminal condition one continuous endpoint, a discrete final closure,
  or a transition to a different geometry?
- If a terminal frequency is derived, does it match the Planck-frequency
  recovery target without fitting a scale or importing a Planck rule?

No minimum radius, maximum frequency, Planck endpoint, or smallest possible
Braid has been established.

#### Candidate terminal mechanisms from the capped geometry

At fixed field speed, continued contraction requires

$$
a_\perp(R)=\frac{c_f^2}{R}\longrightarrow\infty
\qquad\text{as }R\longrightarrow0.
$$

Therefore a smallest radius can arise only when the full capped geometry can
no longer provide the required compatible transverse response. The following
are distinct candidate reasons, all to be derived or falsified from the same
retained causal ledger:

1. **Transverse-response ceiling.** The cap-response map may have a finite
   largest redirected transverse contribution. Then the required curvature
   reaches that ceiling at $R=c_f^2/a_{\mathrm{turn,max}}$ and no smaller
   fixed-speed circle is available.
2. **Root-geometry loss.** As radius and frequency change, an active partner
   or self root can become non-transverse, merge, leave retained history, or
   create a new root family. The ledger may then fail to supply the signed
   transverse directions needed for the next smaller configuration.
3. **Phase-closure exhaustion.** A common-radius Braid may require a precise
   phase relation for all constituent turning directions to close. At some
   radius there may be no neighboring phase assignment that keeps every label
   at field speed and preserves the same persistent topology.
4. **Loss of a turning direction.** The redirected wake must select a
   transverse normal from actual geometry. A symmetric configuration can reach
   a point at which only a forward excess remains, with no distinguished normal
   direction. The contraction route then has no geometrically selected next
   bend.
5. **Collective transition obstruction.** A radius change may demand that all
   linked binaries change phase/radius together. If no finite causal-ledger
   transfer reaches the next globally closed state, the existing state is the
   terminal one for that topology.

These mechanisms are not interchangeable. A transverse-response ceiling gives
a continuous geometric endpoint; phase/root/topology mechanisms can instead
give discrete permitted radii and a candidate $H$-like transition measure. A
complete derivation must identify which one occurs first, if any, without
inserting a Planck-scale cutoff.

#### Equal-radius phase-closure (phase-lock) mechanism

The operator's self-reinforcing “phase-lock” idea can be expressed using no new
interaction: it is a complete vector closure of all causal-wake contributions
on every persistent Braid label. For an equal-radius planar candidate at field
speed, each label must simultaneously have:

$$
A_{\perp,\mathrm{net}}(R,\Phi)=\frac{c_f^2}{R},
\qquad
A_{\parallel,\mathrm{net}}(R,\Phi)=0,
\qquad
A_{z,\mathrm{net}}(R,\Phi)=0,
$$

where $\Phi$ denotes the complete set of phase offsets and retained root
incidences. The first condition provides the required turning; the second
prevents phase drift or speed change; the third preserves the chosen plane.
The same equations must hold for every label under the Braid symmetry, with no
unowned root or exceptional constituent.

For a fixed dimensionless root topology and phase pattern, inverse-square
dilution gives the schematic scale form

$$
A_{\perp,\mathrm{net}}
=
\frac{\kappa |q|^2}{R^2}\,C_\perp(\Phi),
$$

so the fixed-speed turning condition has the corresponding radius balance

$$
R
=
\frac{\kappa |q|^2}{c_f^2}\,C_\perp(\Phi).
$$

This is a scale-analysis template, not a derived Braid formula: actual
multiple roots, transmitter factors, polarity signs, and any cap-state
redirection must be included in $C_\perp$.

The likely native way a lock terminates is therefore not a new cutoff. As $R$
changes, either the delayed-root/phase equations continue to admit a compatible
$\Phi$, or they do not. Failure can occur because a root changes topology, a
required tangential cancellation changes sign, phase advances cannot close
around the persistent Braid, or no common phase pattern supplies every label's
required turning simultaneously. Then no smaller equal-radius phase-closed
geometry exists on that ledger.

The proposed “outside influence below $H/2$” statement is a later basin claim:
it would mean sub-threshold perturbations leave the system in the same
phase-closed configuration, while a complete derived transfer reaches the
next one. It cannot be asserted until the transfer measure called $H$ and the
return/escape ledger are both derived.

#### Circular chord geometry test

The operator's chord picture supplies a concrete first geometry test. For one
uniform circular path of radius $R$ at field speed, let $\theta$ be the angular
advance between emission and reception. The emitter-to-receiver chord is

$$
r(\theta)=2R\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right),
$$

while the causal wake has travelled

$$
c_f\Delta=R\theta.
$$

The same-path causal-root condition is therefore

$$
\theta=2\sin\left(\frac{\theta}{2}\right).
$$

For positive $\theta$, the right side is strictly smaller than the left; only
$\theta=0$ solves the equation. Thus a uniform field-speed circle has no
nonzero ordinary same-path chord crossing. At the diameter angle
$\theta=\pi$, the wake radius is $\pi R$ while the chord is only $2R$, so the
diameter itself is not the root condition.

For a partner on the same circle with phase offset $\phi$, the corresponding
dimensionless chord condition has the form

$$
\theta=2\left|\sin\left(\frac{\theta+\phi}{2}\right)\right|,
$$

before polarity, transmitter factor, and the full Braid geometry are applied.
This equation can create, remove, or make tangent partner-root patterns as
phase offsets change. Because $R$ cancels in this ideal fixed-speed circular
geometry, chord topology by itself selects phase/root structure rather than a
minimum radius. It must combine with inverse-square amplitude balance and the
all-label phase-closure equations to select a radius or terminal basin.

## Inverse-square co-moving wake observation

For an ordinary causal-root contribution, radial dilution is proportional to
$1/r^2$. If a proposed capped model supplies a genuine, positive lower
effective wake radius $r_0$, the radial tail is finite:

$$
\int_{r_0}^{\infty}\frac{dr}{r^2}=\frac{1}{r_0}.
$$

Examples: a lower radius $r_0=1$ gives total tail weight $1$; $r_0=0.1$ gives
$10$. This is a geometric observation only. Exact co-moving field-speed
history in the current sharp law does not yet supply $r_0$ and additionally
has a zero transmitter-side root Jacobian, so the calculation does not by
itself define the plateau contribution. A future ceiling model could test
whether a causally derived maturity radius or boundary update supplies such a
lower limit without deleting ordinary roots.

## Guarded compact-object question

Could a completed ceiling model admit a collective terminal contact or
phase-closed compact configuration: a state in which further inward approach
reaches a cap-state boundary rather than continuing to smaller internal
separation? In informal terms, this is the proposed "hard clink" picture.

The hypothesis is that an already maximally phase-closed interior cannot
contract further; new incoming structure would instead have to join an outer
boundary layer. This is a question for later calculation, not an implication
of the present two-architrino result and not an identification with a
supermassive black hole. It would require, at minimum: a finite collective
geometry; a complete incoming causal-root ledger; a defined cap-state
reception and transfer rule; an outgoing retained history; and a separately
derived external observable signature. The speed ceiling and isolated
co-moving self-contact rule alone establish none of those items.
