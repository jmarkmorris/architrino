## 1. What the instrument is

[darwin-overnight-pair-instrument.mjs](darwin-overnight-pair-instrument.mjs) is a self-contained Node module and command line that integrates $N$ members under the frozen functional of the common brief (manuscript Section 10, exactly as boxed), with $K=1$ and $c_f=1$ as named constants that are not run parameters. At every derivative evaluation it assembles the $3N\times3N$ velocity Hessian $H(X)$ and the right-hand side $G(X,V)$ from the formulas below, solves $HA=G$ by dense partial-pivot LU, records $\det H$ (from the LU) and the exact 2-norm condition number (from a Jacobi eigen-solve of the symmetric $H$; for $N=2$ it is also checked against the closed form), and ends the history with an obstruction event when $|\det H|$ falls below `detTol` or $\operatorname{cond}_2H$ exceeds `condMax`. It never regularizes, pseudo-inverts or steps across a singular $H$. Opposite and same polarity are labelled per pair in every record.

## 2. Equations in brief

With $\mathbf d_{ij}=\mathbf X_i-\mathbf X_j$, $r_{ij}=\|\mathbf d_{ij}\|$, $\mathbf e_{ij}=\mathbf d_{ij}/r_{ij}$, $\mathbf w_{ij}=\mathbf V_i-\mathbf V_j$, $\sigma_{ij}=q_iq_j$, $a_{ij}=\sigma_{ij}K/(2c_f^2r_{ij})$ and $M_{ij}=I+\mathbf e_{ij}\mathbf e_{ij}^{\mathsf T}$, the functional is $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}=\tfrac12\sum_i\|\mathbf V_i\|^2-\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}K/r_{ij}+\sum_{i<j}a_{ij}\,\mathbf V_i^{\mathsf T}M_{ij}\mathbf V_j$, since the bracket of Section 10 equals $\mathbf V_i^{\mathsf T}M_{ij}\mathbf V_j$. The generalized momentum is $\mathbf p=HV$ with $H_{ii}=I_3$ and $H_{ij}=a_{ij}M_{ij}$, which depends on positions only. Differentiating $\mathbf p_i$ in time gives $\sum_jH_{ij}\mathbf A_j+\mathbf C_i$ with the mixed term $\mathbf C_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\big[(\mathbf w_{ij}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbf X_i})(a_{ij}M_{ij})\big]\mathbf V_j$, so the Euler–Lagrange equations read $HA=G$ with $G_i=\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}/\partial\mathbf X_i-\mathbf C_i$. The explicit pair pieces ($\dot a$, $\dot{\mathbf e}$, $\dot M\mathbf v$, and the gradient $\mathbf F_{ij}$ of the pair terms) are written in the file header. Because $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$ is quadratic in the velocities, the energy-like function is $E=\mathbf p\cdot V-\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}=\tfrac12V^{\mathsf T}HV+\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}K/r_{ij}$; translation and rotation invariance of $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$ give $\mathbf P=\sum_i\mathbf p_i$ and $\mathbf J=\sum_i\mathbf X_i\times\mathbf p_i$. For $N=2$ the eigenvalues of $H$ are $\{1\pm2a,1\pm a,1\pm a\}$, so $\det H=(1-4a^2)(1-a^2)^2$ and $\operatorname{cond}_2H=\max|\lambda|/\min|\lambda|$; with $K=c_f=1$ this vanishes at $r=1$ for both polarities (used here only to check the linear algebra and to drive the obstruction controls; the invertibility domain itself belongs to the reduction worker).

The zero-coupling control (velocity-coupling term deleted, inverse-distance term kept) has $H=I$ and, for an opposite-polarity pair, relative motion $\ddot{\mathbf d}=-2K\mathbf e/r^2$, a Kepler problem with $\mu=2K$: the mirror circular orbit at separation $r_0$ has member speed $\sqrt{K/(2r_0)}$ and period $2\pi\sqrt{r_0^3/(2K)}$; radial release from rest reaches $r=xr_0$ at $t=\sqrt{r_0^3/(2\mu)}\,[\sqrt{x(1-x)}+\arccos\sqrt x]$.

## 3. Parameters, events and diagnostics

A case is a JSON preparation: `positions`, `velocities`, `polarities` ($\pm1$), `tMax`, `integrator` (`dp54` adaptive Dormand–Prince 5(4) with `rtol`, `atol`, `hInit`, `hMax`; or `rk4` fixed step with `h`), `outputDt`, event parameters `rContact` (default $10^{-3}$), `rEscape` (default none), `speedBound` (default $0.1$), `epsBound` (default $0.05$, on $K/(c_f^2r)$), obstruction parameters `detTol` (default $10^{-12}$) and `condMax` (default $10^{12}$), bisection time tolerance `tTol` (default $10^{-9}$), and the control-only switches `coupling: 0` (delete the velocity-coupling term) and `interaction: 0` (delete the whole interaction). Both integrators clip steps to the output cadence and to `tMax`, so samples are exact grid states, not interpolants.

Events are detected by sign change of an event function over an accepted step and located by bisection on the sub-step length from the step start to `tTol`; the integration then continues from the accepted step end, so events never alter the step grid. Kinds: `contact` (pair separation below `rContact`; stop), `escape` (above `rEscape`; stop), `turning-point` (sign change of $dr/dT$ for a pair, with direction), `speed-crossing-cf` (member speed through $c_f=1$; recorded, not a stop), `speed-bound` and `eps-bound` (departure from or return to the declared approximation domain; recorded), `obstruction` (stop) and `tMax`. Every event and every sample carries the time, the full state, $E$, $\mathbf P$, $\mathbf J$ and their drifts from the initial values, the running suprema of member speed and of $K/(c_f^2r)$ over pairs, $\det H$, $\operatorname{cond}_2H$ (and the 1-norm Hager estimate as a secondary diagnostic), and the pair separations and $dr/dT$.

Two safeguards govern the singular locus. A sign change of $\det H$ between the step start and any stage evaluation is an obstruction (a singular locus was crossed). A change of $|\det H|$ by more than a factor $2$ within one step is rejected and the adaptive step halved (a fixed-step run that cannot meet this ends with an obstruction event naming the rule). Once a threshold is met, the obstruction is located by creeping sub-steps that halve on failure and whose end states are checked against the thresholds, so the located state lies on the trajectory to within `tTol` rather than at an internal stage of a wide step.

## 5. Defects found and fixed during validation

The first obstruction control stepped across the singular locus at $r=1$: the band $|\det H|<10^{-6}$ is narrower than an accepted step, so no stage evaluation ever landed in it, and the history ran on to contact with $\det H<0$. The determinant sign rule and the factor-2 continuity rule above were added in response. The second defect was in locating the obstruction: a bisection on a single wide sub-step detected the threshold at a low-order internal stage that overshoots the trajectory by about $10^{-5}$ in $r$, placing the event at $r=1.000006$ instead of on the trajectory; the creeping location replaced it, and the located state now sits at the declared threshold ($\operatorname{cond}_2H=9.997\times10^5$ against `condMax` $10^6$). A third change was diagnostic: the Hager 1-norm condition estimate is not continuous in the state (it jumped by a factor $4$ across a $10^{-9}$ step), so the obstruction decision uses the exact 2-norm condition from the eigenvalues of the symmetric $H$, and the 1-norm estimate is recorded only.

## 6. Limits and falsifiers

The instrument establishes agreement with closed forms in the zero-coupling and free cases, internal consistency of the assembly, and the convergence and invariance behaviour above; it establishes nothing about any target preparation. The full-law control (d) is the same code at different settings and is a convergence check, not an independent reference. Turning-point events in an exactly circular preparation are sign changes of a round-off-level $dr/dT$ (control (b) records two or three such events with $r$ constant to $10^{-9}$); a reader should use the recorded $r$ and $dr/dT$ rather than the count. The fixed-step integrator cannot shrink its step near a singular locus and ends with an obstruction event naming the continuity rule; near such a locus only the adaptive integrator gives a located threshold. The sample grid is clipped to the output cadence, which changes the adaptive step sequence (and hence round-off-level results) when the cadence changes. Falsifiers, all checkable in the receipt and run records under `.local-data/master-equation-closure/darwin-overnight/instrument/`: a control whose measured value differs from its closed form by more than the declared tolerance; an RK4 position-difference order outside $[3.5,5.5]$ on control (d); a self-check discrepancy above $10^{-8}$ at the declared steps; a history whose $\det H$ changes sign without an obstruction event; an independent reference (another worker's instrument) disagreeing with this one on a shared preparation beyond both tolerances.
