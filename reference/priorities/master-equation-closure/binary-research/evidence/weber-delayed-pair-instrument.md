# Delayed Weber pair instrument (lane B)

This note documents the coupled-history integrator written for lane B of the [delayed Weber pair investigation](../analysis/weber-delayed-pair-launch-brief.md) under the [frozen preregistration](../analysis/weber-delayed-pair-preregistration.md). The instrument evolves one isolated opposite-polarity pair under the [frozen Section 9a law](../../equation-variants/manuscript.md#9a-selected-delayed-weber-adaptation), unchanged: the Weber bracket on ordinary causal roots with the transmitter weight $c_f/|D_t|$, $\lambda_{\mathrm W}=-1/2$, $\mu_{\mathrm W}=1$, $K=c_f=1$, every positive-delay self root admitted when it exists, the same-time endpoint excluded, no boundary response, no softening, no root exclusion, no ceiling. It is a research comparison instrument in double-precision floating point: not an enclosure, not the EOM solver, and not evidence for any law other than the one it evaluates. The script is [weber-delayed-pair-instrument.mjs](weber-delayed-pair-instrument.mjs) (Node 26, no dependencies); the known-case receipt is [weber-delayed-pair-instrument-controls.json](weber-delayed-pair-instrument-controls.json); the per-run record is [weber-delayed-pair-runs.json](weber-delayed-pair-runs.json); the case files are under `weber-delayed-pair-cases/`; bulky trajectories and summaries are under `.local-data/master-equation-closure/weber-delayed-pair/`. The frozen Section 9 instantaneous instrument [weber-overnight-pair-instrument.mjs](weber-overnight-pair-instrument.mjs) is imported, unmodified, only to supply the Section 9 side of known case 5 and the Section 9 backward pasts of the WP preparations; it is a comparison and a history generator, not a premise.

## 1. The law as implemented, with derivation

For receiver $i$ at absolute reception time $T$ and transmitter $j$, an ordinary causal root is an emission time $S<T$ with

$$
\mathscr R(T)=\|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(S)\|=c_f\,(T-S)>0,\qquad \mathbf n=\frac{\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(S)}{\mathscr R},\qquad D_t=c_f-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j(S)\neq0 .
$$

**Derivation of $\dot{\mathscr R}$ (derived).** Along a locally smooth root branch $S=S(T)$, differentiate the identity once. From the norm, $\dot{\mathscr R}=\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf V_i(T)-S'\,\mathbf V_j(S))$; from the right side, $\dot{\mathscr R}=c_f(1-S')$. Equating and collecting $S'$: $S'(c_f-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j)=c_f-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_i$, so with $D_r=c_f-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_i(T)$,

$$
p:=S'=\frac{D_r}{D_t},\qquad \dot{\mathscr R}=c_f(1-p).
$$

**Derivation of $\ddot{\mathscr R}$ (derived).** Write $\dot{\mathscr R}=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf w$ with $\mathbf w=\mathbf V_i(T)-p\,\mathbf V_j(S)$, which is also the total derivative of $\mathbf r=\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(S)$. Then $\dot{\mathbf n}=(\dot{\mathbf r}-\dot{\mathscr R}\,\mathbf n)/\mathscr R=\mathbf w_\perp/\mathscr R$ with $\mathbf w_\perp=\mathbf w-(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf w)\mathbf n$, and $\dot{\mathbf w}=\mathbf A_i(T)-p'\mathbf V_j(S)-p^2\mathbf A_j(S)$. Hence

$$
\ddot{\mathscr R}=\frac{\|\mathbf w_\perp\|^2}{\mathscr R}+\mathbf n\cdot\big(\mathbf A_i(T)-p^2\mathbf A_j(S)\big)-p'\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j(S).
$$

The right side of the identity gives independently $\ddot{\mathscr R}=-c_f\,p'$. Substituting and solving for $p'$: $-p'\,D_t=\|\mathbf w_\perp\|^2/\mathscr R+\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf A_i-p^2\mathbf A_j)$, so

$$
\ddot{\mathscr R}=\frac{c_f}{D_t}\left[\mathbf n\cdot\big(\mathbf A_i(T)-p^2\mathbf A_j(S)\big)+\frac{\|\mathbf w_\perp\|^2}{\mathscr R}\right].
$$

This agrees with the relations stated in Section 9a. The second derivative contains the unknown present receiver acceleration and the stored delayed transmitter acceleration.

**Present-acceleration block (derived).** Inserting $\ddot{\mathscr R}$ into the frozen law and moving the $\mathbf A_i$ term to the left gives, for each receiver separately, the $3\times3$ system $M_i\mathbf A_i=\mathbf b_i$ with

$$
M_i=I-\sum_{j,S}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K\mu_{\mathrm W}}{\mathscr R\,|D_t|\,D_t}\,\mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T},\qquad
\mathbf b_i=\sum_{j,S}\frac{\sigma_{ij}Kc_f}{\mathscr R^2|D_t|}\left[1+\lambda_{\mathrm W}(1-p)^2+\mu_{\mathrm W}\,\frac{\|\mathbf w_\perp\|^2-\mathscr R\,p^2\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A_j(S)}{c_f\,D_t}\right]\mathbf n,
$$

which at $\lambda_{\mathrm W}=-1/2$, $\mu_{\mathrm W}=1$, $c_f=1$ is the form stated in the assignment. For the pair below the wake speed with $\sigma=-1$ and one partner root, $M_i=I+K\mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T}/(\mathscr R D_t^2)$ is a rank-one update of the identity with $\det M_i=1+K/(\mathscr R D_t^2)>1$ and $M_i^{-1}\mathbf n=\mathbf n/\det M_i$; the solve is unique and well conditioned on every subfield history. Known case 4 measures $\det M_i$ against this closed form at five speeds to $10^{-13}$.

**Propagation of an acceleration jump (derived).** The equation is neutral: it samples $\mathbf A_j(S)$. If the transmitter's acceleration jumps by $\Delta\mathbf A_j$ at emission time $\tau$, then at the reception time $T^\ast$ with $S_{ij}(T^\ast)=\tau$ every other ingredient is continuous and the receiver's acceleration jumps by $\Delta\mathbf A_i=M_i^{-1}\big[-\sigma K\mu_{\mathrm W}p^2(\mathbf n\cdot\Delta\mathbf A_j)/(\mathscr R|D_t|D_t)\big]\mathbf n$, so for the opposite-polarity pair

$$
\|\Delta\mathbf A_i(T^\ast)\|=\frac{K\,p^2\,|\mathbf n\cdot\Delta\mathbf A_j(\tau)|}{\mathscr R D_t^2+K},
$$

with $\mathbf n$, $p$, $\mathscr R$, $D_t$ taken at the reception. On SC-1 this gives $4.64\times10^{-10}$ from the release defect; the instrument measured $4.643\times10^{-10}$. The jumps are not smoothed; each reception time of a jump is itself a new jump time for the other member, with the geometric factor $K/(\mathscr R D_t^2)$ (small for the slow preparations, of order one for WP-3).

## 2. Method

- **State and history.** The state vector holds positions and velocities of the evolving members. At every accepted step the instrument stores $(T,\mathbf X,\mathbf V,\mathbf A)$ for each member; at a breaking point it stores both one-sided accelerations. Between records it interpolates with the quintic Hermite polynomial matching position, velocity and acceleration at both ends (local error $O(h^6)$ in position, $O(h^5)$ in velocity, $O(h^4)$ in acceleration; measured orders 6.1 to 6.3, 5.7 to 6.0 and 4.1 to 4.3 in known case 6). The declared past on $S<0$ is a function: the rigid circle in closed form, or a Section 9 backward trajectory. The latter is generated by running the frozen Section 9 instrument forward from the release state with all velocities reversed (the Section 9 law depends on velocities only through $\dot r^2$ and $\|\mathbf w_\perp\|^2$, so it is even under time reversal; derived), at `gbs`, relative tolerance $10^{-12}$, step cap $0.02$, over $t\in[0,40]$, then mapping $S=-t$, $\mathbf V\to-\mathbf V$, with $\mathbf A$ from the Section 9 solve at each record (2001 to 2025 records) and the same quintic Hermite interpolation.
- **Root solve.** The arrival function $f(S)=\|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(S)\|-c_f(T-S)$ has $f'(S)=D_t=c_f-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j(S)$, which is positive whenever the transmitter is below the wake speed, so the root is unique on any bracket where $D_t>0$. The solver brackets from the latest available history time downward by doubling the lag, then refines by Newton on $f$ with the derivative $D_t$, safeguarded by bisection, to $|f|<10^{-13}$ or until the representable resolution of $S$ is reached; it records the residual, the iteration count, the minimum of $D_t$ over every evaluated point (the monotonicity check), and stops the run as a domain exit if $D_t\le0$ is met or $f$ is not positive at the upper end.
- **Self-root census.** At every evaluation the instrument samples $f_{\mathrm{self}}(S)=\|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_i(S)\|-(T-S)$ at 25 points over the latest four partner lags of the receiver's own history and counts sign changes above a floor of $10^{-11}$; it also checks the receiver's present speed. If $\sup\|\mathbf V_i\|<c_f$ on the history then $f_{\mathrm{self}}<0$ on $S<T$ and no self root exists (derived), so the census is a guard against a history that has left that condition, not a proof on its own. A census change or a speed reaching $c_f$ is a domain exit: the step is shrunk toward the boundary and the run stops there with the event named and the boundary time bracketed to $10^{-10}$. No remedy is applied.
- **Integrator.** Dormand–Prince 5(4) with the standard controller (default; nominal order 5, measured against a closed form in known case 6) or fixed-step classical Runge–Kutta 4 (measured orders 4.17, 4.10, 4.05). Tolerances $10^{-10}$ relative and $10^{-14}$ absolute for the main runs; refinement runs divide both by 100. The step cap is the smaller of the case cap (one fiftieth of the shortest instantaneous period) and one quarter of the current minimum partner lag, so a delayed argument never lands inside the step being computed; a history overrun is detected and the step retried smaller.
- **Breaking points.** The release defect at $T=0$ makes $\mathbf A_j$ jump at emission time $0$. For every pending jump time $\tau$ of a transmitter the instrument predicts the receiver's reception time $T^\ast$ (root of $\|\mathbf X_i(T^\ast)-\mathbf X_j(\tau)\|=T^\ast-\tau$ on the quadratically extrapolated receiver path), ends the step there, corrects the step length by Newton on $S_{ij}(T^\ast)-\tau$ to $10^{-11}$, evaluates all stages of that step on the left side of the jump, then evaluates both one-sided accelerations at $T^\ast$, records the jump, and registers $T^\ast$ as a new jump time of the receiver. Every generation is treated the same way; the jump list in each summary shows the geometric decay.
- **Events and diagnostics.** Per record: $r$, $\dot r$, $h=\|\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}\|$, $\rho_h=h^2/(4K)$, $\varepsilon$ (the Section 9 energy-like quantity, monitored only), member speeds, $\det M_1$, $\det M_2$, self-root census, and for each root $S$, lag, $\mathscr R$, $D_t$, $p$, $\dot{\mathscr R}$, $\ddot{\mathscr R}$, bracket and residual. Events: contact $r<10^{-6}$ and escape $r>10^{3}$ on a crossing (an initial state already past a threshold is listed as an initial violation), speed equality, obstruction $|\det M_i|<10^{-8}$, and turning points of $r$ located by bisection on the Hermite interpolant; the first event is reported with its time. The secular rate $d(\rho_h^2)/dT$ is the least-squares slope of $\rho_h^2$ against $T$ on a uniform grid of 1193 to 4001 points sampled from the stored histories, over the whole window and each half; a slope on the raw non-uniform step samples is also stored and was found to depend on the step sequence at the percent level because of the oscillation of $\rho_h^2$, which is why the uniform grid is the reported one.

## 5. Limits

- Floating point, not an enclosure. Agreement on the known cases does not certify error on a target; the refinement, cross-method and history-density runs below bound it empirically only.
- Root residual floor. The arrival identity is refined to $|f|<10^{-13}$ time units wherever the representation of $S$ allows it. At absolute times $T\gtrsim10^{3}$ the spacing of representable emission times is $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}|T|$, so the attainable residual grows with $T$: measured maxima are $5.9\times10^{-12}$ on SC-1 ($T\le2.5\times10^4$), $2.9\times10^{-11}$ on SC-2 ($T\le2\times10^5$), $3.4\times10^{-13}$ on SC-3, $\le1.1\times10^{-13}$ on WP-1, WP-2 and $5\times10^{-15}$ on WP-3. The corresponding error in the delayed position is below $\beta\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{mach}}|T|\approx10^{-12}$, negligible relative to the ranges, but the preregistered $10^{-13}$ residual is met only where $|T|\lesssim10^{3}$.
- The self-root census is a sampled test with a derived guard, not an enumeration proof; a census change is reported as a domain exit and the run stops.
- Event location of turning points uses the Hermite interpolant within a step and is accurate to the interpolant's order; two turning points inside one step would be missed (the step cap of one fiftieth of a period keeps this unlikely but is not a proof).
- The rate $d(\rho_h^2)/dT$ is a least-squares slope over a window on which $\rho_h^2$ oscillates (the release defect excites a radial oscillation), so the half-window drift is partly the oscillation phase and not only a secular change.
- Lane B shares no code with lane C by construction, but shares the preregistered law, controls and preparations; agreement between the lanes, when checked, is evidence about the implementation of this law, not about the law.

```sh
node $E/weber-delayed-pair-instrument.mjs controls                       # rewrites the receipt
node $E/weber-delayed-pair-instrument.mjs run $E/weber-delayed-pair-cases/SC-1.json            # base run, appends to the runs record
node $E/weber-delayed-pair-instrument.mjs run $E/weber-delayed-pair-cases/SC-1.json --refine   # tolerances / 100
node $E/weber-delayed-pair-instrument.mjs run <case.json> --tEnd 2000 --stem profile-x --no-record   # profiling
node $E/weber-delayed-pair-instrument.mjs past $E/weber-delayed-pair-cases/WP-2.json           # (re)generate and cache the Section 9 backward past
```

A case file names the members (polarity, release position and velocity, and a declared past of kind `rigid-circle`, `stationary`, `affine` or `section9-backward`), the frozen coefficients, the integrator (`dp54` or `rk4`, tolerances, step cap), the event thresholds, the window and the output cadence. Outputs are `<stem>.trajectory.jsonl` (state and event records) and `<stem>.summary.json` under `.local-data/master-equation-closure/weber-delayed-pair/`. The module exports `runCase`, `presentAcceleration`, `partnerRoot`, `selfRootCensus`, `hermite5`, `MemberHistory`, the past constructors, `section9BackwardPast` and `runControls`.
