# Continue the same transformed error from its admitted endpoint

Claim grade: derived. Changing the certified source-support intervals does not change the implemented error coordinate. An admitted endpoint bound for that coordinate can initialize the next receiving cell directly, provided the actual history, prescribed comparison, root convention, intrinsic frames and unscaled transformation $p=u+q$ are literally unchanged. Reinitializing through a new physical-velocity-to-p triangle estimate is unnecessary in that case and can weaken the bound.

This statement applies to the original Section 7 E mirror preparation with $K=c_f=1$, $\beta=3/10$, radius $25/9$, and angular rate $27/250$. It supplements the [source-split theorem](../analysis/maxwell-e-onehour-source-split-2026-10-05.md) and its [finite-refinement addendum](maxwell-e-onehour-subject-proof-support-refinement.md). It supplies no new physical history, equation, response coefficient, coordinate choice or independent target admission.

## 1. The coordinate is determined by the histories, not their enclosures

For either the actual or prescribed comparison history, use its unique ordinary partner emission root and define

$$
q=\frac{n-v}{RD},\qquad p=u+q.
$$

Here $u$ is the receiving physical velocity, $v$ is the physical negative-label source velocity, $R$ is the partner range, $n$ is its unit direction, and $D=1-n\cdot v>0$. These are the implemented quantities from the unchanged exact velocity transformation. The physical interval $P$, the nominal search bracket $J$, and the contracted nominal family $C$ are proof enclosures. None is an argument in the mathematical definition of $q$ or $p$.

Let $r_a,r_c$ denote actual and comparison receiving radii. Express $p_a,p_c$ in their respective intrinsic radial and tangential frames, and denote the components by $p_{a,r},p_{a,t},p_{c,r},p_{c,t}$. Those frames are determined by the corresponding receiving radial directions and the same chosen planar orientation. The intrinsic error vector at metric weight $\nu>0$ is

$$
z_\nu=\bigl(\nu(r_a-r_c),\ p_{a,r}-p_{c,r},\ p_{a,t}-p_{c,t}\bigr).
$$

Its Euclidean norm is the quantity bounded by the signed transformed comparison. Fixed-reception source alignment and complete angular-error estimates help bound its evolution; they do not redefine its endpoint value. An arbitrary reset of relative phase or a change of planar orientation would require a separate proof and is excluded here.

## 2. Exact metric change at the receiving face

Suppose the preceding independently admitted cell ends at $L$ and supplies

$$
\|z_{\nu_-}(L)\|\le W_{-,\mathrm{end}}.
$$

The old weight is $\nu_-$ and the new receiving cell uses $\nu_+$. The vectors are related exactly by a diagonal matrix:

$$
z_{\nu_+}(L)
=\operatorname{diag}(\nu_+/\nu_-,1,1)\,z_{\nu_-}(L).
$$

Its Euclidean operator norm is $\max(1,\nu_+/\nu_-)$. Therefore the valid new initial bound is

$$
W_0=\max(1,\nu_+/\nu_-)W_{-,\mathrm{end}}.
$$

This is the existing metric adjustment. It neither reuses a coefficient outside its original domain nor assumes the new source/clock families are contained in the old ones. The new receiving cell must independently certify its complete source and derivative families before applying its recurrence.

The preceding whole-cell radius error also bounds the radius error at $L$, so it may serve as the new integrated-radius initial value $R_0$. Likewise the retained physical velocity and acceleration bounds remain valid physical source data on their original completed bins. A whole-cell bound is conservative at the endpoint; the sharper endpoint value $W_{-,\mathrm{end}}$ is usable only because the preceding recurrence independently proved it.

## 3. What a literal failed-cell replay must preserve

The source-split replay may use the exact admitted old final row, its endpoint weight and transformed-state error, and the literal originally proposed next-cell trials. It must preserve the immutable actual preparation and comparison identities, the original residual certificate, all completed physical X/V/A/component rows, the entire angular-error primitive and the old cell's endpoint time. The new cell must begin at exactly that time.

No new initialization of $p$ from a bound on $u$ plus a fresh q-error estimate is required. Such a triangle bound is valid when independently established, but it addresses an initialization or transfer problem and needlessly changes the comparison being tested here. Similarly, a field named `p` in a saved error row is an error bound, not a numerical value of the actual transformed velocity. A producer must not interpret it as actual trajectory data.

The final admitted `Wend` and `nu` must be loaded explicitly from the original endpoint row and checked against its admitted identity. Reconstructing only the physical source fields and then using an absent, zero or newly synthesized transformed endpoint would violate this theorem. A same-coordinate direct continuation should record the predecessor endpoint, its `Wend`, its weight, the new weight, the metric multiplier, and $W_0$.

For the exact failed-trial question, keep the recorded next-cell $e_r$, $e_u$, $W$ trial and $\nu_+$ literal. Recalculate the new split source/clock families, signed current matrix, logarithmic norm, forcing, integrated radius and physical velocity conversion. Compare their outputs with those fixed trials. A successful source certificate does not imply those strict receiving inequalities. If the literal trial fails, enlarging it is a separate continuation attempt and must be labeled separately.

## 4. Known controls and falsifiers

If $\nu_+=\nu_-$, the initial transformed-state bound is unchanged. If the error is purely radial and $\nu_+=2\nu_-$, its norm doubles exactly, so omitting the metric factor is false. If $\nu_+=\nu_-/2$, a purely tangential transformed-velocity error keeps its norm unchanged, so multiplying the whole bound by $1/2$ is false; the factor must remain at least one. These exact controls derive the required maximum rather than merely replaying its code.

The theorem fails if the prescribed comparison changes, a scaled or otherwise altered q is selected, the actual history or root convention changes, the intrinsic frames are reset, the old endpoint error lacks independent admission, an angular-history segment is discarded, or the new receiving trial uses old coefficients without a new family proof. A changed support enclosure alone is not one of these failures because it changes a bound, not the bounded coordinate.
