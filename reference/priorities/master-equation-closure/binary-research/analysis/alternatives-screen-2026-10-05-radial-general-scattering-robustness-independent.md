## Complete roots and a sufficient outgoing criterion for every fixed power above one

Suppose a complete joined history through reception $t$ has individual speeds at most $b<1$ and pair gap $d(t)>0$. The residual $r-|X_i(t)-X_j(t-r)|$ starts at $-d(t)$ and increases by at least $(1-b)$ times the increment of $r$. It tends to positive infinity on the entire supplied half-line, by the same chord estimate. It has exactly one positive partner root. Strict subfield chords exclude every positive-delay self root.

At the partner root,

$$
\frac{d(t)}{1+b}\le R_i(t)\le\frac{d(t)}{1-b},
\qquad D_i(t)\ge1-b,\qquad
|A_i(t)|\le C_b d(t)^{-p},\qquad
C_b=\frac{(1+b)^p}{1-b}.
\tag{1}
$$

The comparison with $X_j(t)$ uses the complete interval from emission to reception, including any prescribed old part. Thus no assumption about distant old sources enters (1). The exact source clock is

$$
s_i'(t)=\frac{1-n_i\cdot V_i(t)}{1-n_i\cdot V_j(s_i(t))},
\qquad
\frac{1-b}{1+b}\le s_i'(t)\le\frac{1+b}{1-b}.
\tag{2}
$$

In particular every source eventually enters the generated future, but (1) controls it before then too.

Shift an arbitrary finite entry to $t=0$, retaining the whole past. Suppose its complete supplied speed is at most $b_0<b<1$. Choose a fixed unit vector $e$ with

$$
z_0=e\cdot(X_1(0)-X_2(0))>0,\qquad
w_0=e\cdot(V_1(0)-V_2(0)).
$$

Choose $v>0$ and define

$$
f(t)=z_0+vt,\qquad
I=\frac{C_bz_0^{1-p}}{v(p-1)}.
$$

The sufficient strict entry inequalities are

$$
I<b-b_0,\qquad 2I<w_0-v.
\tag{3}
$$

Bootstrap $|V_i|<b$ and $e\cdot(V_1-V_2)>v$. Then $d(t)\ge f(t)$ and $\int_0^\infty C_bf^{-p}\,dt=I$. The actual velocity changes have norm at most $I$, so both bootstrap margins improve strictly:

$$
|V_i(t)|\le b_0+I<b,\qquad
e\cdot(V_1(t)-V_2(t))\ge w_0-2I>v.
\tag{4}
$$

No contact occurs, and no finite ordinary endpoint remains: on each finite interval there are positive separation, delay and transmitter margins; (1) bounds acceleration; all sampled sources lie in a compact retained interval. The local continuation argument given below therefore extends this chart globally.

Acceleration is integrable for every fixed $p>1$. Writing the terminal velocities as $U_i$,

$$
|V_i(t)-U_i|\le
\frac{C_b}{v(p-1)}f(t)^{1-p},\qquad
e\cdot(U_1-U_2)\ge w_0-2I>v.
\tag{5}
$$

Thus $W=U_1-U_2\ne0$ and $d(t)\sim|W|t$. Individual terminal velocities may vanish for an arbitrary entry satisfying (3); the neighborhood of a positive mirror member constructed below keeps both nonzero. The sufficient criterion itself is valid for every fixed $p>1$, without an upper exponent restriction. The claimed family application here is $1<p<3$.

## Finite-prefix continuity without a hidden source-acceleration norm

Use the same complete-history topology as the earlier Cartesian proof. The supplied histories $H_i:(-\infty,0]\to\mathbb R^3$ are bounded in position and velocity, locally $C^{2,1}$, separated and endpoint-compatible. Around a fixed admitted history $\bar H$, set

$$
\|H-\bar H\|_{1,\infty}
=\max_i\sup_{S\le0}
\max\{|H_i(S)-\bar H_i(S)|,\ |H_i'(S)-\bar H_i'(S)|\}.
\tag{6}
$$

Openness is relative to compatible complete histories in this metric. Compatibility is not open among arbitrary endpoint acceleration jets. This metric preserves the complete old speed and gap margins of the reference circle-tail history. It does not admit a nonzero affine remote-past drift or arbitrary phase drift of a different circle frequency.

Fix a finite reference reception interval $[0,T]$. Its complete speed is below $B<1$, and its supplied circle and regular finite future give a positive complete gap floor $d_*$. Choose $b_1>B$ and a small position/velocity tube with speed below $b_1$ and gap at least $d_*/2$. Put

$$
k=1-b_1,\qquad a=\frac{d_*}{2(1+b_1)}.
$$

All partner delays are at least $a$. If the gap is at most $G$ in this tube through time $T$, every accessed source lies in

$$
[-G/k,T-a].
\tag{7}
$$

Let $M$ bound the reference acceleration on this compact interval, including its supplied portion. No bound on all negative-time accelerations is needed. Let $E(t)$ be the maximum position/velocity discrepancy over both labels and every time through $t$. Comparing residuals at the same trial delay, then using monotonicity, gives

$$
|R_i-\bar R_i|\le2E/k,\qquad
|n_i-\bar n_i|\le4E/(ka).
$$

For source velocities at different emission times, compare perturbed and reference velocities at one common time first; shift only the reference velocity afterward. This gives

$$
|D_i-\bar D_i|
\le\left(1+\frac{4b_1}{ka}+\frac{2M}{k}\right)E.
\tag{8}
$$

Thus a valid reference-dependent acceleration constant is

$$
|A_i-\bar A_i|\le LE,\qquad
L=\frac{(4+2p)a^{-p-1}}{k^2}
+\frac{a^{-p}}{k^2}
\left(1+\frac{4b_1}{ka}+\frac{2M}{k}\right).
\tag{9}
$$

For initial metric discrepancy $\eta_0$,

$$
E(t)\le\eta_0+(1+L)\int_0^t E(u)\,du
\le\eta_0e^{(1+L)t}.
\tag{10}
$$

The second bound follows by differentiating the first bound's right-hand integral envelope. It requires bounded reference acceleration on (7), not closeness of acceleration in (6). Compatible perturbations with large locally bounded higher derivatives are not silently excluded.

Local existence and uniqueness follow by short causal steps. At a step start each source precedes it by at least $a$. Taking the step shorter than $ak/[2(1+b_1)]$ and using (2) keeps all sources in already known history. There the root residual and received acceleration are locally Lipschitz in the reception state: the transmitter margin controls root displacement, and locally bounded source acceleration controls source-velocity evaluation. The position/velocity integral map is a contraction after shortening the step if needed. Endpoint compatibility gives continuous acceleration across release.

The same estimates preserve locally Lipschitz acceleration on finite intervals. If a prospective finite endpoint retains the gap and speed margins, its accessed history is compact and its received acceleration is bounded by (1); the local constants remain finite, allowing another step. This proves both the continuation used in (4) and finite-prefix continuity (10). A sufficiently small $\eta_0$, for example less than half the tube radius times $e^{-(1+L)T}$, keeps the solution in the tube through $T$. Remote history is never discarded: its speed bound proves there are no other roots outside (7).

## Entry of each admitted positive member and genuine Cartesian perturbations

Fix one admitted member with complete global speed bound $B<1$ and terminal velocities $u,-u$, $s=|u|>0$. Let $e=u/s$, take

Its projected separation tends to $2st$ and projected relative velocity tends to $2s$. Choose a finite $T$ with projected separation $\bar z(T)>0$, projected relative velocity at least $3s/2$, and each velocity within $s/8$ of its terminal value. $$
\frac{C_b Z^{1-p}}{v(p-1)}
<\min\{b-b_0,s/8\}.
\tag{11}
$$

Finite-prefix continuity supplies a positive metric neighborhood whose complete speed through $T$ is below $b_0$, whose projected gap at $T$ is at least $Z$, and whose endpoint position/velocity errors are less than $\min\{\bar z(T)/4,s/8\}$. ## Position remainders and exact delayed leading coefficients

First, direct integration of (5) gives

$$
X_i(t)=U_it+
\begin{cases}
O(t^{2-p}),&1<p<2,\\
O(\log t),&p=2.
\end{cases}
\tag{12}
$$

For $2<p<3$, the velocity tail is itself integrable. A finite vector $a_i$ exists and

$$
X_i(t)=U_it+a_i+O(t^{2-p}),\qquad
|X_i(t)-U_it-a_i|
\le\frac{C_b}{v^2(p-1)(p-2)}f(t)^{2-p}.
\tag{13}
$$

The offsets are continuous in the compatible neighborhood by finite-prefix continuity and the locally uniform integrable bound in (13). No offset is supplied by (12).

The exact delayed equation sharpens these orders and demonstrates why ordinary affine offsets fail at and below two. Write $W_i=U_i-U_j$, so $W_1=W$ and $W_2=-W$. The source clock (2) implies $s_i(t)\to\infty$ and $\liminf s_i(t)/t>0$. Because $X_i(t)/t\to U_i$, every subsequential limit $\rho$ of $R_i(t)/t$ solves

$$
\rho=|W_i+\rho U_j|.
$$

This equation has exactly one positive solution,

$$
\rho_i=
\frac{W_i\cdot U_j+
\sqrt{(W_i\cdot U_j)^2+(1-|U_j|^2)|W_i|^2}}
{1-|U_j|^2}.
\tag{14}
$$

It lies below one: the scalar residual is negative at zero and positive at one because $|W_i+U_j|=|U_i|<1$. Hence the whole limit exists, $R_i/t\to\rho_i$ and $s_i/t\to1-\rho_i>0$. The received terminal data are

$$
n_i^*=\frac{W_i+\rho_iU_j}{\rho_i},\qquad
D_i^*=1-n_i^*\cdot U_j>0,\qquad
g_i=-\frac{n_i^*}{\rho_i^pD_i^*}\ne0.
\tag{15}
$$

Taking the limit in the actual received law proves $t^pA_i(t)\to g_i$. This is a delayed asymptotic coefficient depending on both terminal velocities, not an instantaneous central substitute.

Integrating the acceleration tail yields

$$
V_i(t)=U_i-\frac{g_i}{p-1}t^{1-p}+o(t^{1-p}).
\tag{16}
$$

To justify the little-oh integrations, for any prescribed small constant bound the remainder in $t^pA_i-g_i$ uniformly after a sufficiently late time and integrate $t^{-p}$; apply the same argument once more to the velocity remainder. The resulting position expansions are

$$
X_i(t)=
\begin{cases}
U_it-\dfrac{g_i}{(p-1)(2-p)}t^{2-p}
+o(t^{2-p}),&1<p<2,\\[4pt]
U_it-g_i\log t+o(\log t),&p=2,\\[4pt]
U_it+a_i+\dfrac{g_i}{(p-1)(p-2)}t^{2-p}
+o(t^{2-p}),&2<p<3.
\end{cases}
\tag{17}
$$

Since $g_i\ne0$, each individual difference $X_i-U_it$ actually diverges in norm for $1<p\le2$. Thus finite individual affine offsets are excluded there, not merely unproved by an insufficient norm estimate. Relative leading coefficients can cancel, so no such noncancellation is imposed on the relative offset. Changing the finite origin of time affects finite offsets when they exist, not the displayed leading long-range coefficients.

## Directions and finite total angular variation

Let $D=X_1-X_2$, $W=U_1-U_2\ne0$, $g=g_1-g_2$, and $P_W=I-\widehat W\widehat W^{\mathsf T}$. When $p>2$, write $a=a_1-a_2$. Expansion of vector normalization in (17) gives

$$
\frac{D(t)}{|D(t)|}=
\begin{cases}
\widehat W-\dfrac{P_Wg}{|W|(p-1)(2-p)}t^{1-p}
+o(t^{1-p}),&1<p<2,\\[4pt]
\widehat W-\dfrac{P_Wg}{|W|}\dfrac{\log t}{t}
+o(\log t/t),&p=2,\\[4pt]
\widehat W+\dfrac{P_Wa}{|W|t}
+\dfrac{P_Wg}{|W|(p-1)(p-2)}t^{1-p}
+o(t^{1-p}),&2<p<3.
\end{cases}
\tag{18}
$$

For the last line, the quadratic normalization error is $O(t^{-2})=o(t^{1-p})$ precisely because $p<3$. For individual position directions with $U_i\ne0$, the corresponding expansion follows by substituting $U_i,g_i,a_i$ for $W,g,a$. Individual positions are nonzero on a sufficiently late tail. Nothing requires them to avoid an arbitrarily chosen spatial origin on the finite prefix.

Convergence in (18) alone would not prove finite angular variation. Here is a direct projected-acceleration proof that does. The geometric cross product

$$
L(t)=D(t)\times D'(t)
$$

is not asserted conserved. Its actual derivative is $L'=D\times(A_1-A_2)$. Bounded speeds give $|D(t)|=O(1+t)$, and (1), (4) give $|A_1-A_2|=O((1+t)^{-p})$. Therefore

$$
|L(t)|=
\begin{cases}
O(t^{2-p}),&1<p<2,\\
O(\log t),&p=2,\\
O(1),&2<p<3.
\end{cases}
\tag{19}
$$

This retains the projection cancellation $D'\times D'=0$. The crude estimate $|D'|/|D|=O(t^{-1})$ would not suffice. Since $|D|\ge f(t)$,

$$
\left|\frac{d}{dt}\frac{D}{|D|}\right|
=\frac{|L|}{|D|^2}
=
\begin{cases}
O(t^{-p}),&1<p<2,\\
O(\log t/t^2),&p=2,\\
O(t^{-2}),&2<p<3.
\end{cases}
\tag{20}
$$

Every displayed rate is integrable. The finite preceding interval also has finite variation because the pair remains separated and regular. Thus the complete future separation direction has finite total spherical angular variation for every fixed $p>1$ in the outgoing theorem. The same argument applies to each eventual individual position direction when $U_i\ne0$, using $X_i\times V_i$ and $(X_i\times V_i)'=X_i\times A_i$. Its claim is late-tail variation, unless a separate nonvanishing-position hypothesis covers the finite prefix.

Velocity directions also have finite late variation when their terminal velocity is nonzero: their derivative is bounded by acceleration divided by speed, which is $O(t^{-p})$. The relative velocity direction satisfies the same conclusion using its nonzero limit $W$. These statements concern geometric directions in three dimensions and do not select an oriented planar angle or assert angular-momentum conservation.

For $p>2$, $L$ itself has a limit. Equations (13), (16) give that limit $a\times W$. For $p\le2$, $L$ need not remain bounded, yet (20) still proves finite total direction variation. The noncentral delayed coefficient $g$ may have a transverse component in a nonmirror case; no cancellation of that component was used.

## Falsifiers, boundaries and source identities

The complete-root and escape theorem fail if a strict complete speed bound allows an extra partner root or positive-delay self root, if the range/transmitter inequality (1) fails, or if a trajectory satisfying (3) loses its improved margins (4). Finite-prefix robustness fails if (8) cannot be obtained by shifting the bounded-acceleration reference source, or if ordinary continuation stops with all compact-history margins intact. The terminal coefficient is refuted by failure of the source-clock advance, the unique root limit (14), or the limit in the actual acceleration row. Finite angular variation has its separate falsifier: failure of the exact derivative of $D\times D'$ or of the resulting integrable estimate (20).

These are sufficient scattering neighborhoods, not asymptotic stability in a bounded-position-distance metric. Different terminal velocities generally produce linearly diverging trajectories. There is no zero-speed robustness theorem, threshold uniformity, binding claim, post-unit-speed extension, change of preparation or law, memory transfer, or omitted old history. No numerical launch parameter or neighborhood radius is selected. The upper bound $p<3$ restricts the admitted family premise and the last refined direction expansion; the outgoing criterion and finite-direction-variation argument independently hold for every fixed $p>1$.
