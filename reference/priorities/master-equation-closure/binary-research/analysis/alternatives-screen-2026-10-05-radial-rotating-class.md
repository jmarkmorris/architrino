# Rotating mirror pairs under a positive radial delayed response

## Fixed equation and claim boundary

This source concerns the selected opposite-polarity mirror planar pair $X_+(T)=q(T)$, $X_-(T)=-q(T)$, with $c_f=1$. On a complete strictly subfield history it has one ordinary partner root and no positive-delay self root. Write $S<T$, $R=T-S=|q(T)+q(S)|$, $n=[q(T)+q(S)]/R$, and $D=1+n\cdot q'(S)>0$. Its equation is

$$
q''(T)=-\frac{g(R)}D n,
$$

where $g$ is a fixed continuous strictly positive function on positive ranges, locally regular enough for the selected ordinary equation. The selected radial laws have $g(R)=R^{-p}$ for $p=1,3/2,2$; the vector-linear law has $g(R)=kR$, $k=0.2862286103053385$. This common statement does not change any coefficient, all-root convention or singular event rule. It does not apply to the amplitude-gradient, Maxwell, memory, finite-width or time-symmetric rows, whose full acceleration need not point along this one delayed displacement.

Supply a complete separated mirror planar locally $C^1$ past with uniformly subfield speed and nonnegative geometric areal rate $h=q\times q'$, with $h(0)>0$. The cross product here means its signed component normal to the plane; it is a geometric quantity and no physical angular-momentum account is assumed. Consider the maximal separated strict-subfield future of this history. The supplied past need not satisfy the future equation. Compatible complete circular-tail preparations with sufficiently small endpoint patches give examples, but no particular finite-speed launch is admitted by that observation alone.

The conclusions below are derived conditional class statements. They do not prove that the speed remains subfield for all time. A unit-speed event can end this domain and leaves continuation to the selected law's separate event analysis.

## A causal window lies in one open half-plane

Let $a=q(S)$, $b=q(T)$, and $C=(a+b)/2$. Since $T-S=|a+b|=2|C|$ and speed is strictly below one on the compact causal interval, its path length is less than $2|C|$. For every intermediate time $u$,

$$
2|q(u)-C|\le |q(u)-a|+|q(u)-b|
\le\int_S^T|q'(s)|\,ds<2|C|.
$$

Thus the whole path lies in the open ball centered at $C$ with radius $|C|$. This ball lies in the half-plane $C\cdot q>0$, so the continuous angular lift cannot wind around the origin during the causal interval. Moreover $|b-a|<|a+b|$ implies $a\cdot b>0$. The actual lifted angular difference therefore satisfies $|\theta(T)-\theta(S)|<\pi/2$, rather than merely satisfying that inequality modulo $2\pi$. This distinguishes the causal history geometry from an unsupported small-delay expansion.

## Positive rotation is preserved

While the complete past and generated interval have $h\ge0$, their angle is nondecreasing. Continuity and $h(0)>0$ make the angle increment over every receiving causal window at and after release strictly positive. Combining that fact with the half-plane lemma gives $0<\Delta\theta<\pi/2$. The exact areal-rate identity is

$$
h'(T)=\frac{g(R)}{DR}\,r(T)r(S)\sin\Delta\theta>0,
\qquad r=|q|.
$$

At a first prospective loss of positivity the same formula remains strictly positive, so no such loss occurs. Consequently $h(T)\ge h_0=h(0)>0$ throughout the strict-subfield future. Since $h\le r|q'|<r$, the present radius has the fixed lower bound $r>h_0$. Finite contact is therefore impossible in this class before unit speed.

At any proposed finite endpoint, bounded speed bounds position. The root inequality $R\ge2r/(1+b_T)>r$ uses the maximum speed $b_T<1$ on the relevant completed compact history. Thus sources remain a fixed positive time before reception. The complete old-past uniform margin bounds their times below, and their sampled velocities remain in compact strictly subfield source windows. Ordinary coefficients and source regularity cannot fail first. Hence a finite maximal strict-domain endpoint is unit speed at positive separation; a more singular all-root continuation is not supplied here.

## No globally bounded strict-subfield future

Suppose a global strict-subfield future had $r(T)\le M$. Its negative-time sources eventually disappear: if $S\le0$ and the past speed is at most $b_0<1$, then

$$
T-S=|q(T)+q(S)|\le M+r(0)-b_0S,
$$

which implies $T\le M+r(0)$. At all later receptions, the full causal window is generated future with $h\ge h_0$ and $h_0<r\le M$. It follows that

$$
\Delta\theta=\int_S^T\frac{h(u)}{r(u)^2}du
\ge\frac{h_0R}{M^2}.
$$

Let $g_* =\min_{h_0\le R\le2M}g(R)>0$. Since $D<2$, $\sin x\ge2x/\pi$ on $[0,\pi/2]$, and both radii exceed $h_0$, the exact identity yields

$$
h'(T)\ge\frac{g_*h_0^3}{\pi M^2}>0.
$$

This contradicts $h(T)<r(T)\le M$ after sufficiently long time. Therefore a global strict-subfield future cannot remain spatially bounded, even if its speed margin tends to zero. The statement does not exclude increasingly large excursions with bounded returns when no common future speed margin is available.

## Uniformly subfield global futures disperse

Now assume the complete future has one speed bound $|q'|\le b<1$. Suppose $r(T)$ returns below a finite $L$ infinitely often. At every sufficiently late such receiving time, the previous old-source argument gives $S>0$, and root geometry gives $R\le2L/(1-b)$. Every intermediate radius on that causal interval is at most

$$
M_L=L+\frac{2bL}{1-b}.
$$

The same angle and torque argument then gives a uniform positive lower bound for $h'$ whenever $r\le L$, using the minimum of $g$ on the compact range interval $[h_0,2L/(1-b)]$ and the angular denominator $M_L^2$.

Radius is $b$-Lipschitz. Infinitely many visits to $r\le L$ therefore provide infinitely many disjoint intervals of a fixed positive length on which $r\le2L$. Each adds a fixed positive amount to $h$. On the other hand, monotonicity of $h$ and the infinitely many future visits imply $h(T)\le bL$ at every time. This is a contradiction. Thus $r(T)\to\infty$ for every global uniformly subfield future in this rotating mirror class. No terminal velocity or finite total angular advance is proved by this argument.

## Evidence and falsifiers

This theorem was constructed by the coordinator after the initial broad screen. It uses the complete causal chord and an exact geometric areal-rate identity, without numerical trajectories, root truncation, averaging, a near-circle approximation or a physical conserved account. The existing slow canonical theorems remain necessary to prove that their specified preparations actually stay globally subfield; this conditional class result does not replace that existence proof or extend its numerical admission threshold. Independent assessment is pending.

A counterexample to the half-plane inequality, a causal lifted angle outside $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$ under its strict speed hypothesis, loss of positive $h$ while all past areal rates remain nonnegative, a bounded global strict-subfield future, or repeated bounded returns under a uniform global speed margin falsifies the corresponding statement. Histories with reversed past rotation, nonmirror drift, source acceleration response, superfield segments or altered self rules are outside its premises.
