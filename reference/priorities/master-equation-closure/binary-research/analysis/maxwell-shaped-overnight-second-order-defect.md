## Emission derivatives and response differentiation

At one retained root write $R=T-S=|\mathbf r|$, $\mathbf r=\mathbf q_i(T)-\mathbf q_j(S)$, $\mathbf n=\mathbf r/R$, $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v$, and source jets $(\mathbf v,\mathbf a,\mathbf j,\mathbf k)=(\mathbf q_j',\mathbf q_j'',\mathbf q_j''',\mathbf q_j'''')(S)$. Receiver jets are $(\mathbf u,\mathbf A,\mathbf J,\mathbf Q)=(\mathbf q_i',\mathbf q_i'',\mathbf q_i''',\mathbf q_i'''')(T)$. All are the comparison jets. On a smooth piece,

$$
S_1=\frac{1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u}{D},\qquad
\mathbf r_1=\mathbf u-\mathbf vS_1,
$$

$$
S_2=\frac{\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a\,S_1^2-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A-(|\mathbf r_1|^2-(1-S_1)^2)/R}{D}.
$$

Indeed, $R'=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r_1=1-S_1$, while $R''=(|\mathbf r_1|^2-(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r_1)^2)/R+\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf A-\mathbf aS_1^2-\mathbf vS_2)=-S_2$. Rearranging gives the displayed identity. This calculation retains transmitter acceleration and the changing direction of arrival.

$$
z_1=(\mathbf r_1,\mathbf aS_1,\mathbf jS_1,\mathbf A),

$$

## Jerk seams and the integrated remainder

The comparison acceleration, root clock, and response are continuous across a $C^2$ seam. The derivative of its defect can jump. At a receiver jerk seam, its direct contribution is $[\mathbf J]$. At a source jerk seam crossed by one increasing emission clock, its contribution is

$$
-F_a[\mathbf j]S_1.
$$

The coefficient and $S_1$ have continuous values there; receiver acceleration and source velocity/acceleration do not jump. Contributions add when several seams or hits coincide. Thus the complete jump is $[d_1]=[\mathbf J]-\sum_{\text{crossing hits}}F_a[\mathbf j]S_1$. All relevant emission crossings must be enclosed; searching only receiver knot times is insufficient.

For a cell $[l,l+h]$, let its interior seam times be $c_m$, its right initial derivative be $d_1(l+)$, and its piecewise ordinary second derivative satisfy $|d_2|\le B_2$. The distributional fundamental theorem gives, at any $t$ in the cell,

$$
d(t)=d(l)+(t-l)d_1(l+)+\int_l^t(t-s)d_2(s)\,ds+\sum_{l<c_m<t}(t-c_m)[d_1](c_m).
$$

Consequently a valid conservative bound is

$$
|d(t)|\le |d(l)|+h|d_1(l+)|+\frac{h^2}{2}B_2+\sum_m h|[d_1](c_m)|.
$$
