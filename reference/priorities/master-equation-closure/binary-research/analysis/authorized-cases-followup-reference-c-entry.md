## An open finite-event entry region

At an actual generated state $a$ with generated source, put $u=-p(a)>0$, $r_0=r(a)$, $R_0=R(a)$ and $E_0=|v(a)|^2$. A sufficient finite-unit entry condition is

$$
1-E_0<\frac{u^2r_0^2}{128R_0^2}.
\tag{1}
$$

Derivation: suppose a strict-subfield continuation survives the interval of length $\ell=ur_0/8$. Since $|v|<1$, $r\ge r_0-\ell\ge7r_0/8$. The radial equation gives $p'=z^2/r-(e\cdot n)/(RD)\le1/r$, so $p-p(a)\le u/7<u/2$ and $p<-u/2$. The clock identity gives $R'=1-(1-n\cdot v)/D\le1$, hence $R\le R_0+\ell<2R_0$, using $R_0>r_0$ from acute lag. Moreover

$$
n\cdot v=\frac{p(r+r_s\cos\delta)-zr_s\sin\delta}{R}
\le p\frac rR\le-\frac{ur_0}{8R_0}.
\tag{2}
$$

Because $D<2$, the exact squared-speed derivative satisfies

$$
E'=-\frac{2n\cdot v}{RD}\ge\frac{ur_0}{16R_0^2}.
\tag{3}
$$

Integrating for $\ell$ contradicts $E<1$ under (1). The accepted finite-endpoint continuation theorem therefore yields unit arrival no later than $a+ur_0/8$, at positive separation and an ordinary earlier source. The bounds survive to the incoming endpoint; (2) gives $v\cdot v'>0$ there. Its transverse arrival is excluded from the accepted continuous-velocity full-root continuation class. No outgoing law is defined by this argument.

The condition is open in the current velocity, radius and ordinary source data. It is substantially less restrictive than demanding $p$ itself be almost minus one: for any rigorously enclosed negative $p$, a sufficiently small squared-speed deficit compared with $(r/R)^2$ suffices. It is a deterministic sufficient test, not an assertion that the selected family meets it.
