# Blind compact infinite-regime reference without a total-speed margin

**Derived before the new compact-infinite subject is opened.** Retain the existing actual all-future strict-subfield logarithmic family. Use the now accepted eventual radial margin $p\ge-1+\delta$, denominator floor $D\ge\delta$ and delay comparison $R\le2r/\delta$, with member-dependent $\delta>0$. These do not bound total speed away from one. All statements below concern this same branch and complete sampled past.

## Complete causal arcs have a positive radius floor

Take a late generated causal arc $[s,t]$ of length $R$, and a point $u$ in it. Integrating the radial margin on its first part and unit speed on its second part gives

$$
r_s\le r(u)+(1-\delta)(u-s),\qquad
r_t\le r(u)+(t-u).
$$

Since $R\le r_s+r_t$, one obtains $2r(u)\ge\delta(u-s)$. Suppose a sequence had $\min_{[s,t]}r/R\to0$. A minimizing point must satisfy $(u-s)/R\to0$, and then $r_s/R\to0$. The mirror root gives $r_t/R\to1$. The ordinary path chord is consequently almost of length $R$, directed outward. The same elementary unit-speed saturation argument as before proves a uniform outward straight position limit and mean-square outward unit velocity on the full normalized arc.

This outward limit contradicts the actual radial equation. The accepted delay comparison and $D\le2$ imply, throughout every sufficiently late generated interval,

$$
p'\le\frac{1-p^2}{r}-\frac{\delta^2}{8r}.
\tag{1}
$$

Indeed $e_r\cdot n\ge r/R$ and $R\le2r/\delta$. On the middle half of the normalized outward arc, $r/R$ tends uniformly to a positive affine function and $p\to1$ in mean square. Choose endpoints there with $p\to1$, then integrate (1) between them. The first integral tends to zero and the second has a fixed strictly negative limit, contradicting a vanishing endpoint difference. This avoids asserting uniform velocity convergence from a mean-square bound.

Therefore there is an existential $c>0$ such that every sufficiently late complete causal arc satisfies

$$
cR\le r(u)\le2R\qquad(s\le u\le t).
\tag{2}
$$

The upper bound follows from $r_s\le R$ in acute geometry and unit speed. In particular both endpoint radii are comparable. The argument treats a possibly collapsing source endpoint explicitly; endpoint delay comparison alone would not establish (2).

## A vanishing tangential speed would produce an impossible radial limit

Assume $h(t_j)/r(t_j)\to0$ and normalize by $r(t_j)$. On the positive-radius component containing the receiver, (2) covers each complete earlier source arc, and $D\ge\delta$ gives ordinary root inversion and locally bounded acceleration. Source escape and the earlier advancing-time ratio retain only generated source intervals on compact positive-scale domains. The exact row then yields local position/velocity compactness without a strict limiting total-speed margin.

Since $h$ is positive and increasing, its normalized limit vanishes on the whole preceding component. The limiting history lies on a fixed radial ray. Its partner-only row is regular because of the source denominator floor. Local uniqueness continues it radially as long as its radius is positive. This is a limit of the actual partner equations; no assertion about a new physical equality rule or an automatically complete unit-speed limiting root census is needed for this contradiction.

For that radial limit,

$$
p'=-\frac1{RD}\le-\frac{\delta}{4r}.
$$

At a finite right endpoint with radius tending to zero, bounded speed makes $\int du/r$ diverge. If the positive-radius component extends forever, $r(u)\le1+u$ gives the same divergence. Both alternatives force unbounded negative radial velocity, contrary to the inherited unit bound. Hence

$$
\liminf_{t\to\infty}h(t)/r(t)>0.
\tag{3}
$$

This proof uses the complete limiting radial past and ordinary source chart. Zero angular quantity at just the receiving point would not license radial invariance.

## Linear radial growth and normalized compactness

Let $h/r\ge\eta>0$ eventually. By (2), the source lag obeys $\Delta\theta\ge\eta/2$ on each late complete causal arc. The torque identity, $D\le2$, $\sin\Delta\theta\ge2\Delta\theta/\pi$ and the lower endpoint radii in (2) give the sufficient bound

$$
h'\ge\frac{c^2\eta}{2\pi}>0.
$$

Thus $h$ and $r$ have positive linear lower bounds; the upper radius bound follows from unit speed. Their constants are existential and member-dependent. On every compact positive normalized-time interval, receiver and source radii and denominators now have uniform margins. The exact equation bounds scaled acceleration, and its dependence on source velocity with bounded scaled source acceleration supplies equicontinuity. Normalized position histories are therefore precompact locally in $C^2$, modulo a current planar rotation.

A limiting history may contain isolated regular unit-speed touches. No uniform total-speed margin, scalar-speed convergence, fixed phase offset or identified invariant set follows here. In particular this argument does not select finite versus infinite fate for the departing family. It gives the structure of its infinite alternative. The exact spiral is a consistency case. Falsifiers are failure of the complete-arc floor (2), loss of a source from the positive-radius limit component, an invalid radial invariance argument, or a bounded radial limit evading the divergent reciprocal-radius integral.
