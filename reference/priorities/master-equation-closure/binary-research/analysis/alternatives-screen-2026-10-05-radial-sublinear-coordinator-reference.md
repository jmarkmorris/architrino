## A quantitative inward projection from the complete chord

Suppose a global uniform bound $b<1$ exists. Put $r=|q(T)|$, $n=q(T)/r$ and $\Delta=q(T)-q(S)$. Complete speed control gives $|\Delta|\le bR$, while $RN=2rn-\Delta$. Taking squared norms yields

$$
N\cdot n=\frac{R^2+4r^2-|\Delta|^2}{4rR}
\ge\frac{(1-b^2)R^2+4r^2}{4rR}
\ge\sqrt{1-b^2}>0.
$$

The last step is the arithmetic-geometric mean inequality for positive $R,r$. It remains useful at all $b<1$; the weaker subtraction estimate $1-2b$ would not. Also $R\le2r/(1-b)$ and $D\le1+b$. Consequently the exact radial acceleration satisfies

$$
A_r\le-c_b r^{-p},\qquad
c_b=\frac{\sqrt{1-b^2}}{1+b}\left(\frac{1-b}{2}\right)^p>0.
$$

The centrifugal term is a kinematic identity, $r''=h^2/r^3+A_r$. Since $h^2/r^2\le|q'|^2\le b^2$,

$$
r''\le\frac{b^2}{r}-c_b r^{-p}.
$$

Every bound uses the entire actual source interval and the actual transmitter factor. There is no small-delay expansion or instantaneous-law premise.

## Contradiction with uniform-speed dispersal

The antecedent positive-torque theorem proves $r(T)\to\infty$ under the hypothesized global uniform speed margin. Because $p<1$, after a finite time the last radial inequality therefore gives

$$
r''\le-\frac{c_b}{2}r^{-p}<0.
$$

On that tail $r'$ decreases. It must remain positive, since a nonpositive value followed by strict decrease would contradict $r\to\infty$. Radius is thus a valid increasing coordinate. Integrating the actual inequality $d(r'^2)/dr=2r''$ gives, from a fixed tail radius $r_1$,

$$
r'(T)^2\le r'(T_1)^2-\frac{c_b}{1-p}
\left[r(T)^{1-p}-r_1^{1-p}\right].
$$

Its right side becomes negative as $r\to\infty$, a contradiction. This is a direct trajectory inequality, not conservation of a central energy.
