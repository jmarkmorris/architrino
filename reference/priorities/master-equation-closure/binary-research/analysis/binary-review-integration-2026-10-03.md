The nine $\theta=0$ endpoints have truly collinear P0 and P2 pasts, while P1 adds a time-varying lateral component. Even P0/P2 use finite declared history, so applicability requires complete incoming root coverage and the attracting first-exit hypotheses, not endpoint labels alone. The collinear obstruction is a possible terminal mechanism; no whole-configuration fate follows before the campaign's prehistory comparison.

The bound gate uses the last ten absolute time units of a twenty-unit claim window and allows $|\Delta r|\le0.01d$; it is explicitly a finite-window definition. Under a slow circular comparison with member radius $R=d/2$, the mean relative-separation rate would be approximately $K/(dc_f)$, giving a ten-unit change about $10K/(dc_f)$. That can lie below the threshold for sufficiently small coupling or large separation. A per-orbit change cannot be substituted for this fixed ten-unit interval. The campaign speeds $0.25,0.50,0.75$ also lie outside the row lemma's $\epsilon\le1/8$ bound, so the lemma does not classify this grid.
