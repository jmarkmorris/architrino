## 1. Exact source-clock variation

For one directed hit write $s=t-R$, $q=x-Y(s)$, $n=q/R$, $D=1-n\cdot v$, $v=Y'(s)$ and $a=Y''(s)$. The signed row is $F=\sigma E(R,n,v,a)$, where

$$
E=\frac{(1-|v|^2)(n-v)+R[(n-v)(n\cdot a)-Da]}{R^2D^3}.
$$

Let a dot denote variation of a completed source curve and receiver position, rather than physical time derivative; put $b=\dot x-\dot Y(s)$. Implicit differentiation of the norm clock gives

$$
\dot s=-\frac{n\cdot b}{D},\qquad
\dot R=\frac{n\cdot b}{D},\qquad
\dot q=\left(I+\frac{v n^T}{D}\right)b,\qquad
\dot n=\frac{I-nn^T}{R}\dot q.
$$

Consequently

$$
\dot v=\dot Y'(s)+a\dot s,\qquad
\dot a=\dot Y''(s)+j\dot s,\qquad
\dot F=F_R\dot R+F_n\dot n+F_v\dot v+F_a\dot a,
$$

where $j=Y'''$ exists almost everywhere for a $C^{2,1}$ source. These formulas retain the delayed acceleration variation and its moving-clock transport. Current receiver velocity is absent from this E row. Both mirror-directed hits must obey the same actual history relation; treating their source perturbations as independent is a relaxation, not the full variation.

A first variation is not automatically a quadratic finite-error enclosure. At a $C^{2,1}$ seam, acceleration is Lipschitz but need not have a continuous derivative: translation of acceleration need only have a first-order Lipschitz remainder. A valid finite proof either integrates an almost-everywhere homotopy derivative on a domain whose full intermediate clocks and source jets are controlled, or bounds source differences by completed $X,V,A$ discrepancies plus the trial acceleration/jerk transport. It may not assume an unproved uniform second derivative of the delayed row. Crossing a seam requires both source pieces or a proved comparison to their union.

## 2. Exact receiving error and adjoint support

Fix a completed trial source and let $F_c(t,x)$ be its complete signed E row. Let $x_c'=v_c$ and $d=v_c'-F_c(t,x_c)$. For the actual receiver define $\xi=x-x_c$, $\eta=v-v_c$. On a root-complete receiving homotopy,

$$
\xi'=\eta,\qquad
\eta'=A(t)\xi+g_s(t)-d(t),\qquad
A(t)=\int_0^1\partial_x F_c(t,x_c+\theta\xi)\,d\theta,
$$

where $g_s=F_{\rm actual\ source}(t,x)-F_c(t,x)$. This is a finite identity. Source errors are evaluated at the same actual receiving position; receiver displacement is not counted twice.

Choose a concrete integrable signed $2\times2$ matrix $C(t)$ and set

$$
B_0=\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\C&0\end{pmatrix},\quad
f=g_s-d+(A-C)\xi,\quad
e=\binom\xi\eta,\quad e'=B_0e+\binom0f.
$$

For a fixed unit direction $q_*$ at a final time $T$, solve the adjoint terminal-value problem

$$
z'=-B_0^Tz,\qquad z(T)=\binom0{q_*}.
$$

Then the exact directional identity is

$$
q_*\cdot\eta(T)=z(L)\cdot e(L)+\int_L^T z_v(t)\cdot f(t)\,dt.
$$

For a set $S$, write $h_S(w)=\sup_{s\in S}w\cdot s$. If independently certified sets $E_L$ and $G_t$ contain $e(L)$ and $g_s+(A-C)\xi$, respectively, a rigorous lower speed projection is

$$
q_*\cdot v(T)\ge q_*\cdot v_c(T)-h_{E_L}(-z(L))
-\int_L^T h_{G_t}(-z_v(t))\,dt
-\int_L^T z_v(t)\cdot d(t)\,dt.
$$

The signed residual integral can be enclosed directly. Replacing it by an absolute norm is sound but can destroy the intended improvement. Likewise a correlated joint set for source errors can reduce its support; saved scalar magnitudes alone do not establish cancellation. If $E_L$ consists only of separate Euclidean balls of radii $X_L,V_L$, its support is exactly $X_L|z_x|+V_L|z_v|$. An ellipsoid may replace that set only if its full containment is proved from earlier history, not merely because its coordinate projections fit old caps.

For known coefficient error $\Delta C=A-C$, the term $\Delta C\xi$ remains in $G_t$. Its bound must cover the actual receiving tube, all clock and source seams, and the whole cell. A numerical fundamental matrix or adjoint requires a residual/error certificate of its own; agreement with its own saved output is not that certificate.

## 3. What changes relative to the failed scalar method

Trace $B_0=0$ gives determinant one for its fundamental matrix, but does not prevent contraction of a velocity projection. For $C=-\omega^2I$ over $\pi/(2\omega)$, $\eta(T)=-\omega\xi(L)$ exactly: inherited velocity uncertainty disappears from that projection while position uncertainty remains. This exact oscillator is a mathematical instrument control, not an asserted E trajectory. For $C=0$, $\eta(T)=\eta(L)$; this is the complementary no-improvement control.

The old independent-entry matrix boxes admitted a free-direction witness. Any new certificate using only the same relaxed boxes still admits that witness. To improve the physical result, the new coefficient representation must preserve enough actual time/geometry correlation to exclude it, or start from a strictly stronger independently certified error set. Merely replacing scalar integration by a matrix exponential of an arbitrary center of the same box does neither; its remainder must pay for the discarded width.
