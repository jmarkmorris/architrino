# Blind quantitative radial and source-clock reference

**Derived before access to the new C quantitative-dispersal subject.** Retain the admitted all-future pointwise-subfield branch of the same compatible logarithmic family, including positive h, acute unwrapped lag and the actual source clock. Assume the additional open initial inequality $r(0)/(-s_0)<9/10$, inherited by sufficiently small members from the exact base. This restricts the existing existential family and does not replace its history.

Put $\tau=t-s_0>0$, $z=r/\tau$ and $p=r'$. The exact radial identity and acute lag give

$$
p'=\frac{|v|^2-p^2}{r}-\frac{e_r\cdot n}{RD}
\le\frac{1-p^2}{r}-\frac{r}{2\tau^2}.
$$

Indeed $e_r\cdot n=(r+r_s\cos\Delta)/R\ge r/R$, $D\le2$ and $R=t-s\le t-s_0=\tau$. In logarithmic time $\ell=\log\tau$,

$$
\dot z=p-z,\qquad \dot p\le\frac{1-p^2}{z}-\frac z2.
$$

On a possible excursion with $z\ge9/10$ and $p\ge9/10$, pointwise speed below one gives

$$
\dot p\le-\frac{43}{180},\qquad \dot z\le\frac1{10}.
$$

Starting when z first reaches nine tenths, p can remain above nine tenths for at most $18/43$ logarithmic time. During that interval z increases by at most $9/215$. Once p is below nine tenths it cannot cross upward through that value while z remains above nine tenths, because its derivative there is strictly negative. This first-excursion argument applies at every reentry. Consequently

$$
z(t)\le\frac{81}{86}<\frac{19}{20}=:c<1.
$$

This is a radial ceiling relative to the shifted physical clock, not a uniform bound on the full speed. The actual tangential component remains part of the radial inequality.

## Quantitative advancing source

Whenever the source time is nonnegative, apply this ceiling at both endpoints:

$$
t-s=R\le r(t)+r(s)\le c[(t-s_0)+(s-s_0)].
$$

It follows that

$$
\frac{s(t)-s_0}{t-s_0}\ge\frac{1-c}{1+c}=\frac1{39}.
$$

This gives a fixed advancing ratio even though source and receiver speeds may approach one. To make the initial qualification explicit, set $B=\max_{[s_0,0]}r$. If $s(t)\le0$, then $t\le R\le c(t-s_0)+B$, so $t\le20B-19s_0$. Thus $T_0=20B-20s_0>0$ is a sufficient time after which every source is positive. The ratio is available for all $t\ge T_0$ on this all-future branch. All quantities belong to the exact member; no numerical family threshold is claimed.

## A power lower bound

When the source is generated, angular monotonicity improves the endpoint-integral torque estimate by replacing $h_*$ with $h(s)$. Therefore

$$
h'(t)\ge\frac{h(s(t))}{2\pi R(t)}\ge\frac{h(s(t))}{2\pi\tau}.
$$

Let $\tau_0=T_0-s_0$ and choose exponent $\alpha=1/10$. The strict elementary inequality

$$
\frac1{2\pi39^{1/10}}>\frac1{10}
$$

follows, for example, from $\pi<10/3$ and $39<(3/2)^{10}$. Define $C=h(0)/(39\tau_0)^{1/10}$. On the initial interval $\tau_0\le\tau\le39\tau_0$, monotonicity gives $h\ge C\tau^{1/10}$. At a putative first later contact with this lower barrier, the advancing source ratio and the already valid earlier barrier imply

$$
h'\ge\frac{C(\tau/39)^{1/10}}{2\pi\tau}
>\frac C{10}\tau^{-9/10}.
$$

This contradicts a crossing below the barrier. Hence the explicit sufficient bounds are

$$
\boxed{r(t)>h(t)\ge h(0)\left(\frac{t-s_0}{39\tau_0}\right)^{1/10},\qquad t\ge T_0,}
$$

along with $r(t)<(19/20)(t-s_0)$ and the source ratio above. The constants are deliberately conservative and are not claimed optimal. They strengthen the logarithmic radial lower bound without requiring a uniform future speed margin.

The proof does not establish eventual positive radial velocity, linear dispersal, a universal angular growth rate or a terminal speed. Existing positive winding remains independently accepted. Failure of the initial open radial ratio removes this particular barrier argument; loss of pointwise subfield speed or acute lag invalidates its radial comparison. A source ratio asserted before the source becomes generated would also require a separate old-past bound. No target computation, new preparation or balance/spectrum calculation was used.
