# Blind angular-destination reference for the logarithmic pair

**Status: independently derived before disclosure of a new subject on this question.** The selected mirror-planar perturbation family retains the registered row, complete separated preparation and $K=c_f=1$. Assume conditionally that one of its regular continuations exists for all future time, remains separated, and that the complete old and evolved history has one uniform speed bound $\beta<1$. This note does not prove those assumptions after the already accepted finite nonlinear departure.

## Exact root consequences

Write the positive member as $x(t)=r(t)(\cos\theta(t),\sin\theta(t))$ with a continuous unwrapped angle, and write its unique partner source as $s(t)$. The complete chord bound gives no positive-delay self root and exactly one partner root. With $R=t-s=|x(t)+x(s)|$ and $D=1+n\cdot x'(s)$, the equation is

$$
x''(t)=-\frac{n}{RD},\qquad
1-\beta\le D\le1+\beta.
\tag{1}
$$

The stationary remote tail in the fixed preparation makes $|x(s)|$ bounded for $s\le0$. If a future source stayed there, $t-s=|x(t)+x(s)|\le |x(0)|+\beta t+C$ would force $(1-\beta)t\le C+|x(0)|+s$, impossible at arbitrarily large $t$. Thus eventually $s\ge0$. On that future domain, $|x(t)|\le r(0)+\beta t$ and the same bound at $s$ give

$$
(1-\beta)t\le(1+\beta)s+2r(0),
\qquad s(t)\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{2}
$$

The same conclusion holds for a uniformly subfield affine remote tail by its complete chord estimate. This explicit old-history step prevents an invalid replacement of all delayed values by late-time values.

## Finite angle is impossible on this conditional future

Suppose $\theta(t)\to\theta_\infty$ is finite. Set $e=(\cos\theta_\infty,\sin\theta_\infty)$. By (2), both receiving and sampled source directions eventually lie in a cone about $e$ with cosine at least $1/2$. Their radii are positive, so their sum has $e\cdot n\ge1/2$. Since the source is eventually nonnegative, $R=t-s\le t$. Equation (1) therefore implies

$$
e\cdot x''(t)\le-\frac1{2(1+\beta)t}
\quad\text{for all sufficiently large }t.
\tag{3}
$$

Integration gives $e\cdot x'(t)\to-\infty$, contradicting the fixed physical speed bound. Thus an all-future regular uniformly subfield continuation cannot have a finite unwrapped angular limit. In particular, the canonical theorem's finite-angle dispersal destination cannot be the destination of such a logarithmic continuation. The inverse-square canonical row would instead give a $t^{-2}$ lower comparison, whose integral is finite; replacing that power in this argument changes its conclusion.

More generally, eventual confinement of the two-dimensional receiving direction to any fixed closed arc of length strictly less than $\pi$ is impossible. Choose its middle direction and use its positive cone cosine in place of $1/2$. By (2), the delayed source eventually lies in the same arc. The same harmonic divergence follows. This consequence does not require separation tending to infinity.

## Angle and angular variation are distinct

Finite total variation of the continuous unwrapped angle on a future half-line would make it Cauchy and give a finite limit. Therefore the total angular variation must be infinite under the stated conditional continuation assumptions. Without a further sign theorem for $\theta'$, this does not prove $\theta\to+\infty$, $\theta\to-\infty$, unbounded signed angle, a logarithmic winding rate or convergence to the exact spiral. A bounded angle that keeps oscillating across a sufficiently large range is not excluded by this proof.

The exact angular identity is

$$
(r^2\theta')'
=\frac{rr_s\sin(\theta(t)-\theta(s))}{R^2D}.
\tag{4}
$$

Its sign depends on the actual unwrapped delayed angular difference. Positive initial angular momentum alone does not justify replacing the sine by a positive number for every later root. A sign-invariance or delay-angle theorem, if proved from the selected complete preparation, could strengthen the conclusion. It is not assumed here.

Known analytical controls are the accepted exact logarithmic spiral, whose angle grows logarithmically and whose Cartesian velocity never settles, and the stationary direction hypothesis, for which (3) yields an immediate contradiction with bounded speed. The canonical finite-angle tail is a control for the importance of the radial exponent, not evidence for the logarithmic equation. Falsifiers are failure of eventual source escape (2) under the actual complete preparation, a cone sum violating its positive projection, or a bounded velocity satisfying (3).
