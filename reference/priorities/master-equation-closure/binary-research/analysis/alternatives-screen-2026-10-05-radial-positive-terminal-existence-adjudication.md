## Direct reconstruction of the endpoint estimate

Use $a=h^{4/3}$, $x=r/a$, $z=x^{-1/2}$, $y=a^{1/4}u$, $\delta=\epsilon a^{-1/4}$ and weighted clock $d\chi/ds=a^{-5/4}x^{-3/2}$. On the admitted post-transition compact annulus, the actual complete-history rows are

$$
z_\chi=-y/2+(2/3)\delta z+O(\delta^2z),\quad
y_\chi=z^3-1-(1/6)\delta y+O(\delta^2),\quad
\delta_\chi=-(1/3)\delta^2+O(\delta^3).
$$

No autonomous dependence or derivative of these remainders is required. At the physical infinite-time boundary, $z\to0$, $y\to y_\infty\ge0$ and $\delta\to\delta_\infty>0$; $V_\infty=\delta_\infty y_\infty$. The weighted duration is finite by the previously assessed global proof.

For the central comparison define $e=y^2/2+z^4/2-2z$ and $F_0=(-y/2,z^3-1)$. Its compact regular ovals include the regular point $(0,0)$ on the zero level. Set $I(e)=\oint y^2d\chi$ and $Q(e)=I'(e)=\oint d\chi$. Differentiating the action integral verifies this normalization directly. The first-order scalar drift is $\delta F$, where $F=-y^2/6+4(z^4-z)/3$. The identity obtained by integrating $(zy)_\chi$ around an oval gives $\oint Fd\chi=I/2$.

Consequently the orbit primitive $B$ solving $F_0\cdot\nabla B=QF-I/2$ is smooth and bounded with bounded gradient on the compact annulus. Its right side has zero period integral; construction from a smooth transverse section supplies a single-valued primitive. The point $(0,0)$ is not a critical point, so there is no singularity there. With $B_0=B(0,0)$, define

$$
\mathcal A=I(e)-I(0)-\delta(B-B_0).
$$

Direct differentiation cancels the oscillatory order-$\delta$ term and yields

$$
\mathcal A_\chi=\tfrac12\delta I(e)+O(\delta^2)\ge c\delta>0.
$$

Subtracting $\delta B_0$ changes the derivative only at order $\delta^2$. This exact terminal normalization is the new step: at every zero-speed endpoint $\mathcal A\to0$, even when $\delta_\infty$ differs between members. Thus $\mathcal A_0<0$ at each earlier post-transition reception on such a member.

From $\delta_\chi\ge-C\delta^2$ one obtains $\delta(\chi_0+t)\ge\delta_0/(1+C\delta_0t)$. If $L$ is the remaining weighted duration of a zero-speed member, integration to its zero terminal value gives

$$
-\mathcal A_0\ge\frac cC\log(1+C\delta_0L),\qquad
L\le\frac{\exp[C(-\mathcal A_0)/c]-1}{C\delta_0}.
$$

The direction of this bound is important: it is an upper bound conditional on zero terminal speed. For a fixed zero-speed member observed increasingly late, its right side tends to zero. Finite-time complete-history continuity makes the same bound uniformly small for nearby zero-speed parameters. A neighbor with nonnegative $\mathcal A_0$ is already excluded from the zero-speed class.

## Why this forces positive-speed members to occur

The earlier selection proof supplies a nonvanishing complex coordinate $\mathcal W$ whose terminal limit is the positive real number $\sqrt{3/2}$ and whose continuously lifted terminal phase is $2\pi N(\epsilon)$, with integer $N(\epsilon)\to-\infty$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. Fix a zero-speed member. At a sufficiently late finite physical reception, its $z$ is small, and the just-proved remaining weighted time is arbitrarily short. The actual row gives a uniform bound on $|z_\chi|$. Every nearby zero-speed member therefore remains in a small-$z$ strip where $\operatorname{Re}\mathcal W>0$ for its entire remaining future. No additional whole turn occurs there. On the finite preceding interval, nonvanishing of $\mathcal W$ and complete-history continuity make the lifted phase continuous. Its difference from the right-half-plane argument is an integer multiple of $2\pi$, constant in a sufficiently small connected parameter neighborhood. Hence $N$ is locally constant relative to the zero-speed set.

If all members of some interval $(0,\epsilon_1)$ had zero terminal speed, this relative local constancy would be ordinary local constancy on a connected interval. It would force $N$ to be constant there, contradicting its divergence at zero. Thus every sufficiently small interval contains a positive-speed member. The earlier positive terminal strip proves that the positive set is open, so positive-speed intervals occur arbitrarily close to zero. This conclusion uses neither genericity nor continuity of the infinite-time endpoint as an unproved premise.

Each positive member has linear late radius growth and finite angle; each admitted zero-speed member has the separately proved $T^{4/5}$ radius law.
