$$

Thus $h\ge1$ at generated times. This assertion is not imposed on every point of the supplied negative-time patch, whose own complete speed, radius and positive-$h$ bounds are retained separately.

Here $a$ is the radius at which the zero-delay central radial acceleration balances the geometric transverse term at the current $h$. The identities $h^2=a^{3-p}$ and $v=a^{-\alpha}/x$ hold on the actual trajectory. Positive torque gives $a\ge1$ and $0<\delta\le\epsilon$ at generated times.

## Signed second-order row derived for general power

Let $\ell=s-\sigma=2\epsilon rL$ and $L=R_d/(2r)$. In the fixed receiving axes, integral Taylor formulas give

$$
\begin{aligned}
[Y(s)+Y(\sigma)]_r&=2r-\ell u-\tfrac12\ell^2r^{-p}+O_p(\delta^3a),\\
[Y(s)+Y(\sigma)]_t&=-\ell v+O_p(\delta^3a),\\
Y'_r(\sigma)&=u+\ell r^{-p}+O_p(\delta^2a^{-\alpha}),\\
Y'_t(\sigma)&=v+O_p(\delta^2a^{-\alpha}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The powers follow from $\ell=O_p(\epsilon a)$ and the preceding acceleration defect; for example $\ell^2\delta a^{-p}=O_p(\delta^3a)$. The implicit norm equation has a uniformly nonzero derivative near $L=1$, so substituting the following expressions with a cubic residual bounds the error of the actual unique root:

$$
\begin{aligned}
L&=1-\epsilon u+\epsilon^2(u^2-r^{1-p}+v^2/2)+O_p(\delta^3),\\
N_t&=-\epsilon v+O_p(\delta^3),\qquad
N_r=1-\epsilon^2v^2/2+O_p(\delta^3),\\
D&=1+\epsilon u+\epsilon^2(2r^{1-p}-v^2)+O_p(\delta^3).
\end{aligned}
$$

For instance, the radial implicit equation is $L(N_r+\epsilon u)+\epsilon^2L^2r^{1-p}=1+O_p(\delta^3)$. It identifies the displayed $L$ with the delayed root; $L$ is not treated as an independent free variable.

Expanding the range power and transmitter factor separately gives

$$
L^{-p}D^{-1}=1+(p-1)\epsilon u+
\epsilon^2\left[\frac{(p-1)(p-2)}2u^2+(p-2)r^{1-p}+\frac{2-p}{2}v^2\right]+O_p(\delta^3).
$$

Multiplying by the direction supplies the actual signed radial and transverse accelerations:

$$
\begin{aligned}
A_r={}&-r^{-p}\left\{1+\beta\epsilon u+
\epsilon^2\left[\frac{\beta(p-2)}2u^2+(p-2)r^{1-p}-\frac\beta2v^2\right]\right\}+Q_r,\\
A_t={}&r^{-p}\left[\epsilon v+\beta\epsilon^2uv\right]+Q_t,

\end{aligned}

Every remainder is a bounded contribution from the actual retained history. None is assumed autonomous or differentiated. At $p=2$, the quadratic $u^2$ and $r^{1-p}$ coefficients vanish and the independently known canonical row is recovered. ## Changing-scale equations and removal of the constant second-order input

Define

$$
f(x)=x^{-3}-x^{-p},\qquad
c=\frac{\beta(p-2)}m,\qquad
q_p=\frac{\beta^2}{m}-\frac{\beta(p-2)}2.
$$

Differentiating the exact polar identities and the changing scales gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_\eta&=y-k\delta x^{1-p}-k\beta\delta^2yx^{1-p}+O_p(\delta^3),\\
y_\eta&=f(x)+c\delta yx^{-p}
+\delta^2x^{-p}\left[q_py^2-(p-2)x^{1-p}+\frac\beta2x^{-2}\right]+O_p(\delta^3),\\
\delta_\eta&=-d\delta^2x^{-p}-d\beta\delta^3yx^{-p}+O_p(\delta^4),\\
a_\eta/a&=k\delta x^{-p}(1+\beta\delta y)+O_p(\delta^3).
\end{aligned}
$$

The first-order coefficient $c$ includes both the direct radial term $-\beta\delta yx^{-p}$ and the moving velocity unit $\alpha k\delta yx^{-p}$. Dropping that latter contribution would give an incorrect oscillation criterion.

Subtract the leading outward scale drift by setting $w=y-k\delta x^{1-p}$. Product differentiation, including $\delta_\eta$, yields

$$
\begin{aligned}
x_\eta&=w-k\beta\delta^2x^{1-p}w+O_p(\delta^3),\\
w_\eta&=f(x)+\gamma_p\delta x^{-p}w+
\delta^2[q_px^{-p}w^2+G_{0,p}(x)]+O_p(\delta^3),
\end{aligned}
$$

$$
\gamma_p=c+k\beta=\frac{p\beta}{m},\qquad
G_{0,p}(x)=\left[\frac{2\beta^2}{m^2}+2-p\right]x^{1-2p}+\frac\beta2x^{-p-2}.
$$

For clarity, the constant coefficient of $x^{1-2p}$ is $k(c+d)-(p-2)=2\beta^2/m^2+2-p$. The factor $+kd$ comes from differentiating the moving speed parameter and is essential to preserving the release seed.

Let

$$
V_p(x)=\frac1{2x^2}-\frac{x^{1-p}}\beta+\frac1\beta-\frac12,
\qquad V_p'=-f,\quad V_p(1)=V_p'(1)=0,\quad V_p''(1)=m>0,
$$

and put $A_p=2\beta^2/m^2+2-p$ and

$$
G_p(x)=\frac{A_p}{2\beta}(1-x^{-2\beta})+
\frac{\beta}{2(p+1)}(1-x^{-p-1}),\qquad G_p'=G_{0,p}.
$$

The corrected auxiliary potential is $V_{p,\delta}=V_p-\delta^2G_p$. It has one nearby minimum $x_\delta$ satisfying

$$
x_\delta=1+\frac{A_p+\beta/2}{m}\delta^2+O_p(\delta^4),
\qquad \frac{dx_\delta}{d\delta}=O_p(\delta).
$$

The positive second derivative and the implicit equation $V_p'(x_\delta)=\delta^2G_{0,p}(x_\delta)$ prove these statements. The potential and its corrections are analytical devices, not a physical energy account.

## A nonzero release seed and a positive amplitude inequality

Set $X=x-x_\delta$ and define

$$
E=\frac12w^2+V_{p,\delta}(x)-V_{p,\delta}(x_\delta),\qquad
\mathcal L=E-\frac{\gamma_p}{2}\delta Xw,\qquad J=\sqrt{\mathcal L}.
$$

For a fixed sufficiently small neighborhood and sufficiently small $\delta$, $\mathcal L$ is positive definite in $(X,w)$ and comparable to $X^2+w^2$. At the actual release, the frozen preparation has $x=1$, $y=0$, $\delta=\epsilon$, and $w=-k\epsilon$. Therefore

$$
J(0)=\frac{k}{\sqrt2}\epsilon+O_p(\epsilon^3)>0.
$$

The nonzero seed is an exact consequence of the release position and velocity combined with the drift subtraction. It is not inferred from a numerical perturbation or an assumed unstable circle.

Differentiate $E$ using the actual shifted equations. The constant second-order term $\delta^2G_{0,p}$ cancels against the potential correction. Its moving minimum contributes only $O_p(\delta^3|X|)$, since the parameter derivative is $-2\delta[G_p(x)-G_p(x_\delta)]\delta_\eta$. The response remainders are multiplied by $w$ or $V_{p,\delta}'(x)$, giving $O_p(\delta^3\sqrt E)$. The remaining quadratic terms give

$$
E_\eta=\gamma_p\delta x^{-p}w^2+O_p(\delta^2E)+O_p(\delta^3\sqrt E).
$$

Also

$$
(Xw)_\eta=w^2-mX^2+O_p(J^3+\delta J^2+\delta^3J).
$$

The cross correction in $\mathcal L$ consequently removes the leading phase dependence:

$$
|\mathcal L_\eta-\gamma_p\delta\mathcal L|
\le C_p\delta(J+\delta)\mathcal L+C_p\delta^3J.
$$

Where $J>0$, this is equivalent to

$$
\left|\frac{J_\eta}{J}-\frac{\gamma_p}{2}\delta\right|
\le C_p\delta(J+\delta)+C_p\frac{\delta^3}{J}.
$$

Choose a fixed $j_*(p)>0$ small enough, then choose $\epsilon_*(p)>0$ sufficiently small. On the provisional amplitude interval $J\le j_*$, $J\ge k\epsilon/(2\sqrt2)$, the last error divided by $\delta$ is $O_p(\epsilon)$ because $\delta\le\epsilon$. Since $\gamma_p>0$, these choices give

$$
\frac{\gamma_p}{4}\delta\le\frac{J_\eta}{J}\le\frac{3\gamma_p}{4}\delta.
$$

The initial seed lies above the provisional floor, and the positive derivative forbids crossing that floor downward. Thus the estimate preserves the seed until the fixed event. An absolute second-order forcing would not suffice for this conclusion; the displayed displacement correction reduces the dangerous term to cubic order before it is accumulated.

## The fixed-amplitude event is finite

The amplitude bound keeps $x,w$, and hence $y$, strictly inside the chosen neighborhood. Physical speed remains at most $C_p\epsilon a^{-\alpha}<1/2$, the complete root census is retained and radius stays bounded away from zero. The scale equations imply

At any finite $s$, $a$ is finite, source times lie in a compact part of the retained complete history, partner delays have a positive lower bound, and root factors have positive margins. The ordinary position-velocity method of steps therefore continues the solution unless $J=j_*$ occurs.

The first fixed-amplitude event is therefore finite and regular. Separation, complete roots and the strict speed margin all remain valid there.

## Exponents, finite secular control and actual radial turns

The scale exponent can be obtained with integrated errors, without a periodic averaging assumption. Let

$$
\nu_p=\frac{\gamma_p}{2k}=\frac{p(p-1)}4.
$$

Since $|x-1|\le C_p(J+\delta^2)$ and $|y|\le C_p(J+\delta)$, subtraction of $\nu_p(\log a)_\eta$ from $(\log J)_\eta$ gives

$$
\left|\frac d{d\eta}(\log J-\nu_p\log a)\right|
\le C_p\delta J+C_p\delta^2+C_p\delta^3/J.
$$

Up to the fixed event, the amplitude lower derivative bounds $\int\delta J\,d\eta$ by $C_pj_*$, the parameter decrease bounds $\int\delta^2\,d\eta$ by $C_p\epsilon$ and $\int\delta^3\,d\eta$ by $C_p\epsilon^2$, and $J\ge c_p\epsilon$ bounds the last logarithmic error. Thus

$$
\left|\log\frac{J}{J(0)}-\nu_p\log a\right|\le C_p(j_*+\epsilon).
$$

At $J=j_*$ this proves the following family asymptotics, with constants depending on the fixed exponent and amplitude:

$$
\begin{aligned}
a_b\asymp r_b&\asymp\epsilon^{-4/[p(p-1)]},\\
\delta_b\asymp|q'(T_b)|&\asymp\epsilon^{(p+2)/p},\\
s_b&\asymp\epsilon^{-(p+3)/(p-1)},\\
T_b&\asymp\epsilon^{-2(p+2)/(p-1)},\\
|q(T_b)|&\asymp\epsilon^{-2(p+2)/[p(p-1)]}.
\end{aligned}
$$

Here $r_b$ is the scaled member radius; $|q(T_b)|=r_0r_b$ is its physical radius. The transverse velocity $v=a^{-\alpha}/x$ supplies the lower speed bound, while bounded $y,x^{-1}$ supply the upper one. The time estimates follow by integrating $(a^p)'\asymp\epsilon$ and restoring $T=r_0s/\epsilon$. At $p=3/2$ the physical time exponent is $14$ and speed exponent $7/3$; at $p=2$ they are $8$ and $2$, in agreement with the distinct endpoint mechanisms.

On each fixed secular interval $0\le\tau=\epsilon s\le L$, the same estimates give $J=O_{p,L}(\epsilon)$ and $a$ bounded. Integrating $(a^p)'=pk\epsilon+O_{p,L}(\epsilon^2)$ gives

$$
\frac{|q(T)|}{r_0}=
\left(1+\frac{2p}{3-p}\tau\right)^{1/p}+O_{p,L}(\epsilon),
\qquad \tau=\frac{\epsilon^2T}{r_0}.
$$

This finite secular estimate does not assert pointwise increasing radius. In fact the amplitude band $j_*/2\le J\le j_*$ has $\eta$ duration comparable to $1/\delta_b\asymp\epsilon^{-(p+2)/p}$. The continuous nonzero phase

$$
\psi=\arg(\sqrt m\,X+iw)
$$

satisfies $\psi_\eta=-\sqrt m+O_p(J+\delta+\delta^3/J)<-c_p$ there, by direct differentiation of the shifted equations. Every complete phase turn contains positive and negative $w$ bounded away from zero, while $y=w+k\delta x^{1-p}$ differs by a quantity tending uniformly to zero on that band. Thus actual radial velocity has both signs. At a zero $y=0$, the fixed amplitude forces $|X|\ge c_pj_*$ and $y_\eta=f(x)+O_p(\delta^2)$ has nonzero sign opposite the displacement. The member radius undergoes alternating ordinary maxima and minima before the transition; their number grows at least as $c_p\epsilon^{-(p+2)/p}$. This is an actual trajectory statement, not a circular spectrum.

## Power-dependent boundary and remaining scope

Throughout the selected interval, $\gamma_p>0$ and $m>0$, so there is no change from growth to decay within $1<p\le2$. Both the growth coefficient and the normalization become singular as a uniform endpoint problem at $p=1$: $\gamma_p\to0$, while at $p=1$ the defining balance itself would require $\epsilon^2=1/2$ for $K=R_*=c_f=1$, independently of radius. This frozen small-speed family therefore has no $p=1$ endpoint. It is not legitimate to substitute $p=1$ into the fixed-$p$ theorem or infer a uniform threshold from its positivity for each fixed exponent.

The finite event alone establishes no all-future result beyond its regular continuation neighborhood. A global extension needs a new source-window estimate at unbounded relative radius and a bounded orbit corrector on a reciprocal-radius chart. Those are not consequences of the compact signed-row proof and are assigned to a separate successor source.

**Falsifiers:** a source interval violating the changing-scale cubic estimate; a missing coefficient in the signed general-power row; failure of the $G_{0,p}$ displacement correction; a compatible launch whose seed is canceled despite the amplitude inequality; a finite ordinary-domain failure within the strict chart; or indefinite confinement below $j_*$. A numerical disagreement outside an independently quantified admission threshold is not an admitted counterexample.
