**Derived candidate, frozen before reading the assigned complete bounded-normal worker proof.** Fix any exact Section 14 equal-past/future circle with $K=c_f=1$, $0<\beta<1$, $x=\beta\cos x$, $c=\cos x$, $\omega=\beta/R$ and $\tau=2x/\omega$. The previously derived full Cartesian first variation gives normal common/opposite sectors

$$
z''=-kz+\chi\frac{k}{2}[z(t-\tau)+z(t+\tau)],\qquad
k=\frac{\omega^2}{2c^2},\qquad \chi=+1,-1.
$$

The normal root-time and denominator variations vanish because every base source vector lies in the plane. This is a variational equation about an already balanced complete history. Its advanced term is retained; it is not a causal release evolution.

For any globally bounded $C^2$ common-sector solution, the equation itself bounds $z''$. The centered second-difference identity, obtained by integrating $z''$ twice, is

$$
z(t-\tau)-2z(t)+z(t+\tau)
=\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}(\tau-|s|)z''(t+s)\,ds.
$$

Hence $\|z''\|_\infty\le(k\tau^2/2)\|z''\|_\infty=\beta^2\|z''\|_\infty$. Strict subfield speed forces $z''=0$, and boundedness leaves only a constant vertical translation.

For the opposite sector set $f=z''+\omega^2z$, which is bounded. The oscillator identity, obtained from the elementary variation-of-constants formula or two direct differentiations, is

$$
z(t-\tau)+z(t+\tau)=2\cos(\omega\tau)z(t)
+\frac1\omega\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}\sin[\omega(\tau-|s|)]f(t+s)\,ds.
$$

Because $\omega^2=k[1+\cos(\omega\tau)]$, the opposite-sector equation reduces to f equal to minus $k/(2\omega)$ times this integral. The kernel is nonnegative: $0<\omega\tau=2x<\pi/2$. Its norm is

$$
\frac{k}{\omega^2}[1-\cos(\omega\tau)]=\tan^2x<1.
$$

Thus f vanishes. All bounded opposite normal variations are $a\cos\omega t+b\sin\omega t$, the two infinitesimal rigid tilts. These conclusions hold without periodicity or a prescribed period and exclude an additional bounded normal quasiperiodic direction.
