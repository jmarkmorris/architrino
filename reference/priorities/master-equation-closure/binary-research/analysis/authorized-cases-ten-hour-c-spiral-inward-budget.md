# An inward speed budget locates possible source-clock degeneracy

## Derived candidate and scope

For the unchanged admitted logarithmic family, an all-future regular strict-subfield continuation obeys an explicit restriction on every inward passage. With $w=t-s_0$, $r=|x|$, $p=r'$, $E=|v|^2$, and $p_-:=\max(0,-p)$,

$$
1-E(t)\ge\frac{p_-^2(t)}{128}\left(\frac{r(t)}{w}\right)^2.
\tag{1}
$$

Consequently, at every sufficiently late reception whose source is generated, the transmitter factor satisfies

$$
D(t)\ge1-\sqrt{\frac{128}{128+[r(s)/(s-s_0)]^2}}.
\tag{2}
$$

Thus loss of a uniform transmitter margin on an infinite branch requires collapse of the source radius relative to elapsed time, together with almost unit inward radial source velocity. A source radius remaining a positive fraction of elapsed time protects a positive root margin without assuming speed convergence.

There is also a finite-event criterion. If an actual generated state has $p<0$ and

$$
1-E(t)<\frac{p^2(t)}{128}\left(\frac{r(t)}{t-s_0}\right)^2,
\tag{3}
$$

then its maximal strict-subfield continuation ends at unit speed no later than

$$
t+\frac{[-p(t)]r(t)}8.
\tag{4}
$$

This is an open sufficient criterion evaluated on the actual history. No member is proved to enter it here; it does not replace the missing bridge from the accepted finite nonlinear departure.

## Finite inward motion has a minimum speed increase

At every regular point of the admitted acute sector, the radial acceleration is inward and the tangential acceleration is positive. Directly from the response,

$$
p'\le\frac1r,\qquad
E'\ge\frac{(-p)r}{w^2}\quad\text{when }p\le0.
\tag{5}
$$

For clarity, $e\cdot x''=-(r+r_s\cos\delta)/(R^2D)\le-r/(2w^2)$. In $E'=2p(e\cdot x'')+2(h/r)(Je\cdot x'')$, the tangential term is positive and multiplying the radial bound by negative $p$ reverses its inequality. The first inequality in (5) follows from $p'=(|v|^2-p^2)/r+e\cdot x''$.

Fix a time $t$ with $p(t)=-u<0$, and put $r_0=r(t)$, $w_0=t-s_0$, and $\ell=u r_0/8$. Suppose the regular strict continuation exists through $t+\ell$. Its unit speed bound gives $r\ge r_0/2$ on that interval. The accepted upper radius bound $r_0<19w_0/20$ gives $w\le2w_0$ there. Integrating $p'\le2/r_0$ gives $p\le-u/2$. These deliberately loose inequalities are simultaneous throughout the entire interval, since $\ell\le r_0/8$ and the maximum possible increase of $p$ is $u/4$.

Using (5) throughout the interval yields

$$
E(t+\ell)-E(t)
\ge \ell\frac{(u/2)(r_0/2)}{(2w_0)^2}
=\frac{u^2r_0^2}{128w_0^2}.
\tag{6}
$$

An all-future strict continuation has $E(t+\ell)<1$. This proves (1), with a strict inequality when $p<0$; the weak form accommodates $p\ge0$. Conversely, (3) makes (6) incompatible with continuation through this interval. The accepted endpoint theorem supplies the actual remaining possibility: the finite maximal strict-subfield endpoint has limiting speed one and positive separation and source delay. It must occur by (4). This argument says nothing about inclusive-domain continuation after that endpoint.

The same energy-increase estimate previously excluded macroscopic inward passages under speed convergence. Here the upper bound $E<1$ supplies an unconditional budget instead of a convergence hypothesis.

## A quantitative transmitter margin at a noncollapsed source

Resolve the source velocity in its radial and positive tangential axes:

$$
v(s)=p_s e_s+z_sJe_s,\qquad z_s=h(s)/r_s>0.
$$

The acute chord has both source-axis projections positive, with $0<n\cdot e_s\le1$. If $p_s\ge0$, then $D=1+n\cdot v(s)\ge1$. If $p_s<0$, then

$$
D\ge1+p_s.
\tag{7}
$$

It remains to consider $D<1$. Formula (7) implies $-p_s\ge1-D$. Since $E_s\ge p_s^2$, applying (1) at this generated source gives

$$
1-p_s^2\ge1-E_s
\ge\frac{p_s^2}{128}\left(\frac{r_s}{s-s_0}\right)^2.
$$

Hence

$$
(1-D)^2\le p_s^2
\le\frac{128}{128+[r_s/(s-s_0)]^2},
$$

which proves (2). If the source ratio $r_s/(s-s_0)$ is at least any fixed $c>0$, the right side of (2) is a fixed positive number. This is a local geometric protection of the full ordinary source root, valid even when the future speed has no limit.

## What a vanishing denominator would require

Suppose there are receptions $t_j\to\infty$ with $D(t_j)\to0$. The accepted source escape gives $s_j=s(t_j)\to\infty$. Equation (2) forces

$$
\frac{r(s_j)}{s_j-s_0}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{8}
$$

Equation (7) and the strict speed bound force

$$
p(s_j)\longrightarrow-1,\qquad
|v(s_j)|\longrightarrow1,\qquad
\frac{h(s_j)}{r(s_j)}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{9}
$$

Finally $v(s_j)+e_{s_j}\to0$ by (9), while $n_j\cdot v(s_j)=D_j-1\to-1$. Thus

$$
n_j\cdot e_{s_j}\longrightarrow1.
\tag{10}
$$

The source is asymptotically moving inward at unit speed, its positive tangential speed vanishes, and the chord aligns with its radius. These are simultaneous necessary conditions, not a construction of such a branch. The unconditional result $r,h\to\infty$ is compatible with (8)–(9): the physical radius and angular momentum can both diverge while their ratios vanish. The current proof does not exclude that possibility.

Under the separately accepted scalar-speed-limit hypothesis, normalized history instead converges to the admitted spiral and $D\to1/\lambda>1$. Thus (8)–(10) belong entirely to the remaining nonconvergent-speed alternative.

## Known case, boundaries and falsifiers

The exact admitted spiral has $p=a>0$, so (1) holds immediately, and $D=1/\lambda>1$ satisfies (2). Its outward radial motion never enters the sufficient event condition (3). These are analytical consistency checks; no numerical target or newly built instrument is used.

The new finite-event criterion is useful only after an actual permitted family history is proved to enter its open inequality. The existing nonlinear departure theorem supplies a history-norm exit with existential constants and does not supply that sign or inequality. No arbitrary inward state is substituted for the family.

Falsifiers are failure of the acute-lag signs in (5), use of a range beyond $t-s_0$, loss of the strict speed bound before the finite interval without invoking the accepted endpoint alternative, reversal of the negative-radial-velocity inequality in (7), or application of (1) to an old prescribed source rather than a generated one. A collapsing normalized source radius alone is not sufficient for $D\to0$; all three velocity and alignment requirements remain necessary.
