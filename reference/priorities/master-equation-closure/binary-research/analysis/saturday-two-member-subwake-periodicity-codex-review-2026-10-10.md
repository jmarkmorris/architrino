### Causal rows and window geometry

The canonical multiplier with $c_f=1$ and unlike polarity gives $\mathbf a_1=-W_1\mathbf R_1$, $\mathbf a_2=-W_2\mathbf R_2$, where $\mathbf R_i$ points from the delayed partner to the current receiver and $W_i=1/(\tau_i^3D_i)>0$. The strict speed bound gives $D_i>0$. The implicit-root derivative therefore supplies continuous positive delays locally; the positive separation and strict speed margin on compact windows make the coefficients locally bounded. These local bounds, rather than an unproved uniform bound on the entire past, suffice for the comparison argument.

Freeze the first row's unit direction $\mathbf n_1$ and emission time $s$. For $s<u<t$, project both paths from the emission point. The receiver's projection is greater than $\tau-(t-u)=u-s$, while the emitter's is less than $u-s$, by integrating their strictly subwake velocities. At $u=s$ the receiver inequality remains strict, and at $u=t$ the emitter inequality remains strict. Hence $\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf r(u)>0$ throughout the closed window. Exchanging members gives $-\mathbf n_2\cdot\mathbf r(u)>0$. A continuous angle confined to this open half-plane cannot cross to another angle branch: its endpoint change is strictly between $-\pi$ and $\pi$. This supports Lemma 1, including its continuous-angle interpretation and strictly inward relative radial acceleration.

### Exact angular split

Let $\delta_i\mathbf r=\mathbf r(t)-\mathbf r(s_i)$ and choose one scalar $\lambda\in[0,1]$. With $\mathbf c_\lambda=\lambda\mathbf x_1+(1-\lambda)\mathbf x_2$, the transmitter displacements in the two rows are

$$
\boldsymbol\Delta_1=\mathbf c_\lambda(t)-\mathbf c_\lambda(s_2)+(1-\lambda)\delta_2\mathbf r,\qquad
\boldsymbol\Delta_2=\mathbf c_\lambda(t)-\mathbf c_\lambda(s_1)-\lambda\delta_1\mathbf r.
$$

Consequently

$$
\dot J=\mathbf r(t)\times(W_2\boldsymbol\Delta_1-W_1\boldsymbol\Delta_2)=C_\lambda+G_\lambda.
$$

Here $\mathbf r(t)\times\delta_i\mathbf r=\mathbf r(s_i)\times\mathbf r(t)$, which independently fixes both signs and weights in the subject's split. At each observation time a different scalar may be chosen, but that scalar must be held fixed in all three evaluations $\mathbf c_\lambda(t)$, $\mathbf c_\lambda(s_1)$ and $\mathbf c_\lambda(s_2)$. Evaluating a moving selection $\lambda(s_i)$ at the delayed times would define a different expression.

### Strong minimum argument and exclusions

Taking $C\delta^2/2<1$ forces $m=0$. For the left-hand interval, reverse time; $b$ changes sign and the same proof applies. The zero set is then both open and closed in the connected interval. This separately supports Lemma 2.

At an attained all-past angular maximum, put $\varphi=\vartheta_{\max}-\vartheta$. Delayed $\varphi$ is nonnegative. Since $y=\varphi(s)-\varphi(t)\in(-\pi,\pi)$, $\sin y\ge-\varphi(t)$: for positive $y$ the left side is nonnegative, and for negative $y$ it is at least $y\ge-\varphi(t)$. Thus the mirror angular equation falls under the minimum argument. If $\vartheta\to-\infty$ in the past, every earlier running maximum is attained; any subsequent retreat would make an interior maximum over a slightly larger past interval and force constancy. Hence the angle and mirror angular rate are nondecreasing in this positive-winding orientation.

For a repeating separation with positive net turning, the periodic angular rate must then be constant.
