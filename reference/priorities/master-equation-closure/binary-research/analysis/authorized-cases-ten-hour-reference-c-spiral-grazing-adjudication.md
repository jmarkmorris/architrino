## A further actual-family consequence from finite-time dependence

The blind reference also identifies a useful separate theorem: possible first unit-arrival times, and their corresponding source times, escape every fixed finite bound as the family tends to its base. Here is the quantifier argument explicitly. Fix a finite T. The exact base and complete preparation have positive speed, separation and root margins through T. The same compatible finite-time dependence used in the nonlinear departure proof gives a sufficiently small family interval on which every member remains regular and subfield through T. Its first unit arrival, if finite, is therefore later than T. Since T was arbitrary, any sequence of such first-arrival times for perturbations tending to zero tends to infinity.

For a prescribed finite source threshold M, choose a fixed T so large that the base source clock at T exceeds $M+1$. Shrink the family interval so that it has no unit arrival through T and its simple source root at T differs from the base root by less than one. A later first-arrival source time is at least this value because the source clock is increasing. It therefore exceeds M. This proves divergence of those source times too, without assuming that the perturbed clock has a uniform all-future derivative or that a finite arrival exists. It does not give a numerical perturbation threshold or a rate in the family parameter.

Accordingly, geometry of the initially prescribed analytic source segment cannot by itself select a hypothetical asymptotically late grazing endpoint. A source-sensitive actual-flow inequality is still required. The falsifiers of the algebraic assessment are incorrect exact substitutions or curvature signs; the further family theorem would fail if the necessary fixed-time compatible dependence or simple clock continuity did not hold.
