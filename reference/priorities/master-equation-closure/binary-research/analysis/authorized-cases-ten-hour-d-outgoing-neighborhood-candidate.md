## An explicit outgoing criterion

At a receiving time translated to zero, set

$$
Z=X_+-X_-,\quad d=|Z|,\quad N=Z/d,\quad U=V_+-V_-,\quad q=N\cdot U.
$$

The complete supplied speed is at most $b_0<\beta<1$. Define

$$
C_\beta=\frac{K(1+\beta)^2}{1-\beta},\qquad
\gamma^2=q_0^2-\frac{4C_\beta}{d_0}.
$$

Suppose

$$
q_0>0,\qquad \gamma^2>0,\qquad
b_0+\frac{C_\beta}{\gamma d_0}<\beta.
\tag{1}
$$

Then the candidate conclusion is unique all-future ordinary continuation with

$$
d(t)\ge d_0+\gamma t,\qquad q(t)\ge\gamma>0,\qquad
|V_i(t)|\le b_0+\frac{C_\beta}{\gamma d_0}<\beta.
\tag{2}
$$

Both individual velocities converge, and their limiting relative velocity has norm at least $\gamma$. The common-center velocity also converges. This is an explicit open sufficient set of complete nonmirror histories. It is not an assertion that every dispersing history lies in that set.

### Complete roots and the two moving source clocks

Under the provisional complete speed bound $\beta$, each receiver has one partner root and no positive-delay self root. Indeed, the partner gap is strictly monotone in source time with derivative $1-n\cdot V_j(s)\ge1-\beta$, changes sign between the remote past and reception, and has positive reception separation. The uniform complete-past speed bound makes its remote sign definite. The strict chord inequality excludes self roots on the complete past and constructed future together.

For either receiver the delayed range $R_i=t-s_i(t)$ obeys

$$
\frac{d(t)}{1+\beta}\le R_i(t)\le\frac{d(t)}{1-\beta},\qquad
1-n_i\cdot V_j(s_i)\ge1-\beta.
\tag{3}
$$

The two source clocks need not agree. Each satisfies the separate identity and bounds

$$
s_i'(t)=\frac{1-n_i\cdot V_i(t)}{1-n_i\cdot V_j(s_i(t))},\qquad
\frac{1-\beta}{1+\beta}\le s_i'(t)\le\frac{1+\beta}{1-\beta}.
\tag{4}
$$

Thus neither clock stops, reverses, or loses its ordinary-root margin in this bootstrap. The canonical partner row and (3) imply $|A_i(t)|\le C_\beta/d(t)^2$ independently for each label.

### Relative and common-center motion

Exact differentiation gives

$$
d''=\frac{|U|^2-q^2}{d}+N\cdot(A_+-A_-)\ge-\frac{2C_\beta}{d^2}.
\tag{5}
$$

On any interval where $q>0$, multiplying by $2q=2d'$ and integrating yields

$$
q(t)^2\ge q_0^2-4C_\beta\left(\frac1{d_0}-\frac1{d(t)}\right)>\gamma^2.
\tag{6}
$$

A first loss of $q\ge\gamma$ is impossible. Hence $d\ge d_0+\gamma t$ and

$$
\int_0^t|A_i(u)|\,du\le\frac{C_\beta}{\gamma d_0}.
\tag{7}
$$

The last strict inequality in (1) then prevents the provisional speed boundary. For $C=(X_++X_-)/2$, one has $|C''|\le C_\beta/d^2$, so its velocity is controlled by the same integrable bound. This does not subtract a moving frame from the equation; it estimates the actual center motion in the fixed substrate frame.

Every finite time interval has positive separation and range floors, positive transmitter and receiver root-factor floors, a finite delay ceiling, and locally Lipschitz retained source velocities. The ordinary method-of-steps integral map therefore continues uniquely across every finite endpoint. The acceleration integrals converge absolutely by (7), proving the velocity limits. Since $|U(t)|\ge q(t)\ge\gamma$, their relative limit has norm at least $\gamma$. All statements concern the unchanged coupled pair.

## An explicit neighborhood at a finite receiving time

Let a reference complete history at time $T$ have separation $\bar d$, relative velocity $\bar U$, outward radial speed $\bar q$, and complete speed bound $\bar b$. Permit independent perturbations of both full histories, with present position errors at most $\delta_x$ and complete velocity errors at most $\delta_v$ for each label. Fix the irrelevant common constant translation before measuring position errors. Put

$$
D=\bar d-2\delta_x,\qquad
Q=\bar q-2\delta_v-\frac{4\delta_x|\bar U|}{\bar d},\qquad
b=\bar b+\delta_v.
$$

The elementary normalization inequality $|z/|z|-\bar z/|\bar z||\le2|z-\bar z|/|\bar z|$ gives $d\ge D$ and $q\ge Q$. Consequently the explicit conditions

$$
D>0,\quad Q>0,\quad
G^2:=Q^2-\frac{4C_\beta}{D}>0,\quad
b+\frac{C_\beta}{GD}<\beta<1
\tag{8}
$$

admit every such complete perturbed history into the outgoing theorem. These inequalities include both clocks and unrestricted common-center, relative and out-of-plane perturbations. They use the complete velocity norm, not one present speed sample.

A suitable norm on release histories, with fixed positive length and time units $L_*,T_*$, is

$$
\|\Delta\phi\|_*=\max_i\left\{
\sup_{s\le0}\frac{|\Delta X_i(s)|}{L_*(1+|s|/T_*)},\quad
\frac{T_*}{L_*}\sup_{s\le0}|\Delta V_i(s)|
\right\}.
\tag{9}
$$

The class consists of complete $C^1$ histories with locally Lipschitz velocities and a common local Lipschitz bound on every source interval used for finite-time comparison. The weighted position norm allows a small constant center-velocity difference to accumulate displacement. On each finite causal window it still bounds the source-position errors. An additional uniform acceleration bound on the relevant recent interval supplies an explicit finite-time Lipschitz modulus for source-velocity evaluation; mere $C^0$ closeness of positions does not supply it.

For clarity, a finite-window comparison can be written directly. Assume both compared paths lie in a tube with source ranges at least $a>0$, speeds at most $\beta$, and source-velocity Lipschitz constant at most $L$. Write $m=1-\beta$, and let $E_x,E_v$ bound position and velocity differences on that complete finite source window. Source monotonicity gives $|\Delta s_i|\le2E_x/m$, hence

$$
|\Delta S_i|\le\frac{2E_x}{m},\qquad
|\Delta V_j(s_i)|\le E_v+\frac{2LE_x}{m}.
$$

For the row $F(S,v)=-KS/(|S|^3[1-\widehat S\cdot v])$, segmentwise bounds within a common admissible comparison tube are

$$
L_S=K\left(\frac2{a^3m}+\frac\beta{a^3m^2}\right),\qquad
L_V=\frac K{a^2m^2},
$$

so

$$
|\Delta A_i|\le\frac{2(L_S+LL_V)}mE_x+L_VE_v.
\tag{10}
$$

The segment requirement matters: endpoint range floors alone do not guarantee that an interpolating vector avoids zero. A small tube about the reference, with its own smaller common range floor, supplies that requirement. Applying the position-velocity integral inequality on that finite interval yields an explicit exponential continuity bound after choosing the units in (9). Combining it with strict margins in (8) gives a nonzero release neighborhood whenever a reference finite-time outgoing witness is available. This is a conditional construction; this note has not supplied such a witness for the selected slow preparation or evaluated its resulting radius.

## Exact limit of the transfer to the slow mirror theorem

If a selected mirror member has a strictly positive terminal outward speed, the accepted theorem already gives $d\to\infty$ and a strict complete speed margin. Eventually $q$ has a positive lower bound, the inverse-distance term in (1) vanishes, and the remaining impulse margin can be made arbitrarily small. That additional premise would therefore provide a finite outgoing witness and an open nonmirror neighborhood through (8)–(10).

The accepted theorem instead states only a nonnegative terminal speed. Its zero-speed branch need not satisfy $q^2>4C_\beta/d$; radius tending to infinity and integrable acceleration alone do not imply that strict inequality. The precise unsupported transfer premise is therefore a positive terminal-speed or other strict finite outgoing margin for a specified member. Failure to obtain that premise is a limitation of this comparison argument, not a counterexample to nonmirror dispersal. The literal historical source also still needs its complete source identity and separate membership in the resulting neighborhood.

## Analytical controls and falsifiers

The radial comparison in (5)–(6) reduces to direct integration of the scalar control $d''=-2C_\beta/d^2$, where equality gives $q^2=q_0^2-4C_\beta(1/d_0-1/d)$. Constant separated subfield histories give the root bounds (3) directly; an affine source gives (4) by differentiating its explicit causal gap. These are elementary analytical controls, not target trajectories. No computational instrument is used here.

Independent review must check the full-past root exclusion, both moving clocks, continuation regularity, the speed bootstrap, and the finite-window row constants. A history satisfying (1) but losing separation, a root outside the complete census, an acceleration exceeding its stated bound, or an error in (5)–(10) would defeat the relevant claim. A slow member that never satisfies (1) does not refute this sufficient criterion and supplies no physical obstruction by itself.
