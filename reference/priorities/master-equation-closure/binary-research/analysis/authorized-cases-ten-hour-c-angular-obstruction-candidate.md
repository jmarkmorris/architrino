# Logarithmic mirror pairs cannot disperse with finite total angle under a uniform subfield margin

## Fixed case and status

**Derived candidate, awaiting independent assessment.** This Package C argument concerns any continuation of the already admitted mirror-planar compatible perturbation family in the [nonlinear departure theorem](alternatives-screen-2026-10-05-logarithmic-spiral-nonlinear-adjudication.md), provided its complete supplied-plus-evolved history retains a fixed speed bound $\beta<1$, positive separation and the ordinary equation for all future time. It selects no new preparation or geometry. The equation is the registered inverse-distance response, with unchanged opposite polarity, $K_{\log}=c_f=1$, all ordinary roots and the existing diagonal exclusion. The canonical inverse-square law is a separately labeled algebraic control only.

This is a conditional restriction on later fate. It neither proves that the perturbed solution remains uniformly subfield nor determines its next event after local departure. It does show that one particular proposed destination, the canonical mirror theorem's finite-angle outward regime, is unavailable under the stated logarithmic hypotheses.

## Complete roots and a persistent inward radial contribution

Write the positive member as $x(t)$ and the negative member as $-x(t)$, with $r=|x|>0$, $e=x/r$, $v=x'$, $p=e\cdot v$ and signed tangential velocity $z$. The complete speed bound is $|v(s)|\le\beta<1$ at every supplied and evolved time. It gives exactly one partner root and no positive-delay self roots by the complete-past monotonic-gap and strict-chord arguments. At that partner root $s<t$, set

$$
R=t-s=|x(t)+x(s)|,\quad n=\frac{x(t)+x(s)}R,\quad D=1+n\cdot v(s).
$$

The exact acceleration is $A=-n/(RD)$. In particular $1-\beta\le D\le1+\beta$. Put $\Delta=x(s)-x(t)$. The complete speed bound gives $|\Delta|\le\beta R$ and $Rn=2re+\Delta$, whence

$$
\frac{2r}{1+\beta}\le R\le\frac{2r}{1-\beta},
$$

$$
\frac{e\cdot n}{R}
=\frac{R^2+4r^2-|\Delta|^2}{4rR^2}
\ge\frac{1-\beta^2}{4r}+\frac r{R^2}
\ge\frac{1-\beta}{2r}.
\tag{1}
$$

Every inequality uses the actual delayed source, not an instantaneous replacement. Consequently the radial contribution has the uniform signed bound

$$
r\,e\cdot A\le-c_\beta,\qquad
c_\beta:=\frac{1-\beta}{2(1+\beta)}>0.
\tag{2}
$$

No assumption about the sign of the angular velocity or monotonicity of the radius has entered.

## An angular-variation lower bound

The exact radial identity is $p'=z^2/r+e\cdot A$. Introduce the increasing geometric time

$$
\tau(t)=\int_0^t\frac{du}{r(u)}.
$$

On every finite regular interval it is well defined, and (2) gives

$$
\frac{dp}{d\tau}\le z^2-c_\beta.
$$

Integrating and using $|p|\le\beta$ yields

$$
\int_0^{\tau(t)}z^2\,d\sigma
\ge c_\beta\tau(t)+p(t)-p(0)
\ge c_\beta\tau(t)-2\beta.
\tag{3}
$$

Since $z^2\le\beta|z|$ and $|d\theta|=|z|\,d\tau$, the total angular variation satisfies the explicit lower bound

$$
\operatorname{Var}_{[0,t]}\theta
\ge\frac{c_\beta}{\beta}\tau(t)-2
\ge\frac{c_\beta}{\beta^2}
\log\left(1+\frac{\beta t}{r(0)}\right)-2.
\tag{4}
$$

The last inequality follows from $r(t)\le r(0)+\beta t$. For $\beta=0$ the nonzero attractive row already excludes an all-future stationary solution; (4) is stated for $0<\beta<1$. A negative right side at early times is simply a vacuous lower bound.

Thus every all-future regular continuation with this uniform complete-history margin has infinite total angular variation. If its angular velocity eventually has one sign, its unwrapped angle diverges in that sign. Without that additional sign condition, the theorem asserts variation, not infinitely many complete revolutions. It does not exclude bounded-angle oscillations with infinite variation.

In particular, a logarithmic continuation cannot satisfy all of: complete uniform subfield margin, all-future regular separation, and finite total angular variation. This restriction applies whether the radius tends to infinity, remains bounded or has repeated excursions. The later fate of the selected departing family still requires proving which of those hypotheses persists or fails.

## A quantitative low-speed obstruction

Because $z^2\le\beta^2$, the same radial inequality also gives

$$
p(t)\le p(0)-(c_\beta-\beta^2)\tau(t).
$$

When $c_\beta>\beta^2$, an all-future regular solution with that complete speed bound is impossible: the right side eventually falls below $-\beta$. For example, at the exact rational bound $\beta=2/5$,

$$
c_\beta=\frac3{14},\qquad c_\beta-\beta^2=\frac{19}{350}>0.
$$

Any such regular interval must obey

$$
\tau(t)\le\frac{p(0)+\beta}{c_\beta-\beta^2},\qquad
t\le\frac{r(0)}\beta\left[
\exp\left(\frac{\beta[p(0)+\beta]}{c_\beta-\beta^2}\right)-1\right].
\tag{5}
$$

This is a sufficient-domain obstruction, not a physical speed ceiling, a new low-speed case launch, or a claim that the admitted spiral has speed at most $2/5$. It says that preserving such a small complete speed bound indefinitely would contradict the unchanged acceleration equation. Which speed, separation or regularity condition first fails is not determined here.

## Canonical control and relation to the exact spiral

For the canonical inverse-square control, the same geometry gives $e\cdot A\le-c_\beta(1-\beta)/(2r^2)$ with the fixed canonical coefficient one. After the same change of time, its inward term decays as $1/r$. It therefore does not give the persistent negative constant in (2) and does not imply (4). This identifies the mathematical distinction permitting the accepted canonical finite-angle dispersal theorem; it does not use that theorem as a logarithmic premise.

For the exact admitted logarithmic spiral $r=a(1+t)$, $p=a$ and $z=a\omega$ are constant. Its accepted exact radial balance gives $r\,e\cdot A=-a^2\omega^2=-z^2$, so $dp/d\tau=0$. Its angle grows logarithmically and its total variation is infinite. These exact identities are an analytical consistency control, not a repeated balance computation or a new fate result. The new content is the necessary angular-variation bound for a disturbed coupled continuation that retains the stated complete speed margin.

## Evidence boundary and falsifiers

The derivation uses only the registered row, complete-history root census, the chord estimate and elementary radial calculus. No numerical instrument or trajectory is used. Independent review must check the sign in (1), the inverse-distance coefficient in (2), the direction of the integrated inequality (3), and the distinction between angle and total variation. A regular all-future solution with the stated complete margin and finite total variation, a delayed chord violating (1), or an error in those inequalities would refute the corresponding claim. Losing a uniform margin while staying pointwise below unit speed is outside this theorem and is a possible unresolved boundary approach, not a refutation.
