## 1. Exact case family and first quantitative dependency

The law is the already selected ordinary partner-branch amplitude gradient at fixed reception time, with the implicit source clock differentiated. It is not the canonical response or Maxwell E. Write $X_\pm=\pm R_0 y(s)$, $s=v_0T/R_0$, $v_0^2=K/(4R_0)$, $c_f=1$ and $\epsilon=v_0$. The initial data are $y(0)=(1,0)$ and $y'(0)=(0,(1-\epsilon^2/2)^{-1/2})$. The accepted complete family has a fixed, nonshrinking recent window $[-a,0]$ carrying the degree-five polynomial

$$
P_\epsilon(s)=y(0)+s y'(0)+\sum_{m=2}^5J_m(\epsilon)\frac{s^m}{m!}.
\tag{B1}
$$

The unknown endpoint jets solve the exact four compatibility traces: acceleration and the next three reception derivatives. Its older extension uses a fixed cutoff outside this window and has finite, uniform scaled speed and derivative bounds. The source owner proves existence of such choices; it does not assign numerical $a$, cutoff, jet ball, speed bound $V$, or parameter radius. Those quantities must be fixed as one member or parameterization before constants are evaluated. The independent reference does not supply a replacement cutoff or smoother tail.

At $\epsilon=0$ the central circular endpoint gives the exact jets

$$
J_2=(-1,0),\quad J_3=(0,-1),\quad
J_4=(1,0),\quad J_5=(0,1).
\tag{B2}
$$

These follow by differentiating $y''=-y/|y|^3$ along $(\cos s,\sin s)$, as a central comparison control only. The compatibility Jacobian in $J_2,\ldots,J_5$ is triangular with identity diagonal at zero, hence invertible. This proves a qualitative branch, but supplies no numerical positive parameter radius by itself.

For a fixed parameterization, one sufficient explicit validation is to write its compatibility equations as $F(J,\epsilon)=0$, choose a fixed inverse approximation $A$ and a closed jet ball $\|J-J_*\|\le\rho$, and prove uniformly for $0\le\epsilon\le\epsilon_*$ that

$$
q:=\sup\|I-A\partial_JF\|<1,
\qquad
\sup\|AF(J_*,\epsilon)\|\le(1-q)\rho.
\tag{B3}
$$

The map $J\mapsto J-AF(J,\epsilon)$ then maps the ball into itself and contracts. Its entire root chart must remain inside the same recent polynomial window. On the original radius/speed box, $5\epsilon_*<a$ is a simple sufficient window condition. The older cutoff must independently establish $\epsilon_*V\le1/8$ and bounded sixth derivatives. An unspecified finite $V$ or an unevaluated implicit-function neighborhood is the first unsupported numerical inequality for any proposed speed, even though the accepted existential theorem remains valid.

### Case-only handoff and a sharper complete-speed bound

Before this reference freeze, the coordinator supplied only the [new case specification](authorized-cases-ten-hour-b-case.md), without subject conclusions. It fixes $K=1$, $a=1/16$, the local central compatibility branch of (B1), and the older extension $y=e_1+\chi(P_\epsilon-e_1)$, with $\chi(s)=S(16s+2)$ and $S(t)=f(t)/(f(t)+f(1-t))$, $f(t)=e^{-1/t}$ for $t>0$ and zero otherwise. These are choices of parameters left free by the original fixed-window construction. The older source never fixed a different cutoff. This is an allowed parameterization, conditional on proving its exact compatible branch and bounds; it is not a replacement of a previously fixed literal past.

The proposed derivative recursion is sound. If $f^{(k)}=e^{-x}P_k(x)$, $x=1/t$, then $P_{k+1}=x^2(P_k-P_k')$. Its coefficient absolute sum grows by at most $2k+1$, hence is bounded by $2^k k!$. The denominator $f(t)+f(1-t)$ is at least $e^{-2}$. Repeated product differentiation of its reciprocal therefore gives the case file's stated finite sixth-derivative bounds. Flatness at both cutoff endpoints supplies matching derivatives; the zero sixth derivative on the polynomial window does not forbid a generated sixth-derivative jump at release.

A sharper first-derivative bound avoids carrying those deliberately large higher-order constants into the complete-speed restriction. On $(0,1)$ put $g(t)=f(1-t)$ and $D_f=f+g$. Since $x^2e^{-x}\le4e^{-2}$ for $x\ge1$, both $|f'|$ and $|g'|$ are at most $4e^{-2}$. Then

$$
0\le S'=\frac{f'g-fg'}{D_f^2}
\le\frac{4e^{-2}(f+g)}{D_f^2}\le4.
\tag{B3a}
$$

For the proposed jet ball $|J_m-J_m(0)|\le1$ and $\epsilon\le1/16$, all polynomial derivative coefficients have norm at most two. On $[-1/8,0]$, the exponential-series bounds give $|P-e_1|<1/3$ and $|P'|<3$. Thus $|\chi'|\le64$ yields

$$
V_{\rm past}\le3+\frac{64}{3}=\frac{73}{3}.
\tag{B3b}
$$

The explicitly nonempty interval $0<\epsilon\le3/584$ therefore satisfies the complete supplied-past speed condition $\epsilon V_{\rm past}\le1/8$, **conditional on the jet ball and compatible branch existing throughout it**. It also has $5\epsilon<1/16$, so the initial partner root remains in the polynomial window under the original geometry box. This is a conditional preparation bound, not an admitted dispersal range or proof of the compatibility interval. The first still unevaluated requirement is (B3) or an equivalent explicit compatible-branch theorem on a stated positive interval.

## 2. Explicit ordinary-root and highest-jet constants

Assume complete physical speed $\epsilon|y'|\le\beta=1/8$, positive current radius $r$, and the original mirror census. From the chord estimate,

$$
\frac{16r}{9}\le L\le\frac{16r}{7},\quad
D\ge\frac78,\quad
\frac79\le s_d'\le\frac97,\quad
\frac57\le\frac{r_d}{r}\le\frac97.
\tag{B4}
$$

The source-radius bounds follow from $|y(s)-y(s_d)|\le\beta L\le2r/7$. The complete speed hypothesis proves exactly one partner root and excludes every positive-delay self root; (B4) alone at the current sample would not prove completeness.

The exact normalized equation is

$$
y''=-\frac4{L^2D^3}
\left[(1-\epsilon^2|v_d|^2)n+\epsilon Dv_d
-\epsilon^2Ln(n\cdot a_d)\right].
\tag{B5}
$$

Differentiating $k$ times, $0\le k\le4$, the highest sampled jet $y_d^{(k+2)}$ has coefficient $4\epsilon^2nn^{\mathsf T}(s_d')^k/(LD^3)$. All derivatives of that coefficient involve strictly lower source jets. With parabolic weights $r^{\alpha_m}$, $\alpha_m=3m/2-1$, its weighted norm is at most

$$
C_k\frac{\epsilon^2}{r},\qquad
C_k=\frac{1152}{343}\left(\frac97\right)^k
\left(\frac75\right)^{3k/2+2}.
\tag{B6}
$$

Every factor grows with $k$, so a common rational upper bound is

$$
C_k\le C_4=\frac{52907904}{390625}<136.
\tag{B7}
$$

Consequently $272\epsilon_*^2\le r_*$ suffices to make every highest-jet transmission factor at most one half on $r\ge r_*$. Strict admission can use $272\epsilon_*^2<r_*$. This quantifies one previously existential constant, not the lower-jet suprema or the entry radius. For each lower expression $N_k$ on its fixed normalized box, a valid induction still requires $M_{k+2}>2\max(N_k,M_{k+2,e})$. The box and all previously selected lower constants must be stated when evaluating $N_k$; merely renaming a finite supremum is not a numerical bound.

For acceleration alone, the sharper delayed weight is $C_0=1152/175<7$. The non-acceleration row obeys

## 3. Remaining admission chain

The preparation bounds enter the finite secular theorem, then the changing-scale and signed-mode theorem. A quantitative entry must fix one compact comparison level below its outer boundary, evaluate its coordinate and remainder constants, and show that the actual selected history reaches it with strict signed amplitude and inherited sixth-jet control. In particular the asymptotic amplitude $A=(4\sqrt2/3)\epsilon^3H^{3/2}[1+O(\epsilon)]$ needs an actual bound on the relative-error constant to keep its sign. The qualitative entry relation $H_e\asymp\epsilon^{-2}$ needs explicit two-sided constants before it supplies $r_*$ or $M_{m,e}$. A short regular numerical trajectory is not a substitute.

The later radius-weighted expansion has $F_0=-y/r^3$, cubic term $-(4/3)\epsilon^3 F_0'$, and $|Q_4|\le C_Q\epsilon^4r^{-4}$. Its corrected scalar

$$
\mathcal E=\frac{|v|^2}{2}-\frac1r
-\frac{\epsilon^2h^2}{2r^3}
-\frac{\gamma p}{r^2},\qquad
\gamma=\frac43\epsilon^3,
\tag{B9}
$$

is mathematical bookkeeping, with $p=r'$ and $h=y\times v$. Direct differentiation cancels the quadratic radial work and gives

$$
\mathcal E'=\gamma y''\cdot F_0
-\frac{\epsilon^2hh'}{r^3}+v\cdot Q_4.
\tag{B10}
$$

If the total residual is explicitly bounded by $C_E\epsilon^4r^{-9/2}$, then $\epsilon_*/\sqrt{r_*}<2/(3C_E)$ guarantees $\mathcal E'>\gamma/(2r^4)$. This is an explicit inequality only after $C_E$ and $r_*$ are evaluated. Similarly, from $H'/H=\gamma/r^3+rQ_\theta/h$, the sufficient relative-sign condition is $\epsilon_*<2h_*/(3C_Q)$ when $h\ge h_*$. The lower angular margin, lower radius and speed bootstrap must close together; they cannot each assume the other has already been proved.

If $\mathcal E$ stays nonpositive, its strict rate and speed/radius bounds yield $r\to\infty$ and speed tending to zero. If it crosses zero, admission additionally needs explicit constants for the small-outer-angle passage and compact transverse-section comparison, followed by the invariant outward chart until $r\mathcal E=M>4C_A$. The exact tail then has $r_tp_t^2\ge2M+1>4C_A$ and $|a|\le C_A/r^2$. Radial integration gives $p^2\ge p_t^2-2C_A/r_t>p_t^2/2$, while vector acceleration is integrable. The same full history and bounded sixth seams continue; neither a reset past nor a parabolic speed-decay assumption is allowed on the positive-terminal-speed tail.

The numerical admission threshold must be the minimum of all independently evaluated preparation, compatibility, root, weighted-jet, entry, account, passage and tail restrictions. A positive number produced for only (B7) or (B8) is not a dispersal threshold. Choosing which terminal branch occurs is a separate question after this chain closes.
