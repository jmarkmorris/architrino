Second reader's check of the [exploration note](two-member-subwake-periodicity-exploration-2026-10-06.md). Units: wake speed $c_f=1$, coupling $K=1$. The rate of the relative angular momentum was derived by the reader before the subject's derivations were read, and is reproduced under "Pre-target reference". The reading was time boxed; what was not checked is listed under "Limits".

## Verdict

- **Item 1 (exact rate and bound on the aberration angles): accept.** The rate, the sign convention and the bound agree with the pre-target reference (derived twice, independently; algebra also measured by the reader's own session script on 1484 prescribed curved histories, largest relative difference $4\times10^{-11}$). One proviso in the proof is vacuous and one sharper bound is available (Corrections 1 and 2).
- **Item 2 (conditional exclusion; contains the mirror-pair statement): accept with corrections.** The proof is sound, including the claim that a constant drift and a uniformly rotating frame do not matter, and the reduction to the mirror pair is exact. The summary overstates strictness, and the sufficient condition in velocities is worded with the wrong sign for member $2$ (Corrections 3 and 4).
- **Item 3 (rate of $K_i$ and its conditional exclusion): accept.** The rate, the positivity of the emission-time rate, the invariance of $K$ and the stated coverage (not a rotating frame whose centre drifts) are all correct. The summary omits the "not both identically zero" clause (Correction 3).
- **Item 4 (straight-window closed form; no pointwise sign): accept with corrections.** The closed form is correct (derived independently; measured on 1988 straight pairs to $4\times10^{-9}$, limited by the reader's finite-difference velocities). The conclusion needs its grade split by class of history (Correction 5).
- **Item 5 (necessary features of a counterexample): accept with corrections.** Parts (a), (b), (c) are correct. The wording of (a) and of summary item 5 says more than is proved (Correction 6). Three further necessary features follow at once and are added under "On the open question".
- **Corollary 2a (mirror pair): accept.** **Corollary 2b (in line through a fixed centre): accept, with a sharper condition and a completed proof** (Correction 7). **Corollary 2c (polar form; fixed circles): the two identities are accepted; the summary sentence about fixed circles drops two conditions** (Correction 8). **Corollary 2d (three dimensions): accept.**
- **Open question: not closed.** A new exact identity for a pair with a common velocity is established (derived, checked numerically), which changes what an unconditional proof or a counterexample search should look like. See "On the open question".

## Pre-target reference

**Setting.** Two architrinos at $\mathbf x_1(t)$, $\mathbf x_2(t)$ in a plane, polarity product $-1$, speeds below $1$. Member $1$ receives one row, emitted by member $2$ at $s_1<t$ with causal delay $\tau_1=t-s_1=\lvert\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1)\rvert$. With $\mathbf n_1=(\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1))/\tau_1$ and $D_1=1-\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf v_2(s_1)>0$ the equation gives $\mathbf a_1=-\mathbf n_1/(\tau_1^2D_1)$. Member $2$ likewise, with $s_2$, $\tau_2$, $\mathbf n_2=(\mathbf x_2(t)-\mathbf x_1(s_2))/\tau_2$, $D_2=1-\mathbf n_2\cdot\mathbf v_1(s_2)$, $\mathbf a_2=-\mathbf n_2/(\tau_2^2D_2)$. Write $\mathbf r=\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2$, $r=\lvert\mathbf r\rvert$, $\hat{\mathbf r}=\mathbf r/r$, and $\mathbf a\times\mathbf b=a_1b_2-a_2b_1$.

**Rate (derived).** $J=\mathbf r\times(\mathbf v_1-\mathbf v_2)$ has $dJ/dt=\mathbf r\times(\mathbf a_1-\mathbf a_2)$, because the velocity difference crossed with itself is zero. Hence

$$
\frac{dJ}{dt}=-\frac{\mathbf r\times\mathbf n_1}{\tau_1^2D_1}+\frac{\mathbf r\times\mathbf n_2}{\tau_2^2D_2}.
$$

**Geometric form (derived).** With no delay, $\mathbf n_1$ would be $\hat{\mathbf r}$ and $\mathbf n_2$ would be $-\hat{\mathbf r}$. Define $\beta_1$ as the signed angle, counterclockwise positive, from $\mathbf n_1$ to $\hat{\mathbf r}$, and $\beta_2$ as the signed angle from $\mathbf n_2$ to $-\hat{\mathbf r}$. Then $\hat{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf n_1=-\sin\beta_1$ and $\hat{\mathbf r}\times\mathbf n_2=\sin\beta_2$, so

$$
\frac{dJ}{dt}=r\left[\frac{\sin\beta_1}{\tau_1^2D_1}+\frac{\sin\beta_2}{\tau_2^2D_2}\right].
$$

**Displacement form (derived).** Let $\boldsymbol\Delta_2=\mathbf x_2(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1)$ and $\boldsymbol\Delta_1=\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_1(s_2)$ be each member's displacement over the delay window of the row its partner receives. Then $\tau_1\mathbf n_1=\mathbf r+\boldsymbol\Delta_2$ and $\tau_2\mathbf n_2=-\mathbf r+\boldsymbol\Delta_1$, so $\sin\beta_1=-(\hat{\mathbf r}\times\boldsymbol\Delta_2)/\tau_1$, $\sin\beta_2=(\hat{\mathbf r}\times\boldsymbol\Delta_1)/\tau_2$ and $dJ/dt=\mathbf r\times[\boldsymbol\Delta_1/(\tau_2^3D_2)-\boldsymbol\Delta_2/(\tau_1^3D_1)]$.

**Bound (derived).** In the triangle $\mathbf x_1(t)$, $\mathbf x_2(t)$, $\mathbf x_2(s_1)$ the angle at $\mathbf x_1(t)$ is $\lvert\beta_1\rvert$ and the side opposite it is $\lvert\boldsymbol\Delta_2\rvert$, which is less than $\tau_1$, the side opposite the angle at $\mathbf x_2(t)$. The smaller of two angles of a triangle is acute, and the law of sines gives $\lvert\sin\beta_1\rvert\le\lvert\boldsymbol\Delta_2\rvert/\tau_1<1$. So $\lvert\beta_1\rvert\le\arcsin(\lvert\boldsymbol\Delta_2\rvert/\tau_1)<\pi/2$, and the same for $\beta_2$.

**Sign check on a known case (derived, then measured).** For a rigid mirror pair on a circle of radius $R$ turning counterclockwise at rate $\omega$, the inscribed-angle theorem gives $\beta_1=\beta_2=\omega\tau/2>0$, so $J$ increases, in agreement with the [mirror-pair statement](mirror-pair-angular-momentum-monotonicity.md). The reader's script returned $\beta_1=\beta_2=\omega\tau/2=0.450184$ for $R=1$, $\omega=0.5$, and the three forms of the rate agreed to $10^{-16}$; this known case was run before any other use of the script.

## Item-by-item check

**Item 1.** The subject's angle "at $\mathbf x_1(t)$ from the direction of $\mathbf x_2(s_1)$ to the direction of $\mathbf x_2(t)$" is the angle from $-\mathbf n_1$ to $-\hat{\mathbf r}$, which equals the reader's angle from $\mathbf n_1$ to $\hat{\mathbf r}$; the subject's $\beta_2$ is the angle from $-\mathbf n_2$ to $\hat{\mathbf r}$, which equals the reader's. The two displacement forms are identical term by term. The disc argument for the bound is correct and equivalent to the triangle argument. The row census is correct: the root function is strictly decreasing with rate $-1+\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v_j<0$, it equals $-r<0$ at $s=t$, and for a history of the form constant drift plus bounded it tends to $+\infty$ because $\lvert\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_j(s)\rvert\le C+\lvert\mathbf u\rvert(t-s)$ with $\lvert\mathbf u\rvert<1$. The bounds $0<D_i<2$ are correct. The statement that the swept angle about $\mathbf x_1(t)$ equals $\beta_1$ with no extra turn is correct.

**Item 2.** Write a relative-periodic history as $\mathbf x_i(t)=\mathbf c+\mathbf u\,t+R(\Omega t)\,\mathbf p_i(t)$ with $R$ a rotation and $\mathbf p_i$ of period $T$. Then $\mathbf r=R(\Omega t)(\mathbf p_1-\mathbf p_2)$, so $\mathbf r(t+T)=R(\Omega T)\mathbf r(t)$ and, differentiating, the same for the relative velocity; the drift and the centre cancel in the difference. $J$ is unchanged by a rotation, so $J(t+T)=J(t)$ (derived, confirmed). The proof does not need the aberration angles themselves to be periodic, and when the frame both rotates and drifts they are not; only $J$ periodic and $dJ/dt\ge0$ are used. Continuity of the two angles turns "not both identically zero" into a strictly positive integral. For a mirror pair $\mathbf x_2=-\mathbf x_1=-\mathbf x$ with $\mathbf x=\rho(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$: $\mathbf r\times\boldsymbol\Delta_1=2\rho(s)\rho(t)\sin(\theta(t)-\theta(s))$, the two terms are equal, $J=4\ell$, and the rate becomes $d\ell/dt=\rho(t)\rho(s)\sin(\theta(t)-\theta(s))/(\tau^3D)$, which is the mirror-pair rate for opposite polarity; $\tau\sin\beta=\rho(s)\sin(\theta(t)-\theta(s))$ follows from $r=2\rho(t)$. The hypothesis $\beta\ge0$ is then $\theta(t)\ge\theta(s)$ at every causal pair, which is the hypothesis of the mirror statement as weakened by Correction 4 of its [second reading](mirror-pair-angular-momentum-second-reading-2026-10-05.md), relaxed from strict to non-strict with the "not identically zero" clause. The containment claim is accepted. The three equivalent forms of the hypothesis are correct. The like-polarity and reversed-sign versions are correct.

**Item 3.** $dK_1/dt=(\mathbf v_1(t)-\dot s_1\mathbf v_2(s_1))\times\mathbf v_1(t)+(\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1))\times\mathbf a_1=\dot s_1\,\mathbf v_1(t)\times\mathbf v_2(s_1)$, since $\mathbf a_1$ is parallel to $\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1)$ for either polarity (derived, confirmed). Differentiating the delay condition gives $\dot s_1D_1=1-\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf v_1(t)>0$ (confirmed). $K$ is unchanged by a fixed rotation and a fixed shift of the whole history, and the emission times shift with the history, so $K$ is periodic for periodic histories, for rotation about a fixed centre, and for a pure constant drift. For rotation about a drifting centre, $\mathbf x_i(t+T)=R(\Omega T)\mathbf x_i(t)$ plus a shift that depends on $t$, so the velocities are not a rotation of the earlier ones and $K$ is not periodic; the subject excludes this case correctly. The mirror-pair reading $\mathbf v(s)\times\mathbf v(t)\ge0$ is correct. The comparison quantity $e_i$, the rates of $\ell_i$ and $M$, and $J=\ell_1+\ell_2-M$ were rederived and are correct. The statement that the hypotheses of Theorems 2 and 3 do not imply one another was not checked.

**Item 4.** For member $2$ uniform over its window, $\tau_1^2=r^2+2rv_{2\parallel}\tau_1+\lvert\mathbf v_2\rvert^2\tau_1^2$ gives $\tau_1=r(v_{2\parallel}+S_2)/(1-\lvert\mathbf v_2\rvert^2)$ and $\tau_1D_1=\tau_1(1-\lvert\mathbf v_2\rvert^2)-rv_{2\parallel}=rS_2$. With $1-\lvert\mathbf v_2\rvert^2=(S_2-v_{2\parallel})(S_2+v_{2\parallel})$ the member-$1$ term is $-(v_{2\perp}/r)(1-v_{2\parallel}/S_2)$, and exchanging the members with $\mathbf n\to-\mathbf n$ gives $(v_{1\perp}/r)(1+v_{1\parallel}/S_1)$. The closed form follows, with $J/r=v_{1\perp}-v_{2\perp}$ (derived, confirmed). The first-order expansion for $\mathbf a_1\mp\mathbf a_2$ and the rate $(w_\perp^2-w_\parallel^2)/r^2$ were rederived from the straight-window forms and are correct. The sufficient condition in Proposition 5 for $dJ/dt$ to have the sign of $J$ is correct. In the reader's sample $J\,dJ/dt<0$ in 200 of 1988 straight pairs, the same proportion as the subject's 219 of 2000.

**Item 5.** (a) follows from $J(t+T)=J(t)$ for every relative-periodic history. (b) follows from the coverage of Theorem 3. (c) Rotation about a fixed centre $O$ leaves each $\ell_i$ unchanged, $d\ell_1/dt=\mathbf x_1(t)\times\mathbf x_2(s_1)\,W_1$ with $W_1>0$, so the cross product vanishes identically or changes sign, and at a zero the emission point lies on the line through $O$ and the receiver, so the row is along that line. All three are accepted.

**Corollary 2b.** Both displayed products were rederived: $\mathbf r=(1+\lambda)\mathbf x_1$ and $-\mathbf r=(1+1/\lambda)\mathbf x_2$. The "automatic" clause is true but its stated reason is incomplete; see Correction 7.

**Corollary 2c.** Both identities were rederived by expanding $(\mathbf x_2(t)-\mathbf x_1(t))\times(\mathbf x_2(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1))$ and $(\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(t))\times(\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_1(s_2))$ in polar form and using $\sin a-\sin b=2\cos\frac{a+b}{2}\sin\frac{a-b}{2}$. They are correct as printed, signs included. The fixed-circle consequence is correct as stated in the body, where both angle conditions are kept.

**Corollary 2d.** The vector rate is the reader's displacement form with vector cross products. For rotation about a fixed axis direction the component of $\mathbf J$ along the axis is periodic. Accepted.

## Corrections

1. **Vacuous proviso in the bound paragraph.** The condition "provided the partner's path does not pass through $\mathbf x_1(t)$ during the window" always holds: the partner starts the window at distance $\tau_1$ from $\mathbf x_1(t)$ and travels less than $\tau_1$. Delete the proviso.
2. **Sharper bound.** $\lvert\sin\beta_1\rvert\le\lvert\boldsymbol\Delta_2\rvert/\tau_1$, which is at most the mean speed of member $2$ over the window, and likewise for $\beta_2$. The aberration angle is bounded by the arcsine of the partner's speed, not only by a quarter turn. This gives $\lvert dJ/dt\rvert\le r\,[\bar v_2/(\tau_1^2D_1)+\bar v_1/(\tau_2^2D_2)]$ with $\bar v_i$ the window-mean speeds.
3. **Strictness in the summary.** Summary item 2 says "$J$ increases over every interval". Under the stated hypothesis $J$ never decreases, and it strictly increases over every interval that contains an instant at which an aberration angle is positive; that is what Theorem 2 uses and all it needs. Summary item 3 should carry the same "not both identically zero" clause that Theorem 3 has.
4. **Sign in the velocity form of the hypothesis.** The sufficient condition after Theorem 2 should read: $\mathbf n\times\mathbf v_1(u)\ge0$ for $u$ in $[s_2,t]$ and $\mathbf n\times\mathbf v_2(u)\le0$ for $u$ in $[s_1,t]$, with $\mathbf n=\mathbf n(t)$ pointing from member $2$ to member $1$; in words, each member moves counterclockwise about its partner's present position. As written, "a non-negative component counterclockwise across $\mathbf n(t)$" for both members reads as $v_{1\perp}\ge0$ and $v_{2\perp}\ge0$ in the note's own notation, which is wrong for member $2$.
5. **Grade of "no pointwise sign".** The straight-window closed form is an exact kinematic identity, but a straight uniform window does not obey the equation along the window. The conclusion should be graded in three parts. Derived: no sign for $dJ/dt$ follows from the row identity of Theorem 1 alone, and no strictly monotone function of $J$ alone exists on all prescribed histories below wake speed. Measured: on released histories the sign fails. Inferred: it fails on histories that obey the equation on their whole past; the supporting estimate is that over a window of length about $r$ the acceleration, of size about $1/r^2$, displaces a member by an amount of order $1$ from its straight path, against a window displacement of order $r$ times its speed, so at large separation the straight-window sign is the true sign, but the existence of whole-past solutions with a prescribed oblique common velocity at large separation is not shown.
6. **Wording of Proposition 4(a) and summary item 5.** What is proved is that the two aberration angles are not both non-negative throughout and not both non-positive throughout, unless the weighted sum vanishes identically. Two angles of fixed opposite signs are not excluded. "In the sense of the pair's turning" presupposes one sense of turning, which is not assumed. State instead: unless the weighted sum is identically zero, at some instant one aberration angle is negative and at some instant one is positive.
7. **Corollary 2b, sharper and with a complete reason.** Let $\rho_i$ be the distances from the centre. If the common polar angle advanced by half a turn or more over member $2$'s window $[s_1,t]$, each member's path over that window would be at least $\rho_i(s_1)+\rho_i(t)$ long: to its first crossing of the ray opposite its starting direction at distance $\rho^*$ it travels at least $\rho_i(s_1)+\rho^*$, and from there at least $\lvert\rho_i(t)-\rho^*\rvert$. Each path is shorter than $\tau_1\le\rho_1(t)+\rho_2(s_1)$. Applying this to member $2$ gives a contradiction when $\rho_2(t)\ge\rho_1(t)$, and to member $1$ when $\rho_1(s_1)\ge\rho_2(s_1)$. So the advance is automatically below half a turn unless the receiver was the nearer member at emission and is the farther member at reception. This contains the subject's condition (same member farther at both ends) and is weaker. The first-crossing argument alone does not give it; the second piece of the path is needed.
8. **Fixed circles in the summary.** "Members on fixed circles turning non-uniformly in one sense within a quarter turn of opposition are excluded" drops two conditions that the body keeps. The angle condition is $\lvert\delta-\varsigma_2/2\rvert<\pi/2$ and $\lvert\delta+\varsigma_1/2\rvert<\pi/2$, that is, within a quarter turn of opposition after allowing half the window sweep. And each window sweep must lie between zero and half a turn; on fixed circles of radii $\rho_{\rm in}\le\rho_{\rm out}$ this is automatic for the outer member (its sweep is below $1+\rho_{\rm in}/\rho_{\rm out}\le2$ radians) and for the inner member when $\rho_{\rm out}<(\pi-1)\rho_{\rm in}$, and is otherwise an added hypothesis.

## On the open question

The question is not closed. No period-averaged identity with a sign and no certified counterexample was obtained in the time box. The following was established.

**A. More necessary features (derived).** (i) In a history that is periodic, or periodic up to a constant drift, each velocity is periodic, so each member's acceleration has zero mean over a period: $\int_0^T\mathbf n_i/(\tau_i^2D_i)\,dt=\mathbf 0$ for $i=1,2$. The direction of each member's row therefore cannot stay inside an open half plane; each row direction must spread over at least a half turn. (ii) In a strictly periodic history, Proposition 4(c) holds for every point $O$ of the plane, not for one centre, because every $\ell_i$ about every fixed point is periodic. (iii) By Correction 2, in any relative-periodic history the positive and negative parts of $\sin\beta_i$ are each bounded by the partner's window-mean speed, so the cancellation required by Proposition 4(a) is between terms each no larger than $r\,\bar v/(\tau^2D)$.

**B. The common-velocity terms are the gradient of a scalar function (derived by hand; checked numerically).** Take straight uniform windows on which both members have the same velocity $\mathbf V$, $\lvert\mathbf V\rvert<1$. With $V_\parallel=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V$, $V_\perp=\mathbf n\times\mathbf V$, $S=\sqrt{1-V_\perp^2}$ and $\mathbf n^\perp$ the unit vector a quarter turn counterclockwise from $\mathbf n$, the straight-window relations of item 4 give $\mathbf a_1=-(S-V_\parallel)[(S-V_\parallel)\mathbf n+\mathbf V]/(r^2S)$ and $\mathbf a_2=-(S+V_\parallel)[-(S+V_\parallel)\mathbf n+\mathbf V]/(r^2S)$, hence exactly

$$
\mathbf a_1-\mathbf a_2=-\frac{2S}{r^2}\,\mathbf n+\frac{2V_\parallel V_\perp}{S\,r^2}\,\mathbf n^\perp=-\nabla_{\mathbf r}U_{\mathbf V},\qquad U_{\mathbf V}(\mathbf r)=-\frac{2\sqrt{1-(\mathbf n\times\mathbf V)^2}}{r},
$$

$$
\mathbf a_1+\mathbf a_2=\frac{2}{r^2}\bigl[2(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V)\,\mathbf n-\mathbf V\bigr].
$$

The second line says that the velocity sum $\mathbf P=2\mathbf V$ changes at the rate of its own mirror image in the pair's axis, divided by $r^2$; this is the subject's first-order rule for $\mathbf P$ and it is exact in the common speed. The reader's script confirmed both lines on 500 random cases, to $2\times10^{-9}$ relative for the gradient (finite-difference gradient) and $2\times10^{-15}$ for the sum. The cross-axis part of the first line reproduces the subject's $r\,dJ/dt=2V_\perp V_\parallel/S$.

**Consequence 1 (derived within the straight-window, common-velocity model).** With $\mathbf V$ held fixed, the relative motion is an inverse-square attraction whose strength $2\sqrt{1-V^2\sin^2\alpha}$ depends on the angle $\alpha$ between the pair's axis and $\mathbf V$, and $E_{\mathbf V}=\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf w\rvert^2+U_{\mathbf V}(\mathbf r)$ is constant, where $\mathbf w$ is the relative velocity. The "second-order terms that take either sign" in item 4 are therefore not a loss or gain mechanism at this order; they are the direction-dependent part of a gradient, and they exchange $J$ back and forth as the axis turns. When $\mathbf V$ changes by the sum rule, $dE_{\mathbf V}/dt=\nabla_{\mathbf V}U_{\mathbf V}\cdot\dot{\mathbf V}=-2V_\perp^2/(S\,r^3)\le0$. This is one part of the rate of $E_{\mathbf V}$; the terms linear in the relative velocity are of the same size, about $1/r^3$, and for $\mathbf V=\mathbf 0$ they give $+(w_\perp^2-w_\parallel^2)/r^2$, so the full rate has no sign established here.

**Consequence 2 (inferred; no error bound, no numerical test).** Consider the regime in which a bound pair should be sought with small relative speed $\varepsilon$: separation of order $1/\varepsilon^2$, common speed $V$ of order one. Window curvature changes the rates by a relative amount of order $\varepsilon^2$, so through relative order $\varepsilon$ the equation reduces to an ordinary differential system with no delay: each member's acceleration is the straight-window closed form evaluated at the partner's present velocity, $\mathbf a_1=-(S_2-v_{2\parallel})[(S_2-v_{2\parallel})\mathbf n+\mathbf v_2]/(r^2S_2)$ and $\mathbf a_2=-(S_1+v_{1\parallel})[-(S_1+v_{1\parallel})\mathbf n+\mathbf v_1]/(r^2S_1)$. At leading order this is the fixed-$\mathbf V$ direction-dependent attraction of Consequence 1, which is symmetric under reflection in the line along $\mathbf V$ and in the line across it, and which is expected to have closed orbits near the circular one for every $V<1$ (inferred from the two reflection symmetries by the usual perpendicular-crossing argument; not proved here). On such an orbit $J$ oscillates with relative amplitude about $V^2/4$ and returns to its starting value. So nothing at leading order excludes a periodic relative motion; the whole exclusion must come from three slow drifts of relative size $\varepsilon$ per turn: the drift of $E_{\mathbf V}$ and the two components of the drift of $\mathbf P$. A history periodic up to a drift needs all three to vanish on average.

**Consequence 3 (inferred; leading order in $V^2$ and $\varepsilon$, not checked numerically).** For the near-circular orbit with small $V$, $J\approx J_0(1+\tfrac14V^2\cos2\alpha)$ with $J_0^2\approx2r$, and the sum rule integrated over one turn with $dt/r^2=d\alpha/J$ gives a change of $\mathbf P$ of $-\pi V^2\mathbf P/(4J_0)$: the velocity sum decays, by a fraction of about $0.55\,V^2\varepsilon$ per turn, because the pair's axis spends more of its turning with $J$ small when it lies across $\mathbf V$. Over the same turn the first-order term $J/r^2$ adds exactly $2\pi$ to $J$. In this sub-regime the pair therefore behaves as the conjecture predicts: $J$ grows and the common velocity dies away, and no history periodic up to drift exists. This does not cover $V$ of order one, eccentric orbits of the direction-dependent attraction, or pairs that are not slow relative to each other.

**What this suggests for closing the question (guessed).** (1) The second monotone quantity asked for should be sought as a correction of $E_{\mathbf V}$ rather than of $J$: it is the quantity the common-velocity terms conserve exactly, whereas the corrected quantities tried in the subject were built from $J$ and $v_{i\parallel}^2$ and removed only part of a gradient term. (2) A counting argument favours the conjecture but is not a proof: for a given family of closed orbits there is one free parameter, $V$, against three averaged conditions. (3) The no-delay system of Consequence 2 is cheap to integrate and is the right arena for a decisive search at relative order $\varepsilon$: compute the three drifts per turn as functions of $V$ along the closed-orbit family and test whether they can vanish together. A common zero would be a candidate counterexample to carry back to the full equation; a clear absence, with the three drift functions tabulated, would be measured evidence for the slow-relative regime and a target for a proof by averaging.

**Falsifiers.** A straight-window common-velocity configuration at which $\mathbf a_1-\mathbf a_2$ differs from $-\nabla_{\mathbf r}U_{\mathbf V}$, or $\mathbf a_1+\mathbf a_2$ from the displayed sum rule, refutes B. A release at large separation with small common speed, integrated with the full equation, in which the velocity sum grows on average over several turns while the relative speed stays small, refutes Consequence 3.

## Limits

The reading covers the summary, the setting, the exact rates, Theorems 1 to 3, Corollaries 2a to 2d, Propositions 4 and 5 and the first-order expansion. It did not rerun the subject's delay integrator or its release samples, so item 6 and every "measured on solutions" statement stand on the subject's instruments alone. The reader's script (pure Python floats, bisection for the causal roots, central-difference velocities, not retained in the repository) tests algebra and geometry on prescribed histories only; it validated on the rigid mirror circle before use; it is not evidence about solutions of the equation. The claim that Theorems 2 and 3 have independent hypotheses, the Corollary 2c numerical check, and the section "What was tried and failed" were not checked beyond the identities they cite. Consequences 2 and 3 are formal expansions without error bounds and without a numerical test, and the existence of closed orbits of the direction-dependent attraction is asserted from symmetry, not proved. Planar motion and speeds strictly below wake speed are assumed throughout.
