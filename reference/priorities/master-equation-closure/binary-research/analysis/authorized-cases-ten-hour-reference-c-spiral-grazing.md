# Blind finite-unit-arrival and grazing reference for the actual C family

**Derived before access to any new C event-selection subject.** Keep the coefficient-one logarithmic equation, complete fixed preparation family and accepted positive h/acute-lag continuation. The prior finite-endpoint theorem gives a regular earlier source and a finite incoming unit velocity. It does not assert that any member has such an endpoint.

At a first unit arrival let v denote that limiting receiver velocity, $v_s$ the earlier source's mirror-coordinate velocity and $D=1+n\cdot v_s>0$. The original acceleration is $a=-n/(RD)$. Since speed was below one before the endpoint, its incoming derivative is nonnegative. Therefore $n\cdot v\le0$. With receiving polar components $v=p e_r+w e_\theta$ and $n=c e_r-d e_\theta$, positive h and acute lag give c,d,w positive. The arrival projection is

$$
v\cdot a=\frac{dw-cp}{RD}.
$$

Inward or zero radial velocity gives strictly positive arrival and falls under the accepted curved-birth obstruction. Grazing requires $n\cdot v=0$, hence $v=Jn$ by positive h, and is outward.

## Exact grazing curvature and its source dependence

At grazing the source clock derivative is $s'=1/D$. Its chord derivative is

$$
n'=\frac1R\left(v+\frac{(I-nn^{\mathsf T})v_s}{D}\right).
$$

Differentiating the speed a second time at unit speed, with its first derivative zero, cancels derivatives of the scalar acceleration amplitude. The result is

$$
\left.\frac{d^2|v|}{dt^2}\right|_*=
|a|^2+v\cdot a'
=-\frac{\Lambda}{R^2D^2},\qquad
\Lambda=(n+v)\cdot v_s.
$$

A first incoming grazing unit arrival must therefore have $\Lambda\ge0$. Strict positivity gives the negative curvature required by the earlier isolated-touch unchanged-law continuation theorem. Equality leaves its higher-order behavior unresolved. Negative Lambda would make speed locally greater than one on the incoming side and cannot describe this first arrival.

Let $\alpha$ be the angle from n to the receiver radius and $\gamma$ the angle from the source radius to n. Both are positive and $\alpha+\gamma$ is the acute lag. Write $v_s=p_s e_s+w_sJe_s$, where $w_s=h_s/r_s>0$. Then the exact source identity is

$$
\Lambda=p_s(\cos\gamma-\sin\gamma)
+w_s(\sin\gamma+\cos\gamma).
$$

The preserved orientation alone supplies no strictly positive lower bound for this expression. For example, as an algebraic finite-jet control, take $\alpha=\gamma=\pi/6$, equal endpoint radii and $w_s=1/10$. With $p_s=-(2+\sqrt3)/10$, Lambda is zero, source speed is strictly below one and $0<h_s<h$ at the current endpoint where h is the current angular quantity. With $p_s=-1/2$ Lambda is negative; with $p_s=0$ it is positive. These are tests of the claimed pointwise implications, not new prepared histories or examples of an actual first arrival. In particular the negative case is excluded by the separate first-arrival condition just derived.

A sufficient strict condition is $p_s\ge0$ and $\gamma\le\pi/4$, or $p_s\le0$ and $\gamma\ge\pi/4$, because the first term is then nonnegative and the second is positive. Neither follows from the existing nonlinear departure theorem. Near the exact spiral they hold with strict margins where appropriate, but fixed finite-time closeness cannot be extended to an unspecified later unit event.

## Why a possible first event cannot be assigned to a fixed early source segment

For every fixed finite T, finite-time compatible dependence keeps sufficiently small members close to the exact base on $[0,T]$, with a positive speed margin. Hence any sequence of finite first unit-arrival times for members tending to the base has those times tending to infinity. Their endpoint source times also tend to infinity: for any fixed large T, the perturbed source clock at T tends to the base clock there, and its later value is no smaller. The base source clock tends to infinity with T. This is an existential smallness statement for the existing family; it selects neither an amplitude nor a first event.

Consequently the prescribed initial circular/spiral source geometry cannot decide Lambda at a hypothetical family endpoint by itself. The missing bridge is a signed constraint on the later generated source velocity relative to its ray, or a separate mechanism forcing strictly positive projected arrival instead of grazing. The accepted orientation and acute-lag properties constrain possible events but do not select one.

Falsifiers are an incorrect source-clock factor, a hidden derivative of the scalar amplitude surviving tangency, a wrong source-angle sign, or an illicit promotion of the finite-jet controls to actual family trajectories.
