# C signed-gain criterion assessment

Disposition: the [signed-gain subject](authorized-cases-followup-c-signed-gain-entry.md), frozen as `ec85f1d03e11ef0ada5581f9c2ad4bde4c76efa0addc62afba40bb27fa38f412`, is accepted as a derived conditional entry theorem and necessary infinite-branch cancellation identity. Actual branch selection remains unproved. The [separate reference](authorized-cases-followup-reference-c-entry.md) was frozen before reading it. The prior campaign's underlying orientation, finite-endpoint and attained-history results were known; their use here is not a new independent confirmation.

The factor in the angular equation is correct. Resolving $n=(Ae-BJe)/d$, $v=pe+zJe$, $R=rd$ in $v'=-n/(RD)$ gives $(|v|^2)'=2(Bz-Ap)/(rd^2D)$. Multiplication by $dT/d\theta=r/z$ yields exactly $W=2(B-Ay)/(d^2D)$, with no remaining current $z$ factor. The spiral balance makes its numerator zero; $p=0$ gives a positive numerator. Positive angular momentum alone does not fix this numerator, and an outward state can have positive gain when $0<y<B/A$.

For a survival-conditional complete-history tube, the subject correctly uses a lower initial squared-speed bound in the gain test. An upper bound would cover the smallest rather than the largest unknown deficit and would be insufficient. The bound $r\le r_a e^{Yu}$ follows from $d\log r/d\theta=y$; $\chi\le C$ then integrates physical time exactly as stated. A strict branch cannot postpone the specified angular interval indefinitely while respecting these bounds. An earlier endpoint invokes the accepted actual-family endpoint theorem. That is a necessary premise, not a conclusion of gain quadrature alone. Source/history tube membership must be proved from the same actual member, and a failure of the proposed tube estimate is not itself an event.

The elementary sufficient bound $W\ge q/(1+K)^2$ follows from $B-Ay\ge q$, $D<2$ and $d\le1+K$. It preserves the possibility of positive radial velocity. This broadens the form of an entry certificate relative to the independently reconstructed inward condition, while introducing a new whole-interval signed-gap obligation. Neither theorem establishes that an original prepared member enters its region.

On an infinite strict branch, the gain integral telescopes to a squared-speed difference in $(-1,1)$, so its long angular average is zero. This supplies no pointwise convergence, recurrence sign, or exclusion of a nonconstant viable orbit. The local modal calculation is adequate for the stated obstruction: an oscillating nonzero scalar first variation has both signs, and a zero coefficient gives no first-order gain. In neither case does it control a fixed finite-amplitude event. No uniform linear approximation at diverging departure times is licensed.

The fixed family realization and modal phase remain unchanged. A rigorous attained exit neighborhood with a robust gain tube could select nearby actual members; a complete limiting connection alone could equally be obtained from finite members with arbitrarily long survival. The subject respects that distinction. Its accepted new content is a checkable angular integral and finite time criterion, not a proof of either actual fate. The first unresolved assertion is an attained member or exit neighborhood for which the full moving-clock gain has the required cumulative positive lower bound. A source-clock omission, wrong initial-bound direction, loss of actual tube membership, or transfer from an unattained limiting history would defeat the application.

Validation is analytical reconstruction of equations and quantifiers, followed by the existing known-first document checker for this assessment. No numerical target, symbolic target, solver or long process was run.
