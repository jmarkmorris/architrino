Define the explicit phase-test subset

$$
 \mathcal G=\{0<\varepsilon\le e:
 \operatorname{dist}(\Theta(\varepsilon),2\pi\mathbb Z)
 >2^{150102}\varepsilon\}.
 \tag{1}
$$

Then every member in $\mathcal G$ has

$$

 \tag{2}

Moreover, for every $0<a\le e$,

$$
 |(0,a]\setminus\mathcal G|<2^{150109}a^2,

 \tag{3}
$$

Thus the explicit lower bound holds on a subset of one-sided parameter density one at zero. At the admitted endpoint, the complement's absolute length is below $2^{-249891}$ and its relative length below $2^{-49891}$. This is ordinary length on the original parameter axis, not a probability distribution or a statement about arbitrary complete histories.

## Uniform positive-tail chart coverage

The threshold in (1) exceeds the uniform necessary zero-speed tolerance $2^{191000}\varepsilon^2$, because their ratio is $2^{-40898}/\varepsilon\ge2^{159102}$. The accepted all-future dichotomy therefore first establishes positive terminal velocity for each member of $\mathcal G$, without using (2).

This implies the broader hypothesis $\nu\le\varepsilon^7$ used in the [accepted terminal strengthening](authorized-cases-ten-hour-reference-b-terminal-strengthening-adjudication.md). The proof of that domain extension is uniform here: it uses only the original all-future preparation range and fixed constants multiplying positive powers of the parameter.

Before this entry, the accepted passage proof supplies the actual parabolic chart, $h\ge1/2$, and increasing account $\mathcal E\le\mathcal E_t<v_\infty^2$. ## Complement length

Let $M=2^{150102}$. On a dyadic shell $[a/2,a]$, enlarge the phase tolerance to $Ma<\pi$. The independently accepted derivative interval bounds its phase length by $(511/18)a^{-9}$, so fewer than $7a^{-9}$ integer-turn windows intersect its image, including both endpoint windows. The inverse derivative is at most $4a^{10}$. Each enlarged phase window has width $2Ma$, and hence preimage length at most $8Ma^{11}$. The shell complement has length at most $56Ma^2$. Summing over the dyadic shells gives $(224/3)Ma^2<128Ma^2$, exactly (3).
