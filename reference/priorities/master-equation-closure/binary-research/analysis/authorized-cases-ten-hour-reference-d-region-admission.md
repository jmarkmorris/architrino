# Blind reference for admission to the slow anisotropic account region

Frozen before the region-admission subject is opened. The account subject has now been inspected, but this followup is derived independently from its stated exact scalar inequality. Retain its conditional actual region, write $\chi=K/d\le1/100$, and suppose $J\le0$ and $J'\ge3K^2/(2d^3)$. No source membership or all-future region is assumed by this derivation.

## Negative-account bounds without angular momentum

The definition of $J$ and $g\le1$ imply

$$
 |U|^2\le4\chi+2\chi|U|,
 \qquad
 |U|\le\chi+\sqrt{\chi^2+4\chi}<\frac{21}{10}\sqrt\chi.
$$

Thus $d|U_\perp|^2/K<441/100<8$. The transverse bound is automatic on the negative-account interval; an angular lower bound is unnecessary for this algebra. This controls relative velocity, not the common center velocity.

A separation barrier also follows. Set $F=J-(2/5)\chi^{3/2}$. Since $\chi'=-\chi u/d$,

$$
 F'=J'+\frac35\frac{\chi^{3/2}u}{d}
 \ge\left(\frac32-\frac{63}{50}\right)\frac{\chi^2}{d}
 =\frac6{25}\frac{K^2}{d^3}>0.
$$

Therefore, on any interval retaining $J\le0$,

$$
 \chi(t)^{3/2}
 \le\chi(T)^{3/2}-\frac52J(T).
$$

This is an explicit positive separation floor in terms of the actual state at $T$. It does not reset the past or assume a physical energy conservation law. For a near-circular initial negative account of size $O(\chi(T))$, this only gives a later $\chi=O(\chi(T)^{2/3})$ floor, so its size must be checked against the chosen slow tube. It is not an automatic preservation of the original separation scale.

## The remaining center and crossing gaps

The scalar monotonicity bounds $\int d^{-3}dt$ while its nonlinear midpoint estimate requires $\int d^{-2}dt$. Neither the separation floor nor the negative-account relative-velocity bound converts the former into the latter.

An explicit inequality control illustrates the distinction without claiming a physical solution. Set $W=0$, $H=0$ and prescribe $d(t)=2\sqrt{Kt}$ on a sufficiently late interval. Then $U=d'N$, $\chi=\sqrt K/(2\sqrt t)$, and the account evaluates exactly to

$$
 J(t)=-\sqrt K\,t^{-1/2},\qquad
 J'(t)=\frac{\sqrt K}{2}t^{-3/2}
 >\frac{3K^2}{2d^3}.
$$

It obeys the negative-account relative bound and its separation barrier at late times, and $\int d^{-3}<\infty$, but $\int d^{-2}=\infty$. This is solely a counterexample to an implication between the proposed inequalities. It does not satisfy the actual relative acceleration row and is not a dynamical counterexample.

If $J$ crosses zero, the preceding algebra no longer bounds the transverse ratio or separation in the same way. A positive account alone does not fix the sign of $u$, so an outward impulse criterion cannot be invoked without another argument. A completed neighborhood theorem must control the actual center throughout the negative-account interval and handle any zero crossing with the original source windows retained. Falsifiers are an error in the account derivative used as premise, loss of the slow region before the barrier applies, or promotion of the inequality control into an actual trajectory. No numerical target or new physical history is used.
