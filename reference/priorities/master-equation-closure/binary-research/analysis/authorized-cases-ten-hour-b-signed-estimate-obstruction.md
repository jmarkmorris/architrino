## 1. A known oscillator control for the inference being tested

The central radial comparison at unit radius linearizes to $z''+z=0$. A cubic velocity seed $z(0)=0$, $z'(0)=-a\epsilon^3$, with $a=8/3$, gives $z(s)=-a\epsilon^3\sin s$. It is not a linearization about a claimed delayed circular equilibrium; the central comparison and the actual gradient pair are distinct.

Consider the mathematical uncertainty class obtained by replacing a known remainder with only its magnitude constraint $|f|\le\epsilon^4$ in

$$
z''+z=f(s).
\tag{1}
$$

This is an instrument for checking an inference from a bound. No $f$ is added to the physical equation or executed on its history. Choose any integer $N\ge2$, set $T=2\pi N$, and use the fixed envelope

$$
g(s)=\sin^6\!\left(\frac{s}{2N}\right),\qquad 0\le s\le T,
$$

extended by zero outside that interval. It is $C^{5,1}$ across both joins; its sixth derivative may have a finite jump. Elementary Fourier orthogonality gives

$$
\int_0^T g\sin s\cos s\,ds=0,
\qquad
\int_0^T g\cos^2s\,ds
=\int_0^T g\sin^2s\,ds=\frac{5T}{32}.
\tag{2}
$$

To check the second identity, the average of $\sin^6$ is $5/16$. Its nonconstant frequencies are $1/N$, $2/N$ and $3/N$, none equal to two when $N\ge2$, so the product with $\cos2s$ integrates to zero over $T$. The first identity also follows directly by reflection about $T/2$. These are known analytical integrals before using the uncertainty class.

With $f=cg\cos s$, variation of constants at the exact period endpoint gives

$$
z(T)=0,\qquad z'(T)=-a\epsilon^3+c\frac{5T}{32}.
$$

Choosing $c=32a\epsilon^3/(5T)$ removes the seed exactly. Choosing its negative doubles the seed magnitude. Both obey $|f|\le\epsilon^4$ once

$$
T\ge\frac{32a}{5\epsilon}.
\tag{3}
$$

For a nonzero initial position seed, adding a $g\sin s$ component controls the endpoint position independently by the other integral in (2). Thus the same absolute remainder class permits different endpoint amplitudes and phases on a time scale of order $\epsilon^{-1}$, starting from the same initial comparison data. The envelope retains the relevant seam regularity.

This proves a narrow logical obstruction: a proof that uses only an order-four forcing magnitude cannot protect an order-three oscillatory seed over that interval. It does not prove that either chosen $f$ is the remainder produced by the delayed law. The delayed law supplies additional structure, and that structure must be used to obtain the needed actual phase information.
