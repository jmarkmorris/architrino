## 1. The Euler–Lagrange equations as an implicit acceleration solve

Members $i=1,\dots,N$ have positions $\mathbf X_i$, velocities $\mathbf V_i$, accelerations $\mathbf A_i=d\mathbf V_i/dT$, pair separations $r_{ij}=\|\mathbf X_i-\mathbf X_j\|$ and unit vectors $\mathbf e_{ij}=(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf X_j)/r_{ij}$. With $c_f=1$ define the symmetric pair coupling matrix

$$
M_{ij}(\mathbf X)=\frac{\sigma_{ij}K_{ij}}{2r_{ij}}\left(I+\mathbf e_{ij}\mathbf e_{ij}^{\mathsf T}\right)=M_{ji},
\qquad
\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}=\frac12\sum_i\|\mathbf V_i\|^2-\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K_{ij}}{r_{ij}}+\sum_{i<j}\mathbf V_i^{\mathsf T}M_{ij}\mathbf V_j .
$$

The generalized momentum is $\mathbf p_i=\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}/\partial\mathbf V_i=\mathbf V_i+\sum_{j\ne i}M_{ij}\mathbf V_j$, so the $3N\times3N$ velocity Hessian $H=\partial^2\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}/\partial\mathbf V\partial\mathbf V$ has blocks $H_{ii}=I$ and $H_{ij}=M_{ij}$ for $i\ne j$. The functional is quadratic in velocities, so $H$ depends on positions only: $H=H(\mathbf X)$ (derived). Differentiating $\mathbf p_i$ along a history gives $d\mathbf p_i/dT=\sum_j H_{ij}\mathbf A_j+\sum_{j\ne i}\dot M_{ij}\mathbf V_j$, where $\dot M_{ij}$ is the time derivative of $M_{ij}$ through $\mathbf X_i,\mathbf X_j$. The Euler–Lagrange line $d\mathbf p_i/dT=\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}/\partial\mathbf X_i$ is therefore

$$
H(\mathbf X)\,\mathbf A=\mathbf G(\mathbf X,\mathbf V),\qquad
\mathbf G_i=\frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}}{\partial\mathbf X_i}-\sum_{j\ne i}\dot M_{ij}\mathbf V_j ,
$$

and the subtracted term is exactly the mixed second derivative $\partial^2\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}/\partial\mathbf V_i\partial\mathbf X_k$ contracted with $\mathbf V_k$. In closed form, with $S_{ij}=\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf V_j+(\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})(\mathbf V_j\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})$, $\dot r_{ij}=\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot(\mathbf V_i-\mathbf V_j)$ and the transverse relative velocity $\mathbf w_{ij}=(I-\mathbf e_{ij}\mathbf e_{ij}^{\mathsf T})(\mathbf V_i-\mathbf V_j)$,

$$
\frac{\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}}{\partial\mathbf X_i}=\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K_{ij}}{r_{ij}^2}\left[\left(1-\tfrac12S_{ij}\right)\mathbf e_{ij}+\tfrac12\left((\mathbf V_j\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})\mathbf V_i+(\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})\mathbf V_j-2(\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})(\mathbf V_j\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})\mathbf e_{ij}\right)\right],
$$

$$
\dot M_{ij}\mathbf u=\frac{\sigma_{ij}K_{ij}}{2r_{ij}^2}\left[-\dot r_{ij}\left(\mathbf u+(\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot\mathbf u)\mathbf e_{ij}\right)+(\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot\mathbf u)\mathbf w_{ij}+(\mathbf w_{ij}\cdot\mathbf u)\mathbf e_{ij}\right].
$$

For $N=2$ write $r=r_{12}$, $\mathbf e=\mathbf e_{12}$, $m=\sigma K/(2r)$ and $M=m(I+\mathbf e\mathbf e^{\mathsf T})$. Then

$$
H=\begin{pmatrix}I&M\\ M&I\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf G=\begin{pmatrix}\ \ \mathbf g-\dot M\mathbf V_2\\ -\mathbf g-\dot M\mathbf V_1\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\mathbf g=\frac{\sigma K}{r^2}\left[\left(1-\tfrac12S\right)\mathbf e+\tfrac12\left((\mathbf V_2\cdot\mathbf e)\mathbf V_1+(\mathbf V_1\cdot\mathbf e)\mathbf V_2-2(\mathbf V_1\cdot\mathbf e)(\mathbf V_2\cdot\mathbf e)\mathbf e\right)\right].
$$

Both closed forms were checked against central finite differences of a direct evaluation of $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$ at random states for $N=2$ and $N=3$, both polarities (Section 12, maximum absolute discrepancy $1.2\times10^{-8}$ for $H$ and $3.8\times10^{-9}$ for $\mathbf G$, consistent with step-size noise). Consequence for the manuscript's claim: because $H$ is velocity-independent, setting $\mathbf V=0$ leaves $H$ unchanged and reduces $\mathbf G$ to the inverse-square gradient alone, so the zero-velocity acceleration is $H(\mathbf X)^{-1}$ applied to the inverse-square gradient, not the inverse-square gradient itself. For the mirror pair at rest this gives $\mathbf A_1=\dfrac{\sigma K\,\mathbf e}{r^2}\cdot\dfrac{1}{1-\sigma K/r}$ (derived, Section 4), that is, the inverse-square value multiplied by $r/(r+1)$ for opposite polarity and by $r/(r-1)$ for same polarity. The manuscript's statement that zero velocity does not remove the coupling from the solve is confirmed and quantified: the correction is first order in $\epsilon=K/r$ and vanishes only in the weak limit.

## 2. Invertibility domain of $H$ for an isolated pair

The block matrix $H=\begin{pmatrix}I&M\\M&I\end{pmatrix}$ is diagonalized by the common mode $\mathbf A_1=\mathbf A_2$ (eigenvalues $1+\mu$) and the relative mode $\mathbf A_1=-\mathbf A_2$ (eigenvalues $1-\mu$), where $\mu$ ranges over the eigenvalues of $M$: $2m$ along $\mathbf e$ (longitudinal) and $m$ on the transverse plane (double). With $m=\sigma/(2r)$ the six eigenvalues of $H$ are

| Mode | Eigenvalue | $\sigma=-1$ (opposite) | $\sigma=+1$ (same) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| common, longitudinal | $1+\sigma/r$ | $1-1/r$, zero at $r=1$ | $1+1/r>0$ |
| common, transverse (×2) | $1+\sigma/(2r)$ | $1-1/(2r)$, zero at $r=1/2$ | $1+1/(2r)>0$ |
| relative, longitudinal | $1-\sigma/r$ | $1+1/r>0$ | $1-1/r$, zero at $r=1$ |
| relative, transverse (×2) | $1-\sigma/(2r)$ | $1+1/(2r)>0$ | $1-1/(2r)$, zero at $r=1/2$ |

Hence $\det H=(1-1/r^2)(1-1/(4r^2))^2$ for both polarities (derived): positive for $r>1$, zero at $r=1$ (simple) and $r=1/2$ (double), negative on $0<r<1$ away from those points. $H$ is positive definite exactly on $r>1$ for either polarity. The domain is independent of velocity because $H$ is. The polarities differ in which modes fail: for opposite polarity the singular modes at $r=1$ and $r=1/2$ are the common-centre modes and the relative (mirror) block $I-M$ is positive definite for every $r>0$; for same polarity the singular modes are the relative ones and the common block is always regular. In units with $K=c_f=1$ the singular separations are $K/c_f^2$ and $K/(2c_f^2)$, far inside the declared approximation domain $K/r\le0.05$ ($r\ge20$). Falsifier: a state with $r_{12}>1$ at which a direct numerical factorization of $H$ from Section 1 reports a zero or negative pivot, or a state with $r_{12}=1$ at which it does not.

## 3. Exact invariants

These are invariants of an adapted law; they are not physical energy, momentum or angular-momentum accounts. All three are derived.

Time translation. $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$ has no explicit time dependence, so the Legendre transform $E=\sum_i\mathbf p_i\cdot\mathbf V_i-\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$ is constant along solutions. Because the velocity part is a homogeneous quadratic form, $E$ equals the velocity part plus the sign-reversed position term:

$$
E=\frac12\sum_i\|\mathbf V_i\|^2+\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K_{ij}}{r_{ij}}+\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K_{ij}}{2r_{ij}}\left[\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf V_j+(\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})(\mathbf V_j\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})\right].
$$

Spatial translation. $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$ depends on positions only through differences, so $\sum_i\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}/\partial\mathbf X_i=0$ and $\mathbf P=\sum_i\mathbf p_i=\sum_i\mathbf V_i+\sum_{i<j}M_{ij}(\mathbf V_i+\mathbf V_j)$ is constant. For $N=2$, $\mathbf P=(I+M)(\mathbf V_1+\mathbf V_2)$.

Rotation. Every term is built from norms and scalar products of vectors that rotate together, so $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$ is invariant under simultaneous rotation of all $\mathbf X_i,\mathbf V_i$, and $\mathbf J=\sum_i\mathbf X_i\times\mathbf p_i$ is constant. For $N=2$, $\mathbf J=\mathbf X_1\times(\mathbf V_1+M\mathbf V_2)+\mathbf X_2\times(\mathbf V_2+M\mathbf V_1)$.

Galilean boost. Under $\mathbf V_i\to\mathbf V_i+\mathbf u$ the quadratic term changes by a total time derivative, but the coupling term acquires $\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K_{ij}}{2r_{ij}}\left[\mathbf u\cdot(\mathbf V_i+\mathbf V_j)+(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})\big((\mathbf V_i+\mathbf V_j)\cdot\mathbf e_{ij}\big)+\|\mathbf u\|^2+(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf e_{ij})^2\right]$, which is not a total time derivative because $r_{ij}$ and $\mathbf e_{ij}$ vary along the history (the $\|\mathbf u\|^2/r_{ij}$ term alone already fails). A boost is not a symmetry, there is no centre-of-weight theorem, and the centre motion does not separate from the relative motion: for $N=2$ the centre velocity is $\tfrac12(I+M(\mathbf X_1-\mathbf X_2))^{-1}\mathbf P$, which varies with the relative configuration whenever $\mathbf P\ne0$ (derived). The one case in which the centre is at rest for all time is $\mathbf P=0$ with $I+M$ invertible, which forces $\mathbf V_1=-\mathbf V_2$; for opposite polarity $I+M$ is invertible for $r>1$, for same polarity always.

Along the flow, with $\mathbf A$ obtained by solving the Section 1 system at random states ($N=2,3$, both polarities), the rates $dE/dT$, $d\mathbf P/dT$, $d\mathbf J/dT$ evaluated by a symmetric second-order Taylor step are at most $5.4\times10^{-11}$ against $E$ of order one (Section 12), consistent with the $O(\delta T^2)$ truncation of that check; this check is necessary, not sufficient, and is recorded as a spot check only.

## 4. The mirror invariant subspace and the reduced planar problem

Let $\mathcal S$ be the map $(\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2,\mathbf V_1,\mathbf V_2)\mapsto(-\mathbf X_2,-\mathbf X_1,-\mathbf V_2,-\mathbf V_1)$, the composition of central inversion with the member swap. Both factors preserve $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$ for the symmetric pair ($\sigma_{12}=\sigma_{21}$, $K_{12}=K_{21}$; $\mathbf e_{12}\to-\mathbf e_{12}$ enters only quadratically), so $\mathcal S$ maps solutions to solutions. Its fixed-point set is the mirror subspace $\mathbf X_1=-\mathbf X_2$, $\mathbf V_1=-\mathbf V_2$. Wherever $H$ is invertible the initial value problem has a unique solution, so a solution starting in the fixed set coincides with its $\mathcal S$-image and stays there: the mirror subspace is invariant (derived). The reduced Lagrangian is justified by the principle of symmetric criticality for the finite group $\{1,\mathcal S\}$ (Palais: for a compact group acting on configurations and preserving the functional, critical points of the restriction to the fixed set are critical points of the full functional), and independently by direct substitution: the circular solution of Section 6 is substituted into the full six-dimensional equations and its residual is round-off (Section 12). The reduced description is valid only while the full $H$ is invertible; at $r=1$ and $r=1/2$ (opposite polarity, common modes) the full solve is non-unique even though the reduced equations below remain regular, which is why the collinear file records those separations as obstruction events.

Write $\mathbf X_1=-\mathbf X_2=\tfrac12 r\,\mathbf e$ and planar polar coordinates $(r,\theta)$ for the separation vector. With

$$
a(r)=1-\frac{\sigma}{r},\qquad b(r)=1-\frac{\sigma}{2r},
$$

the relative longitudinal and transverse eigenvalues of Section 2, the reduced Lagrangian, angular momentum and energy-like function are

$$
L_{\mathrm{red}}=\tfrac14a(r)\dot r^2+\tfrac14b(r)r^2\dot\theta^2-\frac{\sigma}{r},\qquad
\ell=\tfrac12b(r)r^2\dot\theta=J_z,\qquad
E=\tfrac14a(r)\dot r^2+U_{\mathrm{eff}}(r),\qquad
U_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)=\frac{\ell^2}{b(r)r^2}+\frac{\sigma}{r}.
$$

Here $\ell$ and $E$ are the restrictions of the full $\mathbf J$ and $E$ of Section 3 (derived by substitution). The radial equation is $\tfrac12a\ddot r=-\tfrac14a'\dot r^2+\tfrac14(br^2)'\dot\theta^2+\sigma/r^2$ with $a'=\sigma/r^2$ and $(br^2)'=2r-\sigma/2$. Out-of-plane motion of the mirror pair is excluded by conservation of $\mathbf J$ (the separation vector stays in the plane normal to $\mathbf J$), so the planar reduction loses nothing within the mirror subspace. At zero velocity the reduced equation gives $\ddot r=2\sigma/(r^2a(r))$, equivalently the per-member acceleration of Section 1.

## 5. Zero-coupling control in closed form

Deleting the velocity-coupling term gives $a=b=1$ and the instantaneous inverse-square mirror problem $\ddot r=2\sigma/r^2$. For opposite polarity: circular orbit $\dot\theta_0^2=2/r_0^3$, individual speed $u_0=1/\sqrt{2r_0}$, period $P_0=2\pi\sqrt{r_0^3/2}$, energy-like value $E_0=-1/(2r_0)$, $\ell_0^2=r_0/2$; the apsides of a bound orbit are the roots of $Er^2+r-\ell^2=0$; the radial period equals the orbital period (no precession); and the radial free-fall time from rest at $r_0$ to contact is $T_{\mathrm{ff}}=\pi r_0^{3/2}/4$ ($785.3981634$ for $r_0=100$). These are the known cases on which the quadrature code was run before any target use (Section 11).

## 6. Exact circular balance of the opposite-polarity mirror pair

A circle is a critical point of $U_{\mathrm{eff}}$ at fixed $\ell$. With $D(r)=b(r)r^2=r^2-\sigma r/2$, $U_{\mathrm{eff}}'=0$ gives $\ell^2=-\sigma D^2/(r^2D')$ and hence

$$
\dot\theta_0^2=\frac{-2\sigma}{r_0^2\left(r_0-\sigma/4\right)}.
$$

For same polarity ($\sigma=+1$) the right side is negative for every $r_0>1/4$: no circle exists in the whole region $r_0>1/4$ (derived), and in particular none in the approximation domain. The formal circles at $r_0<1/4$ have individual speed $u^2=1/(2(1/4-r_0))\ge2$, above $c_f$, inside the region where both relative eigenvalues of $H$ are negative; they are recorded as exact solutions of the adapted law outside every declared domain and not used further.

For opposite polarity ($\sigma=-1$) a circle exists for every $r_0>0$, with (derived)

$$
\dot\theta_0=\frac1{r_0}\sqrt{\frac{2}{r_0+\tfrac14}},\qquad
u_0=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2r_0+\tfrac12}},\qquad
P_0=2\pi r_0\sqrt{\frac{r_0+\tfrac14}{2}},\qquad
\ell_0=\tfrac12\left(1+\frac1{2r_0}\right)r_0^2\dot\theta_0,\qquad
\ell_0^2=\frac{(2r_0+1)^2}{2(4r_0+1)},\qquad
E_0=-\frac{2}{4r_0+1}.
$$

Compared with the zero-coupling circle at the same $r_0$, the angular rate is lower by the factor $(1+1/(4r_0))^{-1/2}$, the individual speed by the same factor, the period longer by its inverse, and the energy-like value $-1/(2r_0+\tfrac12)$ instead of $-1/(2r_0)$; to first order the angular rate and speed are lower by $K/(8r_0)$ relative and the magnitude of the energy-like value by $K/(4r_0)$ relative. The full six-dimensional residual $\|H\mathbf A-\mathbf G\|_\infty$ of this circle at $r_0\in\{25,100,400,0.3\}$ is at most $2.2\times10^{-19}$ against right-hand sides of order $10^{-3}$ to $10$ (Section 12), while the zero-coupling circle inserted into the coupled law leaves a residual $2.5\times10^{-7}$ at $r_0=100$, so the check is not vacuous. Existence as a solution of the full equations with a regular $H$ holds for $r_0\in(0,\infty)\setminus\{1/2,1\}$; at $r_0=1/2$ and $r_0=1$ the circle still satisfies the equations but the common-mode acceleration is not determined uniquely (the common component of $\mathbf G$ vanishes by symmetry, so solutions form an affine line), which the frozen rule classifies as an obstruction. Falsifier: a direct evaluation of Section 1 at the stated state returning a residual above round-off, in `evidence/darwin-overnight-reduction-predictions.mjs` or any independent evaluation of the same box.

## 7. Stability of the circle

Reduced planar mirror problem. Linearizing $\tfrac12a\ddot r+\tfrac14a'\dot r^2+U_{\mathrm{eff}}'=0$ at fixed $\ell$ about $r_0$ gives $\omega_r^2=2U_{\mathrm{eff}}''(r_0)/a(r_0)$. For opposite polarity $U_{\mathrm{eff}}''(r_0)=8/\big(r_0(4r_0+1)(2r_0+1)\big)$ and (derived)

$$
\omega_r^2=\frac{16}{(4r_0+1)(2r_0+1)(r_0+1)},\qquad
\frac{\omega_r^2}{\dot\theta_0^2}=\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac1{2r_0}\right)\left(1+\frac1{r_0}\right)},\qquad
\Delta\varphi=2\pi\left(\sqrt{\left(1+\tfrac1{2r_0}\right)\left(1+\tfrac1{r_0}\right)}-1\right)\approx\frac{3\pi}{2r_0}.
$$

Since $\omega_r^2>0$ for every $r_0>0$, every opposite-polarity circle is a nondegenerate minimum of $U_{\mathrm{eff}}$ and is linearly stable against radial perturbations within the reduced planar mirror problem; the radial period exceeds the orbital period, so the apsides advance (prograde) by $\Delta\varphi$ per radial period. The zero-coupling control gives $\omega_r=\dot\theta_0$ and $\Delta\varphi=0$. Within the reduced problem the statement is stronger than linear: for $\ell^2>1/2$ the effective potential has a unique minimum (Section 9), so every reduced history with $E$ slightly above the minimum is a bound orbit with two finite apsides. This covers the perturbation space $(\delta r,\delta\dot r,\delta\ell)$ inside the mirror plane and nothing else.

Full Cartesian linearization. In a frame rotating at $\dot\theta_0$ the circle is an equilibrium of the autonomous system $\ddot{\mathbf Y}=H(\mathbf Y)^{-1}\mathbf G(\mathbf Y,\dot{\mathbf Y}+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf Y)-2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\dot{\mathbf Y}-\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf Y)$, which follows from the rotational covariance of $H$ and $\mathbf G$; its twelve-dimensional linearization commutes with $\mathcal S$ and with reflection through the orbital plane and so splits exactly into a relative in-plane, a relative out-of-plane, a common in-plane and a common out-of-plane block. The relative blocks are the linearization of the reduced problem of Section 4 and were computed from central differences of the closed-form field (`.tmp/darwin-overnight/reduction/spot-check.mjs`, Faddeev–LeVerrier and Durand–Kerner on each block, off-block leakage at most $7.7\times10^{-14}$): relative in-plane $\{0,0,\pm i\omega_r\}$ with $\omega_r$ equal to the closed form above to eight digits (the double zero is the orbital phase and the period shift along the circle family); relative out-of-plane $\pm i\dot\theta_0$ (the two tilts of the orbital plane, rotation symmetries). The common blocks are derived in closed form. Write $\boldsymbol\rho=\mathbf X_1-\mathbf X_2$, $\mathbf R=\tfrac12(\mathbf X_1+\mathbf X_2)$ and $\mathbf U=\dot{\mathbf R}$; then exactly $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}=\mathbf U^{\mathsf T}(I+M(\boldsymbol\rho))\mathbf U+\tfrac14\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathsf T}(I-M(\boldsymbol\rho))\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}-\sigma/r$, $\mathbf R$ is cyclic with conserved momentum $\mathbf P=2(I+M)\mathbf U$, and the only coupling between centre and relative motion is the term $\mathbf U^{\mathsf T}M(\boldsymbol\rho)\mathbf U$, quadratic in $\mathbf U$. The circle has $\mathbf U=0$ and $\mathbf P=0$, so at first order the gradient of that term vanishes (no back-reaction of the centre on the relative motion) and $\delta\mathbf U=\tfrac12(I+M(\boldsymbol\rho_0(T)))^{-1}\delta\mathbf P$ with $\delta\dot{\mathbf P}=0$ (the variation of $(I+M)^{-1}$ with $\delta\boldsymbol\rho$ multiplies $\mathbf P_0=0$): the common block decouples from the relative block in both directions and is itself block triangular in the inertial frame, $\delta\dot{\mathbf R}=\tfrac12(I+M(\boldsymbol\rho_0(T)))^{-1}\delta\mathbf P$, $\delta\dot{\mathbf P}=0$. Its monodromy over one period is $\begin{pmatrix}I&C\\0&I\end{pmatrix}$ with $C=\tfrac12\int_0^{P_0}(I+M(\boldsymbol\rho_0))^{-1}dT=\tfrac{P_0}{4}\operatorname{diag}(\beta_1+\beta_2,\beta_1+\beta_2,2\beta_2)$, where $\beta_1=r_0/(r_0-1)$ and $\beta_2=2r_0/(2r_0-1)$ are the longitudinal and transverse eigenvalues of $B=(I+M_0)^{-1}$, both positive for $r_0>1$: all six Floquet multipliers equal one, with one Jordan block of size two per axis (linear drift of the centre with a periodic wobble, the wobble being the only trace of the anisotropy $\beta_1\ne\beta_2$). In the rotating frame, with $\mathbf p$ the rotating image of $\delta\mathbf P$ (so $\dot{\mathbf p}=-\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf p$) and $J$ the in-plane rotation generator, the in-plane block in the variables $(\mathbf Y,\mathbf p)$ is $\begin{pmatrix}-\dot\theta_0J&\tfrac12B_\perp\\0&-\dot\theta_0J\end{pmatrix}$ with $B_\perp=\operatorname{diag}(\beta_1,\beta_2)$, and in the variables $(\mathbf Y,\dot{\mathbf Y})$ used by the numerical Jacobian, with $\kappa=\beta_1/\beta_2=(2r_0-1)/(2r_0-2)$,

$$
A_{\mathrm c}=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&1&0\\0&0&0&1\\ \dot\theta_0^2\kappa&0&0&\dot\theta_0(1+\kappa)\\0&\dot\theta_0^2/\kappa&-\dot\theta_0(1+1/\kappa)&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad
\det(\lambda I-A_{\mathrm c})=(\lambda^2-\dot\theta_0^2\kappa)(\lambda^2-\dot\theta_0^2/\kappa)+\lambda^2\dot\theta_0^2(1+\kappa)(1+1/\kappa)=(\lambda^2+\dot\theta_0^2)^2 .
$$

The anisotropy $\kappa$ cancels identically in the characteristic polynomial, as it must: a block triangular matrix has the eigenvalues of its diagonal blocks whatever its coupling block, and the two coordinate systems are related by the invertible similarity $\dot{\mathbf Y}=\tfrac12B_\perp\mathbf p-\dot\theta_0J\mathbf Y$. The spectrum of the common in-plane block is therefore $\pm i\dot\theta_0$, each of algebraic multiplicity two and geometric multiplicity one: with $w=(1,i)$ the eigenvector of $-\dot\theta_0J$ for $i\dot\theta_0$, the coupling compressed onto that eigenspace is $\bar w^{\mathsf T}\tfrac12B_\perp w=\tfrac12(\beta_1+\beta_2)\ne0$, so each eigenvalue carries one Jordan block of size two (equivalently $A_{\mathrm c}^2+\dot\theta_0^2I\ne0$ while $(A_{\mathrm c}^2+\dot\theta_0^2I)^2=0$). The anisotropy enters only the generalized eigenvectors, through $\bar w^{\mathsf T}B_\perp\bar w=\beta_1-\beta_2$, which couples the $+i\dot\theta_0$ and $-i\dot\theta_0$ chains, and cannot split the eigenvalues at any order. The common out-of-plane block is $\dot Y_z=\tfrac12\beta_2p_z$, $\dot p_z=0$: a double zero with one Jordan block (free drift along the axis). Correction (round 2). The earlier version of this paragraph reported the common in-plane block as two purely imaginary pairs at $\dot\theta_0(1\mp3.3\times10^{-5})$ for $r_0=100$ and attributed the split to the anisotropy of $(I+M)^{-1}$. That was an artefact of numerical differentiation: a perturbation of size $\delta$ to a Jordan block of size two moves its eigenvalue by order $\sqrt\delta$, and the central-difference Jacobian with step $10^{-6}$ differs from $A_{\mathrm c}$ by $1.4\times10^{-14}$ in absolute terms, $7.2\times10^{-9}$ relative to $\dot\theta_0^2$, whose square root is the reported split. Every exponent of the full linearization has zero real part: the circle is spectrally stable in the full twelve-dimensional phase space with neutral drift modes (phase, plane tilt, centre drift with its Jordan blocks); this is a linear statement about an exact solution, covers common-centre, transverse in-plane and out-of-plane perturbations, and is not a Lyapunov or global-persistence claim (the Jordan blocks make the linearized centre displacement grow linearly in time, as free drift must, which is one reason the circle cannot be Lyapunov stable in the full space without quotienting the drift). ## 9. Planar classification of the opposite-polarity mirror problem

With $\sigma=-1$, $U_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)=\ell^2/(r^2+r/2)-1/r$. As $r\to\infty$, $U_{\mathrm{eff}}\to0^-$. As $r\to0^+$, $b r^2\to r/2$ and $U_{\mathrm{eff}}\to(2\ell^2-1)/r$. The critical-point condition $\ell^2=g(r)$ with $g(r)=(2r+1)^2/(2(4r+1))$ has $g(0)=1/2$ and $g'(r)=4r(2r+1)/(4r+1)^2>0$, so $g$ increases strictly from $1/2$ to $\infty$ (derived). The classification by $(E,\ell)$ is:

- $\ell^2>1/2$: $U_{\mathrm{eff}}$ rises to $+\infty$ at contact, has exactly one critical point, the circle $r_0=g^{-1}(\ell^2)$, and it is a minimum with $U_{\mathrm{eff}}(r_0)=E_0(r_0)<0$. For $E_0<E<0$ the history is bound with two finite apsides, the roots of $Er^2+(1+E/2)r+(1/2-\ell^2)=0$ (a quadratic, derived); for $E\ge0$ it has one pericentre and escapes; $E=E_0$ is the circle. No collision occurs.
- $\ell^2<1/2$: $U_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is strictly increasing from $-\infty$; there is no circle and no pericentre. For $E<0$ the history has a finite apocentre and then falls to contact; for $E\ge0$ it either falls to contact or escapes according to the sign of $\dot r$, without turning.
- $\ell^2=1/2$: $U_{\mathrm{eff}}=-2/(2r+1)$, finite at contact, strictly increasing; a history with $E<0$ falls from a finite apocentre to contact arriving with $\dot r\to0$ in finite time.

The qualitative difference from the zero-coupling control is that nonzero angular momentum no longer excludes contact: the transverse coefficient $b(r)r^2\to r/2$ weakens the centrifugal term from $\ell^2/r^2$ to $2\ell^2/r$, the same order as the attraction. The threshold $\ell^2=1/2$ equals $K^2/(2c_f^2)$ in general units. On a contact history the limiting individual speed is $\sqrt{1+2\ell^2}\ge c_f$ (from $\tfrac14a\dot r^2\to1-2\ell^2$ and $r^2\dot\theta^2\to16\ell^2$), reached only at contact and only after the full $H$ has become singular at $r=1$ and $r=1/2$; so these contact histories are reduced-problem statements, and under the frozen rule the full law ends them at $r=1$ with an obstruction event. Inside the declared domain ($r\ge20$, individual speed $\le0.1$) every history with $\ell\ne0$ has $\ell^2\gg1/2$ and the classification coincides qualitatively with the Kepler-type control, with the precession of Section 7 as the leading difference.

### 13.1 Exact reduction by translation: the Routhian at fixed $\mathbf P$

Section 7 gives $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}=\mathbf U^{\mathsf T}(I+M(\boldsymbol\rho))\mathbf U+\tfrac14\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathsf T}(I-M(\boldsymbol\rho))\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}+1/r$ for $\sigma=-1$, with $\mathbf R$ cyclic and $\mathbf P=2(I+M)\mathbf U$ constant. Eliminating the cyclic velocity by the Routh procedure, $\mathcal R_{\mathbf P}=\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}-\mathbf P\cdot\dot{\mathbf R}$ with $\mathbf U=\tfrac12B\mathbf P$, $B=(I+M)^{-1}$, gives exactly (derived)

$$
\mathcal R_{\mathbf P}(\boldsymbol\rho,\dot{\boldsymbol\rho})=\tfrac14\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathsf T}(I-M(\boldsymbol\rho))\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}+\frac1r-W_{\mathbf P}(\boldsymbol\rho),\qquad
W_{\mathbf P}=\tfrac14\mathbf P^{\mathsf T}B(\boldsymbol\rho)\mathbf P=\tfrac14\left[\beta_2(r)\|\mathbf P\|^2+(\beta_1-\beta_2)(r)(\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf e)^2\right],
$$

with $\beta_1=r/(r-1)$, $\beta_2=2r/(2r-1)$ and $\beta_1-\beta_2=r/((r-1)(2r-1))>0$ for $r>1$. The relative motion at fixed $\mathbf P$ is therefore a natural Lagrangian system on $\{r>1\}$: the same positive definite velocity form $\tfrac14(I-M)$ as the mirror problem (eigenvalues $\tfrac14a$, $\tfrac14b$ with $a=1+1/r$, $b=1+1/(2r)$) and the amended potential $V_{\mathbf P}=-1/r+W_{\mathbf P}$, which is quadratic in $\mathbf P$ exactly, so the drift acts on the relative motion at second order and at no lower order. The reduced field was checked against the relative acceleration $\mathbf A_1-\mathbf A_2$ of the full twelve-dimensional solve $H\mathbf A=\mathbf G$ at 20 random states with $\mathbf P\ne0$: maximum relative discrepancy $4.8\times10^{-16}$ (`fieldCheck` in the script output). The absence of boost symmetry is what makes $W_{\mathbf P}$ depend on $\boldsymbol\rho$; translation symmetry alone suffices for the reduction. The residual symmetry of $\mathcal R_{\mathbf P}$ is rotation about $\hat{\mathbf P}$, with the conserved component $\ell_{\mathbf P}=\hat{\mathbf P}\cdot\mathbf L$ of the relative angular momentum $\mathbf L=\tfrac12\boldsymbol\rho\times(I-M)\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}=\tfrac12b\,\boldsymbol\rho\times\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}$; the full $\mathbf L$ obeys $d\mathbf L/dT=-\mathbf U\times\mathbf P=-\tfrac12(\beta_1-\beta_2)(\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf e)(\mathbf e\times\mathbf P)$ (derived; these are invariants of the adapted law, not physical accounts).

### 13.2 Averaged second-order system about the circle

Let the unperturbed relative motion be the circle of radius $r_0$ in the plane with unit normal $\mathbf n$, polar angle $\theta$, and decompose $\mathbf P=\mathbf P_\parallel+(\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf n$ with in-plane direction $\varphi_P$. Then $(\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf e)^2=\tfrac12P_\parallel^2\left(1+\cos2(\theta-\varphi_P)\right)$ and $W_{\mathbf P}=\bar W(r)+W_2(r)\cos2(\theta-\varphi_P)$ with

$$
\bar W=\tfrac14\beta_2\|\mathbf P\|^2+\tfrac18(\beta_1-\beta_2)P_\parallel^2,\qquad W_2=\tfrac18(\beta_1-\beta_2)P_\parallel^2 .
$$

Writing $r=r_0+x$, $\ell=\ell_0+\lambda$ with $\ell=\tfrac12br^2\dot\theta$, the in-plane equations linearized in $x,\lambda$ and kept to first order in $\|\mathbf P\|^2$ are $\dot\lambda=-\partial_\theta W_{\mathbf P}=2W_2\sin2(\theta-\varphi_P)$ and $\tfrac12a\ddot x+U_{\mathrm{eff}}''(r_0)x=\dot\theta_0\frac{4r_0+1}{r_0(2r_0+1)}\lambda-\bar W'(r_0)-W_2'(r_0)\cos2(\theta-\varphi_P)$, where $U_{\mathrm{eff}}''(r_0)=8/(r_0(4r_0+1)(2r_0+1))$ from Section 7. Four closed-form consequences follow (derived).

Secular shift of the oscillation centre from the averaged potential: $\delta r_{\mathrm{sec}}=-\bar W'(r_0)/U_{\mathrm{eff}}''(r_0)$ with $\beta_2'=-2/(2r-1)^2$ and $(\beta_1-\beta_2)'=(1-2r^2)/((r-1)^2(2r-1)^2)$, both negative, so the centre moves outward. Modulation of the angular invariant: $\lambda=\lambda_2\cos2(\theta-\varphi_P)$ with $\lambda_2=-W_2(r_0)/\dot\theta_0=-(\beta_1-\beta_2)P_\parallel^2/(8\dot\theta_0)$; because the preparation fixes $\ell(0)$ and not the mean $\bar\ell=\ell(0)-\lambda_2\cos\psi$, $\psi=2(\theta(0)-\varphi_P)$, the mean circle radius shifts by a second amount $\delta r_\ell=-2\ell_0\lambda_2\cos\psi/g'(r_0)$ with $g(r)=(2r+1)^2/(2(4r+1))$ the circle relation of Section 9; the total centre shift is $\delta r_c=\delta r_{\mathrm{sec}}+\delta r_\ell$. Second-harmonic response: $x_2=A_2\cos2(\theta-\varphi_P)$ with $A_2=F/\big(U_{\mathrm{eff}}''-2a\dot\theta_0^2\big)$ and $F=-\tfrac18P_\parallel^2\big[(\beta_1-\beta_2)\tfrac{4r_0+1}{r_0(2r_0+1)}+(\beta_1-\beta_2)'\big]$; both numerator and denominator are negative, so $A_2>0$ and the separation is largest where $\mathbf e\parallel\mathbf P_\parallel$. Tilt: averaging the torque of Section 13.1 over the circle, $\langle(\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf e)\mathbf e\rangle=\tfrac12\mathbf P_\parallel$, gives $d\mathbf n/dT=\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf n$ with $\boldsymbol\Omega=-\tfrac14(\beta_1-\beta_2)(\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf n)\mathbf P/\ell$: the relative plane precesses about the drift direction $\hat{\mathbf P}$ at the rate $|\boldsymbol\Omega|=\tfrac14(\beta_1-\beta_2)|\mathbf P\cdot\mathbf n|\|\mathbf P\|/\ell$ with the cone angle $\angle(\mathbf n,\hat{\mathbf P})$ conserved by the averaged system; the tilt relative to the fixed $z$ axis changes at the rate $d(\arccos n_z)/dT=-(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf n)_z/\sqrt{1-n_z^2}$. The radial period of the mean motion is $2\pi/\omega_r'$ with $\omega_r'^2=2U''(r_c)/a(r_c)$ evaluated with analytic second derivatives at the circle $r_c$ of $U=\bar\ell^2/(br^2)-1/r+\bar W$ (a numerical second difference of $U\sim10^{-6}$ loses five digits and was replaced after it produced a spurious period shift).

Because the apsidal line advances by $\Delta\varphi$ per radial period while $\varphi_P$ is fixed, the second-harmonic term sampled at the $k$-th pericentre is $A_2\cos(2k\Delta\varphi+\psi)$, and at the $k$-th apocentre $A_2\cos((2k+1)\Delta\varphi+\psi)$: the recorded apsides are modulated with the period $\pi/\Delta\varphi$ radial periods, in phase for pericentre and apocentre. For a launch at pericentre ($\dot r(0)=0$, $r(0)=r_p$) the pericentres are $r_{p,k}=r_p+A_2[\cos(2k\Delta\varphi+\psi)-\cos\psi]$, independent of $\delta r_c$, and the apocentres are $r_{a,k}=r_{a}^{(0)}+2\delta r_c+A_2[\cos\psi+\cos((2k+1)\Delta\varphi+\psi)]$ with $r_a^{(0)}$ the apocentre of the $\mathbf P=0$ baseline at the same $\ell$ and $E$; the times of the minima shift by $\tau_k=2\dot\theta_0A_2\sin(2k\Delta\varphi+\psi)/(|A|\omega_r^2)$ with $A$ the first-order radial amplitude, so the mean spacing of $n$ consecutive minima is the mean-motion radial period plus $(\tau_n-\tau_1)/(n-1)$; this phase drift is not a period shift.

### 13.5 Nonlinear orbital stability

(i) Mirror subspace ($\mathbf P=0$). Theorem (derived). Let $\sigma=-1$, $K=c_f=1$, $r_0>0$ with $r_0\notin\{\tfrac12,1\}$, and let $\mathcal C(r_0)$ be the circular solution of Section 6 in any plane through the origin with any phase. For every $\varepsilon>0$ there is $\delta>0$ such that every solution of the full equations with mirror initial data ($\mathbf X_2=-\mathbf X_1$, $\mathbf V_2=-\mathbf V_1$) satisfying $|r(0)-r_0|<\delta$, $|\dot r(0)|<\delta$ and $|\dot\theta(0)-\dot\theta_0|<\delta$ exists for all $T$, remains in the mirror subspace, keeps its relative plane fixed, and satisfies $|r(T)-r_0|<\varepsilon$, $|\dot r(T)|<\varepsilon$ and $|\dot\theta(T)-\dot\theta_0|<\varepsilon$ for all $T$. Proof. The mirror subspace is invariant wherever $H$ is regular (Section 4), and $H$ is regular on $|r-r_0|<\varepsilon$ for $\varepsilon$ small. On it the dynamics is the natural system $L_{\mathrm{red}}=\tfrac14\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}^{\mathsf T}(I-M)\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}+1/r$ with velocity form $\tfrac14(a\,\mathbf e\mathbf e^{\mathsf T}+b(I-\mathbf e\mathbf e^{\mathsf T}))$, $a=1+1/r>1$, $b=1+1/(2r)>1$: positive definite for every $r>0$, so the energy-like $E=\tfrac14a\dot r^2+\tfrac14br^2|\dot{\mathbf e}|^2-1/r$ bounds $\dot r^2$ and $r^2|\dot{\mathbf e}|^2$ below $4(E+1/r)$. The vector $\mathbf L=\tfrac12b\,\boldsymbol\rho\times\dot{\boldsymbol\rho}$ is conserved (rotation symmetry) and $\boldsymbol\rho\perp\mathbf L$, so the motion stays in the plane normal to $\mathbf L(0)$; in that plane $\ell=|\mathbf L|$ is conserved and $E=\tfrac14a\dot r^2+U_{\mathrm{eff}}(r;\ell)$ with $U_{\mathrm{eff}}=\ell^2/(br^2)-1/r$. By Section 9, for $\ell^2>\tfrac12$ the function $U_{\mathrm{eff}}(\cdot;\ell)$ has exactly one critical point $r_c(\ell)=g^{-1}(\ell^2)$, a strict minimum with $U_{\mathrm{eff}}''=8/(r_c(4r_c+1)(2r_c+1))>0$, $U_{\mathrm{eff}}\to+\infty$ at $r\to0$ and $U_{\mathrm{eff}}\to0^-$ at $r\to\infty$; $\ell_0^2=g(r_0)>\tfrac12$ and $g'>0$, so $r_c(\ell)$ depends continuously on $\ell$ and $r_c(\ell_0)=r_0$. The Lyapunov function $\Lambda=E-U_{\mathrm{eff}}(r_c(\ell);\ell)\ge0$ is conserved, vanishes exactly on the circle of radius $r_c(\ell)$, and is positive definite in $(r-r_c(\ell),\dot r)$ uniformly for $\ell$ near $\ell_0$ because $U_{\mathrm{eff}}''$ is bounded away from zero there and $a\ge1$; the Dirichlet–Lagrange argument then confines $(r,\dot r)$ to a neighbourhood of $(r_c(\ell),0)$ that shrinks with $\delta$, and $\dot\theta=2\ell/(br^2)$ follows. The solution stays in a compact set of the regular region, so it is global. $\square$ What the theorem says and does not say: it is nonlinear orbital stability of the circle family inside the mirror subspace, with the plane, phase and radius-within-the-family as the neutral directions (the perturbed solution orbits the circle of radius $r_c(\ell)$ in its own plane, as the DE-100 and WE-150 records show); it holds for every $r_0>0$ off the two singular separations, including outside the declared domain; it is a statement about the adapted law. It does not cover any perturbation with $\mathbf P\ne0$. Falsifier: a mirror history of the Section 1 system, with certified error, that leaves every neighbourhood of the circle family from arbitrarily small mirror perturbations (the Section 9 classification already excludes this for $\ell^2>\tfrac12$).

(ii) Full twelve-dimensional space, $\mathbf P\ne0$. Lyapunov stability of the full state is impossible (derived): any neighbourhood of the circle contains states with $\mathbf P\ne0$, whose centre moves as $\mathbf R(T)=\tfrac12\int_0^TB(\boldsymbol\rho_0)\mathbf P\,dT'$, unbounded and linear in $T$ (the size-two Jordan blocks of Section 7 are the linearization of this drift), so two solutions with different $\mathbf P$ separate without bound. The correct notion is orbital stability of the relative motion modulo the centre: stability of the circle family in the reduced system of Section 13.1, which is exactly the quotient by translations. That reduced system is Lagrangian with the definite velocity form $\tfrac14(I-M)$ for every $\mathbf P$ and every $r>1$ (derived, Section 13.1), so the obstruction is not the loss of a variational structure or of a definite kinetic form; it is that the circle is no longer a relative equilibrium unless $\mathbf n\parallel\hat{\mathbf P}$, and that the amended potential $V_{\mathbf P}$ has only $S^1$ symmetry. Two cases follow. (a) Circles with $\mathbf n=\pm\hat{\mathbf P}$ (relative plane normal to the drift). In spherical coordinates with polar axis $\hat{\mathbf P}$, latitude $\chi$ and azimuth $\phi$, the Routhian kinetic form is diagonal, $\tfrac14[a\dot r^2+br^2\dot\chi^2+br^2\cos^2\chi\,\dot\phi^2]$, so Routh reduction by $\phi$ at fixed $\ell_{\mathbf P}$ introduces no velocity cross terms and leaves the natural two-degree-of-freedom system with $V_{\mathrm{am}}(r,\chi)=\ell_{\mathbf P}^2/(br^2\cos^2\chi)-1/r+\tfrac14\beta_2\|\mathbf P\|^2+\tfrac14(\beta_1-\beta_2)\|\mathbf P\|^2\sin^2\chi$. At $\chi=0$ the mixed derivative vanishes by the symmetry $\chi\to-\chi$, $\partial_\chi^2V_{\mathrm{am}}=2\ell_{\mathbf P}^2/(br^2)+\tfrac12(\beta_1-\beta_2)\|\mathbf P\|^2>0$ because $\beta_1>\beta_2$ for $r>1$, and $\partial_r^2V_{\mathrm{am}}$ at the critical radius is $U_{\mathrm{eff}}''$ plus $\tfrac14\beta_2''\|\mathbf P\|^2$ with $\beta_2''=8/(2r-1)^3>0$ (with $\ell_{\mathbf P}^2$ fixed by the critical-point condition; at $r_c=100$ it is $9.926\times10^{-7}$ for $\|\mathbf P\|=\|\mathbf P_{\mathrm{DG}}\|$ and $9.989\times10^{-7}$ for $\|\mathbf P\|=0.1$, and $1.213\times10^{-4}$ at $r_c=20$, $\|\mathbf P\|=0.1$). Theorem (derived, same proof as (i) with $\Lambda=E_{\mathbf P}-V_{\mathrm{am}}(r_c,0)$ on the two-dimensional reduced configuration space): for every $\mathbf P$ and $r_c>1$ at which $\partial_r^2V_{\mathrm{am}}(r_c,0)>0$, the circle of radius $r_c$ with normal $\pm\hat{\mathbf P}$ is nonlinearly orbitally stable modulo the centre drift and the azimuthal phase, for perturbations that keep $\mathbf P$ fixed; for perturbations that change $\mathbf P$ the statement holds with $r_c$, $\mathbf n$ replaced by the continuously varying equilibrium of the new $\mathbf P$. No instrument run of this configuration exists; falsifier: a run from such a preparation with certified error whose separation leaves a shrinking neighbourhood of $r_c$ or whose plane turns away from $\hat{\mathbf P}$ by a finite angle from an arbitrarily small perturbation. (b) Circles with $\mathbf n\not\parallel\hat{\mathbf P}$, the DG-100 geometry ($\mathbf n\perp\hat{\mathbf P}$ to within $0.2$ rad). Here the motion is not near a relative equilibrium of the reduced system: the averaged latitude potential $\tfrac18(\beta_1-\beta_2)\|\mathbf P\|^2\sin^2\angle(\mathbf n,\hat{\mathbf P})$ is maximal, and the averaged system conserves the cone angle only through the precession of 13.2, so no energy–momentum argument applies at second order in $\mathbf P$; the reduced problem is a two-degree-of-freedom near-integrable system ($r$ and $\chi$ after the $S^1$ reduction) and a persistence statement needs a KAM-type argument. Theorem target: the unperturbed ($\mathbf P=0$) integrable system must satisfy the isoenergetic nondegeneracy condition, which for the circle family reads $\partial(\omega_r/\dot\theta)/\partial E|_\ell\ne0$ with $\omega_r/\dot\theta=2\pi/(2\pi+\Delta\varphi(E,\ell))$ and $\Delta\varphi$ the apsidal advance of Section 11; the zero-coupling control is degenerate ($\Delta\varphi\equiv0$, the Kepler degeneracy), and the frozen law is not: from the quadratures at $\ell_0^2=g(100)$, $d\Delta\varphi/dE|_\ell=-1.9343\times10^{-3}$ and $\partial(\omega_r/\dot\theta)/\partial E|_\ell=+3.078\times10^{-4}$, stable to four digits over $E-E_0\in[5\times10^{-9},8\times10^{-8}]$ (eccentricities $10^{-3}$ to $4\times10^{-3}$; `kam` in the script output), equivalently $\Delta\varphi(E)-\Delta\varphi_0\approx-9.7\times10^{-6}e^2$ (derived-and-computed). What remains for a theorem (theorem target, not claimed): the same nondegeneracy for the second frequency pair ($\chi$ nutation at $\dot\theta_0$ against $\omega_r$, with the tilt family's own degeneracy because a tilted circle is again a circle), a smallness condition on $\|\mathbf P\|^2$ relative to the gaps that a KAM theorem with two degrees of freedom requires, and the fact that in two degrees of freedom invariant tori separate the energy surface, which would then give confinement of $r$ for all time at fixed $\mathbf P$ and $\ell_{\mathbf P}$; outside those hypotheses the honest statement is the measured 20-period and derived 200-period behaviour of 13.3–13.4. Falsifier for the nondegeneracy value: an independent quadrature of $\Delta\varphi(E,\ell_0)$ giving a slope of different sign or vanishing at the circle.
