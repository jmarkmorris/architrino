Thus slow instantaneous bounds and a negative account do not themselves make that floor invariant. This control is not the historical circular preparation, a generated ancient solution, or a counterexample to dispersal.

Use the unchanged canonical equation with $c_f=1$, source-fixed $K>0$, and opposite polarities. The [exact affine row](authorized-cases-ten-hour-d-primary-anisotropic-account.md) supplies an analytically known control: both partner clocks are solved for the actual affine histories, with every ordinary channel retained. Fix orthonormal planar vectors $N,T$, a floor $H_*>0$, and $a>0$ sufficiently small that $a<1/10$ and

$$
a^2<4\sqrt{1-a^2}\,\frac K{H_*}.
\tag{1}
$$

Define the complete supplied past, for every $s\le0$, by

$$
Z(s)=dN+a^2sT,\qquad C(s)=a(N-T)s,
\qquad d=\frac{H_*}{a^2},\qquad X_\pm(s)=C(s)\pm\frac12Z(s).
\tag{2}
$$

These are two straight source histories with constant velocities. Their complete speed is strictly below one for the stated small $a$. Their separation never vanishes: $|Z(s)|^2=d^2+a^4s^2$. Strict subfield speed gives exactly one positive-delay partner root per receiver and no positive-delay self root; the coincident self root remains canonically excluded. The histories are specified on the full past, so no source completion or suppressed channel is used.

At release,

$$
U=a^2T,\qquad W=aN-aT,\qquad H=|Z\times U|=H_*,
\qquad \chi=\frac{Ka^2}{H_*},\qquad g=\sqrt{1-a^2}.
\tag{3}
$$

For an explicit census, let $z_-=-a-a^2/2$, $z_+=-a+a^2/2$, and $q_\pm=\sqrt{1-z_\pm^2}$. The positive receiver samples the negative member at delay $R_+=d/(q_--a)$; the negative receiver samples the positive member at delay $R_-=d/(q_++a)$. Both denominators are positive. These are the unique positive roots of the two respective affine chord equations. They need not coincide, and both sampled source times belong to the fully specified past (2).

The radial relative velocity is zero. The exact scalar account is

$$
\mathcal J(0)=\frac{a^4}{2}-2g\frac{Ka^2}{H_*}
=a^2\left(\frac{a^2}{2}-2g\frac K{H_*}\right)<0.
\tag{4}
$$

Also $\Lambda=d|U|^2/K=(H_*/K)a^2$ tends to zero with $a$, while $\chi$ and every member speed tend to zero. Thus any fixed strict small-speed, small-$\chi$, and upper-$\Lambda$ requirements are met by taking $a$ smaller.

For a source velocity with radial component $a$ and tangential component $z$, write $f(z)=z/\sqrt{1-z^2}$. The exact two-clock affine formula gives the tangential relative acceleration at reception time zero:

$$
(A_+-A_-)\cdot T
=\frac K{d^2}\left[a^2+a\left\{f(-a-a^2/2)+f(-a+a^2/2)\right\}\right].
\tag{5}
$$

Both arguments of $f$ are negative, and $f(z)\le z$ there. Their sum is therefore at most $-2a$. Equation (5) yields

$$
H'(0)=d(A_+-A_-)\cdot T
\le-\frac{Ka^2}{d}<0.
\tag{6}
$$

The planar orientation of $Z\times U$ is positive at release, so this is also the derivative of its magnitude. Ordinary local continuation from the smooth supplied histories retains the root and separation margins. The acceleration may jump at release from its supplied zero value to the canonical value; velocity remains continuous. By continuity of the future acceleration, $H(t)<H_*$ for sufficiently small positive $t$, while the scalar account remains negative and speeds remain strict.
