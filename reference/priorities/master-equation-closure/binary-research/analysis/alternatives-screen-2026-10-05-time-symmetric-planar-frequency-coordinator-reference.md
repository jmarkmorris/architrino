# Coordinator reference: a noninteger planar frequency at slow exact circles

**Derived candidate, frozen before receiving the independently assigned planar-frequency result.** The case is the exactly balanced Section 14 equal-past/future opposite-polarity circle, $K=c_f=1$, $0<\beta<1$, $x=\beta\cos x$, with its full ordinary past/future partner roots and no noninstantaneous self roots. This source examines bounded solutions of the full Cartesian linear variational equation, not a nonlinear family or a causal initial-value evolution.

Use the already derived complete tensor, including the shifted source-velocity acceleration term, and let m be an arbitrary real rotating-frame frequency in units of the base angular frequency. The earlier fixed-period matrix formulas are analytic in m and remain valid for the exponential ansatz at real noninteger m. In the opposite exchange sector write the Hermitian block

$$
H_{-,m}=\begin{pmatrix}a&if\\-if&d\end{pmatrix}.
$$

A direct small-x expansion of the tensor coefficients gives, uniformly for m in a fixed compact neighborhood of one,

$$
A=1+O(x^4),\quad C=-\tfrac12+O(x^4),\quad
U=1+x^2+O(x^4),\quad V=-\tfrac12+O(x^4),\quad
W=O(x^3),\quad \kappa=x+O(x^5).
$$

Here $A,C,U,V,W,\kappa$ are the exact coefficients in the frozen [speed-family formulation](alternatives-screen-2026-10-05-time-symmetric-speed-family-formulation.md), not adjustable parameters. To reconstruct the leading terms, $\alpha=1+x^2/2+O(x^4)$, $\gamma=-x/2+O(x^5)$, $\zeta=-1/2-x^2/2+O(x^4)$, $\beta=x/\cos x$ and $D=1+x^2+O(x^4)$. Substitution in the complete tensor gives

$$
a=-m^2-3-x^2+O(x^4),\qquad
d=-m^2-m^2x^2+O(x^4),\qquad
f=-2m-mx^2+O(x^4).
$$

Consequently

$$
\det H_{-,m}=m^2(m^2-1)+m^4x^2+O(x^4).
$$

At $(x,m)=(0,1)$ the determinant vanishes and its derivative in m equals two. All exact coefficients are real analytic near this point; their denominator factors are nonzero. The elementary analytic implicit-function theorem therefore yields a unique nearby simple real branch

$$
m(x)=1-\frac{x^2}{2}+O(x^4).
$$

The even remainder follows from the exact even dependence of the determinant on x. On a sufficiently small positive interval, $0<m(x)<1$ and $m'(x)<0$. The constants and interval are existential analytic bounds, not a numerical admission threshold. Each such branch is a noninteger rotating-frame normal mode in the opposite planar sector. It is distinct from the opposite phase mode at m zero and the common translation mode at m one.

The null vector tends to $(-i,2)$ up to a nonzero scalar. Real and imaginary parts of $R(\omega t)w\exp(im\omega t)$ give real bounded Cartesian variations of opposite labels. Their two circular components have frequencies $(1-m)\omega$ and $(1+m)\omega$, both nonzero for small positive x. At the limiting vector neither circular component vanishes, so this remains true nearby. These bounded directions are not fixed-base-period perturbations. If m is irrational their inertial frequencies are incommensurate. Since m is strictly monotone on a sufficiently small interval, rational m values occur at most countably many x; no numerical case is identified by this observation.

These bounded variations and their derivatives are uniformly bounded. Multiplying them by a sufficiently small amplitude produces complete separated uniformly subfield trial paths, preserving the ordinary two-sided root census. Their equation residual vanishes to first order only. A nonlinear quasiperiodic solution, a periodic bifurcation, or stability of any history requires additional proof. The previous local fixed-period and nearby-period circular classification is not contradicted: it concerns solutions in its declared normalized periodic neighborhood, whereas a noninteger rotating-frame mode generally fails that periodicity condition.

Falsifiers are a missing source-clock term in the complete tensor, a wrong $m^4x^2$ determinant coefficient, failure of analyticity or the simple derivative two at the limiting root, or mistaken classification of a noninteger mode as a fixed-period perturbation. No numerical instrument or external physical law enters this reference.
