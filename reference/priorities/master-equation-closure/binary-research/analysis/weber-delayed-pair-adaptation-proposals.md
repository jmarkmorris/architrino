# Delayed Weber pair: adaptation proposals

**Status: ○ proposals only, not approved, not executed; item 7 applies because the [investigation verdict](weber-delayed-pair-investigation.md#8-verdict) is loss of binding.** Item 7 of the [launch brief](weber-delayed-pair-launch-brief.md) asks that, if Section 9a loses binding on the pair, a separate document name at most three causal-delay adaptations that the analysis indicates could retain it, each in the format of the [approval rule](../../equation-variants/README.md#operator-approval-for-deviations): baseline, plain-language change, exact specification, and what it would test. This document does that and nothing more. Listing a candidate permits description and discussion, not execution or adoption. The analysis that motivates the proposals is in the [investigation](weber-delayed-pair-investigation.md), Sections 4 and 6.

## What the analysis indicates

Section 9a loses the slow pair at first order in $1/c_f$, before its Weber bracket acts. The mechanism is the canonical delayed prefactor: on a receiver the delayed line of action $\mathbf n$ differs from the present direction $\mathbf e$ by $\mathbf v_{j\perp}/c_f$, where $\mathbf v_{j\perp}$ is the transmitter's present velocity transverse to the separation; combined with the delayed range and the transmitter weight this produces the acceleration correction $\sigma K(\mathbf v_j-2(\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v_j)\mathbf e)/(r^2c_f)$ ([Theorem 4.1](weber-delayed-pair-investigation.md#4-slow-motion-expansion-and-the-first-departure-from-section-9)). For an opposite-polarity mirror pair its transverse part is a forward push on each member, $K\|\mathbf v_{i\perp}\|/(r^2c_f)$, which raises the angular quantity $h$ at the rate $Kh/(r^2c_f)$ and expands the pair at the canonical rate $d(\rho_h^2)/dT=K/c_f$. [Corollary 4.4](weber-delayed-pair-investigation.md#4-slow-motion-expansion-and-the-first-departure-from-section-9) shows that no scalar bracket multiplying $\mathbf n$, with any coefficients, changes this first-order transverse push. Any adaptation that retains binding on the slow pair must therefore change the line of action or add a component of acceleration outside the line of action; adjusting $\lambda_{\mathrm W}$, $\mu_{\mathrm W}$ or the bracket's functional form cannot do it.

The three candidates below are the ones the analysis points to. Each keeps causal support: every quantity is evaluated on ordinary causal roots of the transmitter's actual past. None is a future-supported interaction, which the operator has withdrawn.

## Proposal P1: Weber bracket on an extrapolated line of action

- **Baseline.** Section 9a as frozen.
- **Plain-language change.** Keep the causal root, the delayed range and the transmitter weight, but point the acceleration along the direction from the transmitter's position extrapolated from the emission event to the reception time at its emission velocity, instead of from the emission position itself. The first-order transverse misalignment is then cancelled; the pair is pulled toward where the partner would now be if it had kept its emission velocity.
- **Exact specification.** Replace $\mathbf n$ in the frozen law by $\tilde{\mathbf n}=(\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(S)-\mathbf V_j(S)(T-S))/\|\cdot\|$ in the direction factor only; retain $\mathscr R$, $D_t$, the bracket and all other clauses unchanged. Added term: none. Removed term: none. Distance dependence, support, self and partner channels, root set, speed domain: unchanged. The extrapolated vector has norm $\mathscr R\,\|\mathbf n-\mathbf V_j(S)/c_f\|$, which is positive on subfield histories, so the direction is defined wherever the frozen law is.
- **What it would test.** Whether removing the first-order transverse push alone restores a bound class on the slow pair, with the Weber bracket then acting at second order as in Section 9. The first check is the slow-motion expansion of the modified prefactor: the first-order term should become purely radial, $-\sigma K(\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v_j)\mathbf e/(r^2c_f)$, and the second-order secular rate of $h$ then decides. Falsifier of the motivation: a nonzero first-order transverse term after the substitution.

## Proposal P2: Weber bracket on the Section 7 Maxwell-shaped transmitter prefactor

- **Baseline.** Section 9a as frozen, with the [complete Maxwell-shaped transmitter response](../../equation-variants/manuscript.md#7-complete-maxwell-shaped-transmitter-response) of Section 7 as the comparison prefactor.
- **Plain-language change.** Keep the Weber bracket and the differentiation of the delayed range, but multiply it by the Section 7 velocity-field prefactor instead of the canonical one. That prefactor's slow expansion has no first-order velocity term, so the forward push would start at second order, where the Weber bracket can compete with it.
- **Exact specification.** Replace $\sigma Kc_f\mathbf n/(\mathscr R^2|D_t|)$ by the Section 7 velocity-field vector on the same ordinary root, exactly as Section 7 defines it, and multiply by the frozen bracket $1-\dot{\mathscr R}^2/(2c_f^2)+\mathscr R\ddot{\mathscr R}/c_f^2$. Acceleration-field terms of Section 7, if any are part of its selection, remain as Section 7 specifies. Coefficients, support, root set, self channels and speed domain unchanged from Section 9a.
- **What it would test.** Whether a first-order-clean prefactor combined with the Weber bracket yields a bound class at second order, and whether the combined second-order terms (Section 7's own plus the Weber bracket's) have a sign that binds. The decisive first calculation is the second-order secular rate of $h$ on a slow mirror circle under the combined law; a positive rate at second order would already rule it out for the slow pair.

## Proposal P3: a transverse delayed term proportional to the emission-velocity misalignment

- **Baseline.** Section 9a as frozen.
- **Plain-language change.** Add to each hit a transverse acceleration proportional to the transmitter's emission velocity component across the line of action, with a coefficient chosen so that the first-order transverse push cancels, leaving the radial Weber structure untouched.
- **Exact specification.** Add to each ordinary-root contribution the term $-\sigma K c_f\,\gamma\,(\mathbf V_j(S))_\perp/(\mathscr R^2|D_t|c_f)$ with $(\mathbf V_j(S))_\perp=\mathbf V_j(S)-(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j(S))\mathbf n$ and the single new dimensionless coefficient $\gamma=1$, which is the value that cancels the first-order transverse term of Theorem 4.1. Everything else unchanged. This is an added term with a fixed coefficient and causal support; it is not a future-supported interaction and not a change of the root set.
- **What it would test.** Whether the first-order cancellation is enough by itself, and separately whether $\gamma=1$ is forced or whether a range of $\gamma$ binds. A coefficient sweep would be a model swept over its own knob and would report only what was built in; the honest test is the fixed value and its slow expansion.

## Ordering and recommendation

P1 changes no coefficient and adds no term; it is the smallest change that addresses the identified mechanism, and its slow expansion can be done by hand in the same way as Theorem 4.1. P2 reuses an already-registered prefactor. P3 is the most explicit but introduces a coefficient. The analysis supports examining P1 first, by expansion before any evolution. None of the three is approved by this document.
