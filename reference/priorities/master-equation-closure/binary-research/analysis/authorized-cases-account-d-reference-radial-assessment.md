## Equality also forces a positive relative terminal speed

The sentence in the frozen subject that equality permits a zero relative terminal velocity is not supported by its own sharper derivative. Preserve the source, but replace that limitation in any accepted roll-up with the following deduction. For every positive $u$,

$$
P'\le ku\chi\left[\frac6{2+u}-3\right]
=-\frac{3ku^2\chi}{2+u}<0.
\tag{1}
$$

Thus an entry with $P(T)=0$ immediately has $P(t)<0$ at every later finite time, and the subject's strict-$P$ argument already gives a positive radial lower speed thereafter.

A quantitative uniform consequence follows without choosing a later witness. Since $u\ge2\sqrt\chi$ and $\chi\le\chi_0$,

$$
\frac{3u\chi}{2+u}\ge
\frac{3\chi^{3/2}}{1+\sqrt{\chi_0}}.
$$

Combine this with $-\chi'=ku>0$ and integrate (1):

$$
P(t)\le P(T)-\frac{6}{5(1+\sqrt{\chi_0})}
\left[\chi_0^{5/2}-\chi(t)^{5/2}\right].
\tag{2}
$$

The already proved growth sends $\chi(t)$ to zero. Therefore, writing

$$
\eta_*=-P(T)+\frac{6\chi_0^{5/2}}{5(1+\sqrt{\chi_0})}>0,
$$

one has $\liminf u(t)\ge2\sqrt{\eta_*}$. The convergent relative velocity $U_\infty$ and time averaging of $Z'=U$ then imply

$$
|U_\infty|\ge2\sqrt{\eta_*}>0,
\qquad d(t)/t\longrightarrow |U_\infty|.
\tag{3}
$$

It supplies a positive linear tail for every admitted $P(T)\le0$, including equality.
