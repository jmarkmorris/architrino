# Recurrent unit-speed approach requires outward speed peaks

## Scope and conclusions

Claim grade: derived candidate, awaiting independent assessment. Consider an all-future regular strict-subfield continuation of the admitted sufficiently small logarithmic perturbation family. Keep every ordinary root and the unchanged self treatment, as fixed in the [method admission](authorized-cases-ten-hour-c-spiral-method-admission.md). The accepted positive angular momentum and acute partner lag apply throughout this continuation.

Every interior local maximum of physical speed has strictly positive radial velocity and speed greater than $1/\sqrt3$. If its all-future upper limiting speed is $B$, then

$$
B^6+2B^2-1\ge0.
\tag{1}
$$

If $B=1$, speed must repeatedly fall below some fixed number less than one, and arbitrarily late interior local maxima approach one. Thus an all-future unit-speed approach can only occur through nonconvergent speed excursions. Neither their existence for an actual member nor a finite unit-speed event is proved here.

The [speed-limit rigidity theorem](authorized-cases-ten-hour-c-spiral-speed-limit-rigidity.md), independently assessed in [the reference assessment](authorized-cases-ten-hour-reference-c-spiral-limit-adjudication.md), is used only to handle convergent speed and to exclude a limit equal to one. All identities below are direct consequences of the fixed response row.

## Exact speed curvature at a stationary speed

Write $v=x'$, $b=|v|$, and use the ordinary chord variables

$$
R=t-s=|x(t)+x(s)|,\qquad n=\frac{x(t)+x(s)}R,
\qquad D=1+n\cdot v(s)>0.
$$

Then

$$
v'=-\frac n{RD},\qquad
b'=-\frac{n\cdot v}{bRD},\qquad
s'=\frac{1-n\cdot v}D.
\tag{2}
$$

Positive geometric angular momentum makes $b>0$. At an interior stationary speed, $n\cdot v=0$, and $s'=1/D$. Differentiate the chord to obtain

$$
n'=\frac{v+v(s)s'-nR'}R.
$$

At the stationary point this gives

$$
(n\cdot v)'=\frac{b^2D+v\cdot v(s)-1}{RD},
$$

and hence the exact curvature identity

$$
b''=\frac{1-b^2D-v\cdot v(s)}{bR^2D^2}.
\tag{3}
$$

The response is differentiable at every regular interior time: the complete path is at least $C^2$, the ordinary clock has positive denominator, and differentiating the acceleration uses only source acceleration already defined by the given history or generated path. Thus an interior local maximum satisfies $b''\le0$, including a degenerate maximum.

## A speed maximum is outward and exceeds a universal lower bound

Let $e=x/r$ and let $J$ denote the positive planar quarter turn. The acute positive lag $\delta=\theta(t)-\theta(s)$ gives

$$
n=\frac{r+r_s\cos\delta}{R}e
-\frac{r_s\sin\delta}{R}Je.
$$

At a stationary speed the sign of angular momentum selects $v=bJn$. Consequently

$$
r'=v\cdot e=\frac{b r_s\sin\delta}{R}>0.
\tag{4}
$$

At a local maximum, (3) gives

$$
1\le b^2D+v\cdot v(s)
=b^2+(b^2n+v)\cdot v(s).
\tag{5}
$$

Since $n\cdot v=0$, one has $|b^2n+v|=b\sqrt{1+b^2}$. Every sampled source remains strictly subfield, so Cauchy–Schwarz yields the strict inequality

$$
1<b^2+b\sqrt{1+b^2}.
$$

For $0<b<1$, this is equivalent to $b>1/\sqrt3$. The claim concerns maxima of the speed magnitude, not maxima of the radius. Formula (4) makes the distinction explicit.

As a known consistency case, the exact admitted constant-speed spiral has $b'=b''=0$ everywhere. Its balance identities give equality in (5), while its speed exceeds $1/\sqrt3$; the strict source-speed estimate is therefore consistent with that case. At the conditional unit-speed equality, the numerator in (3) becomes $-(n+v)\cdot v(s)$, matching the already established grazing-curvature expression. Neither check modifies the law at equality.

## The all-future upper envelope

Suppose first that $b(t)$ does not converge. Let $A=\liminf b$ and $B=\limsup b$, so $A<B$. Choose any number strictly between them. Arbitrarily late values approaching $B$ can be bracketed on both sides by values below that number. Maximizing $b$ on each such compact bracket gives interior local maxima $t_j\to\infty$ with $b(t_j)\to B$. The source clock escapes to infinity, so $|v(s(t_j))|\le B+o(1)$. Apply (5) at these maxima and pass to the limit:

$$
1\le B^2+B^2\sqrt{1+B^2}.
\tag{6}
$$

For $0\le B\le1$, (6) is equivalent to (1). To see this, set $y=B^2$. The inequality $y\sqrt{1+y}\ge1-y$ has nonnegative sides, so squaring gives $y^3+2y-1\ge0$. This cubic is strictly increasing on $[0,1]$ and has a unique zero there.

If $b$ converges, the speed-limit theorem fixes $B=\nu_*=a\sqrt{1+\omega^2}$. The admitted rectangle gives $a>277/1000$ and $\omega>229/100$, hence $\nu_*>69/100$. This is more than enough for (1): at $B=69/100$, one has $B^2>47/100$ and therefore $B^6+2B^2-1>(47/100)^3+94/100-1>0$. The cubic increases with $B$. Thus (1) holds on every all-future branch, whether or not speed converges.

When $B=1$, the speed-limit theorem excludes $A=1$. Choose $q$ with $A<q<1$. The bracketing construction gives arbitrarily late outward local maxima tending to one, separated by passages below $q$. This rules out a monotone or eventually convergent approach to unit speed on an infinite branch. It does not provide a numerical value of $q$ or prove that any member has $B=1$.

## No Cartesian terminal velocity on an infinite branch

A further corollary is that the Cartesian velocity vector has no limit, without assuming a uniform future subfield margin. If $v(t)\to V$, the scalar speed theorem gives $|V|=\nu_*>0$. The elementary averaging identity then gives $x(t)/t\to V$, and therefore $x(t)/|x(t)|\to V/|V|$. A continuous, strictly increasing angle that tends to infinity cannot have its direction converge on the circle: it passes through every successive full turn. This contradicts the accepted unbounded positive winding theorem. The previous terminal-velocity result assumed a uniform speed margin; this corollary uses the stronger all-future winding and speed-limit results for this fixed family.

## Remaining distinction and falsifiers

These conclusions sharpen possible infinite fate. They still do not choose finite unit arrival, asymptotic spiral return, or nonconvergent speed for a specified nonzero family member. A speed-extremum calculation cannot replace the missing actual-history bridge from the finite nonlinear departure.

Falsifiers are an error in differentiating the ordinary clock or chord in (3); loss of the angular-momentum sign selecting $v=bJn$; a source outside the strict-subfield history; failure of the escaping source clock along the peak sequence; or a defect in the independently assessed speed-limit theorem. A future speed excursion that has no local maximum does not itself violate the local statement, but an all-future bounded nonconvergent speed supplies the bracketed maximum sequence used in (6).
