## An outgoing criterion that retains the complete past

Set the new release clock to zero for this lemma only. The complete compatible supplied past has individual speeds at most $b_{past}<b<1$. Choose a fixed unit vector $e$, a positive projected present gap

$$
D=e\cdot(X_1(0)-X_2(0))>0,
$$

and positive outward projected relative velocity

$$
w=e\cdot(V_1(0)-V_2(0))>0.
$$

Write $B=\max_i|V_i(0)|<b$, $u=w/2$ and $C_b=(1+b)^p/(1-b)$. A sufficient strict condition is

$$
I:=\frac{C_bD^{1-p}}{u(p-1)}
<\min\left(b-B,\frac w4\right).
$$

Suppose provisionally that future individual speeds stay below $b$ and $e\cdot(V_1-V_2)>u$. Then actual separation $d(T)=|X_1-X_2|$ is at least $D+uT$. Complete transmitter displacement gives $d\le(1+b)R$ and $D_t\ge1-b$ for either partner hit. Therefore

$$
|A_i(T)|\le C_b(D+uT)^{-p},\qquad
\int_0^\infty |A_i|\,dT\le I.
$$

These bounds include sources in any old supplied segment. They do not require old separation to exceed $D$, nor replace an old source by an affine extrapolation. The partner residual increases from a negative present value to positive infinity with derivative at least $1-b$, so the complete census is one partner hit. Strict complete chords exclude self hits.

Integration gives $|V_i(T)|\le B+I<b$ and $e\cdot(V_1-V_2)\ge w-2I>w/2=u$. Both bootstrap boundaries are strict. Separation is bounded below, every finite root/source chart has positive delay and denominator, and bounded speed precludes finite escape to infinity. Ordinary continuation supplies a unique global separated future with the same complete speed margin.

The acceleration bound is integrable because $p>1$, giving terminal Cartesian velocities. Their projected relative difference is at least $w-2I>u>0$, so they are distinct. Relative separation is asymptotically linear. The velocity tail is $O(T^{1-p})$; integrating it yields the usual $O(T^{2-p})$, $O(\log T)$ or $O(1)$ position correction for $1<p<2$, $p=2$ or $p>2$. Thus the relative direction has finite remaining total angular variation. This is scattering, not binding or a constant-center law.
