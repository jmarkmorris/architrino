## 1. The source clock and torque preserve orientation

The partner source clock is increasing because

$$
s'(t)=\frac{1-n\cdot v(t)}{1+n\cdot v(s(t))}>0.
$$

It therefore never resamples a time older than $s_0$. At every finite subfield stage the complete past has a strict bound $\beta_t<1$. Its chord inequality gives

$$
|x(t)-x(s)|\le\beta_t|x(t)+x(s)|.
$$

Writing $r_s=|x(s)|$ and $\delta=\theta(t)-\theta(s)$, this implies

$$
\cos\delta\ge
\frac{(1-\beta_t^2)(r^2+r_s^2)}{2(1+\beta_t^2)rr_s}>0.
\tag{1}
$$

The lag is a continuous unwrapped quantity. Starting in $(0,\pi/2)$, it cannot cross $\pi/2$ because of (1). While all sampled angular motion is positive, it also cannot reach zero, since $\delta=\int_s^t h(u)/r(u)^2\,du>0$. The exact torque is

$$
h'(t)=\frac{r(t)r(s(t))\sin\delta(t)}{R(t)^2D(t)}>0.
\tag{2}
$$

A first zero of current $h$ would therefore contradict its positive derivative throughout the preceding interval. This closes the coupled first-exit argument: $h(t)\ge h(0)\ge h_*$ and $0<\delta(t)<\pi/2$ persist on the regular subfield continuation. The statement uses the actual two-time geometry, not an assumed right-hand rule or an imposed angular direction.

Since $h\le r|v|<r$, it also gives the absolute radius floor $r(t)>h_*$. Thus collision cannot be the first finite event while this subfield orientation argument applies.

## 2. A fixed complete speed margin forces unbounded separation

Now add the conditional hypothesis that the entire continuation and its supplied past have one fixed bound $|v|\le\beta<1$. Set $L_\beta=(1+\beta)/(1-\beta)$. At every reception,

$$
\frac{2r}{1+\beta}\le R\le\frac{2r}{1-\beta},\qquad
\frac r{L_\beta}\le r_s\le L_\beta r.
$$

Every intermediate radius on $[s,t]$ is also at most $L_\beta r$, since its displacement from the reception is at most $\beta R$. All angular motions there are at least $h_*$, including the initially supplied portion. Therefore

$$
\delta=\int_s^t\frac{h(u)}{r(u)^2}\,du
\ge\frac{h_*R}{L_\beta^2r^2}.
$$

Using $\sin\delta\ge2\delta/\pi$ on $[0,\pi/2]$ and $D\le1+\beta$ in (2) gives

$$
h'(t)\ge\frac{2h_*}{\pi L_\beta^3R(1+\beta)}
\ge\frac{h_*}{\pi L_\beta^4r(t)}.
\tag{3}
$$

With $\tau(t)=\int_0^tdu/r(u)$, this proves the explicit bounds

$$
h(t)\ge h(0)+\frac{h_*}{\pi L_\beta^4}\tau(t),
$$

$$
r(t)\ge\frac{h(t)}\beta
\ge\frac{h(0)}\beta+
\frac{h_*}{\pi\beta^2L_\beta^4}
\log\left(1+\frac{\beta t}{r(0)}\right).
\tag{4}
$$

Here $\tau(t)\ge\beta^{-1}\log(1+\beta t/r(0))$ follows from the speed bound. Consequently any all-future continuation with this uniform margin has $h(t)\to\infty$ and $r(t)\to\infty$. This is actual separation tending to infinity, not merely an unbounded sequence of radii. Radial speed need not be positive at every time; finite radial returns are not excluded.

The accepted angular-variation estimate becomes an unwrapped-angle estimate because (2) preserves its sign:

$$
\theta(t)-\theta(0)\ge
\frac{1-\beta}{2\beta^2(1+\beta)}
\log\left(1+\frac{\beta t}{r(0)}\right)-2
\longrightarrow\infty.
\tag{5}
$$

Thus the conditional outward fate has endless winding and cannot be the finite-angle canonical dispersal regime. No convergence to another spiral, fixed limiting speed, stability, attraction or binding follows.
