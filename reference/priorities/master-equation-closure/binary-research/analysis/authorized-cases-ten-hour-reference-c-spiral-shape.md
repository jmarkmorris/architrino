# Blind extension: scalar convergence determines the normalized spiral shape

**Derived conditional reference, frozen before the new asymptotic-shape subject is opened.** Retain the actual logarithmic family and suppose an all-future strict-subfield member has a scalar speed limit. The accepted [speed-limit assessment](authorized-cases-ten-hour-reference-c-spiral-limit-adjudication.md) identifies it as $\nu_*=a\sqrt{1+\omega^2}<1$. Consequently a fixed strict speed margin holds on the entire sufficiently late future. Together with the compact earlier future and fixed supplied past, this gives a complete margin $\beta<1$. This is now a consequence of convergence; it was not available for arbitrary all-future branches.

## Radius-scale compactness removes a possible collapse

For any causal interval, write $C=[x(s)+x(t)]/2=Rn/2$. The complete speed bound gives, at every intermediate point $u$,

$$
|x(u)-C|\le\frac\beta2R,
\qquad \frac{1-\beta}{2}R\le r(u)\le\frac{1+\beta}{2}R.
\tag{1}
$$

Thus the entire sampled arc, not only its endpoints, has radius comparable to its delay and to the current radius. Also $D\ge1-\beta$ and $1-n\cdot v\ge1-\beta$. These provide regular source charts at every positive-radius point of a normalized limit.

Choose an arbitrary sequence $t_j\to\infty$, set $r_j=r(t_j)\to\infty$, and consider $q_j(u)=x(t_j+r_ju)/r_j$. Along a subsequence $t_j/r_j\to L\in[1,\infty]$, using the accepted linear upper radius bound. Position Lipschitz compactness gives a limit on $u>-L$, with $|q(0)|=1$. On the positive-radius component containing zero, (1) places every limiting causal arc inside the same component and bounds its radius away from zero on compact receiving intervals. The accepted advancing source ratio keeps those source times strictly above the scaled lower boundary; when $L=\infty$, their bounded delays also do so. Actual times on each such compact source interval tend to infinity.

Equation (1), the fixed margin and the exact row bound acceleration locally. Repeating this bound over source intervals gives source-velocity equicontinuity. The clocks and row therefore pass to the limit there, with speed exactly $\nu_*$, ordinary roots and no self hits. Positive angular orientation and acute lag pass as in the speed-limit proof; the nonzero constant-speed row makes the orientation strict. This constructs the needed regular limit even when $r(t_j)/t_j$ tends to zero.

On that component, the constant-speed normal equation has a discrete heading-lag level set, so $D$ is constant. Integration and the exact chord remove the Cartesian integration center and give an exact logarithmic spiral on the component. Both endpoints are in the component by (1), so the chord identity is available throughout. The classified parameters are the fixed $(a,\omega,\lambda)$. In particular the radial derivative at the normalized receiving point is $a$, independent of the spiral's possible time origin or phase. Since the original sequence was arbitrary,

$$
r'(t)\longrightarrow a>0. \tag{2}
$$

This reasoning can alternatively be expressed as a contradiction sequence for failure of (2); every such sequence has a regular radius-normalized subsequence with radial derivative $a$ at zero. Integration gives $r(t)/t\to a$. Hence a uniform linear lower radius is a conclusion, not a compactness premise. Moreover $h/r=\sqrt{|v|^2-r'^2}\to a\omega$ by positive orientation, and $h/t\to a^2\omega$.

## Full normalized sampled shape

Now normalize by elapsed physical time and remove the current radial phase. On any fixed compact interval of positive $u$, the paths $e^{-i\theta(t)}x(tu)/t$ have radii tending uniformly to $au$. The complete margin and (1) supply uniform source-clock and velocity regularity there. Every subsequential limit solves the same row with constant speed $\nu_*$, has radius $au$, and has radial direction $e_1$ at $u=1$. The spiral classification and radius identity fix its formal time origin to zero and its phase to zero. Therefore every subsequence has the same limit:

$$
\frac{e^{-i\theta(t)}x(tu)}t\longrightarrow a u e^{i\omega\log u},\qquad
e^{-i\theta(t)}v(tu)\longrightarrow a(1+i\omega)e^{i\omega\log u}.
$$

Convergence is locally uniform on positive $u$ and in the corresponding position/velocity history norm. The exact row then gives locally uniform scaled acceleration convergence once source intervals are included. The clocks obey $s(tu)/t\to\lambda u$ and $D(tu)\to1/\lambda$ locally uniformly. In particular the complete normalized sampled segment converges, including its moving source endpoint; a compact interval slightly larger than $[\lambda,1]$ controls that endpoint.

These conclusions imply $t\theta'(t)\to\omega$, and hence $\theta(t)=\omega\log t+o(\log t)$ up to a constant convention. They do not imply that $\theta(t)-\omega\log t$ has a finite limit, since the residual angular derivative has not been proved integrable. Nor do they prove that scalar speed convergence occurs for a departing member. Finite capture is already excluded separately; asymptotic shape convergence modulo the current rotation is compatible with that exclusion.

The exact spiral is the analytical known case. Falsifiers are failure of the full causal-arc comparability in (1), loss of a source at the radius-scaled time boundary, failure of derivative compactness on a positive-radius component, or a classified constant-speed shape with a different radial derivative. No numerical computation or new physical history was introduced.
