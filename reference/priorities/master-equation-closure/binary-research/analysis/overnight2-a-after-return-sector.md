## The attained angular-sector continuation

$$

## The all-future sector branch disperses

On the last alternative, the finite account budget gives

$$
\int_{T_B}^{\infty}\frac{K^2}{d(t)^3}\,dt
\le\frac23j_B<\infty.
\tag{3}
$$

The existing speed bound gives $|d'|\le|U|<3\times10^{-12}$ uniformly. Together, these two facts force $d(t)\to\infty$, not merely an unbounded subsequence. To see this directly, fix any finite radius $D_*>d(T_B)$. If $d$ were eventually bounded by $2D_*$, (3) would diverge. Otherwise infinitely many returns to $d\le D_*$ would require infinitely many disjoint crossings from $D_*$ to $2D_*$. Each crossing lasts at least $D_*/(3\times10^{-12})$ and contributes at least

$$
\frac{K^2}{(2D_*)^3}\frac{D_*}{3\times10^{-12}}>0
$$

to (3), again a contradiction. The same reasoning for every finite radius gives divergence of separation. The negative-account kinetic bound then gives

$$
|U(t)|^2<4.04K/d(t)\longrightarrow0.
$$

This is conditional all-future dispersal with zero relative terminal speed for an actual coupled branch. Monotone separation is unnecessary. It does not prove convergence of either individual velocity: (1) bounds midpoint magnitude and does not establish a finite total variation of $W$. Nor does it identify this branch as the one taken by the original member.
