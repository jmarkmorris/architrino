# Nonlinear departure from the expanding spiral modulo its symmetries

## Theorem and exact claim boundary

**Derived theorem, pending independent assessment.** For the fixed inverse-distance equation $p=1$, $K=R_*=c_f=1$, there are complete separated uniformly subfield $C^{2,1}$ mirror-planar histories arbitrarily close to the admitted spiral's specified complete preparation whose actual futures depart by a fixed positive amount from the local family of symmetry-related spirals, measured in a similarity-history norm. The departure occurs at finite physical time, and the complete supplied-plus-evolved history stays separated and uniformly subfield through that time. No subsequent perturbed fate is asserted.

The base and its full root census are those in [the admitted exact spiral](alternatives-screen-2026-10-05-logarithmic-spiral-adjudication.md). The only spectral premise is the independently accepted growing relative-planar pair in [the perturbation assessment](alternatives-screen-2026-10-05-logarithmic-spiral-perturbation-adjudication.md): one exponent satisfies

$$
\alpha=\Re k\ge\alpha_0=0.0138698363660541>0.
$$

No completeness of the computed spectrum is assumed. There may be additional growing modes. The proof below converts this single admitted mode into actual finite departure, checks the state-dependent-delay hypotheses, preserves the specified old preparation through a finite entry step, and separates departure from symmetry drift.

More precisely, there are constants $\varepsilon_d>0$, $\eta_0>0$ and a one-parameter compatible family $q^\eta$ tending to the specified complete past in $C^2$, supported as a variation on a fixed finite past interval, such that for every sufficiently small $0<\eta<\eta_0$ some finite $T_\eta$ obeys

$$
\operatorname{dist}_{C^1}(Z^\eta_{\tau_\eta},\mathcal S)\ge\varepsilon_d,
\qquad \tau_\eta=\log(1+T_\eta).
$$

Here $Z=(U,U')$ contains all twelve Cartesian position/velocity coordinates, $Z_\tau$ is a finite similarity-history segment, and $\mathcal S$ is the local seven-dimensional family generated by translations, rotations and time-origin changes of the exact spiral. Spatial/time dilation introduces no extra independent parameter on this orbit. There is no artificial restriction of the full equation to planar or mirror perturbations; the witnessing family lies in that invariant sector.

The construction gives $\tau_\eta\asymp\log(1/\eta)$ with unspecified positive constants. For each chosen $0<\beta<\alpha_0$, it can be arranged that

$$
\tau_\eta\le C_\beta+\beta^{-1}\log(1/\eta),\qquad
1+T_\eta\le C'_\beta\eta^{-1/\beta}.
$$

These are existence bounds, not a measured departure time or an asymptotic equality with exponent $1/\alpha$. The neighborhood and constants are not numerically certified.

## Functional-analytic theorem used and checked

The mathematical input is the solution-manifold theorem in Hartung, Krisztin, Walther and Wu, [Section 3.2, Theorem 3.2.1](https://aimath.org/WWN/variabletimelag/sur0b.pdf). It concerns a functional $f$ on an open subset of $C^1([-H,0],\mathbb R^n)$: $f$ is $C^1$; each derivative extends continuously to $C^0$ directions; and the extended evaluation $(\phi,\chi)\mapsto D_e f(\phi)\chi$ is jointly continuous. With a nonempty compatibility set $X_f=\{\phi:\phi'(0)=f(\phi)\}$, it gives a $C^1$ solution manifold, a continuous local semiflow, $C^1$ time maps, and their variational derivatives. These are the only state-dependent-delay theorem conclusions imported below. The instability and symmetry-distance arguments are derived here.

This is a mathematical regularity theorem, not a standard-physics premise. Its hypotheses are checked for the actual clock and transmitter response in the following section, rather than inferred from a frozen-delay model.

## Open full-state chart and implicit source clock

Retain the exact similarity variables from the [Cartesian formulation](alternatives-screen-2026-10-05-logarithmic-spiral-perturbation-formulation.md):

$$
t=1+T=e^\tau,\qquad q_i=t e^{\Omega\tau}U_i,\qquad
\Omega(x,y,z)=\omega(-y,x,0),\qquad V_i=U_i'.
$$

Choose a fixed history width $H>h_*=-\log\lambda$, with $H=1$ one possible choice since the admitted $\lambda>0.6$. Let $\phi=(u_i,v_i)_{i=\pm}\in C^1([-H,0],\mathbb R^{12})$. The equilibrium history is $\phi_*=(A,0;-A,0)$ in member-wise position/velocity order. Near it define each source ratio $\ell_i$ by

$$
G_i(\ell,\phi)=1-\ell-
\left|u_i(0)-\ell e^{\Omega\log\ell}u_j(\log\ell)\right|=0,
\qquad j\ne i.
$$

Choose fixed bounds $0<h_-<h_*<h_+<H$ and restrict the local root to $\ell\in(e^{-h_+},e^{-h_-})$. The chord at the base is nonzero. Evaluation of a $C^1$ function at a variable interior point is $C^1$: its differential is $\chi(s)+\phi'(s)\,ds$. Thus $G_i$ is $C^1$ in this neighborhood. Its clock derivative is

$$
\partial_\ell G_i=-1+n_i\cdot e^{\Omega\log\ell}
\big[(I+\Omega)u_j(\log\ell)+u_j'(\log\ell)\big].
$$

At the equilibrium this equals $-D=-1/\lambda<0$. It stays bounded away from zero on a sufficiently small open $C^1$ ball. The implicit-function theorem therefore supplies a $C^1$ local clock map. Its directional derivative uses only values of $\chi_u$ at $0$ and $\log\ell$, divided by this fixed nonzero clock derivative. It contains no derivative of the direction $\chi$. Consequently that derivative extends to $C^0$ directions, with joint continuity of its evaluation.

For arbitrary phase histories in this open ball, $u_j'$ and the independent component $v_j$ need not coincide. This is harmless and is not silently ignored: $u_j'$ enters only the mathematical derivative of the implicit clock, while the equation's physical source velocity is defined by

$$
W_j=e^{\Omega\log\ell_i}\big[v_j(\log\ell_i)+(I+\Omega)u_j(\log\ell_i)\big].
$$

They coincide on physical trajectories, where $u'=v$. Put $C_i=u_i(0)-\ell_i e^{\Omega\log\ell_i}u_j(\log\ell_i)$, $d_i=1-\ell_i$, $n_i=C_i/d_i$, and $D_i=1-n_i\cdot W_j$. Both $d_i$ and $D_i$ have strict positive lower bounds on a smaller ball. Define the first-order functional

$$
f_i(\phi)=\left(v_i(0),
-(I+2\Omega)v_i(0)-(\Omega+\Omega^2)u_i(0)
-\frac{C_i}{d_i^2D_i}\right).
$$

This is the actual transformed equation. Its derivative contains $u_j'$ and $v_j'$ as coefficients multiplying the clock variation, but contains no derivative of the variation direction. It therefore extends to $C^0$ directions. Each coefficient and delayed evaluation depends continuously on $\phi$ in $C^1$, and evaluating a varying continuous direction at a varying clock is jointly continuous. This verifies all three smoothness conditions of the cited theorem. It does not assert the stronger and generally false operator-norm continuity of moving-point evaluation on $C^0$.

The equilibrium belongs to $X_f$ by the admitted full Cartesian balance. Hence the local solution manifold and differentiable time maps exist. Physical compatible data satisfy $u'=v$ throughout their segment; this relation is preserved by the first component of the equation and holds throughout every generated segment after a full history width. The ambient twelve-component manifold is a convenient open-space construction, not permission to use inconsistent physical histories as witnesses. Starting with the specified $C^{2,1}$ physical histories, that regularity persists on each finite chart: bounded state derivatives and the nonzero clock derivative bound the source-clock speed; delayed position/velocity and the smooth denominator response are then Lipschitz in time, giving locally Lipschitz physical acceleration across compatible seams.

On physical segments sufficiently close to $\phi_*$,

$$
q_i'=e^{\Omega\tau}[V_i+(I+\Omega)U_i]
$$

has a common bound strictly below one, and $|U_+-U_-|$ has a positive lower bound. The complete earlier supplied history has its own strict speed margin. Therefore the full history through any trajectory that stays in this chart has no positive-delay self roots and exactly one partner root per member, by the complete causal chord argument. The local clock above is that complete root. The local chart does not impose a speed ceiling on other histories.

## Why the original preparation requires a finite entry step

The specified old held tail and its preparation patch are preserved. In particular, the original history is not replaced by a spiral on $[-H,0]$: when $H>h_*$, part of this segment can reach the nonanalytic supplied patch. Treating that entire supplied segment as the constant equilibrium would change the preparation and would invalidate an instability statement about the specified history.

Instead use the accepted complete compatible perturbation construction from the [independent perturbation assessment](alternatives-screen-2026-10-05-logarithmic-spiral-perturbation-adjudication.md#complete-compatible-history-tangent). On the retained analytic source segment its first variation is a nonzero real combination of the certified modes. Extend that variation with matching jets over a fixed compact interval before the source cut and set it to zero on the remote held tail. The endpoint acceleration mismatch is $o(\eta)$; a fixed-width endpoint patch changes it exactly without changing any nearby release source, preserving the modal tangent. This supplies actual complete $C^{2,1}$ compatible histories converging in $C^2$ to the original history. Their uniform subfield bound and separation persist for sufficiently small $\eta$.

Fix a finite entry time $\tau_e>H$, for example $\tau_e=2$ when $H=1$. On the corresponding fixed physical interval $0\le T\le e^{\tau_e}-1$, the admitted base has positive separation, positive delays, a simple clock, bounded source jets and a fixed complete speed margin. The same smoothness check applies in physical-time charts with a finite history width containing every source on this compact interval: the root is $h=|q_i(0)-q_j(-h)|$, with nonzero derivative $1-n\cdot q_j'(-h)$ on physical histories. Finitely many such regular charts and the solution-manifold theorem give existence and $C^1$ dependence through this entry interval. Equivalently, successive ordinary delay steps and the same implicit derivative give this finite-time conclusion.

At $\tau_e$ the whole similarity-history segment consists of generated future. The base is exactly $\phi_*$. In a local manifold chart centered there, the perturbed entry state has

$$
z_\eta=\eta\chi_e+o(\eta),\qquad \chi_e\ne0,
$$

where $\chi_e$ is the actual certified modal history advanced by $\tau_e$. This is the precise transfer from the original complete preparation into the autonomous equilibrium phase space. No inverse of a delay time map or reachability assumption is needed.

## Compact linear time map and an uncomputed spectral split

Let $\mathcal T(t)$ be the variational semigroup on $E=T_{\phi_*}X_f$, with its $C^1$ norm. The admitted mode lies in this tangent space and satisfies $\mathcal T(t)\chi=e^{kt}\chi$ after complexification. At equilibrium the variational equation has constant coefficients and only current and fixed delayed state evaluations. In first-order form it is

$$
\xi'=L\xi_\tau,
$$

with a bounded linear map $L:C^0([-H,0],\mathbb R^{12})\to\mathbb R^{12}$. In particular it is retarded, not neutral: no derivative of a delayed state is an input. Its physical source acceleration in the clock differential appears only as a fixed coefficient.

For fixed $a>H$, $\mathcal A=\mathcal T(a)$ is compact on $E$. Here is a direct check. Bounded $C^1$ compatible input segments give bounded $\xi$ and $\xi'$ on each fixed finite interval by the integral equation and Gronwall's inequality. Differentiating the constant-coefficient equation for positive time gives $\xi''=L(\xi')_\tau$, with a uniform bound. The segment at time $a$ lies entirely after time zero, so its values and first derivatives are bounded and equicontinuous. Arzela–Ascoli gives compactness in $C^1$, and the compatibility relation is closed. This argument concerns the linear derivative only; no unproved compactness assertion for the nonlinear map is needed.

The compact operator $\mathcal A$ has the certified multipliers $e^{ka}$ and $e^{\bar k a}$ with modulus at least $e^{\alpha_0a}>1$. Choose a radius $r_c$ strictly between $1$ and that modulus and avoiding its spectrum. Compact-operator spectral decomposition gives a finite-dimensional real invariant space $E_u$ for all eigenvalues with modulus above $r_c$, and a closed invariant complement $E_s$. This choice includes the certified modal pair and may include further uncomputed modes. It makes no claim that the scout found them all.

Choose $1<b<m$ so that

$$
r(\mathcal A_s)<b<m<\min\{|\zeta|:\zeta\in\sigma(\mathcal A_u)\}.
$$

There are equivalent norms with

$$
\|\mathcal A_s\|\le b,\qquad
\|\mathcal A_u^{-1}\|\le m^{-1}.
$$

For completeness, one can use $\|s\|_* =\sup_{n\ge0}b^{-n}\|\mathcal A_s^n s\|$ and $\|u\|_* =\sup_{n\ge0}m^n\|\mathcal A_u^{-n}u\|$. Their finiteness and equivalence follow from the strict spectral-radius inequalities. Use the maximum of these two norms on $E_u\oplus E_s$. Nonzero eigendirections are physical: the range of a linear time map after $H$ satisfies the kinematic identity, and its nonzero generalized eigenspaces lie in that range.

## Direct nonlinear cone lemma

Let $\Psi$ be a $C^1$ chart of $X_f$ at $\phi_*$, with $D\Psi(0)$ the identity on $E$. The time-$a$ map in this chart is

$$
P(z)=\mathcal A z+R(z),\qquad
\|R(z)\|\le\epsilon\|z\|
$$

on a sufficiently small ball, for any prescribed $\epsilon>0$. This is the uniform local consequence of $C^1$ differentiability. No quadratic remainder is presumed.

Write $z=(u,s)\in E_u\oplus E_s$. Choose $\epsilon$ so small that

$$
\gamma=m-\epsilon>1,\qquad b+\epsilon<m-\epsilon.
$$

If $\|s\|\le\|u\|$, then

$$
\|u_+\|\ge(m-\epsilon)\|u\|=\gamma\|u\|,\qquad
\|s_+\|\le(b+\epsilon)\|u\|\le\|u_+\|.
$$

Thus the cone is forward invariant while the iterates stay in the chart, and its unstable component grows by a fixed factor greater than one at every step. Also $\|P(z)\|\le M\|z\|$ for some finite $M>1$ there.

The actual entry family has $\chi_e\in E_u\setminus\{0\}$, so $u_\eta=\eta\chi_e+o(\eta)$ and $s_\eta=o(\eta)$. Hence it starts inside the cone for every sufficiently small positive $\eta$, with $c_1\eta\le\|u_\eta\|\le c_2\eta$. Fix a small threshold $d_0>0$ such that the ball of radius $Md_0$ is inside the chart. The first index $N_\eta$ for which $\|u_{N_\eta}\|\ge d_0$ exists and satisfies

$$
d_0\le\|u_{N_\eta}\|\le Md_0,\qquad
\frac{\log(d_0/\|u_\eta\|)}{\log M}
\le N_\eta
\le 1+\frac{\log(d_0/\|u_\eta\|)}{\log\gamma}.
$$

Between the sampled times the solution stays in a slightly larger regular chart. Indeed the extended derivative of $f$ is uniformly bounded on a smaller $C^1$ ball, yielding a local $C^0$ Lipschitz bound on that convex ball. Its integral equation bounds the state difference over $[0,a]$ by Gronwall, and the equation bounds its derivative difference. This gives $\sup_{0\le t\le a}\|F(t,\phi)-\phi_*\|_{C^1}\le C_a\|\phi-\phi_*\|_{C^1}$ for sufficiently close compatible data. Decrease $d_0$ once so that $C_aMd_0$ is inside the speed, separation and source-clock margins. Ordinary local continuation therefore persists through every step and through the departure time.

This proves actual finite-amplitude departure from the equilibrium neighborhood. The next section proves it is also departure from the full symmetry family.

## Removing the actual symmetry family

At a similarity reference time, nearby symmetry-related spirals have physical representatives

$$
q_\pm(T)=c\ \pm\ Q\big[a(1+T+b)e^{i\omega\log(1+T+b)}\big],
$$

with $c\in\mathbb R^3$, $Q\in SO(3)$ near the identity and $b$ near zero; the complex vector is embedded in the original plane. On a fixed similarity window the bracket is smooth for sufficiently small $b$. Transforming this family into $(U,V)$ histories defines a $C^1$ embedded seven-dimensional submanifold $\mathcal S\subset X_f$ near $\phi_*$. The parameter differential is injective: the three common translation tangents are independent, the three rotation tangents contain one planar and two normal relative directions, and the time-origin tangent has a planar radial component independent of axial rotation. Dilation is already represented by a combination of $b$ and axial rotation.

The tangent space of $\mathcal S$ is spanned by the accepted symmetry modes: $-1$, $-1\pm i\omega$, $0$ and $\pm i\omega$. Their time-$a$ multipliers have modulus at most one. The unstable spectral projection therefore annihilates this entire tangent space. In the chart $\Psi$, it follows that points $w=(w_u,w_s)$ of the local symmetry family satisfy

$$
\|w_u\|=o(\|w_s\|).
$$

This uses injectivity of the symmetry parameter differential in $E_s$, not any assumption that all of $E_s$ consists of symmetry directions. On a sufficiently small neighborhood arrange $\|w_u\|\le\kappa\|w_s\|$ with fixed $\kappa<1/4$.

For a cone point $z=(u,s)$, $\|s\|\le\|u\|$, suppose its distance to a local symmetry point were less than $c\|u\|$, with $c=1/4$. Then

$$
\|w_u\|>(1-c)\|u\|,\qquad
\|w_s\|<(1+c)\|u\|,
$$

contradicting $\|w_u\|\le\kappa\|w_s\|$. Hence its distance from the local symmetry family is at least $\|u\|/4$ in the adapted chart norm. Equivalence of chart and history norms turns the threshold $\|u\|\ge d_0$ into a fixed positive $\varepsilon_d$ in the theorem.

The word local is material: the statement concerns the actual symmetry orbit near the admitted history, with equivalent nearby parameter representatives identified. The group parameters can be recovered locally from the common center and relative position/velocity history, so no hidden infinitesimal symmetry is omitted. This proof does not claim a global metric separation from every remotely parameterized or singular formal spiral.

The witnessing solution is mirror-planar because that subspace is invariant under the exact pair equation and the compatible initial construction. Allowing translations, tilts, other Cartesian perturbations and all actual symmetry adjustments in the comparison family nevertheless cannot remove the departure just proved.

The departure can also be detected without including acceleration in the observation norm. At sufficiently late departure times the preceding interval of length $2H$ is generated and remains in the regular chart. For any nearby symmetry representative $w$, the same continuous-direction Lipschitz bound gives

$$
\|Z_{\tau_\eta}-w_{\tau_\eta}\|_{C^1([-H,0])}
\le(1+L)\|Z-w\|_{C^0([\tau_\eta-2H,\tau_\eta])}.
$$

Indeed the value part is immediate, and each derivative difference on the shorter interval is the difference of $f$ evaluated on a history contained in the longer interval. Taking the infimum over the same local symmetry family yields a fixed positive departure in the uniform position/velocity history norm on the doubled window. The linear relation between $(U,V)$ and $(U,e^{-\Omega\tau}q')$ is uniformly invertible. Thus this is a relative position/physical-velocity deformation over a finite similarity window, not merely a large acceleration derivative.

## Departure scale and scope

Take the physical departure time corresponding to $\tau_\eta=\tau_e+aN_\eta$. The two cone bounds give $\tau_\eta\asymp\log(1/\eta)$ and $\tau_\eta\to\infty$ as $\eta\downarrow0$. For any fixed $0<\beta<\alpha_0$, choose the spectral cut $r_c$ between $e^{\beta a}$ and $e^{\alpha_0a}$, avoiding the finite nonzero spectrum there. Then choose $m$ and the remainder tolerance so that $\gamma>e^{\beta a}$. The upper cone bound yields

$$
\tau_\eta\le C_\beta+\beta^{-1}\log(1/\eta).
$$

The physical polynomial upper bound follows from $1+T=e^\tau$. A corresponding lower polynomial bound has an unspecified positive exponent $a/\log M$. No equality with the certified mode's inverse growth rate is claimed, because additional growing modes and nonlinear transfer into them have not been excluded.

The primary departure is measured in the natural similarity phase-history norm; the doubled-window consequence also uses only relative position and physical-velocity-equivalent coordinates. It is not a claim of monotone radius deviation, a certified large displacement at one instant, collision, departure from the class of dispersing trajectories, or a speed-ceiling event. The complete root chart is retained until departure. What happens afterwards remains a distinct nonlinear continuation question.

## Validation, falsifiers and ownership

No new numerical instrument or target was needed. The prior independent Cartesian interval certificate supplies the positive spectral premise. The analytic controls here are the explicit physical root derivative, the bounded extension to continuous directions, the kinematic first-order identity, the closed compatibility manifold, the direct compactness proof, and the cone and symmetry-distance inequalities. The only imported state-dependent-delay theorem was read in its primary author source and its stated hypotheses were checked above; an unverified generic instability theorem was not substituted.

Load-bearing falsifiers are failure of the clock derivative to stay away from zero on the declared chart; a derivative term involving a derivative of the variation direction, which would invalidate the continuous-direction extension; failure of the fixed finite entry map to carry the complete compatible modal family to its asserted tangent; noncompactness of the stated linear time map; violation of the uniform $o(\|z\|)$ remainder; or an additional genuine symmetry tangent with a positive multiplier that would invalidate the transversality argument. Any such issue blocks the nonlinear theorem even though the already admitted linear mode remains valid.
