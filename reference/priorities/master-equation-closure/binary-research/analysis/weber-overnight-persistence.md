## 1. Setting and what is taken from the subject derivation

The law is the boxed Section 9 family of the [equation-variant manuscript](../../equation-variants/manuscript.md#9-weber-inspired-relative-motion-response) at the frozen coefficients $\lambda_{\mathrm W}=-1/2$, $\mu_{\mathrm W}=1$, with normalized wake speed $c_f=1$ in every numerical value, equal fixed coupling $K>0$ (numerically $K=1$), two members of opposite polarity ($\sigma=-1$), instantaneous support, no self term and unit integration weights, because architrinos carry no mass. Notation follows the subject: $\boldsymbol\rho=\mathbf X_1-\mathbf X_2$ is the relative position, $r=\|\boldsymbol\rho\|$ the present separation, $\mathbf e=\boldsymbol\rho/r$, $\mathbf w=\mathbf V_1-\mathbf V_2$ the relative velocity, $\dot r=\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf w$, $\mathbf h=\boldsymbol\rho\times\mathbf w$ the relative angular momentum with $h=\|\mathbf h\|$, $\mathbf C=(\mathbf X_1+\mathbf X_2)/2$ the centre of velocity and $\mathbf V_c=\mathbf C'$ its velocity, $k=2K$, $\kappa=2K/c_f^2$, $x=rc_f^2/K$ the dimensionless separation. The subject's results used below, all graded derived there and reconfirmed by its addendum, are:

  $$
  f=-\frac{K}{r(r+\kappa)}\left[1-\frac{\dot r^2}{2c_f^2}+\frac{h^2}{c_f^2r^2}\right],\qquad \Delta(r)=1+\frac{\kappa}{r}\ge1 .

  $$

  $$

  $$
  which equals $2(E-\|\mathbf V_c\|^2)$ for the energy function $E$ of the Lagrangian (subject 4.2). The relative motion is planar with normal $\hat{\mathbf h}=\mathbf h/h$ whenever $h>0$.
- **The circles.** Exact uniform circular histories exist at every $r_0>0$ with $h^2=kr_0$, relative speed $\sqrt{k/r_0}$, angular rate $\Omega=\sqrt{k/r_0^3}$ (subject 7.1); their linear spectrum in the full pair phase space has no growing direction (subject 7.3 as corrected by addendum item 1), and their radial oscillation frequency is $\omega_r^2=\Omega^2/(1+2/x)$ (subject eq. 7.1).

A.4).

Three different stability notions are kept apart throughout. *Lyapunov stability of a solution* means that nearby initial states produce histories that stay close to that solution at equal times. *Orbital stability* means that they stay close to the solution's orbit as a set, with no control over the phase along it. *Asymptotic stability* adds that the deviation tends to zero. The circular histories here are orbitally stable and not asymptotically stable; Section 2.4 explains why the first is also the strongest statement available.

## 2. Orbital stability of the circular family

### 2.1 The excess above the circle: an exact identity

Fix an angular momentum $h>0$. On the level set $\{\mathbf h\ \text{fixed}\}$ the first integral (1.2) is a function of $(r,\dot r)$ alone. Its minimum over that set is attained exactly at the circle $r=r_h:=h^2/k$, $\dot r=0$, with value $\varepsilon_{\min}(h)=-k^2/(2h^2)$. Define the *excess*

$$
\Lambda:=\varepsilon+\frac{k^2}{2h^2}\ \ge 0 .
\tag{2.1}
$$

The excess has an exact closed form. Expanding $V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)+k^2/(2h^2)=\tfrac12\left(h/r-k/h\right)^2$ and writing $h/r-k/h=(k/h)(r_h/r-1)$,

$$
\Lambda=\tfrac12\Delta(r)\,\dot r^2+\frac{k^2}{2h^2}\left(\frac{r_h}{r}-1\right)^2 .
\tag{2.2}
$$

Both terms are nonnegative, $\Delta\ge1$, and $\Lambda=0$ exactly when $\dot r=0$ and $r=r_h$, that is on the circular states. Equation (2.2) is an algebraic identity in the state variables; it holds at every state, not only near a circle, and it holds for the zero-coefficient control with $\Delta\equiv1$. The circular family is therefore the exact zero set of a nonnegative conserved function, which is the structure that gives orbital stability without any linearization.

The quadratic form promised in the task is the Hessian of (2.2) at the circle in the coordinates $(\delta r,\delta\dot r)$ at fixed $h$:

$$
Q_h(\delta r,\delta\dot r)=\tfrac12\Delta(r_h)\,\delta\dot r^2+\tfrac12\frac{k^4}{h^6}\,\delta r^2,\qquad \frac{k^4}{h^6}=\frac{k}{r_h^3}=\Omega^2,
\tag{2.3}
$$

which is positive definite for every $h>0$ because $\Delta(r_h)=1+\kappa/r_h>0$ and $\Omega^2>0$. The ratio of its two coefficients is $\Omega^2/\Delta(r_h)=\Omega^2/(1+2/x)$, which is the radial frequency (7.1) of the subject, so the linear result is recovered as the quadratic part of the exact identity. For the circle $x=1$ the coefficients are $\Delta=3$ and $\Omega^2=2$; for $x=4$ they are $\Delta=1.5$ and $\Omega^2=1/32$.

### 2.2 The all-time bounds that follow

Let a relative state have $h>0$ and $\varepsilon<0$, equivalently $\Lambda<k^2/(2h^2)$. Define

$$
e:=\frac hk\sqrt{2\Lambda}\in[0,1),\qquad r_p=\frac{r_h}{1+e},\qquad r_a=\frac{r_h}{1-e}.
\tag{2.4}
$$

These are the subject's turning radii (7.3) in a form that shows their dependence on the excess: $e$ is the eccentricity, and $r_{p,a}$ are the radii at which the second term of (2.2) alone exhausts $\Lambda$. From (2.2), along the entire history (Section 3 supplies that it exists for all $T\in\mathbb R$):

$$
\left|\frac{r_h}{r(T)}-1\right|\le e\quad\Longrightarrow\quad r_p\le r(T)\le r_a,\qquad
|\dot r(T)|\le\sqrt{\frac{2\Lambda}{\Delta(r(T))}}\le\sqrt{\frac{2\Lambda}{1+\kappa/r_a}}\le\sqrt{2\Lambda}=\frac{k\,e}{h}.
\tag{2.5}
$$

The plane is fixed exactly: $\boldsymbol\rho(T)\perp\mathbf h$ and $\mathbf w(T)\perp\mathbf h$ for all $T$, with $\mathbf h$ the conserved vector. The transverse relative speed is $\|\mathbf w_\perp\|=h/r\in[h/r_a,h/r_p]$.

Let $\mathcal C_{\mathbf h}$ denote the circular orbit with angular momentum vector $\mathbf h$: the set of states $(r_h\mathbf e,\ (h/r_h)\,\hat{\mathbf h}\times\mathbf e)$ with $\mathbf e$ ranging over unit vectors perpendicular to $\mathbf h$. Compare the state at time $T$ with the point of $\mathcal C_{\mathbf h}$ having the same direction $\mathbf e(T)$. The position difference is $|r-r_h|=r\,|r_h/r-1|\le r_a e$, and the velocity difference is $\big\|\dot r\,\mathbf e+(h/r-h/r_h)\,\hat{\mathbf h}\times\mathbf e\big\|\le|\dot r|+(h/r_h)|r_h/r-1|\le ke/h+(k/h)e$, using $h/r_h=k/h$. Hence, with the Euclidean distance on $(\boldsymbol\rho,\mathbf w)\in\mathbb R^6$,

$$
\operatorname{dist}\big((\boldsymbol\rho(T),\mathbf w(T)),\,\mathcal C_{\mathbf h}\big)\ \le\ e\left(\frac{r_h}{1-e}+\frac{2k}{h}\right)\qquad\text{for all }T\in\mathbb R .
\tag{2.6}
$$

The right side depends only on the two invariants of the perturbed state. It is linear in $e$, hence proportional to $\sqrt{\Lambda}$: a perturbation of size $\vartheta$ in the state produces, by (2.2), an excess $\Lambda=O(\vartheta^2)$ and so an all-time deviation $O(\vartheta)$ from the circle $\mathcal C_{\mathbf h}$, with no secular growth.

### 2.3 The theorem

**Theorem 2.1 (orbital stability of the circular family, relative phase space).** Let $\sigma=-1$ with the frozen coefficients, and let $\mathcal C_0=\mathcal C_{\mathbf h_0}$ be the circular orbit of radius $r_0=h_0^2/k$ in the plane perpendicular to $\mathbf h_0\ne\mathbf 0$. Let a relative initial state $(\boldsymbol\rho,\mathbf w)$ have invariants $\mathbf h=\mathbf h_0+\delta\mathbf h$ and $\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{\min}(h_0)+\delta\varepsilon$ with $h=\|\mathbf h\|>0$, and define its excess relative to its own angular momentum

$$
\Lambda=\delta\varepsilon-\frac{k^2}{2}\left(\frac1{h_0^2}-\frac1{h^2}\right)=\delta\varepsilon-\frac{k^2\,\big(2\mathbf h_0\cdot\delta\mathbf h+\|\delta\mathbf h\|^2\big)}{2h^2h_0^2}\ \ge0 .
\tag{2.7}
$$

If $\Lambda<k^2/(2h^2)$, equivalently $\varepsilon<0$, then the history through that state exists for all $T\in\mathbb R$ and satisfies, with $e=(h/k)\sqrt{2\Lambda}$ and $r_h=h^2/k$:

1. $r_h/(1+e)\le r(T)\le r_h/(1-e)$ and $|\dot r(T)|\le ke/h$ for all $T$;
2. the plane of relative motion is fixed with normal $\hat{\mathbf h}$, tilted from $\hat{\mathbf h}_0$ by the angle $\phi$ with $\sin\phi=\|\hat{\mathbf h}_0\times\delta\mathbf h\|/h\le\|\delta\mathbf h\|/h$;
3. the distance from the state to the circular orbit $\mathcal C_{\mathbf h}$ obeys (2.6) for all $T$, and $\mathcal C_{\mathbf h}$ lies within $|r_h-r_0|+r_h\phi+|k/h-k/h_0|+(k/h_0)\phi$ of $\mathcal C_0$ in the same distance (a rotation by $\phi$ moves a vector of length $\ell$ by $2\ell\sin(\phi/2)\le\ell\phi$), where $|r_h-r_0|=|h^2-h_0^2|/k$.

Consequently the family of circular orbits is orbitally stable: for every $\eta>0$ there is $\vartheta>0$ such that every relative state within $\vartheta$ of $\mathcal C_0$ generates a history that stays within $\eta$ of $\mathcal C_0$ for all time. The quantities $\varepsilon$, $\mathbf h$ and $\Lambda$ are explicit algebraic functions of the initial state, so $\vartheta$ can be read off from (2.6) and item 3 for any given $\eta$.

*Proof.* Items 1 and 2 are (2.5) and the constancy of $\mathbf h$, once global existence is known; Theorem 3.1 below gives existence on all of $\mathbb R$ for every state with $h>0$, $\varepsilon<0$, and the invariants are constant on the whole history. Item 3 is (2.6) together with the triangle inequality applied to corresponding points of $\mathcal C_{\mathbf h}$ and $\mathcal C_0$ (same azimuth measured from the line of nodes). For the consequence: $\mathbf h$ and $\varepsilon$ are continuous functions of the state on $\{r>0\}$, $\Lambda$ is continuous and vanishes on $\mathcal C_0$, and $\varepsilon<0$ on $\mathcal C_0$; hence for states close enough to $\mathcal C_0$ we have $\varepsilon<0$, $\|\delta\mathbf h\|$ small and $e$ small, and the right sides of (2.6) and item 3 are below any prescribed $\eta$. $\square$

The estimate is sharp at the level of the turning radii: the bounds in item 1 are attained (at pericentre and apocentre), so no better all-time confinement of $r$ exists; the measured turning radii of Section 6 sit on these bounds to the integration error.

**Theorem 2.2 (full pair phase space).** In the twelve-dimensional space of $(\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2,\mathbf V_1,\mathbf V_2)$ the motion is $\mathbf X_{1,2}(T)=\mathbf C_0+\mathbf V_cT\pm\tfrac12\boldsymbol\rho(T)$, $\mathbf V_{1,2}(T)=\mathbf V_c\pm\tfrac12\mathbf w(T)$, with $\mathbf V_c$ exactly constant and $(\boldsymbol\rho,\mathbf w)$ as in Theorem 2.1. Hence:

1. *Relative stability.* In the coordinates $(\boldsymbol\rho,\mathbf w,\mathbf V_c)$ every circular history (with any centre velocity) is orbitally stable: a perturbation $(\delta\boldsymbol\rho,\delta\mathbf w,\delta\mathbf V_c)$ produces for all time the deviations of Theorem 2.1 in the relative part and the constant deviation $\delta\mathbf V_c$ in the centre velocity.
2. *Uniform drift.* The absolute positions are not Lyapunov or orbitally stable: a perturbation $\delta\mathbf V_c$ of the centre velocity displaces both members by $\delta\mathbf V_c\,T$, which is unbounded. This is the only unbounded direction, and it grows exactly linearly, $\|\delta\mathbf C(T)\|\le\|\delta\mathbf C_0\|+\|\delta\mathbf V_c\|\,|T|$; it corresponds to the size-two Jordan blocks of the centre in the subject's spectrum (addendum item 1).
3. *Speed labels.* The supremum of individual speed is $\sup_T\big[\|\mathbf V_c\|^2+\tfrac14\|\mathbf w\|^2+|\mathbf V_c\cdot\mathbf w|\big]^{1/2}$ by (A.4), bounded above by $\|\mathbf V_c\|+h/(2r_p)$; a perturbed circle keeps the strict label if this is below $c_f$. A circle *on* the equality boundary ($x=1/2$, individual speed $c_f$ with centre at rest) does not: every perturbation with $e>0$ has pericentre speed $h/(2r_p)>h/(2r_h)$, which exceeds $c_f$, so the inclusive label is not preserved under perturbation at the boundary although the dynamics is unchanged.

*Proof.* The decomposition is the content of subject 4.1 (sum of velocities conserved, accelerations equal and opposite). Items 1 and 2 follow from it and Theorem 2.1. Item 3 is (A.4) and (7.6); for the boundary circle, $h/(2r_h)=\tfrac12\sqrt{k/r_h}$ equals $c_f$ at $x=1/2$ and $r_p<r_h$ when $e>0$. $\square$

### 2.4 What the theorem does and does not establish

It establishes orbital stability of the circular family in the sense above, with an explicit, attained all-time bound. It does *not* establish Lyapunov stability of an individual circular solution: a perturbed history with a slightly different radius has a slightly different angular rate (and a slightly different radial period), so its phase separates from the unperturbed circle linearly in time; the phase is neutral. It does *not* establish asymptotic stability, and none exists: $\Lambda$ is conserved, so a perturbed history never approaches the circle; the family is a continuum of neutrally stable states, which is the only possibility for a system with these invariants (Section 4). The centre of velocity is neutral and its position drifts uniformly. The theorem concerns an adapted instantaneous law; it carries no claim about the delayed canonical Master Equation, where the invariants (1.2) are not available, and no claim that the pair is "physically bound" in any sense beyond the mathematical confinement of $r$.

## 3. Global existence and regularity of the bound class

This section consolidates Theorem 7.4 of the subject into one self-contained statement about the full twelve-dimensional system. It cites the subject for the algebraic facts (1.1)–(1.2) and supplies the continuation argument in full, which the subject's proof carried out on the reduced radial equation and then transferred by quadrature. Two points are filled in: that the Cartesian solution produced by the Picard theorem is the one the reduced analysis describes (uniqueness), and that it extends to all time because it is confined to a compact subset of the domain where the vector field is locally Lipschitz.

**Theorem 3.1 (global regular existence on the bound class).** Let $\sigma=-1$ with the frozen coefficients, and let the initial state $(\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2,\mathbf V_1,\mathbf V_2)$ have $r>0$, $h>0$ and $\varepsilon<0$. Then:

1. there is exactly one solution of the pair law, and it is defined for all $T\in\mathbb R$;
2. along it the acceleration matrix is invertible with determinant $\Delta(r)\in[1+\kappa/r_a,\ 1+\kappa/r_p]\subset(1,\infty)$;
3. $r(T)\in[r_p,r_a]$ with $r_p=r_h/(1+e)>0$, where $e<1$ by (2.4);
4. $\|\mathbf w(T)\|^2=\dot r^2+h^2/r^2\le2\Lambda+h^2/r_p^2$ and, sharper, $\sup_T\|\mathbf w\|=h/r_p$ attained at pericentre; the individual speeds satisfy $\|\mathbf V_i(T)\|\le\|\mathbf V_c\|+h/(2r_p)$ and, with centre at rest, $\sup_T\|\mathbf V_i\|=h/(2r_p)=\tfrac12\big(k/h+\sqrt{k^2/h^2+2\varepsilon}\big)$;
5. the accelerations are bounded, $|f|\le\dfrac{K}{r_p(r_p+\kappa)}\Big[1+\dfrac{\Lambda}{c_f^2}+\dfrac{h^2}{c_f^2r_p^2}\Big]$;
6. $r(T)$ is periodic with the radial period $T_r$ of subject eq. (7.4) if $e>0$, and constant if $e=0$; the relative motion is quasi-periodic in the fixed plane perpendicular to $\mathbf h$ with apsidal angle $\Phi$ of subject eq. (7.5); the centre moves uniformly.

*Proof.* *Local existence and uniqueness.* On the open set $U=\{(\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2,\mathbf V_1,\mathbf V_2):\mathbf X_1\ne\mathbf X_2\}$ the right side of the first-order system, $(\mathbf V_1,\mathbf V_2,f\mathbf e,-f\mathbf e)$ with $f$ from (1.1), is a rational function of $(\boldsymbol\rho,\mathbf w)$ whose denominators $r^2$ and $r(r+\kappa)$ do not vanish, composed with the smooth maps $\boldsymbol\rho\mapsto r$ and $\mathbf e$ on $\boldsymbol\rho\ne\mathbf 0$. It is therefore $C^\infty$, hence locally Lipschitz, on $U$. By the Picard–Lindelöf theorem there is a unique solution on a maximal open interval $(T_-,T_+)\ni0$, and it is $C^\infty$. Since (1.1) is the unique solution of the six-by-six acceleration system when $\Delta\ne0$ (subject 3.2), a solution of the first-order system is a solution of the law and conversely, so uniqueness for the law follows.

*Conservation.* On $(T_-,T_+)$, $\mathbf h'=\mathbf w\times\mathbf w+\boldsymbol\rho\times2f\mathbf e=\mathbf 0$ and $\mathbf V_c'=\tfrac12(f\mathbf e-f\mathbf e)=\mathbf 0$; $\varepsilon'=0$ is the subject's Check D and its eq. (5.4), obtained there from the energy function of the Lagrangian or directly from the reduced equation. These hold on the whole maximal interval because they are identities of the smooth solution.

*A priori bounds.* With $\mathbf h$ and $\varepsilon$ constant, the identity (2.2) holds at every time, so items 2 and 3 follow from (2.5) and $\Delta$ decreasing in $r$. For item 4, $\|\mathbf w\|^2=\dot r^2+\|\mathbf w_\perp\|^2=\dot r^2+h^2/r^2$, and $\dot r^2\le2\Lambda$ by (2.5); the sharper pericentre supremum is subject eq. (7.6) with the monotonicity argument completed in addendum item 6; the individual speeds follow from $\mathbf V_{1,2}=\mathbf V_c\pm\mathbf w/2$. Item 5 follows from (1.1) by the triangle inequality on the bracket, $|1-\dot r^2/(2c_f^2)+h^2/(c_f^2r^2)|\le1+\Lambda/c_f^2+h^2/(c_f^2r_p^2)$, using $\dot r^2/2\le\Lambda$ from (2.5) and $r\ge r_p$ in the prefactor; no sign of the bracket is needed, and none is claimed.

*Continuation to all time.* Suppose $T_+<\infty$. On $[0,T_+)$ the relative state $(\boldsymbol\rho,\mathbf w)$ remains in the compact set $Q=\{r_p\le\|\boldsymbol\rho\|\le r_a,\ \|\mathbf w\|\le W\}$, $W^2=2\Lambda+h^2/r_p^2$, which is contained in the open set $\{\boldsymbol\rho\ne\mathbf 0\}\times\mathbb R^3$, and the centre $\mathbf C(T)=\mathbf C_0+\mathbf V_cT$ remains in a bounded set because $T_+$ is finite. Hence $(\mathbf X_1,\mathbf X_2,\mathbf V_1,\mathbf V_2)(T)=(\mathbf C\pm\boldsymbol\rho/2,\ \mathbf V_c\pm\mathbf w/2)$ remains in a compact subset of $U$ on $[0,T_+)$. The standard continuation theorem for locally Lipschitz vector fields (a maximal solution leaves every compact subset of the domain as $T\to T_+$ when $T_+<\infty$) gives a contradiction. So $T_+=\infty$, and symmetrically $T_-=-\infty$. This is item 1.

*Periodicity and quasi-periodicity.* The pair $(r,\dot r)$ moves on the level curve $\{\tfrac12\Delta(r)\dot r^2+V_{\mathrm{eff}}(r)=\varepsilon\}$, which by (2.2) is the closed curve $\{\tfrac12\Delta\dot r^2+(k^2/2h^2)(r_h/r-1)^2=\Lambda\}$: compact (contained in $[r_p,r_a]\times[-\sqrt{2\Lambda},\sqrt{2\Lambda}]$), and a smooth simple closed curve when $\Lambda>0$ because $\Lambda$ has the nondegenerate minimum (2.3) at $(r_h,0)$ and no other critical point ($\partial_{\dot r}=\Delta\dot r$ vanishes only at $\dot r=0$, where $\partial_r=-(k^2/h^2)(r_h/r-1)r_h/r^2$ vanishes only at $r=r_h$). The reduced vector field $(\dot r,\ \ddot r)$ with $\ddot r$ from subject eq. (5.2) has no zero on that curve (its only equilibrium is the circle, where $\Lambda=0$), so the motion along the closed curve is periodic; its period is the quadrature (7.4). The azimuth advances by $\dot\theta=h/r^2>0$, by $2\Phi$ per radial period, with $\Phi$ from (7.5), and the plane is fixed. The centre moves uniformly by $\mathbf V_c'=\mathbf 0$. $\square$

What Theorem 3.1 adds to Theorem 7.4 is the explicit continuation argument on the twelve-dimensional system, the explicit compact set $Q$, the bounded-acceleration estimate (item 5) and the identification of the reduced level curve as the zero set of the excess. No defect was found in the statement of Theorem 7.4; the one gap found in its proof (the dispersal class) was already repaired in the addendum and does not concern the bound class.

> Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: a regular opposite-polarity history with $h>0$, $\varepsilon<0$ whose separation leaves $[r_p,r_a]$, whose acceleration matrix determinant falls below $1+\kappa/r_a$, whose relative speed exceeds $h/r_p$, or which ceases to exist at a finite time, produced by a separately written integrator; or an explicit state in the bound class at which (1.1) is not locally Lipschitz.

## 4. Integrability, and why no KAM-type argument is needed

The question that a long-horizon persistence study usually raises is whether small nonlinear effects accumulate over many periods: whether the quasi-periodic motion survives for all time or slowly drifts, which is the subject of the Kolmogorov–Arnold–Moser (KAM) theory for *near*-integrable systems. Here that question does not arise, for a structural reason.

The frozen law is the Euler–Lagrange system of $L=\sum_i\tfrac12\|\mathbf V_i\|^2-\Phi(r,\dot r)$ with $\Phi=(\sigma K/r)(1+\dot r^2/(2c_f^2))$ (subject 4.2). For $\sigma=-1$ the velocity Hessian $\partial^2L/\partial\mathbf V\partial\mathbf V$ is the acceleration matrix $M=I_6-\alpha\mathbf u\mathbf u^{\mathsf T}$ with $\alpha<0$, so $M\succeq I_6$ (subject 4.3, criterion 1): the Legendre transform is regular everywhere on $r>0$ and the system has an equivalent Hamiltonian form on the twelve-dimensional phase space with canonical momenta $\mathbf p_i=\mathbf V_i\mp\Phi_{\dot r}\mathbf e$. The canonical relative momentum is $\mathbf p_{\mathrm{rel}}=\tfrac12(\mathbf p_1-\mathbf p_2)=\tfrac12\mathbf w-\Phi_{\dot r}\mathbf e$, and because the correction is along $\boldsymbol\rho$, the canonical relative angular momentum is $\boldsymbol\rho\times\mathbf p_{\mathrm{rel}}=\tfrac12\mathbf h$. Its three components satisfy the standard rotation brackets, so $\|\mathbf h\|^2$ and $h_z$ are in involution, both commute with the Hamiltonian by rotation invariance, and the three components of the total momentum $\sum_i\mathbf p_i=\sum_i\mathbf V_i$ commute with everything else by translation invariance. The six functions $(P_x,P_y,P_z,\ H,\ \|\mathbf h\|^2,\ h_z)$ are thus pairwise in involution and independent on the open dense set $h>0$, $e>0$; by the Liouville–Arnold theorem the system is completely integrable. After quotienting the free centre motion, the relative system has three degrees of freedom and three commuting integrals $(\varepsilon,\|\mathbf h\|^2,h_z)$; on the bound class its level sets are compact (Theorem 3.1) and are three-dimensional tori on which the flow is linear, with frequencies $2\pi/T_r$ (radial), $2\Phi/T_r$ (azimuthal) and $0$ (the azimuth of $\mathbf h$ about the $z$ axis, which does not move). Each individual history fills the two-dimensional sub-torus in its own plane densely when $\Phi/\pi$ is irrational, and closes when it is rational.

Three consequences matter for persistence. First, the radial period $T_r$ and the apsidal angle $\Phi$ are functions of $(h,\varepsilon)$ alone, by the quadratures (7.4)–(7.5); a secular drift of either along a history is impossible, so the preregistered prediction for their trend is exactly zero, and any measured trend is integration error. Second, there is no small parameter and no perturbation of an integrable system: the exact flow *is* integrable, so the all-time confinement of Theorem 2.1 holds for every history of the class, not merely for a Cantor set of tori of large measure, and no nondegeneracy (twist) condition is needed. Third, asymptotic stability is excluded: the flow preserves the Liouville measure and its three integrals, so no open set of states can be attracted to the circular family. A KAM-type question would arise only for a non-integrable modification: a third member (the many-member matrix $M_N$ then has like-polarity pairs and no second angular-momentum-type integral beyond the total), a delayed variant of the law (an infinite-dimensional hereditary system in which $\mathbf h$ and $\varepsilon$ are no longer invariants), or coefficients off the line $\lambda_{\mathrm W}=-\mu_{\mathrm W}/2$ for three or more members. For the isolated pair with any coefficients the reduced radial motion still has the first integral (4.9) of the subject, so even off that line the pair remains integrable in the reduced sense; that case is not analysed here.

> Claim grade: derived (Liouville integrability and its consequences) from the subject's Lagrangian structure and the invariants; the measurable consequence is the zero trend tested in Section 6. Falsifier: a bound history of the frozen pair whose measured radial period or apsidal angle changes with time by more than the integration-error estimate of the instrument that measured it.

## 7. The contact-step obligation

The target runs found that at `rtol` $10^{-10}$ one accepted step carried WB-6 and WC-1 from $r=4.6\times10^{-5}$ on one side of $r=0$ to $r=3.3\times10^{-6}$ on the other without raising the contact event, because the event function $r-10^{-6}$ was positive at both step ends (target-run report, Section 4, item 1). The instrument was not modified in this round. The obligation below states, as a requirement on any future version, the step restriction that makes this impossible for a law whose contact speed is finite, and then verifies it on WB-6 by imposing the restriction by hand with the unchanged instrument.

**Obligation (step bound near contact).** Let the pair approach contact with $|\dot r|\le W$ on the approach, where $W$ is finite (for the frozen opposite-polarity law $W=\sqrt2\,c_f$ on every radial level $\varepsilon\le c_f^2$, by addendum item 3; for a bound history with $h>0$ contact does not occur). Let $\theta\in(0,1)$. If every accepted step starting at separation $r_n$ has length

$$
h_n\le\theta\,\frac{r_n}{W}\qquad\text{and the step's position error is below }(1-\theta)r_n ,
\tag{7.1}
$$

then the separation at the step end satisfies $r_{n+1}\ge r_n-Wh_n-\text{(error)}\ge(1-\theta)r_n-\text{(error)}>0$. No accepted step can end on the far side of $r=0$, so the first step whose end has $r_{n+1}<r_{\mathrm{contact}}$ brackets a sign change of the event function and the contact event is located. The bound is sufficient, not necessary; it costs about $\log_{1/(1-\theta)}(r_0/r_{\mathrm{contact}})$ additional steps (about $21$ for $\theta=1/2$ from $x=2.5$ to $10^{-6}$), independent of the tolerance. A law with divergent contact speed, such as the zero-coefficient control, is caught without this bound because the error controller shrinks the step as $|\dot r|\to\infty$; the finite contact speed of the frozen law is exactly what removes that protection, and the obligation restores it. The bound in (7.1) uses the derived supremum $W$ of the law under study; a version that uses the current $|\dot r_n|$ in place of $W$ needs the additional condition that $|\dot r|$ cannot grow by more than a fixed factor within a step, which holds here because $\ddot r$ stays finite on the approach (the target runs measured $-0.75$ at the contact radius from rest at $x_0=4$ and $-0.90$ from rest at $x_0=2.5$).

**Verification on WB-6 (measured).** The instrument's CLI accepts one fixed `hmax` per run, so the schedule was imposed through its module export `runCase`, unchanged, by restarting the same specification from the located state: stage $s$ starts at separation $r_s$ with `hmax` $=\theta r_s/\sqrt2$, $\theta=1/2$, and stops when $r=r_s/2$ (the contact event with threshold $r_s/2$ used as a measuring stop, as the target runs used an escape radius as a measuring stop), the final stage stopping at $r=10^{-6}$; all at `rtol` $10^{-10}$, `atol` $10^{-14}$. Script [contact-schedule.mjs](../evidence/weber-overnight-promoted/round2/persistence/contact-schedule.mjs), output [contact-schedule.out.json](../evidence/weber-overnight-promoted/round2/persistence/contact-schedule.out.json). ## 8. What remains open, claims and falsifiers
