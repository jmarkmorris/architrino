## Both sources and their own sources are generated

Let the complete past have a uniform speed bound $b_0<1$, while the generated interval $[0,t]$ has member speed at most $\beta\le1/20$. The complete strict bound gives the ordinary root census. It does not have to equal the smaller generated bound.

Put $m=1-\beta$ and assume $t>3d(t)$. For each actual receiver and partner, the delay gap at $R=d/(1+\beta)$ is nonnegative and at $R=d/m$ is nonpositive, by the speed bound along the generated source interval. Both candidate delays are less than $t$. The unique complete-history partner root therefore has

$$
\frac{d(t)}{1+\beta}\le R\le\frac{d(t)}m,\qquad
s=t-R\ge t-\frac{d(t)}m>0.
\tag{1}
$$

The same bounds hold for the center homotopy $X_\pm^\lambda=\lambda C\pm x$, $-1\le\lambda\le1$: its velocities are convex combinations of the original member velocity and the negative of the other member velocity, and its same-time separation is unchanged. Thus both independently moving clocks remain covered.

For every $s\in[t-d(t)/m,t]$, the generated relative speed bound gives

$$
d(s)\le d(t)+2\beta(t-s)
\le\frac{1+\beta}{m}d(t).
\tag{2}
$$

Its own candidate partner delay is at most $d(s)/m$. Consequently

$$
s-\frac{d(s)}m
\ge t-\frac{d(t)}m-\frac{1+\beta}{m^2}d(t)
=t-\frac{2d(t)}{m^2}>0,
\tag{3}
$$

because $2/m^2\le800/361<3$. The same gap-bracketing argument therefore places each sampled source's own partner source in the generated interval. This justifies the generated acceleration bounds used on the entire sampled window, including the center homotopy. No remote speed bound has been substituted into a small-speed estimate.

The property persists: $(t-3d(t))'\ge1-6\beta\ge7/10$. The full generated midpoint supremum $M(t)$ covers all the displayed source times. Only the current-affine normal component in equation (15) of the frozen theorem factors through the current normal midpoint component; the actual delayed error remains unfactored.

## Explicit physical time

Fix $d(T)<D\le D_*$, and let $T_D$ be the first future attainment of $d=D$. The accepted finite-radius result preserves its strict speed, angular and source-window bounds up to this event.

During the negative-account stage before $T_D$,

$$
\mathcal J'>\frac{3K^2}{2d^3}\ge\frac{3K^2}{2D^3},
\qquad \mathcal J(T)\ge-\frac{21}{10}\epsilon^2.
\tag{4}
$$

Thus either $D$ is attained or the account first reaches zero within $7\epsilon^2D^3/(5K^2)$ units of physical time. Once the account is nonnegative it remains so. The inherited positive-account virial quantity $I=du=Z\cdot U$ satisfies

$$
I'\ge\frac{9K}{5d}\ge\frac{9K}{5D},
\qquad |I|\le d|U|<\frac D{10},
\tag{5}
$$

because each member speed is less than $1/20$. Its entire available range before attainment is less than $D/5$. Therefore the positive stage lasts less than $D^2/(9K)$ before $D$ is attained. Combining both stages gives the conservative bound

$$
T_D<T+\frac{7\epsilon^2D^3}{5K^2}+\frac{D^2}{9K}.
\tag{6}
$$

This is an analytical upper bound in physical wake-speed-normalized time, not a practical numerical estimate or an assertion about the later trajectory. With symbolic $R_0$, $K=4\epsilon^2R_0$ and all terms in (6) have units of time when $c_f=1$.

The argument is falsified by failure of the complete strict root census, a source outside (1)–(3), failure of the preserved generated speed ceiling, or failure of the inherited account/virial inequalities (4)–(5).
