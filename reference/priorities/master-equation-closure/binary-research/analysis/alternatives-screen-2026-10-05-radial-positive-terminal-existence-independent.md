# Positive terminal speeds accumulate in the original three-halves family

## Fixed theorem and new proof obligation

Thus every sufficiently small interval $(0,\epsilon_1)$ contains a member whose actual global delayed future has a strictly positive outward terminal velocity magnitude.

The admitted zero-terminal-speed accumulation theorem remains unchanged. Together, the two statements establish occurrence of both terminal alternatives arbitrarily far down the same original one-parameter slow family. The positive set is open by the existing positive terminal strip. No particular positive parameter, threshold, ordering, discreteness of the zero set, genericity, density or new law is asserted.

The new step is a short weighted-time estimate conditional on zero terminal speed. Subtracting the corrected action's value at zero-speed infinity produces a quantity that is strictly increasing and tends to zero at every zero-speed endpoint. This forces all nearby zero-speed members to finish quickly in the weighted clock after a sufficiently late finite reception. Their terminal winding integer is consequently locally constant relative to the zero set. If every sufficiently small member were zero-speed, that integer would be constant on a connected interval, contrary to its admitted divergence at zero.

This proof does not infer positive existence from an open set that might be empty. It rules out that empty-set alternative. It uses no numerical trajectory, generic perturbation premise, physical conserved energy, new preparation or continuation through the infinite-radius coordinate boundary.

## A corrected action measured from zero-speed infinity

For the central comparison only, define

$$
W(z)=\tfrac12z^4-2z,\qquad
e=\tfrac12y^2+W(z),\qquad e_{\min}=-\tfrac32,
\qquad F_0=(-y/2,z^3-1).
$$

The field is smooth on the full plane. Each scalar level above the unique minimum is a regular compact oval; at $(z,y)=(0,0)$ the zero level is regular because $F_0=(0,-1)$. This auxiliary extension is not a physical continuation to negative radius.

Let

$$
I(e)=\oint y^2\,d\chi,\qquad Q(e)=I'(e)=\oint d\chi.
$$

Both are positive and smooth on the compact annulus. Along the actual path,

$$
e_\chi=\delta F(z,y)+O(\delta^2),\qquad
F=-\tfrac16y^2+\tfrac43(z^4-z).
$$

Integrating $(zy)_\chi$ around a central oval gives

$$
\oint(z^4-z)\,d\chi=\tfrac12 I(e),\qquad
\oint F\,d\chi=\tfrac12 I(e).
$$

Thus a bounded $C^1$ orbit primitive $B(z,y)$ exists on the full annulus and satisfies

$$
\nabla B\cdot F_0=Q(e)F-\tfrac12I(e).
$$

It is obtained by integrating the zero-mean right side around each oval from a smooth transverse section. The period and section parametrization are smooth, so the primitive and its first derivatives are bounded, including at $(0,0)$. Its arbitrary additive normalization is fixed once and used for every parameter.

Write $B_0=B(0,0)$ and introduce the new scalar

$$
\mathcal A(z,y,\delta)
=I(e)-I(0)-\delta[B(z,y)-B_0].
$$

This is the original corrected action minus its value on the zero-speed boundary at the current $\delta$. Direct differentiation along the actual delayed path gives

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal A_\chi
&=Q(e)[\delta F+O(\delta^2)]
-\delta\nabla B\cdot[F_0+O(\delta)]
-\delta_\chi(B-B_0)\\
&=\tfrac12\delta I(e)+O(\delta^2).
\end{aligned}
$$

All error constants are uniform on the inherited annulus. The positive minimum of $I$ therefore supplies constants $c_A,C_\delta>0$ and a sufficiently small common launch threshold such that

$$
\mathcal A_\chi\ge c_A\delta,\qquad
\delta_\chi\ge-C_\delta\delta^2
$$

throughout every post-transition future. The new subtraction changes the derivative only at order $\delta^2$; no leading drift is discarded.

For a zero-speed member, $z,y\to0$ and $\delta\to\delta_\infty>0$, so

$$
\mathcal A\to0.
$$

Strict increase then implies $\mathcal A(\chi)<0$ at every earlier post-transition reception. This terminal normalization is exact even though the physical histories and $\delta_\infty$ differ from member to member.

## The zero-speed endpoint has a uniform local finishing-time bound

Take any post-transition reception on a zero-speed member and denote its state by $\mathcal A_0<0$, $\delta_0>0$. The lower differential bound on $\delta$ gives, for elapsed weighted time $t\ge0$ before the endpoint,

$$
\delta(t)\ge\frac{\delta_0}{1+C_\delta\delta_0t}.
$$

Let $L$ be the remaining weighted duration. Integrating the positive action derivative to the zero terminal value yields

$$
-\mathcal A_0
\ge\frac{c_A}{C_\delta}
\log(1+C_\delta\delta_0L).
$$

Consequently

$$
L\le
\frac{\exp[C_\delta(-\mathcal A_0)/c_A]-1}
{C_\delta\delta_0}.
$$

This is a conditional upper bound for zero-speed members, not a continuity claim about terminal speed. As a fixed zero-speed member approaches its endpoint, $\mathcal A_0\to0$ and $\delta_0\to\delta_\infty>0$, so the upper bound tends to zero.

It is also uniform for nearby zero-speed parameters observed at the same sufficiently late finite physical reception. Finite-history parameter continuity makes their $\mathcal A_0$ arbitrarily close to the base value and keeps their $\delta_0$ bounded below. If a nearby parameter has $\mathcal A_0\ge0$, strict increase already excludes zero terminal speed, so it causes no exception to the conditional estimate.

## Relative local constancy of the winding integer on the zero set

Let $\kappa=\sqrt{3/2}$ and retain the smooth corrected minimum $x_\delta=1+O(\delta^2)$ from the finite-transition proof. The existing nonvanishing coordinate is

$$
\mathcal W=
\kappa(1-x_\delta z^2)
i\left[z^2y-\tfrac43\delta z^3\right].
$$

It is nonzero at every finite generated reception and tends to $\kappa>0$ at every terminal alternative. Its release argument has one continuous branch near $-\pi/2$. The lifted terminal argument is

$$
\psi_\infty(\epsilon)=2\pi N(\epsilon),\qquad N(\epsilon)\in\mathbb Z.
$$

The admitted phase theorem gives $N(\epsilon)\to-\infty$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$. More quantitatively, the last transition band alone produces a negative phase advance of order at least $\epsilon^{-7/3}$, and the phase keeps decreasing afterwards.

Fix now one zero-speed parameter $\epsilon_0>0$. Choose a small constant $\zeta>0$ such that

$$
z<\zeta\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\operatorname{Re}\mathcal W>\kappa/2
$$

for every admitted small $\delta$. The global rows give a fixed bound $|z_\chi|\le M_z$ on the common box. Choose a finite physical time $T_0$ sufficiently late on this member that $z(T_0)<\zeta/4$, $\delta(T_0)>\delta_\infty/2$, and the preceding finishing-time bound is smaller than $\zeta/(4M_z)$.

The first fixed-amplitude event is transverse, since the admitted transition has strictly positive amplitude derivative there. Thus nearby parameters have already crossed it by this sufficiently late $T_0$. Complete-source finite-time continuity then gives, in a parameter neighborhood of $\epsilon_0$, $z(T_0)<\zeta/2$, a positive lower bound on $\delta(T_0)$, and sufficiently small $|\mathcal A(T_0)|$ that every zero-speed member in that neighborhood has remaining weighted duration below $\zeta/(2M_z)$.

Every such zero-speed member therefore stays within $z<\zeta$ for its whole remaining future. Its $\mathcal W$ remains in the right half-plane and adds no whole winding turn.

For the finite prefix up to $T_0$, the lifted phase depends continuously on parameter: the base prefix is compact with a positive minimum modulus of $\mathcal W$, and the histories and ordinary solution depend continuously on parameter on the compact accessible source interval. In the right half-plane let $\arg_R$ denote the continuous argument in $(-\pi/2,\pi/2)$. The integer

$$
M_{\mathrm{prefix}}(\epsilon)=
\frac{\psi(T_0,\epsilon)-\arg_R\mathcal W(T_0,\epsilon)}{2\pi}
$$

is continuous and hence constant on a sufficiently small connected neighborhood. For every zero-speed member there, the terminal strip gives

$$
N(\epsilon)=M_{\mathrm{prefix}}(\epsilon).
$$

Therefore $N$ is locally constant relative to the zero-speed set at each of its members. This does not assert local constancy on neighboring positive-speed members, which may leave the small strip and make additional turns. It also does not assume continuity of the infinite-time endpoint as input; the short finishing-time estimate is the new reason the zero-speed endpoint is controlled.

## Positive-speed existence and accumulation

Suppose some sufficiently small interval $(0,\epsilon_1)$ contained no positive-speed parameter. The admitted nonnegative-terminal theorem would make every member of that interval zero-speed. Relative local constancy on the zero set would then be ordinary local constancy of $N$ at every point of the connected interval. Hence $N$ would be constant there.

This contradicts the proved divergence $N(\epsilon)\to-\infty$ at zero. Thus every such interval contains a positive-speed member. Selecting each next positive parameter below half the preceding one gives a positive-speed sequence decreasing to zero.

The existing positive terminal strip makes the positive-speed set open, so it contains open parameter intervals arbitrarily close to zero. The separately admitted zero-speed accumulation remains compatible with this result. This proof does not show that the zero set has empty interior away from zero, that its points are isolated, or that adjacent positive intervals have an ordered speed or winding label.

For every positive member supplied by the theorem, the admitted global reconstruction already gives linear late radius growth, a finite total polar angle, and a nonzero outward radial terminal velocity. The new argument establishes occurrence of that alternative; it changes none of those memberwise asymptotic statements and locates no numerical parameter.

## Falsifiers, limitations and source identities

The new proof fails if the corrected action or its gradient is unbounded at the regular comparison point $(0,0)$; if the terminal subtraction contributes at order $\delta$ rather than $\delta^2$; if the post-transition annulus does not cover the whole final path; if a zero-speed endpoint does not give exactly $\mathcal A_\infty=0$; or if a zero-speed member violates the integrated finishing-time inequality. A nearby zero-speed member completing an additional winding after the chosen finite reception while satisfying that time bound and $|z_\chi|\le M_z$ would falsify the local constancy step. The complete-source continuity and inherited divergent phase remain separate load-bearing premises.

No target computation, executable checker, Python process, physical conservation import or genericity premise was used. The analytical validation route is to reconstruct the terminal-subtracted derivative and the scalar time integral independently, then check the finite-prefix and terminal-strip winding match. This separates the genuinely new argument from the already admitted all-future and phase theorems.
