# Blind reference for collapse of a complete preceding causal arc

**Derived before the causal-collapse subject is opened.** Retain the existing all-future strict-subfield logarithmic mirror family, its positive angular motion, acute lag and complete prescribed past. Set $E=|v|^2$, $p=r'$, $w=t-s_0$, and let $R(t)=t-s(t)$ be the actual partner delay. No uniform total-speed margin is assumed.

## Local delay replaces elapsed time

The earlier inward budget used $R\le w$. It can retain the actual current delay instead. Acute geometry gives $r<R$, and monotonicity of the source clock gives $R(t+u)\le R(t)+u$. At an inward state $p(t)=-q<0$, use $\ell=qr(t)/8$. On this interval, unit speed and $p'\le1/r$ imply $r\ge r(t)/2$, $p\le-q/2$ and $R\le2R(t)$. The exact radial projection gives

$$
E'\ge\frac{(-p)r}{R^2}\quad(p\le0).
$$

Consequently every all-future branch satisfies

$$
1-E(t)\ge\frac{p(t)^2r(t)^2}{128R(t)^2}\quad(p(t)<0).
\tag{1}
$$

This is also a sufficient finite-unit-arrival test when the corresponding strict reverse inequality holds at an actual state, by the already accepted finite-endpoint alternative. It is a conditional test, not an occurrence statement.

## Complete arc forced by a degenerating transmitter factor

Suppose $D(t_j)\to0$. Put $u_j=s(t_j)$, $a_j=s(u_j)$ and $L_j=u_j-a_j$. Previously established source-frame inequalities imply $p(u_j)\to-1$, $E(u_j)\to1$ and $r(u_j)/(u_j-s_0)\to0$. Applying (1) at $u_j$ strengthens this to

$$
r(u_j)/L_j\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2}
$$

Since $r(u_j)\to\infty$, also $L_j\to\infty$. The clock theorem gives $a_j\to\infty$, so the complete preceding arc is eventually generated. Let $e_j=x(a_j)/|x(a_j)|$. The exact mirror clock $L_j=|x(u_j)+x(a_j)|$ and (2) give $r(a_j)/L_j\to1$. The speed bound then almost saturates the ordinary path chord between the two endpoints.

For $0\le q\le1$, define $Z_j(q)=x(a_j+qL_j)/L_j$. Unit speed, the endpoint data and the triangle inequality prove uniformly

$$
Z_j(q)-(1-q)e_j\longrightarrow0.
\tag{3}
$$

For example every point lies within its elapsed-time ball about the first endpoint and its remaining-time ball about the last endpoint; these two balls collapse onto the indicated line as the endpoint path length tends to one. An equivalent direct proof uses the nonnegative length deficit along $e_j$.

There is also a velocity statement with its correct topology:

$$
\int_0^1|v(a_j+qL_j)+e_j|^2\,dq
\le2+2\frac{[x(u_j)-x(a_j)]\cdot e_j}{L_j}
\longrightarrow0.
\tag{4}
$$

Thus normalized positions converge uniformly to a straight inward unit-speed segment and velocities converge in $L^2$. In particular speed tends to one and radial velocity to minus one in measure on every closed subinterval before the final endpoint. No pointwise endpoint-velocity convergence or regular limiting delayed equation follows from this bound.

## The delay itself is sublinear in elapsed time

In fact $L_j/(u_j-s_0)\to0$. If a subsequence had this ratio bounded below, (3) would place the middle third of the preceding arc at radius bounded below by a fixed positive fraction of its elapsed time. By (4), select an interior point there with speed tending to one and radial velocity tending to minus one. The earlier elapsed-time inward budget would then bound its speed deficit below by a fixed positive constant, a contradiction. This uses actual generated points in the complete arc and does not infer a pointwise velocity limit from $L^2$ convergence.

The resulting hierarchy is

$$
r(u_j)=o(L_j),\qquad L_j=o(u_j-s_0),\qquad
r(a_j)\sim L_j,\qquad a_j/(u_j-s_0)\to1.
$$

The last ratio is understood with the immaterial fixed time-origin shift; equivalently $(a_j-s_0)/(u_j-s_0)\to1$. This is a necessary degeneration geometry, not a proof that such a sequence exists or that it is impossible. It does not by itself control the shrinking endpoint layer near $q=0$ or $q=1$ in velocity. That layer is an explicit missing step in any attempted contradiction based on treating (3) as a regular unit-speed solution.

The exact spiral is a consistency control: its source factor stays above one, so the degeneration hypothesis fails. Falsifiers are an incorrect radial sign, a failure to retain the source clock's monotonicity, use of a prescribed rather than generated interior point, or promotion of (4) to unsupported uniform velocity convergence. No numerical target was run and no source history was changed.
