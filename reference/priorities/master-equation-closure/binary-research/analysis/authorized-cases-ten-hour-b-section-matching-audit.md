## 1. The section is an implicit function of amplitude

Let the retained inverse normal-form transformation give the original $(w,u)$ as analytic functions of $(a,\psi,\delta)$. Near the central apocenter, the exact finite comparison section $u=0$ has a unique phase

$$
\psi=\psi_u(a,\delta),
$$

because $\partial_\psi u=-1+O(|a-1|+\delta^2)$ there. Denote its original radial coordinate by

$$
m(a,\delta)=w(a,\psi_u(a,\delta),\delta).
\tag{1}
$$

The central controls are $\psi_u=\pi$, $m=1-a$. On a sufficiently small fixed neighborhood, the finite coefficient bounds at the exact parameter give the quantitative margins

$$
|\partial_\psi u|>1/2,\qquad
-2<\partial_a m<-1/2,
\qquad |\partial_\delta m|\le C\delta.
\tag{2}
$$

The constant and domain in the last inequality must come from the retained inverse transformation. Its leading noncentral order is two. The curve $m(a_g(\delta),\delta)=0$ is the corrected grazing-amplitude curve; $a=1$ is not substituted for it.

For each candidate integer N, the reduced event equation is

$$
\psi(a)-\psi_u(a,\delta(a))=2\pi N.
\tag{3}
$$

The known normal-form coefficients give $d\psi/da\sim1/(2\delta^3a)$. The derivative of $\psi_u$ is bounded on the fixed analytic section, while $d\delta/da\sim-2\delta/(3a)$. Thus the explicit derivative target near $a=1$ is

$$
\frac{d}{da}\{\psi(a)-\psi_u(a,\delta(a))\}
>\frac1{4\delta^3}.
\tag{4}
$$

This is the section inverse margin the completed map must verify. It turns a phase error into a much smaller amplitude error; no division by the small radial velocity at the apocenter is used.
