# A quantitative lower terminal speed after admitted canonical outward entry

**Derived strengthening candidate, pending independent assessment.** The accepted outward theorem proves individual velocity limits, and the [positive-terminal assessment](authorized-cases-two-hour-d-coordinator-terminal-assessment.md) excludes a zero relative limit. Its proof can be made quantitative on a finite radius interval. The result proposed here is

$$
|U_\infty|>\frac1{6000}\left(\frac K{d_e}\right)^{3/4},
\tag{1}
$$

where $d_e$ is the separation at any admitted entry to that same outward nonnegative-account theorem. No equation, root treatment, history class or speed condition is changed. The estimate is deliberately conservative; it is a lower bound, not a predicted terminal speed or an inferred value from a finite tail.

All displayed speeds and $\chi$ use the inherited wake-speed normalization. Restoring dimensions, (1) means $|U_\infty|/c_f>[K/(c_f^2d_e)]^{3/4}/6000$; the dimensionless coefficient does not equate a dimensional speed with a three-halves power of another speed. The numerical instantiation throughout remains $c_f=1$.

Use the unchanged canonical opposite pair with $c_f=1$ and fixed $K>0$. Write $Z=dN$, $U=Z'$, $u=d'$, $v=U-uN$, $k=K/d^2$, $\chi=K/d$ and $\mathcal J=|U|^2/2-(2g+u)\chi$. Assume exactly the [independently accepted outward entry](authorized-cases-outgoing-d-reference-assessment.md): all-future ordinary continuation, $\mathcal J\ge0$, increasing $d\to\infty$, complete generated speeds below $\beta=1/20$, integrable individual acceleration, $\chi\le10^{-4}$, generated nested-source coverage and velocity limits. Complete remote histories retain their own strict speed bounds; they are not assigned the generated ceiling.

## Uniform estimates on a finite tail segment

Let $\chi_e=K/d_e$ and $\delta=|U_\infty|$. The accepted virial inequality for $I=du$ is $I'>(9/5)K/d$. As reconstructed in the terminal assessment, for $d\ge100d_e$ it implies

$$
u>\frac95\sqrt\chi,
\qquad \int_T^\infty k(s)\,ds\le\frac{10}{9}\sqrt{\chi(T)}.
\tag{2}
$$

The second inequality uses the first throughout the later monotone-radius tail. Each exact member acceleration is at most $(441/380)k$ by the complete-speed chord bound. The actual convergent-velocity identity therefore gives the nonzero-limit version of the earlier bound:

$$
|U(T)|\le\delta+\frac{49}{19}\sqrt{\chi(T)}.
\tag{3}
$$

Suppose $\chi\ge400\delta^2$. Then

$$
|U|\le\left(\frac1{20}+\frac{49}{19}\right)\sqrt\chi
=\frac{999}{380}\sqrt\chi<\frac83\sqrt\chi,
\qquad \Lambda:=|v|^2/\chi<7.
\tag{4}
$$

The last strict inequality follows from $999^2<7(380)^2$. The exact scalar-account estimate in the accepted positive-terminal proof then yields

$$
\mathcal J'>\frac14k\chi
\tag{5}
$$

on this entire finite segment. This use of (5) does not assume a zero terminal limit; that older proof used zero only to obtain $\Lambda<7$. Equation (4) supplies the same condition under the stated finite-radius restriction. All other terms retain the accepted $\beta=1/20$ and $\chi\le10^{-4}$ bounds.

## Contradiction to an excessively small terminal speed

The previously accepted positive-terminal theorem supplies $\delta>0$. Assume for contradiction that

$$
\delta\le\chi_e^{3/4}/6000.
\tag{6}
$$

Put $d_\delta=K/(400\delta^2)$, so $\chi(d_\delta)=400\delta^2$. Since $\chi_e\le10^{-4}$,

$$
\frac{d_\delta}{d_e}
=\frac{\chi_e}{400\delta^2}
\ge\frac{90000}{\sqrt{\chi_e}}
\ge9\times10^6>200.
$$

The solution attains every radius between $100d_e$ and $d_\delta$ in finite time by its existing monotone dispersal theorem. On that whole interval (4)–(5) hold. Integrate (5) first from $100d_e$ to $200d_e$, using $u<(8/3)\sqrt{K/d}$ from (4):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal J(200d_e)-\mathcal J(100d_e)
&>\frac{3K^{3/2}}{32}\int_{100d_e}^{200d_e}r^{-5/2}\,dr\\
&=\frac{1-2^{-3/2}}{16000}\chi_e^{3/2}
>\frac1{25000}\chi_e^{3/2}.
\end{aligned}
\tag{7}
$$

Here $2^{-3/2}<9/25$ follows by squaring the positive inequality $25<18\sqrt2$, equivalent to $625<648$. Since $\mathcal J\ge0$ at the earlier radius and (5) remains positive afterwards,

$$
\mathcal J(d_\delta)>\chi_e^{3/2}/25000.
\tag{8}
$$

At $d_\delta$, equation (3) gives $|U|\le(999/19)\delta$. Also $2g+u>0$ because the tail is outward and its speeds are strict. Therefore

$$
\mathcal J(d_\delta)\le\frac12|U|^2
\le\frac{999^2}{2(19)^2}\delta^2
<1400\delta^2
\le\frac7{180000}\chi_e^{3/2}.
\tag{9}
$$

But $7/180000<1/25000$ since $175000<180000$. Equations (8)–(9) contradict each other and prove (1). The proof uses an actual finite segment of the accepted all-future tail; it does not extend a numerical curve or postulate a physical energy account.
