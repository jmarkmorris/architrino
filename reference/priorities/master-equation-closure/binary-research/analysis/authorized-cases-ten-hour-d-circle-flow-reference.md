## Why this curve leaves a fixed neighborhood

At the equilibrium, the variational equation is a finite constant-delay linear equation with bounded evaluation coefficients, since all reference roots are constant. For a time $T>H$, its time map on compatible $C^1$ histories is compact. Indeed, after the initial history has left the window, bounded histories yield uniformly bounded first and second derivatives by the linear equation; Arzelà–Ascoli then gives compactness in $C^1$. The characteristic vector is an eigenvector of this map with multiplier $e^{\lambda T}>1$.

Choose a spectral modulus cut strictly between one and that multiplier and not meeting the compact operator's spectrum. Its outer spectral subspace is finite-dimensional and contains the certified mode; all other growing modes beyond the cut are included without claiming to count them. Equivalent norms give a strictly expanding lower bound on that subspace and a smaller upper bound on its complement. Differentiability of the nonlinear time map makes its remainder smaller than any prescribed multiple of the distance to equilibrium in a sufficiently small solution-manifold chart. A narrow cone around the outer spectral subspace is then invariant while the iterates remain in that chart, and its expanding component grows by a factor greater than one on each step.

The older cutoff must be handled before applying that cone. The difference between the truncated tangent and the full characteristic mode vanishes on the entire relevant initial delay window. Thus the derivative of the time-T map applied to our repaired physical curve is exactly $e^{\lambda T}q$, even though the curve's initial full H-history tangent was truncated. The nearby fixed spatial translations and rotations of the exact complete circle form a finite-dimensional smooth symmetry family in history space. At a sufficiently small fixed exit radius, the linear lower bound dominates that quadratic symmetry displacement.
