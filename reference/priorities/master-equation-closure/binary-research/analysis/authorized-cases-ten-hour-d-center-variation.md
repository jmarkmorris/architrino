## Actual reference and admissible variations

On each finite receiving interval, consider differentiable one-parameter perturbations of complete positions in $C^1$, with locally uniformly bounded Lipschitz velocities on the sampled supplied past. The common-center tangent $c$ has bounded past velocity, and its complete past positions are absolutely continuous. A convenient full-past tangent norm is

$$
\|c\|_{\mathrm{past}}=|c(0)|+\sup_{u\le0}|c'(u)|.
\tag{1}
$$

The derivative bound permits linear position growth into the remote past; it does not silently replace that past. A sufficiently small finite perturbation with this derivative norm retains strict complete-past speed. The linear theorem below applies to all tangents of this class. It asserts no all-future nonlinear tube for their finite multiples.

Decompose the two position variations as $\delta X_+=\xi+c$, $\delta X_-=-\xi+c$. Reflection exchanges the two labels and makes the common-center and relative equations decouple at first order. All formulas below are in the ambient Cartesian space, so $c$ may have an out-of-plane component.

## Differentiate both clocks before the acceleration

For the mirror reference, the common partner source time $s=s(t)$ is uniquely defined by

$$
R=t-s=|x(t)+x(s)|,\qquad
n=\frac{x(t)+x(s)}R,\qquad
D=1+n\cdot v_s,
\quad v_s=x'(s),\quad a_s=x''(s).
\tag{2}
$$

There is exactly one partner root per receiver and no positive-delay self root, by the complete strict speed margin. Source acceleration exists almost everywhere on the recent supplied past and is continuous at the generated source times except where a permitted seam propagates. All derivative equations involving $a_s$ are interpreted almost everywhere and in their time-integrated form.

Set $d=c(t)-c(s)$, $P=I-nn^{\mathsf T}$ and

$$
B=I-\frac{v_sn^{\mathsf T}}D.
\tag{3}
$$

The two clocks respond oppositely:

$$
\delta s_+=-\frac{n\cdot d}{D},\qquad
\delta s_-=+\frac{n\cdot d}{D}.
\tag{4}
$$

For the positive receiver, $\delta S=Bd$, $\delta R=(n\cdot d)/D$, $\delta n=PBd/R$, and

$$
\delta D=\frac{v_s^{\mathsf T}PBd}{R}
-\frac{(n\cdot a_s)(n\cdot d)}D-n\cdot c'(s).
\tag{5}
$$

The source acceleration in (5) comes from differentiating the source velocity at its moving clock. It is not an added acceleration term in the underlying canonical law. Differentiating $-Kn/(R^2D)$ now gives the exact common-center variational equation

$$
c''(t)=L(t)[c(t)-c(s(t))]+V(t)c'(s(t)),
\tag{6}
$$

$$
V=-\frac{Knn^{\mathsf T}}{R^2D^2},
$$

$$
L=-\frac K{R^3D}
\left[
PB-\frac2Dnn^{\mathsf T}
-\frac1Dnv_s^{\mathsf T}PB
+\frac{R(n\cdot a_s)}{D^2}nn^{\mathsf T}
\right].
\tag{7}
$$

For comparison, the relative tangent satisfies $\xi''=L[\xi(t)+\xi(s)]-V\xi'(s)$. Its non-difference position term prevents applying the common-center estimate to that equation without further work.

The finite-interval differentiation does not require a supplied jerk. Ordinary clocks are uniformly increasing and have upper and lower derivative bounds from both strict speed margins. Thus null sets in source time pull back to null sets in receiving time. For a Lipschitz supplied velocity, express each shifted velocity difference as the integral of its almost-everywhere acceleration. Approximate that bounded acceleration in $L^1$ on the finite sampled interval by continuous functions. The clock bounds control the approximation error after composition, while the continuous approximants have the ordinary chain rule. Passing to the limit establishes (5) in $L^1$ and the differentiated position–velocity integral equation. On each finite interval its bounded causal coefficients give the usual linear integral uniqueness estimate. This is the first variation of the actual finite-interval solution map, not a stability calculation about a prescribed nonequilibrium path.

## Known analytical controls precede the global estimate

If $c$ is a constant translation, (6) vanishes, as required by exact translation symmetry. For the separately prescribed static-row control, $v_s=a_s=0$, so $L=-K(I-3nn^{\mathsf T})/R^3$ and $V=-Knn^{\mathsf T}/R^2$. Inserting an affine common variation $c(t)=ut$ gives

$$
c''_{\mathrm{row}}=\frac K{R^2}(2nn^{\mathsf T}-I)u.
\tag{8}
$$

This is exactly the first derivative of the independently derived complete affine-history center output. The control's prescribed static path is not treated as an equilibrium or coupled future. ## An integrable coefficient controls every center velocity tangent

The actual slow solution obeys

$$
\frac{2r}{1+\beta}\le R\le\frac{2r}{1-\beta},\quad
D\ge1-\beta,\quad \|B\|\le(1-\beta)^{-1}.
\tag{9}
$$

On a generated source window, $r(s)\ge r-\beta R\ge r(1-3\beta)/(1-\beta)>0.99r$. The accepted generated acceleration bound is $|x''|<0.253K/r^2$. It therefore implies $|a_s|<K/r^2$ there. If the source is supplied, the accepted release construction places the receiver before its scaled time $10\epsilon$, where $r<1.01R_0$ and supplied physical acceleration is at most $8\epsilon^2/R_0=2K/R_0^2$. Consequently the single conservative bound

$$
|a_s|\le\frac{4K}{r^2}
\tag{10}
$$

holds at almost every sampled source, including that initial layer. Together with $K/r\le16\epsilon^2/h_0^2$, it gives $R|a_s|<1/1000$. Substitution into (7), using $\beta<0.003$, yields the deliberately loose but explicit bounds

$$
\|L\|\le\frac{4K}{R^3},\qquad
\|V\|\le\frac{2K}{R^2},\qquad
R\|L\|+\|V\|\le\frac{8K}{r^2}.
\tag{11}
$$

Since $c(t)-c(s)=\int_s^t c'(u)\,du$, let $M(t)=\sup_{u\le t}|c'(u)|$. The linear integral equation gives

$$
M(t)\le M(0)+\int_0^t\frac{8K}{r(u)^2}M(u)\,du.
\tag{12}
$$

The mirror theorem's finite angle and positive angular quantity supply an explicit total integral. In its dimensionless time $\tau=\epsilon t/R_0$ and radius $\rho=r/R_0$,

$$
\int_0^\infty\frac K{r(t)^2}\,dt
=4\epsilon\int_0^\infty\rho(\tau)^{-2}\,d\tau
\le\frac{4\epsilon\theta_\infty}{h_0}
<\frac{32}{h_0\epsilon}.
\tag{13}
$$

Thus every common-center tangent has bounded all-future velocity and an absolutely integrable acceleration:

$$
\sup_{t\ge0}|c'(t)|
\le M(0)\exp\!\left(\frac{256}{h_0\epsilon}\right),
\qquad
\int_0^\infty|c''(t)|\,dt<\infty.
\tag{14}
$$

It follows that $c'(t)$ converges to a finite vector. The exponent is intentionally very conservative; this theorem establishes global linear control, not a useful finite perturbation radius for the literal historical record. A constant position translation has $M(0)=0$ and remains constant, consistently with (14).

## What this resolves and what remains

The first common-center response is controlled globally along every accepted slow mirror reference, with both clock derivatives retained. This rules out an unbounded center-velocity tangent on that reference and shows why a frozen-clock calculation is unnecessary. It does not control the nonlinear common-center/relative coupling. In particular, $|c(t)|$ can grow linearly while the reference radius can have zero terminal speed. Bounded center velocity therefore does not establish uniform relative-history closeness.

A nominal phase defect about an exactly mirrored pair has, after a fixed spatial rotation, a first-order common-center tangent and only a second-order relative-position change. This observation identifies the relevance of (6), but does not bind the finite historical tokens to a differentiable complete family or bound their nonlinear remainder. The old record's source identity and complete-history membership remain separate obligations. No phase token, finite source certificate or source completion is relabeled by this result.

Falsifiers are an incorrect sign in either clock variation, a missing moving-source term in (5), failure of the independently checked affine control, loss of the supplied-source coverage used in (10), an acceleration bound inconsistent with the original scaling, or a center tangent violating the integrated inequality (12). A finite nonlinear perturbation that leaves the mirror neighborhood does not refute this linear theorem. No numerical instrument or target run was used.
