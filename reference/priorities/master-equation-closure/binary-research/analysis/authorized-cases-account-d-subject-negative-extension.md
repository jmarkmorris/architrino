## A finite actual extension with no angular denominator

Define the running history supremum

$$
M(t)=\max\left\{m_D,\sup_{T_D\le s\le t}|W(s)|\right\}.
\tag{8}
$$

The [nonlinear midpoint bound](authorized-cases-ten-hour-d-primary-nonlinear-center.md), on the complete generated windows and the much smaller (6), gives

$$
|W'(t)|\le5k(t)M(t).
\tag{9}
$$

It retains source acceleration and both clock derivatives. Its constant is conservative at $\beta=.01$, and does not require $H>0$. Integrating (4) while $J\le0$ yields

$$
\int_{T_D}^t k\chi\,ds\le\frac23j_D.
\tag{10}
$$

On any such partial continuation with $d\le R$, $k\le(R/K)k\chi$. Integral Gronwall applied to (8)–(10) gives the explicit actual bound

$$
M(t)\le m_D
\exp\left(\frac{10R}{3K}j_D\right).
\tag{11}
$$

This estimate uses an upper radius only for a finite continuation. It will not be sent to infinity.

Take $R=4D$. Equations (2) and (11) make the exponent less than $26.68<27$. The elementary inequalities $e<11/4$ and $(11/4)^{27}<10^{12}$ imply

$$
M(t)<1.65\times10^{-5},\qquad
\max_i|V_i(t)|
\le M(t)+\frac12|U(t)|
<2\times10^{-5}<\frac1{100}.
\tag{12}
$$

Thus no speed exit occurs before either $J=0$ or $d=4D$. Equations (6)–(7) exclude a separation boundary; complete source regularity and the retained strict root chart exclude an ordinary finite continuation boundary. Angular motion may vanish without causing a singularity in this argument.

One of the two declared events must occur in finite time. Otherwise $d<4D$ and (4) imply
$J'\ge3K^2/[2(4D)^3]$, contradicting $J<0$ indefinitely. More precisely the first event time $\tau$ satisfies

$$
\tau-T_D
\le\frac{128j_DD^3}{3K^2}
<86\,\frac{D^2}{K}.
\tag{13}
$$

All times here are physical times with $c_f=1$.

This proves the following actual alternative for every history in the already declared nominal/spatial class:

1. If $J(T_D)\ge0$, the preceding accepted theorem gives all-future dispersal.
2. If $J(T_D)<0$, the actual ordinary solution continues to $d=4D=1.2\times10^{30}R_0$ or to its first $J=0$ event, with (6), (7), and (12) throughout, independently of any angular floor.
3. At an account-zero event with $u\ge0$, the accepted outward account theorem gives all-future dispersal. Its distance and speed requirements have strict reserve by (7) and (12).
4. An account-zero event with $u<0$, or arrival at $4D$ with $J<0$, remains unresolved by these estimates. First attainment of $D$ does not prevent a later inward turn while $J<0$.

In particular (11) cannot prove an all-future midpoint bound: its exponent grows with $R$. The much improved lower distance does not bound the upper distance, and cannot reverse that inequality. The new actual theorem advances beyond the old $dM^2$ angular reserve but does not claim that either remaining alternative fails.

## Exact current-affine comparison with a nonzero angular limit

This section is a logical limitation test on the retained identities and unsigned error estimates. It does not instantiate a new physical preparation or assert that affine evaluations at successive receiving times form one canonical source history.

Set the midpoint identically zero in the exact current-affine relative row. Work in a plane and write the transverse speed as $v_\theta=H/d$. Define

$$
q_v=\sqrt{1-v_\theta^2/4}.
$$

The full affine equations, including the transverse central difference, are

$$
d'=u,\qquad
u'=\frac{v_\theta^2}{d}-k(2q_v+u),\qquad
H'=kH\left(1+\frac{u}{2q_v}\right).
\tag{14}
$$

The factor $u/(2q_v)$ in the last equation is essential: it is the exact affine $Q_t$ contribution. It has not been dropped. The midpoint row is zero by exact parity. In the accepted decomposition this comparison sets $e_{\rm rel}=e_{\rm ctr}=0$, which satisfies every unsigned error upper bound but does not identify the error of an actual delayed solution.

Introduce

$$
x=\sqrt{K/d},\qquad u=xy,\qquad h=H/K,\qquad
q_v=\sqrt{1-h^2x^4/4}.
$$

Equations (14) are equivalent on an outward interval to

$$
x\,\frac{dy}{dx}
=\frac{4q_v}{y}+2x-\frac{2h^2x^2}{y}-y,
\qquad
\frac{dh}{dx}=-\frac{2h}{y}-\frac{hx}{q_v},
\tag{15}
$$

$$
\frac{dx}{dt}=-\frac{x^4y}{2K}.
\tag{16}
$$

We construct the zero-terminal-speed solution with $y(0)=2$ and any specified $h_\infty>0$.

### Explicit existence and remainder

Let $L=1+h_\infty^2$ and $a=1/(100L)$. Put $z=y-2$, $p=\log(h/h_\infty)$. On $0\le x\le a$, equations (15) have the Volterra form

$$
z(x)=x^{-2}\int_0^x s
\left[
2s-\frac{2h_\infty^2e^{2p(s)}s^2}{2+z(s)}
+\frac{4(q_v(s)-1)}{2+z(s)}
+\frac{z(s)^2}{2+z(s)}
\right]ds,
\tag{17}
$$

$$
p(x)=-\int_0^x
\left[\frac2{2+z(s)}+\frac{s}{q_v(s)}\right]ds.
\tag{18}
$$

Both expressions have continuous zero values at $x=0$. Consider the closed set $|z(x)|\le x$, $-2x\le p(x)\le0$, with norm
$\max\{\sup_{x>0}|z(x)|/x,\sup_{x>0}|p(x)|/x\}$ for differences. Throughout it, $2+z\ge1.99$, $h\le h_\infty$ and $q_v>.99$.

The bracket in (17) differs from $2s$ by at most $2Ls^2$. Thus its image obeys

$$
\left|z(x)-\frac23x\right|\le\frac L2x^2<.005x.
\tag{19}
$$

The integrand in (18) is positive and below $2$, so its image lies in the prescribed $p$ interval. For the bracket $F$ in (17), direct derivatives give conservative bounds
$|F_z|\le2s+h_\infty^2s^2$ and $|F_p|\le3h_\infty^2s^2$ on this set. For the bracket $G$ in (18), $|G_z|<1$ and $|G_p|\le h_\infty^2s^5$. Integration with the weighted norm bounds the two Lipschitz factors respectively by

$$
\frac a2+\frac45h_\infty^2a^2<.006,\qquad
\frac a2+\frac17h_\infty^2a^6<.006.
\tag{20}
$$

Hence the map is a contraction. Its unique fixed point gives the claimed solution and explicit remainder (19). In addition,

$$
h_\infty e^{-2x}\le h(x)\le h_\infty.
\tag{21}
$$

These are local analytical existence bounds at infinity for the comparison equations, not an invocation of an unspecified asymptotic expansion.

### Negative account, positive flux, and asymptotic exhaustion

For this comparison, $g=1$, $|U|^2=x^2y^2+h^2x^4$, and therefore

$$
J=x^2\left[\frac{y^2}{2}-2-xy\right]+\frac12h^2x^4.
\tag{22}
$$

Substitution of (19), with $Lx\le1/100$, gives

$$
\left|\frac{J}{x^3}+\frac23\right|
\le\frac52Lx\le\frac1{40},
\qquad
-\frac7{10}x^3<J<-\frac35x^3<0.
\tag{23}
$$

The exact affine account identity is also strictly positive. Here

$$
Q_r=2k(1-q_v),\qquad
Q_\theta=\frac{kuv_\theta}{2q_v},
$$

so

$$
J'
=k\chi(2+u)
+2k(u-\chi)(1-q_v)
+\frac{kuv_\theta^2}{2q_v}
>2k\chi.
\tag{24}
$$

The inequality uses $u=x(2+z)>\chi$ on the constructed interval. Nonetheless (23) stays negative for every finite time and tends to zero.

Equation (16) gives infinite physical time as $x\downarrow0$, while $d=K/x^2\to\infty$ and $|U|\to0$. Equation (21) gives $H\to Kh_\infty>0$. Choosing $h_\infty=2H_*/K$ makes $H>H_*$ throughout this comparison tail. Its member speeds are below $.01$ for $x\le a$, and the midpoint is exactly zero. The angular direction can be reconstructed by $\theta'=H/d^2$; together with (15), this verifies the full vector affine row, not only a scalar radial reduction.

Consequently

$$
\int_{t_0}^{\infty}J'(t)\,dt=-J(t_0),
\qquad
J(t_0)+\int_{t_0}^{t}J'(s)\,ds<0
\quad\text{for every finite }t.
\tag{25}
$$

Even the stronger lower flux $J'>2K^2/d^3$, retained angular positivity, small speed, finite angle, and arbitrarily large separation do not force finite sign crossing in this comparison system. The positive integral asymptotically exhausts the deficit.

For $h_\infty\downarrow0$, (15) reduces to the exact radial control
$\chi=u-2\log(1+u/2)$, whose expansion has $u=2\sqrt\chi+(2/3)\chi+O(\chi^{3/2})$. This independently checks the leading coefficient in (23). The nonzero-$h_\infty$ construction above is needed to show that the issue is not merely an omitted angular floor.

## What this establishes and what it does not

The actual theorem (2)–(13) retains the full physical history and both clocks. It obtains a new, much larger separation floor and a finite negative-account continuation without the earlier angular or spatial-account reserve. Its unresolved exits are explicitly given.

The comparison (14)–(25) proves that the exact affine identities together with unsigned actual-delay error enclosures cannot, by themselves, establish the proposed finite signed-integral overshoot. A proof specific to the actual nominal trajectory must use additional signed delayed-source information or a stronger actual state estimate; replacing those correlations by error intervals admits the displayed comparison. This is a limitation of that information set, not impossibility of a future proof using the full canonical history equation.

Neither the actual bounds nor the comparison supplies a lower radial-velocity margin at the attained event. They therefore do not establish a sharper radial escape-account test there, and they do not exclude an inward first account-zero event. The operator-facing outcome remains a genuine analytical extension plus a precise method obstruction, not nominal all-future closure.
