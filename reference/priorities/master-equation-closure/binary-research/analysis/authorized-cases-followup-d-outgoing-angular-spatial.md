# Angular drift and spatial control on an actual outgoing canonical tail

**Derived subject theorem, frozen for independent assessment.** On an actual linearly separating slow canonical tail, the relative angular-motion vector has an explicit logarithmic term determined by the terminal common velocity. A negative signed planar coefficient forces failure of any positive angular floor even though that solution disperses. The additional spatial term in the earlier midpoint account is integrable on such a tail whenever angular magnitude stays bounded away from zero. These conclusions sharpen the previous conditional route; they do not establish an outgoing tail or its terminal coefficient for the literal nominal preparation.

This is a new authorized follow-up after the ten-hour campaign closed. It uses the unchanged canonical equation, source-fixed $K=\kappa q^2>0$, and $c_f=1$. There is no new history, numerical target, response, exponent or boundary rule. Current-affine rows below are functional evaluations used in a proved comparison, never substituted physical histories.

## Actual tail hypotheses and full history

Let an actual ordinary solution have complete supplied and generated member speeds at most $\beta$, where $0<\beta\le1/20$. Define

$$
Z=X_+-X_-=dN,\quad U=V_+-V_-,\quad
W=(V_++V_-)/2,\quad
\boldsymbol H=Z\times U,\quad H=|\boldsymbol H|.
\tag{1}
$$

Assume, for $T>0$ and $c>0$,

$$
d(t)\ge ct\quad(t\ge T),\qquad d(T)\le3\beta T.
\tag{2}
$$

Time is physical absolute time with its fixed origin, not slow or similarity time. An additive change of origin does not change the logarithmic coefficient. The second condition can always be reached later on a linearly separating tail because $d'\le2\beta$. The [strict outgoing criterion](authorized-cases-ten-hour-d-outgoing-neighborhood-candidate.md) supplies such a tail from a separately proved finite witness. The nominal time-ten entry is not that witness.

Complete strict speed gives exactly one ordinary partner root for each receiver and no positive-delay self root. The coincident self event remains canonically excluded. For each independent delay $R_i$ and transmitter factor $D_i$,

$$
\frac d{1+\beta}\le R_i\le\frac d{1-\beta},
\qquad D_i\ge1-\beta.
\tag{3}
$$

Put

$$
D=3\beta,\quad \rho=\frac D{1-\beta}<1,\quad
\eta=1-\rho>0,\quad
A=\frac{K(1+\beta)^2}{(1-\beta)c^2}.
\tag{4}
$$

For $t\ge T$, one has $d(t)\le Dt$ and $|A_i(t)|\le A/t^2$. Individual velocities therefore converge, with

$$
|V_i(t)-V_{i,\infty}|\le A/t.
\tag{5}
$$

Set

$$
U_\infty=V_{+,\infty}-V_{-,\infty},\quad
v=|U_\infty|,\quad n=U_\infty/v,\quad
c_\infty=(V_{+,\infty}+V_{-,\infty})/2.
\tag{6}
$$

Integration of $U-U_\infty=O(t^{-1})$ gives $Z/t\to U_\infty$, hence $c\le v\le2\beta$ and $|c_\infty|\le\beta$.

## The two actual source windows and affine error

For $t\ge T/\eta$, both source times satisfy $t-R_i\ge\eta t\ge T$. On either complete source-to-reception interval, actual acceleration is at most $A/(\eta^2t^2)$. Integral Taylor expansion gives

$$
|X_j(t-R)-X_j(t)+V_j(t)R|\le E_0:=\frac{A\rho^2}{2\eta^2},
\qquad
|V_j(t-R)-V_j(t)|\le \frac{V_0}{t},
\quad V_0=\frac{A\rho}{\eta^2}.
\tag{7}
$$

Compare each clock separately with the affine evaluation using that current source position and velocity. Its gap slope is at least $1-\beta$, so its delay error and chord error are at most

$$
R_{\rm err}=\frac{E_0}{1-\beta}.
\tag{8}
$$

For the chord, add the Taylor error and current velocity times delay error. The two receiver delays have never been identified.

For

$$
t\ge t_1:=\max\left\{T/\eta,\frac{2(1+\beta)R_{\rm err}}c,T\right\},
\qquad \ell=\frac c{2(1+\beta)},
\tag{9}
$$

every chord on the comparison segment has norm at least $\ell t$, and every interpolated source velocity has norm at most $\beta$. For the canonical row $F(S,V)=-KS/(|S|^3[1-\widehat S\cdot V])$, define

$$
C_x=\frac K{\ell^3}\left(\frac2{1-\beta}+
\frac{\beta}{(1-\beta)^2}\right),\qquad
C_v=\frac K{\ell^2(1-\beta)^2},\qquad
E_3=C_xR_{\rm err}+C_vV_0.
\tag{10}
$$

Its derivatives are bounded by $C_x/t^3$ and $C_v/t^2$. Each actual-minus-current-affine acceleration is thus at most $E_3/t^3$. Source acceleration entered (7) through the actual coupled equation; it was not deleted, set to zero or differentiated. The complete history, both clocks and locally Lipschitz velocity are retained.

## Angular asymptotics and an explicit remainder

Write $u=N\cdot U$, $U_\perp=U-uN$, $a=N\cdot W$, $b=W-aN$ and $g=\sqrt{1-|b|^2}$. Removing only transverse relative velocity from the two current-affine source velocities gives the exact relative row

$$
A_{\rm rel}^{\rm aff,rad}
=-\frac K{d^2}\left\{(2g+u)N-\frac{2a}{g}b\right\}.
\tag{11}
$$

The radial $u$ term is retained and cancels from torque exactly. This follows by evaluating source velocities $W\mp uN/2$ in the accepted affine formula. Removing $U_\perp$ may enlarge the source speed bound to $2\beta$. Use

$$
g_0=\sqrt{1-\beta^2},\quad g_1=\sqrt{1-4\beta^2},\quad
L_z=1+\frac{6\beta}{g_1}+\frac{8\beta^3}{g_1^3}.
\tag{12}
$$

The derivative of the dimensionless affine row with respect to source velocity is at most $L_z$ on that enlarged segment. The two velocity changes have magnitudes $|U_\perp|/2$, and $|U_\perp|=H/d$. Hence

$$
\boldsymbol H'=\frac{2Ka}{dg}N\times W+\mathcal E_H,\qquad
|\mathcal E_H|\le\frac{KL_zH}{d^2}+\frac{2dE_3}{t^3}.
\tag{13}
$$

No bounded-angular-motion assumption was used. Put

$$
B(t)=d(T)+2\beta T+2A\log(t/T),\qquad
\overline H(t)=4\beta A+(2\beta+2A/T)B(t).
\tag{14}
$$

Directly from (5),

$$
|Z-tU_\infty|\le B(t),\quad
|N-n|\le\frac{2B(t)}{ct},\quad
H(t)\le\overline H(t),\quad |W-c_\infty|\le A/t.
\tag{15}
$$

Define $a_\infty=n\cdot c_\infty$, $g_\infty=\sqrt{1-|c_\infty|^2+a_\infty^2}$ and

$$
\boldsymbol\tau=\frac{2K}{vg_\infty}
a_\infty(n\times c_\infty).
\tag{16}
$$

For $f(N,W)=a(N\times W)/g$,

$$
|f(N,W)-f(n,c_\infty)|
\le\frac{2\beta}{g_0^3t}
\left(A+\frac{2\beta B(t)}c\right).
\tag{17}
$$

Indeed the changes in both $a$ and $N\times W$ are at most $(A+2\beta B/c)/t$, the change in $b$ is at most twice that, and the derivative of $g$ has norm at most $\beta/g_0$. The reciprocal terms combine using $g_0^2+\beta^2=1$. Also $|1/d-1/(vt)|\le B(t)/(c^2t^2)$. Consequently

$$
\left|\boldsymbol H'-\frac{\boldsymbol\tau}{t}\right|
\le\frac{F(t)}{t^2},
\tag{18}
$$

$$
F(t)=\frac{KL_z\overline H(t)}{c^2}+2DE_3
+\frac{4K\beta}{cg_0^3}\left(A+\frac{2\beta B(t)}c\right)
+\frac{2K\beta^2B(t)}{c^2g_0}.
\tag{19}
$$

Write this nonnegative affine function as $F(t)=F_0+F_1\log(t/T)$. Its tail integral is explicit:

$$
\mathcal E(t)=\int_t^\infty F(s)s^{-2}\,ds
=\frac{F_0+F_1[\log(t/T)+1]}t.
\tag{20}
$$

There is a finite vector $\boldsymbol h_\infty$ with

$$
\boxed{\boldsymbol H(t)=\boldsymbol\tau\log(t/T)+
\boldsymbol h_\infty+r(t),\qquad |r(t)|\le\mathcal E(t).}
\tag{21}
$$

The bounds are uniform for fixed admitted entry constants. No values of those constants have been established for the nominal member.

## Necessary and sufficient angular-floor tests

For a planar solution, choose its fixed oriented normal and write signed angular motion $h$ and signed coefficient $\tau$. If $\tau<0$, then $h$ falls below every positive floor although the solution separates linearly. More quantitatively, for $\zeta>0$ and $h(t_1)>H_*$,

$$
t\ge t_1\exp\left(
\frac{h(t_1)-H_*+\mathcal E(t_1)+\zeta}{|\tau|}
\right)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad h(t)\le H_*-\zeta.
\tag{22}
$$

Integrate (18) from $t_1$ to prove this bound. Continuity supplies an earlier crossing. This is an implication for actual tails satisfying (2), not an assertion that the nominal source realizes $\tau<0$.

An all-future positive planar floor on this tail therefore requires $\tau\ge0$. If $\tau=0$, it also requires $h_\infty\ge H_*$. Equality is not excluded: strict finite-time inequalities may approach equality.

For a sufficient vector test, set

$$
D_{\rm ray}=\inf_{s\ge0}|\boldsymbol H(t_1)+s\boldsymbol\tau|.
\tag{23}
$$

This is $H(t_1)$ if $\boldsymbol H(t_1)\cdot\boldsymbol\tau\ge0$ or $\boldsymbol\tau=0$; otherwise it is the norm of the component of $\boldsymbol H(t_1)$ perpendicular to $\boldsymbol\tau$. If

$$
D_{\rm ray}>H_*+\mathcal E(t_1),
\tag{24}
$$

then $H(t)>H_*$ for all $t\ge t_1$. Equation (18) bounds the accumulated deviation from that ray by $\mathcal E(t_1)$. In the planar case $\tau\ge0$ and $h(t_1)>H_*+\mathcal E(t_1)$ suffice. Complete finite-prefix floor control is still needed before $t_1$.

## Integrability of the additional spatial term

The [midpoint correction](authorized-cases-ten-hour-d-primary-center-state-correction.md) uses

$$
Y=W-\frac K{2d}\frac bg,\qquad
L=\boldsymbol H/H,\qquad c_Y=Y\cdot L.
\tag{25}
$$

Its additional positive spatial term is

$$
\mathcal P(t)=\frac{4K^2a^2c_Y^2}{dgH^2}.
\tag{26}
$$

Assume $H(t)\ge H_*>0$ on the tail, for example by (24). Then $\mathcal P$ is integrable in every terminal-geometry case. This controls the displayed missing term; it does not establish an outgoing entry or the preceding floor.

If $\boldsymbol\tau\ne0$, (16) gives $\boldsymbol\tau\cdot c_\infty=0$. From (21), $W-c_\infty=O(t^{-1})$ and $Y-W=O(t^{-1})$,

$$
p(t):=\boldsymbol H(t)\cdot Y(t)
\longrightarrow\boldsymbol h_\infty\cdot c_\infty.
\tag{27}
$$

Thus $c_Y=p/H=O(1/\log t)$ while $H\sim|\boldsymbol\tau|\log(t/T)$, and

$$
\mathcal P(t)=O\!\left(t^{-1}\log^{-4}(t/T)\right).
\tag{28}
$$

The logarithmic angular drift suppresses the normal component relative to the actual moving plane. A fixed-plane estimate misses this relation.

For an explicit tail bound choose $t_2>\max(t_1,T)$ such that $H(t)\ge|\boldsymbol\tau|\log(t/T)/2$ for $t\ge t_2$, using (21), and let $P_2$ bound $|p|$ there. Such a bound follows explicitly from (14), (20), (21) and

$$
|Y-c_\infty|\le\frac{A+K\beta/(2cg_0)}t.
\tag{29}
$$

Then

$$
\int_{t_2}^\infty\mathcal P(t)\,dt
\le\frac{64K^2\beta^2P_2^2}
{3cg_0|\boldsymbol\tau|^4\log^3(t_2/T)}.
\tag{30}
$$

If $\boldsymbol\tau=0$, then $\boldsymbol H\to\boldsymbol h_\infty$ with $|\boldsymbol h_\infty|\ge H_*$ and $\boldsymbol h_\infty\cdot n=0$. If $a_\infty=0$, (15) gives $a=O(\log t/t)$. Otherwise (16) forces $c_\infty$ parallel to $n$, so $\boldsymbol h_\infty\cdot c_\infty=0$ and (21), (29) give $c_Y=O(\log t/t)$. In both cases

$$
\mathcal P(t)=O\!\left(t^{-3}\log^2(t/T)\right).
\tag{31}
$$

The finite preceding interval is integrable by ordinary regularity. This tail conclusion does not require the pointwise damping condition $2a^2\le gH^2/(Kd)$. The constants are not uniform across a family merely because $\boldsymbol\tau$ approaches zero; (30) displays that limitation explicitly.

## Nominal application and exact remaining obligation

The additional spatial term is controlled on an actual linearly separating tail with a maintained angular floor. A universal positive signed floor is stronger than dispersal: its sign is constrained by (16), and a negative sign gives (22). No new physical case or counterexample has been selected or constructed.

The literal nominal finite-entry theorem proves closeness through time ten and entry into the nonpositive-account planar region. It supplies neither (2), the terminal pair $(U_\infty,c_\infty)$ nor the sign of (16). Its exact phase defect cannot be replaced by zero in these missing quantities. A strict finite witness for the [outgoing criterion](authorized-cases-ten-hour-d-outgoing-neighborhood-candidate.md) would give (2) and a complete-history dispersal neighborhood. Until then the zero-relative-terminal-speed possibility and the nonpositive-account interval remain unclosed. No unconditional angular floor, robustness radius or nominal dispersal theorem is claimed.

The sharper proof boundary is that a dispersal argument may need to permit angular-floor crossings. They are not physical breakdown events. The present theorem provides a terminal-geometric test and finite versus infinite accumulated-drift distinction, rather than turning an unrelated affine release into a nominal counterexample.
