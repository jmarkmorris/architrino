The subject additionally derives center-velocity convergence rather than assuming it; that strengthening is checked below.

The initial hypotheses $d\to\infty$, $U\to0$ and the complete uniform speed margin make both actual source intervals escape, with length $O(d)$. Over those intervals the change of separation is $o(d)$, so actual source accelerations are uniformly $O(d^{-2})$ by the canonical complete-root bound. Integrating them gives the stated $O(1)$ affine position defect and $O(d^{-1})$ source-velocity defect. Uniform ordinary inversion therefore gives an $O(d^{-3})$ acceleration-row error. This argument needs no preexisting center-velocity limit.

The exact affine identity is correct when the current source velocities are $W\mp uN/2$. Both have the same tangential component relative to $N$, hence the same square-root factor $g$. The radial relative velocity contributes $(uN)$ to the full relative row and $-uW_\perp/(2g)$ to the center row, while it cancels exactly from the relative transverse row. Removing the actual $U_\perp=O(d^{-1})$ only changes acceleration by $O(d^{-3})$. Thus all three actual equations for $d'',H',W'$ have the claimed error orders.

The positive lower bound for $g$ makes $d''$ eventually negative between fixed multiples of $-d^{-2}$. Since $d'\to0$, its eventual monotonic decrease is from positive values. Integrating against $2d'$ proves $d'\asymp d^{-1/2}$ and $d\asymp t^{2/3}$. It follows that individual acceleration and $N'=H\times N/d^2$ are integrable. This supplies both $W\to c$ and $N\to N_\infty$, removing the extra hypothesis used in the earlier blind reference. The sharper coefficient $d^{3/2}/t\to3\sqrt{Kg_\infty}$ then follows by one more radial integration.

The fixed-sign limiting torque projection gives the same necessary parallel/perpendicular alignment as the earlier independent theorem. Under the additional convergence of $H$, the integral $J(t)\sim1/\sqrt{Kg_\infty d}$ gives the displayed first corrections for $N$ and $W$. The $uW_\perp$ center term contributes only $O(d^{-1})$ after integration, so it does not change those leading corrections.

Substitution into the sharper torque equation agrees with the blind calculation. Parallel nonzero $c$ gives the positive logarithmically nonintegrable coefficient proportional to $|c|^2H_\infty$. Perpendicular nonzero $c$ gives the negative coefficient proportional to $(H_\infty\cdot[N_\infty\times c])(N_\infty\times c)$. The constants $2\sqrt K/g_\infty^{3/2}$ and the signs are correct. Convergence of $H$ forces the corresponding coefficient to vanish. In three dimensions the nonzero exceptional possibility is $c\perp N_\infty$ and $H_\infty\parallel c$; in a planar pair a nonzero limiting angular quantity therefore forces $c=0$.

Falsifiers are a missed current-source acceleration error, loss of the radial-velocity cancellation, or an unaccounted logarithmic torque term.
