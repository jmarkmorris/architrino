## Complete source exclusion and positive accumulation

The subject's old-source bound is valid uniformly over the whole past. For every fixed $\delta>0$, the strictly positive incoming integral $c_\delta=\int_{-\delta}^0(1-|v|)$ bounds the chord deficit for every source $s\le-\delta$, while the small outgoing velocity integral tends to zero. Thus all possible self roots become recent, with uniformly vanishing delay. This improves the blind reference's unnecessary use of the preparation's uniform remote margin: complete incoming strict speed alone suffices.

At each recent root the unit chord direction is the average velocity over its source interval. Continuity at the unit endpoint puts all such directions in one arbitrarily narrow cone about the endpoint velocity. Every logarithmic self term therefore has positive projection on that fixed direction and on the current velocity. The partner term remains bounded because separation excludes new partner sources and the unique old partner source stays in an ordinary compact neighborhood. Absolute convergence of the full row permits summing these inequalities. Integrating the fixed projection between positive times and passing to the continuous trace proves $\int_0^{t_0}S<\infty$. This step neither assumes bounded acceleration nor removes any root.

## The superfield set and the patched delay

On the open superfield set a self root exists by the diagonal sign and the uniformly negative older chord gap. At equation-valid times the ordinary-root convention makes it an admissible term. Its speed projection tends uniformly to infinity near the endpoint, so $b'\ge1$ there after shrinking the interval. Local absolute continuity then prevents a superfield connected component from ending at speed one before the right edge. Consequently accumulating superfield times would fill one initial interval. This topological reduction is valid even when the original crossing is infinitely flat.

On $H=\{t:|x(t)-x(0)|>t\}$, strict incoming speed gives one incoming root with $D_s>0$. Its implicit derivative yields $|\partial_t\log\rho|\le2VS$. At a positive boundary of $H$, the root tends to source zero: any negative limiting source would coexist with the zero source root, contradicting strict incoming monotonicity. The remote exclusion makes this limit argument compact.

Outside $H$, the endpoint chord is nonpositive but a superfield reception has a positive near-diagonal chord. Hence there is a source in $[0,t)$, including zero when equality holds. Its delay is at most $t$, and $|D_s|\le1+V$, giving $1/t\le(1+V)S$ almost everywhere. A degenerate endpoint source does not invalidate this step: such a reception is excluded only on the null exceptional set already required by the ordinary-root-a.e. class. No outgoing root branch or fold is followed.

Define $L=\rho$ on $H$ and $L=t$ elsewhere. It is positive and continuous, and tends to zero at the endpoint. The important regularity step is justified as follows. On a positive compact interval, $f=\log(L/t)$ is continuous, vanishes off $H$, and has integrable derivative on the open components of $H$. Integrating on a component truncated away from its endpoints and passing to the endpoint limits gives its endpoint difference. The sum over all full components is absolutely convergent and zero; at most two partial components contribute to any subinterval. Therefore $f$ satisfies the integral formula on every subinterval and is absolutely continuous. This excludes a hidden singular variation on the complement; continuity alone would not have done so.

Thus $|\partial_t\log L|\le\max(2V,1+V)S$ almost everywhere. Its integral is finite uniformly down to zero, contradicting $L\to0$. This closes precisely the wider-class gap left in the blind reference.

## Consequent exact local selection

Every admitted continuous-velocity continuation is locally at most unit speed. Equality in a self chord would require a constant unit velocity on its entire interval. Such an interval cannot meet the strict past; an entirely outgoing one produces a continuum of nonordinary self roots on a positive interval, violating the specified class. The full equation therefore reduces to the unique fixed-past partner-only local equation. Its source chart is Lipschitz in receiver position under the stated incoming regularity, and the integrability argument extends the integral equation through the endpoint.

Existence is consequently equivalent to this uniquely reconstructed partner-only curve remaining at most unit speed locally. In that case its nonzero partner acceleration excludes a unit straight segment, verifying the complete self-root exclusion and sufficiency. If it crosses above one arbitrarily near the endpoint, no continuation exists in the full stated continuous-velocity class. Finite-order odd crossings, even touch-returns and infinitely flat behavior are correctly distinguished by the reconstructed right-side sign. No actual perturbed member is asserted to reach any of these cases.

The result is confined to the unchanged logarithmic coefficient-one response, complete strict incoming history, separated ordinary partner chart, continuous endpoint velocity, positive-time local absolute continuity, and ordinary full roots almost everywhere with absolutely convergent sums. It does not address velocity jumps, singular velocity measures, positive-measure root degeneracy, or a singular partner row. It does not add a physical ceiling rule. The strict-domain and inclusive-domain-only labels remain distinct.

Falsifiers are failure of the complete old-source inequality, a nonpositive recent self projection, failure of incoming-root boundary matching, or failure of the componentwise integral formula for $f$. Each is isolated above in an operator-checkable argument. No numerical computation was used. This assessment writes only this new reference; subject and prior reference files remain frozen.
