# Independent backward reconstruction on the compact limit regime

Claim grade: derived from the accepted complete normalized compact regime and its unit-limit geometry, before reading the proposed invertible-flow subject. These are limits of the already selected family; arbitrary complete histories are not admitted.

Write $N=1-n\cdot v$ for the receiving clock factor and retain $D>0$ for the source factor. The complete compact regime has positive source margins, comparable radii and ordinary partner roots. If a sequence in this regime had $N\to0$, compactness would give $|v|=1$ and $v=n$ at a limiting interior time. The admitted unit-limit geometry instead requires $n\cdot v=0$ at every unit-speed point of a viable complete limit. This contradiction gives a uniform positive receiving factor on each such compact invariant collection. It does not prove a strict total-speed ceiling margin.

Let the exact observed future arc be $x(t)$ and $A(t)=x''(t)$. The logarithmic row gives

$$
n=-A/|A|,\qquad RD=1/|A|.
$$

For its source time $s=t-R$, the complete clock identity is $s'=N/D$. Eliminate $D$ to obtain the linear reconstruction equation

$$
R'=1-RN|A|.
$$

Thus a known future arc determines the delay throughout that arc once one delay value is fixed. The source path is then reconstructed by

$$
x(s(t))=R(t)n(t)-x(t).
$$

This is a mirror identity; the physical partner location is the negative of the right-hand source path.

Suppose two complete normalized viable trajectories coincide on $[a,b]$ with $b>39a$. The accepted source advance $s(b)\ge b/39>a$ puts both endpoint roots within their common arc. Root uniqueness therefore fixes the same $R(b)$ for both. The linear equation fixes the same $R$ on all of $[a,b]$, and the positive receiving and transmitting factors make $s$ strictly increasing. The source identity extends equality to $[s(a),s(b)]$, hence to $[s(a),b]$.

Iterate this construction. The compact regime also gives a positive lower bound on $R(t)/t$, so each left endpoint shrinks by a fixed factor below one. Equality therefore extends to every positive time. No assumption of a globally prescribed earlier past is needed, and the reconstruction does not continue through the singular normalized origin. The strict inequality $b>39a$ avoids an endpoint-only source overlap.

For an actual normalized omega-limit history set with window length $H>\log39$, forward shifts have the usual continuous dependence on the compact ordinary-root chart. Surjectivity follows by taking subsequences of actual earlier time shifts. If two complete limit histories have the same shifted history, that common arc has physical-time ratio $e^H>39$; the reconstruction proves equality of their entire positive-time trajectories, hence injectivity. A continuous bijection of a compact set to itself has a continuous inverse. This yields an invertible shift flow on that attained compact limit set, without selecting which invariant set is attained.

Falsifiers are loss of the admitted compact source margins, a viable unit point with $n\cdot v\ne0$, failure of the source advance or delay-ratio bounds, or a root ambiguity on the common arc. The result concerns exact histories and their attained limits, not numerical inversion stability. No numerical target is run.
