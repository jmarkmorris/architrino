## Constant speed fixes the normal geometry

Suppose $|v(t)|=\nu$ for all $t\ge t_0$, with $0<\nu<1$. The zero case is impossible because $h>0$. Write $J$ for rotation by $\pi/2$ in the positive plane, and retain

$$
R=t-s=|x(t)+x(s)|,\qquad n=\frac{x(t)+x(s)}R,
\qquad D=1+n\cdot v(s),\qquad v'=-\frac n{RD}.
$$

Constant speed gives $n\cdot v=0$. Since $x\cdot n>0$ and $x\times v>0$, the correct perpendicular branch is

$$
v=\nu Jn,\qquad n'=\frac{Jn}{\nu RD},\qquad s'=\frac1D.
\tag{1}
$$

These are actual equation identities, not an imposed normal acceleration or a boundary response. The direction $n$ rotates strictly positively. Its angle is denoted $\varphi(t)$.

For sufficiently late $t$, the entire interval $[s(t),t]$ lies after $t_0$. Put $\gamma=\varphi(t)-\varphi(s)>0$. The direction $n(t)$ lies strictly between the two positive-radius endpoint directions, whose positive angular separation is smaller than $\pi/2$. Thus the angle $\psi(t)=\arg v(t)-\arg x(t)$ satisfies $0<\psi(t)<\pi/2$; the same holds at the source. Therefore

$$
\gamma=\theta(t)-\theta(s)+\psi(t)-\psi(s)\in(0,\pi).
\tag{2}
$$

In receiving normal axes, $v(s)=\nu(\sin\gamma,\cos\gamma)$. The transmitter factor is $D=1+\nu\sin\gamma$. Independently differentiating the chord direction gives

$$
n'=\frac{I-nn^{\mathsf T}}R\left(v+\frac{v(s)}D\right).
$$

Equating its $Jn$ component with (1) yields the scalar identity

$$
\nu^2\bigl(1+\nu\sin\gamma+\cos\gamma\bigr)=1.
\tag{3}
$$

For fixed $\nu>0$, this nonconstant trigonometric function has finitely many zeros of the indicated level in $(0,\pi)$. The actual $\gamma(t)$ is continuous. It must therefore be one fixed value on the late connected time interval. Consequently $D>1$ is constant and $s'=\lambda=1/D\in(0,1)$.

## Every regular constant-speed tail has the exact spiral shape

There is a finite time-origin parameter $c$ such that

$$
s(t)=c+\lambda(t-c),\qquad
R(t)=(1-\lambda)(t-c).
$$

Positive range gives $t-c>0$ on the tail. By (1),

$$
\varphi(t)=\omega\log(t-c)+\varphi_0,
\qquad \omega=\frac{\lambda}{\nu(1-\lambda)}>0.
$$

Integrating $v=\nu Jn$ in complex planar coordinates gives

$$
x(t)=C+A(t-c)^{1+i\omega},\qquad
A=\frac{i\nu e^{i\varphi_0}}{1+i\omega},
\qquad |A|=\frac\nu{\sqrt{1+\omega^2}}.
\tag{4}
$$

The constant vector $C$ must vanish. Indeed, at sufficiently late times both endpoints use (4), and the exact chord equation gives

$$
2C(t-c)^{-1-i\omega}+A(1+\lambda^{1+i\omega})
=(1-\lambda)e^{i\varphi_0}.
\tag{5}
$$

Letting $t\to\infty$ fixes equality of the constant terms. Equation (5) then gives $C=0$ at every late time. Thus constant physical speed forces the self-similar shape; it was not assumed at the start of the argument.

The finite earlier generated segment and the supplied past have a common strict speed margin, as does the tail with speed $\nu<1$. The complete history therefore meets the strict speed-domain hypotheses of the [accepted spiral classification](alternatives-screen-2026-10-05-logarithmic-spiral-classification-adjudication.md). After the harmless change of time origin and planar phase, (4) is its ansatz. The classified parameters are the admitted exact $(a,\omega,\lambda)$, and $\nu$ is its admitted constant speed. No other constant-speed magnitude is available in this regular positive-orientation class.

As a known-case check, the admitted exact spiral itself satisfies (1)–(5), with its exact $D=1/\lambda$ and $R=(1-\lambda)(1+t)$. This uses its exact two balances and does not re-establish their certificate or promote a numerical approximation.

## The actual equation propagates an exact spiral tail back to release

$$

\qquad n\cdot q'=0.
$$

Hence the actual range, even while its source is not yet known to lie on the spiral, solves the scalar ordinary differential equation

$$
R'=1-s'=1-|q''(t)|R.
\tag{6}
$$

$$
x(s(t))=R(t)n(t)-q(t)=q(s(t)).

The second equality is the exact spiral chord identity. Because $s(t)$ is strictly increasing, this extends agreement down to $c+\lambda(u_0-c)$. ## A unit-speed tail is also impossible as a regular limiting trajectory

There is a related mathematical obstruction useful for later asymptotic analysis. Suppose a regular mirror trajectory has positive orientation, acute lag, ordinary single-partner response and speed exactly one on a whole tail, with its source eventually in that tail. Apply (1)–(3) with $\nu=1$. Then

$$
\sin\gamma+\cos\gamma=0,\qquad 0<\gamma<\pi,
$$

so $\gamma=3\pi/4$, $D=1+1/\sqrt2$, and $\omega=\lambda/(1-\lambda)=\sqrt2$. But the actual affine source clock and logarithmic normal angle also require

$$
\gamma=\omega\log(1/\lambda)
=\sqrt2\log(1+1/\sqrt2)<2<3\pi/4,
$$

a contradiction. The elementary inequalities are $1+1/\sqrt2<2$, $\log2<1$, $\sqrt2<2$ and $\pi>3$. Thus such an exact regular unit-speed tail does not exist.

This is an obstruction to a potential limiting trajectory, not an assertion that a speed-approaching-one member has such a limit. Passing to it would require compactness in a normalization that preserves positive separation, ordinary source denominators and the full delayed equation. The power lower bound on unnormalized radius alone does not give positive radius after division by physical time. Isolated grazing unit touches remain governed by their separate accepted local theorem and do not contradict this whole-tail obstruction.

## Validation and falsifiers

The proof is analytical. It checks the two independent expressions for $n'$, the continuity-to-constant-angle step, exact integration of the rotating normal, the chord constraint removing $C$, the linear backward range equation, and the family's tangent transversality on a fixed sampled interval. No numerical instrument or target trajectory is used; no old source or evidence is edited, and no owned computation is active.

Falsifiers are a sign error in $v=\nu Jn$ or the source normal-angle lag; a continuous nonconstant solution of the fixed finite-level equation (3); a nonzero $C$ satisfying (5) on a tail; failure of uniqueness for the displayed finite-interval linear range equation; a reconstructed source segment not actually covered by its increasing affine clock; or the admitted modal tangent lying in the two spiral tangents on $I$. A merely asymptotic approach, rather than exact eventual constancy, lies outside the no-capture conclusion. Independent assessment is required before integration.
