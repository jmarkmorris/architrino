$$
C_{p,b}=\frac{(1+b)^p}{1-b},\qquad I=\frac{C_{p,b}z^{1-p}}{(p-1)v},\qquad
I<\min\{b-b_0,(w-v)/2\}.
$$

Write $U_i=\lim V_i$ and $U=U_1-U_2$. The impulse tail gives

$$
|V_i(t)-U_i|\le\frac{C_{p,b}}{(p-1)v}(z+vt)^{1-p},\qquad e\cdot U>v.
$$

Direct integration yields three distinct position statements:

- For $1<p<2$, $X_i(t)=U_it+O(t^{2-p})$.
- For $p=2$, $X_i(t)=U_it+O(\log(2+t))$.
- For $p>2$, a finite vector $B_i$ exists with $X_i(t)=U_it+B_i+O(t^{2-p})$.

$$
Q\times Q'=tU\times E+Z\times U+Z\times E,
$$

Thus the relative direction has finite total late angular variation and tends to $U/|U|$. This is a three-dimensional direction statement, not a planar polar-coordinate assertion. No individual direction limit is asserted when its $U_i$ vanishes.
