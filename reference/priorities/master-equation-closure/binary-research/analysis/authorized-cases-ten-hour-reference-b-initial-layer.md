## An independent row-derivative control

For a fixed receiver $e_1$, radial source displacement $d$, radial source velocity $w$ and radial source acceleration $a$, all measured as variations at the static unit-radius geometry, the exact selected row has radial component

$$
-\frac4{(2+d)^2(1+\epsilon w)^2}
+\frac{4\epsilon^2a}{(2+d)(1+\epsilon w)^3}.
$$

Its differential at $d=w=a=0$ is $d+2\epsilon w+2\epsilon^2a$. This is a closed-form known control for the three independent source channels. The actual central tangential velocity changes these leading radial coefficients only at higher order. Keeping just source acceleration would miss the first two contributions.

Thus the first acceleration defect, evaluated at $z=u-2<0$, is

$$
y''(\epsilon u)-F_{16}(y(\epsilon u),y'(\epsilon u))
=\epsilon^6 K(u)e_1+O(\epsilon^7),
$$

$$
K(u)=\frac{(u-2)^4[(u-2)^2+12(u-2)+60]}{720}
\quad(0\le u\le2),\qquad K(u)=0\quad(u\ge2). \tag{1}
$$

The remainder here is uniform on a fixed bounded $u$ interval, not a global future bound. The nominal finite-autonomous defect of order seventeen is smaller. At the root, the position defect is $O(\epsilon^6)$, hence its induced clock shift is $O(\epsilon^7)$. The bounded central velocity then changes sampled position by $O(\epsilon^7)$, sampled velocity by $O(\epsilon^7)$ and sampled acceleration by $O(\epsilon^7)$; none changes (1). This explicitly retains source transport.

## Compatibility feeds back two orders later

Let $\Delta J_m=J_m-Y^{(m)}(0)$. Differentiating (1) at the release side through three orders predicts and is consistent with the compatibility equations:

$$
\Delta J_2=\frac89\epsilon^6e_1+O(\epsilon^7),\quad
\Delta J_3=-\frac85\epsilon^5e_1+O(\epsilon^6),\quad
\Delta J_4=2\epsilon^4e_1+O(\epsilon^5),\quad
\Delta J_5=-\frac43\epsilon^3e_1+O(\epsilon^4). \tag{2}
$$

These differing powers cannot be replaced by a common jet error of order six. Yet each contributes only $O(\epsilon^8)$ to polynomial position at $t=O(\epsilon)$, $O(\epsilon^7)$ to velocity and $O(\epsilon^6)$ to acceleration. Through the three row channels they change the acceleration defect only at order eight. The compatible finite-jet system is therefore a contraction perturbation at this leading layer order: its feedback does not alter (1). This is also why the genuine sixth-derivative seam must remain visible instead of being removed by formal smoothness.

Root displacement of the transition has no effect on the first coefficient.

## Signed position and velocity effect

With the common release values, define $I_0(u)=\int_0^uK(v)\,dv$ and $I_1(u)=\int_0^u(u-v)K(v)\,dv$. Then

$$
y'(\epsilon u)-Y'(\epsilon u)=\epsilon^7I_0(u)e_1+O(\epsilon^8),\qquad
y(\epsilon u)-Y(\epsilon u)=\epsilon^8I_1(u)e_1+O(\epsilon^9).
$$

The exact elementary moments are $\int_0^2K=8/21$ and $\int_0^2vK=2/15$. Consequently for fixed $u\ge2$,

$$
I_0(u)=\frac8{21},\qquad I_1(u)=\frac8{21}u-\frac2{15}.
$$

In particular $I_1(2)=22/35$. The leading layer kick is radially outward. This is a preparation effect, not a sign claim about the eventual physical account or final passage.
