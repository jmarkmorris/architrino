# Blind reference: stationary speed and unit-speed subsequences

**Derived reference, frozen before opening the new extrema subject.** Retain the same logarithmic mirror row, positive angular quantity and acute source lag. Write $\nu=|v|>0$, $b=n\cdot v_s$, $c=Jn\cdot v_s$, $D=1+b$, and $R=t-s$. At any stationary speed, $n\cdot v=0$ and positive orientation gives $v=\nu Jn$. The exact clock has $s'=1/D$, and chord differentiation gives $Jn\cdot n'=(\nu+c/D)/R$. Differentiating the speed identity once more, only at the stationary point, yields

$$
\nu''=\frac{1-\nu^2D-\nu c}{\nu R^2D^2}. \tag{1}
$$

Thus an interior local maximum must satisfy

$$
\nu^2(1+b)+\nu c\ge1. \tag{2}
$$

The second derivative may vanish, so this is necessary, not sufficient. A strictly positive value of the left side minus one gives a strict local maximum. At a unit stationary point the numerator becomes $-(n+v)\cdot v_s$, consistently recovering the earlier grazing identity.

Cauchy's inequality gives $\nu^2b+\nu c\le\nu\sqrt{1+\nu^2}|v_s|$. In particular a subfield local maximum obeys

$$
|v_s|\ge\frac{1-\nu^2}{\nu\sqrt{1+\nu^2}},\qquad
\nu>\frac1{\sqrt3}. \tag{3}
$$

The strict inequality uses the strict source speed bound one. A stationary maximum below or at $1/\sqrt3$ is therefore impossible in this class, irrespective of the detailed old source direction. This is a local geometric restriction, not an assertion that every value above it occurs.

## A lower bound on the future upper envelope

Let $M=\limsup_{t\to\infty}|v(t)|$ on an all-future branch. If speed converges, the accepted rigidity theorem gives $M=\nu_*$. Otherwise choose interior local maxima tending to $M$ between later crossings of a fixed lower speed. The source times tend to infinity by the accepted clock result, so their source speeds have upper limit at most $M$. Taking upper limits in (2) gives

$$
1\le M^2+M^2\sqrt{1+M^2}.
$$

Let $z_*$ be the unique positive root of $z^3+2z-1=0$; monotonicity and squaring the nonnegative sides show that the displayed condition is $M\ge\sqrt{z_*}$. The convergent branch also satisfies it because the exact spiral obeys equality in its stationary-speed row before the Cauchy relaxation. Therefore every all-future member has this positive lower bound on its limiting upper speed envelope. This does not bound its lower envelope or prove oscillation.

## If the upper envelope is one

If $M=1$, the speed cannot converge, by the accepted scalar-limit theorem. Hence its lower envelope is strictly less than one. There are arbitrarily late interior maxima approaching one, separated by speed values bounded away from one; their ordinary source times also tend to infinity. At each such maximum (2) is an exact necessary source-projection condition. No actual occurrence of these maxima, no uniform size of their time separation, and no finite unit arrival are asserted. The all-future alternative remains a hypothesis.

The exact base spiral is the analytical known stationary-speed case: (1) vanishes and (2) is an equality in the original row. Falsifiers are a sign error in the normal derivative, a local maximum violating the second-derivative condition, or loss of generated-source coverage in the envelope passage. No computation or new physical preparation was introduced.
