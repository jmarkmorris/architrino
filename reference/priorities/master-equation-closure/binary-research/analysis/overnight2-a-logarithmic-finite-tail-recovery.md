## Recovering the source clock from a receiving trajectory

In the registered coefficient-one inverse-distance case, an opposite-polarity ordinary partner row is

$$
A_i(T)=-\frac{N_i(T)}{R_i(T)D_i(T)},
\qquad R_i=T-s_i>0,
\qquad D_i=1-N_i\cdot V_j(s_i)>0,
$$
$$
X_i(T)-X_j(s_i)=R_iN_i,
\qquad c_f=1.
\tag{1}
$$

Complete strict-speed chords give the unique partner root and exclude every positive-delay self root; no admitted source is suppressed. Because separation is positive and the ordinary row is finite, $a_i:=|A_i|>0$ and $N_i=-A_i/a_i$. Both quantities are therefore determined by the receiving position function and its actual second derivative.

Differentiate the causal distance using $s_i'=1-R_i'$. This uses only continuously differentiable positions and the ordinary-root implicit derivative:

$$
R_i'=N_i\cdot[V_i-V_j(s_i)(1-R_i')].
$$

Hence $D_i(1-R_i')=1-N_i\cdot V_i$. Substituting the acceleration magnitude from (1) gives the exact scalar equation

$$
\boxed{R_i'=1-a_i(T)[1-N_i(T)\cdot V_i(T)]R_i.}
\tag{2}
$$

Its coefficient is a known continuous function of the receiving trajectory on any regular generated interval. No source acceleration or derivative of acceleration is discarded: neither is required for this particular identity. Locally Lipschitz source velocity and the allowed release seam are sufficient. Given one value of $R_i$ within that interval, uniqueness of the scalar linear equation determines it in both time directions within the interval. After recovering the clock, the sampled partner position is exactly

$$
X_j(s_i(T))=X_i(T)-R_i(T)N_i(T).
\tag{3}
$$

Equations (2)–(3) reconstruct only sampled source values. They make no claim to recover remote preparation segments which no future root visits.

As a separately labeled canonical control, retaining its inverse-square response instead gives $a_i=K/(R_i^2D_i)$ and $R_i'=1-(a_i/K)[1-N_i\cdot V_i]R_i^2$. Local uniqueness still holds for positive finite $R_i$. This control is not transferred into the logarithmic coefficient-one formula; it checks where the distance power enters. In both cases the ordinary source-clock derivative is strictly positive on the strict-speed domain.

## Exact-tail equality propagates backwards over a positive delay

Suppose an actual logarithmic pair and the exact original mirror spiral, with any one fixed common spatial rotation allowed by the original symmetry, coincide for every $T\ge T_*\ge0$. Both actual labeled positions coincide; equality of only separation, speed or a plotted curve is insufficient. The exact spiral has constant member speed strictly below one. At sufficiently late reception time, the partner source lies after $T_*$: the causal gap at fixed source time $T_*$ grows to positive infinity because receiver displacement grows at speed below one. Complete root monotonicity then puts the unique root in the common future.

At such a late time both source roots and ranges agree. On the common receiving interval $[T_*,\infty)$, the functions $a_i,N_i,V_i$ in (2) agree, so uniqueness propagates equality of $R_i$ backwards to $T_*$. Equations (3) then propagate equality of partner positions to every source time swept by the common clock. For the original spiral,

$$
1+s_i(T)=\lambda(1+T),\qquad 0<\lambda<1,
\tag{4}
$$

with the exact admitted value of $\lambda$. Both mirror clocks have this ratio; a fixed rotation changes neither one. Thus equality of the two actual positions extends from $[T_*,\infty)$ to $[\lambda(1+T_*)-1,\infty)$ wherever that interval is a generated interval or sampled supplied history.

If the new endpoint is still positive, repeat the argument. After $n$ repetitions its value is

$$
T_n=\lambda^n(1+T_*)-1.
$$

For some finite $n$, $T_n\le0$. All uses of the actual evolution equation have receiving time at least zero; the argument stops when the whole generated future has been recovered. It does not impose the spiral equation on the actual earlier held tail or extend its analytic formula through its singular origin. Therefore eventual exact equality implies equality of the entire generated future $T\ge0$ with that rotated original spiral.

The known-case control is the unperturbed compatible spiral preparation: it can have a distinct inaccessible remote held tail and still generate the exact spiral. The conclusion is correctly equality of the generated future and the sampled prefix, not equality of the complete remote histories. A theorem asserting full-past injectivity would fail that control.

## Consequence for the admitted locally departing family

The existing nonlinear departure and exit-circle results supply the actual locally departing members under their frozen sufficiently small positive-amplitude hypotheses. Such a member cannot have its complete generated future equal to a fixed rotation of the original exact spiral: that would contradict its established departure from the corresponding spiral orbit. Applying the preceding recovery result therefore excludes eventual exact merging into that orbit at finite time for those same members.

The result does not exclude an asymptotic return, a homoclinic connection in normalized history space, another outward regime, a speed boundary or a different nonconvergent future. It neither proves that an actual member returns nor strengthens the conditional scalar-speed-to-fixed-phase theorem into an unconditional fate. It also does not classify arbitrary time-shifted, rescaled or different exact balances; only the original admitted spiral orbit is used here.

The argument requires an actual ordinary continuation throughout the compared generated interval. It cannot restart a solution after an excluded equality, collision or nonordinary source event. An inclusive domain label supplies no such restart. Fixed source coefficients, complete roots and the strict speed domain remain essential.

Falsifiers are an incorrect sign or distance power in (2), failure of source-clock monotonicity, a late root outside the asserted common tail despite its strict speed bound, an attempt to recover an inaccessible old past, or a local-departure claim that did not in fact exclude equality with the fixed spiral orbit. Independent review must check the exact scope of that last premise before accepting the family consequence. No new numerical instrument, target evolution or physical preparation is introduced; this is a proposed mathematical obstruction to one precise later-fate alternative.
