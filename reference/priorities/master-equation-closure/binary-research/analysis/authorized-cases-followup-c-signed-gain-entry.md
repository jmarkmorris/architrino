## 2. Exact gain identity and analytical controls

Set $E=|v|^2=(1+y^2)/\chi^2$. This is squared speed; no mass, mechanical energy conservation or external physical law is assumed. Resolve $n=(Ae-BJe)/d$, where $e=x/r$ and $J$ is the positive planar quarter turn. Since $v=pe+zJe$ and $dT/d\theta=r/z$, direct differentiation of (1) gives

$$
\frac{dE}{dT}=\frac{2(Bz-Ap)}{r d^2D},
\qquad
\boxed{\frac{dE}{d\theta}=W(\theta):=\frac{2(B-Ay)}{d^2D}.}
\tag{4}
$$

The current factor $z$ cancels exactly in the angle derivative. Current and delayed velocities still enter the actual clock, $y$ and $D$; (4) is not a velocity-independent replacement law. Integrating along any regular actual angular interval gives

$$
E(\theta_b)-E(\theta_a)=\int_{\theta_a}^{\theta_b}W(\theta)\,d\theta.
\tag{5}
$$

Two analytical controls precede use of this identity. First, the exact admitted spiral has $y_*=1/\omega$, $\kappa=\lambda$ and $D=1/\lambda$. Its exact balances give $\lambda\sin\delta_*=(1+\lambda\cos\delta_*)/\omega$, so $B-Ay_*=0$ and (4) gives its known constant speed. This uses the exact balance zero, not the printed decimal parameters. Second, at any regular kinematic radial turning state $p=0$, direct evaluation of $2v\cdot v'$ in (1) is strictly positive because $z>0$ and $B>0$. Formula (4) gives the same positive value after the time change. This is a differential sign control, not a claim that such a state is attained by the selected family. Both controls are exact hand derivations; no instrument was built or run.

The sign boundary is explicit:

$$
W>0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad y<\frac B A.
\tag{6}
$$

Because $B/A>0$, the interval $0<y<B/A$ has positive radial velocity and increasing speed. Thus a finite event test derived from (5) need not first prove radial reversal. Conversely positive angular momentum or positive tangential acceleration alone says nothing about the sign of $W$.

## 3. Finite survival-conditional entry theorem

Fix an admitted actual member, its actual $T_a$, its actual complete history there and $\theta_a=\theta(T_a)$. Suppose a proved complete-history tube has the following meaning: for as long as this member remains strict with $\theta\in[\theta_a,\theta_a+L]$, its entire sampled history belongs to the tube and retains the original ordinary root census. The tube proof must establish that membership from this actual input. A prescribed path, an arbitrary point in a chart, or a cluster history with no attained-neighborhood transfer does not satisfy this premise.

Suppose that tube provides finite constants $C>0$, $Y\ge0$, a nonnegative integrable function $w_-$, and an actual initial upper bound $E_a^+<1$ such that

$$
\chi\le C,\qquad y\le Y,\qquad W(\theta_a+u)\ge w_-(u),
\qquad E(\theta_a)\le E_a^+,
\tag{7}
$$

and an actual initial lower bound $E_a^-$ such that

$$
\int_0^L w_-(u)\,du>1-E_a^-.
\tag{8}
$$

The lower bound in (8), rather than $E_a^+$, is essential: the largest possible speed defect must be covered. One may instead use the exact initial value throughout. Then the actual strict branch has a finite unit-speed endpoint no later than

$$
T_a+r(T_a)C\int_0^L e^{Yu}\,du.
\tag{9}
$$

For $Y=0$ the integral is $L$; for $Y>0$ it is $(e^{YL}-1)/Y$. An upper enclosure of $r(T_a)$ may replace its exact value.

**Proof.** If the actual member reaches a finite strict endpoint before the indicated angular interval is completed, the accepted finite-endpoint theorem already identifies unit limiting speed and positive regular geometry. Otherwise the global alternative ensures that an infinite branch has unbounded angle. On any surviving part of the specified interval, $dr/d\theta=ry$ and (7) give $r(\theta_a+u)\le r(T_a)e^{Yu}$; the exact relation $dT/d\theta=r\chi$ yields (9). Thus the angular endpoint cannot be postponed to infinite physical time under these tube bounds. Survival through the entire interval would give, from (5) and (8), $E(\theta_a+L)>1$, contradicting strict survival. An earlier finite endpoint has the same time bound by integration over the shorter interval. The existing actual-family continuation theorem excludes another finite strict obstruction. This proves the statement.

The nonnegative lower envelope is a convenient sufficient form, not a necessary restriction on the exact identity. A signed integrable lower envelope with the same total bound also works. An event conclusion under this theorem does not by itself classify transverse versus grazing arrival or inclusive continuation. A positive lower bound for $W$ persisting to a finite endpoint with positive angle rate would separately give a positive one-sided speed derivative; no such endpoint bound is asserted without its own tube coverage.

A particularly transparent sufficient tube condition is

$$
B-Ay\ge q>0,\qquad \kappa\le K.
\tag{10}
$$

The strict source bound gives $D<2$, while $d\le1+\kappa\le1+K$. Consequently

$$
W\ge\frac{q}{(1+K)^2},\qquad
\frac{qL}{(1+K)^2}>1-E_a^-

$$

is sufficient. These inequalities may hold with $p>0$ throughout the surviving interval. No actual such interval is supplied here. Near the exact spiral, $B-Ay=0$ at the base; (10) therefore cannot be inferred merely by naming a small chart around it.

## 4. Necessary cancellation on every infinite actual branch

On an actual infinite branch, angle tends to infinity. From (5) and $0<E<1$, every later angular interval obeys the exact bounds

$$
-E(\theta_a)<\int_{\theta_a}^{\theta_b}W\,d\theta<1-E(\theta_a).
\tag{12}
$$

In particular, for each fixed $\theta_a$,

$$
\lim_{L\to\infty}\frac1L\int_{\theta_a}^{\theta_a+L}W\,d\theta=0.
\tag{13}
$$

This is an exact necessary cancellation, not pointwise convergence of $W$ and not convergence to the spiral. For fixed angular sections $\theta_j=\theta_a+j\ell$, define $I_j=\int_{\theta_j}^{\theta_{j+1}}W\,d\theta$. Every consecutive partial sum telescopes to a squared-speed difference and lies in $(-1,1)$. Thus a proof that every sufficiently late attained return has $I_j\ge\mu>0$, or a cumulative lower bound exceeding the actual remaining defect, would select finite arrival. Compactness of the attained history set provides continuity and boundedness of these finite-interval functionals where the root chart is regular; it does not provide this sign.

The accepted branch-dependent compact regime bounds $\kappa$ from above and $D$ away from zero, and bounds $y,\chi$ on complete sampled arcs. These protect the functional (4). They still permit cancellation between intervals with $y<B/A$ and intervals with $y>B/A$. No such orbit is constructed or declared attained by these possibilities.

$$

$$
