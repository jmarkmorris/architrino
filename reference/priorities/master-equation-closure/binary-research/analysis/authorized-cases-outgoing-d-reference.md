## Exact quantities and source coverage

$$
J=\frac{|U|^2}{2}-2g\chi-u\chi.
$$

Each actual receiver has its own partner root and row $A_i=-K n_i/(R_i^2D_i)$, $D_i=1-n_i\cdot V_j(t-R_i)$. Complete remote strict speed $b_0<1$ gives the unique partner roots and excludes positive-delay self roots. It is not replaced by the smaller generated speed ceiling.

At an entry $T>3d(T)$, a generated speed bound $\beta\le1/20$ places both partner roots and their sampled sources' own roots in generated history, by the [source-window companion](authorized-cases-followup-d-bootstrap-source-time-bound.md):

s_{\mathrm{nested}}\ge t-\frac{2d}{(1-\beta)^2}>0.
$$

This remains true because $(t-3d)'\ge1-6\beta>0$. The homotopy used in the prior actual-row estimates retains the same coverage. These facts make the following criteria finite-state **and generated-history** tests; instantaneous coordinates alone are insufficient.

## A strict radial escape test, independent of angular floor

Let $d_0=d(T)$, $u_0=u(T)>0$, and

$$
C_\beta=\frac{K(1+\beta)^2}{1-\beta},\qquad
\gamma^2=u_0^2-\frac{4C_\beta}{d_0}>0.
$$

Suppose the generated prefix is strictly below $\beta$, the complete supplied past is uniformly strictly subfield, $T>3d_0$, and the current member-speed bound $v_0$ satisfies

$$
v_0+\frac{C_\beta}{\gamma d_0}<\beta.
$$

Then the original [outgoing criterion](authorized-cases-ten-hour-d-outgoing-neighborhood-candidate.md) localizes to the generated windows above; its stronger whole-remote-history small-speed hypothesis is unnecessary in this setting. Indeed $|A_i|\le C_\beta/d^2$ and

$$
d''=\frac{|U|^2-u^2}{d}+N\cdot(A_+-A_-)
\ge-\frac{2C_\beta}{d^2}.
$$

Until a hypothetical first loss of $u>0$, integration with $2u$ gives

$$
u^2\ge u_0^2-4C_\beta\left(\frac1{d_0}-\frac1d\right)>\gamma^2.
$$

Thus $d\ge d_0+\gamma(t-T)$ and $\int_T^\infty |A_i|\,dt\le C_\beta/(\gamma d_0)$. The strict speed margin closes the bootstrap and ordinary-root continuation. Both member velocities converge and the limiting relative velocity has magnitude at least $\gamma$. Separation is asymptotically linear. No angular-floor condition is used. This is a sufficient criterion, not a necessary characterization of escape.

## A weaker all-future dispersal transfer at an outward nonnegative account

There is a useful transfer even if the strict radial test fails. Set $\beta=1/20$ and assume

$$
J(T)\ge0,\quad u(T)\ge0,\quad \chi_0=K/d_0\le10^{-4},\quad T>3d_0,
$$

with the same generated-prefix and remote strict-speed qualifications. It suffices that

$$
v_0+1.161\pi\sqrt{\frac{K}{3.6d_0}}<\frac1{20}.
\tag{1}
$$

The accepted [positive-account estimates](authorized-cases-ten-hour-d-primary-positive-account-continuation.md) are dimension independent. Their $H$ floor was used to obtain a separation floor and hence small $\chi$, not in the actual-row or scalar boundary calculation itself. Here those bounds follow from outward monotonicity. On a bootstrap region $d\ge d_0$, $J\ge0$, speeds below $\beta$, the boundary $J=0$ has $\Lambda=|U_\perp|^2d/K\le4.2$ and $J'>K^2/(2d^3)>0$. Also

$$
I=du,\qquad I'>\frac{1.8K}{d}.
\tag{2}
$$

The latter follows from the exact virial row

$$
I'=2J+\frac Kd(2g+u)+dQ_r+d e_{\mathrm{rel},r},
$$

where $Q_r\ge0$ and $|e_{\mathrm{rel}}|\le10K^2/d^3$. Since $I(T)\ge0$, (2) makes $I>0$ subsequently, so $d$ increases and $\chi$ decreases. This closes the separation/account part without $H$.

Along that single outward segment,

$$
\frac{d(I^2)}{dd}=2d I'\ge3.6K,
\qquad
\int_T^\infty\frac K{d^2}\,dt
\le\int_{d_0}^\infty\frac{K\,dd}{d\sqrt{3.6K(d-d_0)}}
=\pi\sqrt{\frac{K}{3.6d_0}}.
$$

The single-segment factor is one, rather than the factor two needed for an initially inward passage. With $|A_i|<1.161K/d^2$, (1) prevents a speed exit. Positive separation, strict roots and finite-interval regularity close all-future continuation. A bounded $d$ would contradict (2) and $d'=I/d$, so $d\to\infty$. The acceleration integral is finite and both velocities converge.

**Exact limitation:** these inequalities alone do not prove that the limiting relative velocity is nonzero. A zero-relative-speed dispersal remains compatible with this certificate. Nor does boundary invariance prove $J(t)\ge J(T)>0$ for all later times. A claim of a linear outgoing tail needs the strict radial test above or another separately proved positive-relative-speed argument.

These deliberately loose rational comparisons follow already from $\pi<4$, $1.161<2$ and $3.6>1$. Thus the speed margin needed for the nonnegative-account transfer is ample. This arithmetic is analytical, not a new numerical target.

The missing datum is $J(T_D)$. The proven alternatives are:

1. If $J(T_D)\ge0$, the second theorem gives actual all-future separation and convergent member velocities without maintaining the angular floor. Strictly positive relative terminal speed is an additional question.
2. If $J(T_D)<0$, the finite certificate alone has not supplied escape. At first attainment it only gives

$$
-2.1\frac KD\le J(T_D)<0,\qquad |U(T_D)|^2<4.2\frac KD.
$$

In particular the fixed-$\beta=.05$ radial criterion above cannot pass in this negative-account alternative, since $4C_\beta/K>4.2$. The tiny scale of the deficit is not a sign determination.

An algebraic control illustrates the information gap: take $W=0$, $u=0$ and $|U_\perp|=h/D$ with $h>H_*$ fixed. Then $J=h^2/(2D^2)-2K/D<0$ for sufficiently large $D$, while separation, angular floor and small speed inequalities all hold. This is only a control on the finite inequalities, not a new physical preparation, a realizable nominal event, or a counterexample to dispersal.

A future finite witness may establish the strict radial test, or a nonnegative-account outward entry. Neither an increased first-attainment distance nor its finite time upper bound alone supplies that witness.
