# Independent continuation on a maintained angular-floor positive-account region

Claim grade: derived conditional estimate, frozen before the positive-account subject. Use the unchanged canonical pair with complete speed at most $\beta=1/20$, $\chi=K/d\le10^{-4}$ and generated source windows. The exact current-affine decomposition and its ordinary clocks remain comparison identities for the actual equation.

At this larger speed bound the source acceleration constant is

$$
A_0=\frac{(1+\beta)^2(1-\beta)}{(1-3\beta)^2}<1.45.
$$

The clock/chord error is less than $0.85K$, the source-velocity error is less than $1.53\chi$, and the entire chord comparison segment has range above $0.95d$. The row derivative constants can be taken as $2.6K/d^3$ and $1.3K/d^2$. Thus each actual-to-affine acceleration error is below $4.3K^2/d^3$, the relative error below $8.6K^2/d^3$ and midpoint error below $4.3K^2/d^3$.

At an account-zero boundary, its algebra gives $|U|^2\le4.2\chi$ and hence transverse ratio $\Lambda\le4.2$. Substitute these and the larger delay constants into the exact account derivative. Its leading coefficient exceeds 1.89; the affine quadratic and midpoint terms cost less than 0.322, and the actual relative/midpoint errors cost less than 0.862. Thus $\mathcal J'>0.70K^2/d^3>0$ at the boundary. A solution entering $\mathcal J\ge0$ cannot cross back while the other region hypotheses hold. A sign theorem throughout the positive region is not required.

Let $I=dd'$. Direct substitution of the account definition and the exact radial relative row gives

$$
I'=2\mathcal J+\frac Kd(2g+d')+dQ_r+d(e_{\rm rel})_r.
$$

Here $Q_r\ge0$, $2g+d'>1.89$ and $d|e_{\rm rel}|\le8.6\chi K/d$. Consequently $I'>1.8K/d$ on the nonnegative-account interval. The radial motion has at most one turn, from inward to outward.

On any monotone radial segment,

$$
\frac{d(I^2)}{dd}=2dI'\ge3.6K.
$$

For an inward partial segment, use its current endpoint radius as the integration base; this does not assume a future turn. For an outward segment use its initial minimum radius. The nonnegative base value of $I^2$ gives

$$
\int\frac K{d^2}\,dt
\le\pi\sqrt{\frac K{3.6d_{\rm base}}}
$$

on each such segment, by the exact integral of $1/[d\sqrt{d-d_{\rm base}}]$. With at most two segments and $d\ge d_{\min}$, the sum is at most twice this bound with $d_{\min}$.

Assume the actual angular magnitude remains above $H_*=2\epsilon R_0$, with $K=4\epsilon^2R_0$. The speed bound gives $d\ge H_*/(2\beta)=20\epsilon R_0$, so $K/d\le\epsilon/5<10^{-4}$ for $\epsilon\le1/2900$. The exact per-member row satisfies $|A_i|\le[(1+\beta)^2/(1-\beta)]K/d^2<1.17K/d^2$. Therefore its total possible velocity change is less than

$$
2\pi(1.17)\sqrt{\epsilon/18}<0.033.
$$

Entry speed below 0.0095 then stays below 0.0425, strictly inside the provisional 0.05 bound. This closes speed and separation continuation as long as the angular floor persists. The generated-window condition also persists since $(t-3d)'\ge0.7$.

This is dimension-independent for the maintained angular magnitude. Combining it with the planar negative-account stage requires a common angular floor strong enough for both stages; $H_*=2\epsilon R_0$ exceeds $2K$ at the selected small speed. That stronger maintained floor is an explicit remaining hypothesis. The argument does not itself prove that the nominal member avoids angular-floor loss, identify historical build membership or settle the full three-dimensional neighborhood.
