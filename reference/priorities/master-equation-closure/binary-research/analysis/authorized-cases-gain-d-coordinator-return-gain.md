## The four geometric contributions

The [signed-memory subject](authorized-cases-two-hour-d-subject-signed-memory.md), at its independently assessed scope, gives

$$
P'=-G-\frac{u}{2+u}e_r,
\qquad
G=G_W+G_T+G_Q+G_\chi,
$$

$$
G_W=\frac{2ku(1-g)}{2+u},\quad
G_T=\frac{uH^2}{d^3(2+u)},\quad
G_Q=\frac{uQ_r}{2+u},\quad
G_\chi=3ku\chi.
\tag{1}
$$

Here $Q_r\ge0$ is the actual current-state affine comparison remainder; $e_r$ is the separately retained actual-minus-affine source defect. The four terms in (1) are algebraic components of one canonical functional decomposition, not additional interactions.

First, one contribution is exactly path-independent, including returns:

$$
\int_a^b G_\chi\,dt
=\frac32\{\chi(a)^2-\chi(b)^2\}.
\tag{2}
$$

This follows immediately from $\chi'=-ku$ and has no sign requirement on $u$. Over the whole first-$D$ to first-$L=4D$ passage it equals $45\chi_D^2/32$. ## A simple outward-and-return loop

Assume an actual interval consists of a strictly outward branch from $r_a$ to $r_b>r_a$, followed by a strictly inward branch back to $r_a$, with isolated turning points allowed at the endpoints. For almost every interior radius $r$, denote its earlier outward and later inward data by $(u_o(r),H_o(r))$ and $(u_i(r),H_i(r))$. The strict negative-stage angular increase gives

$$
H_i(r)>H_o(r)>0,\qquad
u_o(r)>0>u_i(r),\qquad 2+u_i(r)>0.
$$

Consequently the exact transverse contribution on that loop is

$$
\int_{\rm loop}G_T\,dt
=\int_{r_a}^{r_b}\frac1{r^3}
\left\{\frac{H_o(r)^2}{2+u_o(r)}
-\frac{H_i(r)^2}{2+u_i(r)}\right\}dr<0.
\tag{3}
$$

Both comparisons have the same sign: the later numerator is larger and its denominator is smaller. The change of variables is valid first on compact subintervals of strict monotonicity and then by the integrable endpoint limit. The accepted finite receiving interval, positive separation and speed bounds make the original integrands bounded. Formula (3) requires this simple pairing; no assertion is made that an arbitrary history has only finitely many turning points or has already been decomposed into such loops.

The reverse order, inward followed by outward, has no sign determined by angular increase alone. Its later outward numerator is larger but so is its denominator. This is the exact distinction between knowing that $H$ grows and knowing how radial phase and angular magnitude correlate.

## Analytical controls and limitations

The variable-change rule was checked algebraically on a prescribed two-leg radius trace before its actual-history application. With constant positive $H$ and constant branch speeds $u_o=s$, $u_i=-s$, $0<s<2$, the bracket in (3) is

$$
H^2\left(\frac1{2+s}-\frac1{2-s}\right)
=-\frac{2sH^2}{4-s^2}<0.
\tag{4}
$$

Direct integration of the original time expression $G_T=uH^2/[d^3(2+u)]$ on the two linear-radius legs gives the same expression times $\int_{r_a}^{r_b}r^{-3}dr$. Likewise direct evaluation of (2) gives zero when the initial and final radius agree. These are elementary change-of-variable controls, not coupled solutions or histories admitted for scientific target use. Increasing $H$ between the two legs only strengthens the comparison in (3).

The sign of the *whole* loop gain does not follow from (3): $G_W$ and $G_Q$ depend on different actual midpoint and transverse data at the two visits. Although their coefficients have nonnegative outward signs, neither has been proved to preserve an ordering between visits. The delayed defect remains included in the assessed whole-passage allowance $9\times10^{-59}$. No combination of (2) and (3) selects the sign of $P(T_L)$.

The precise useful conclusion is that a proposed proof which sums favorable outward transverse gain and invokes angular growth to disregard inward returns is invalid. It needs a signed return estimate or an independently proved monotone final segment with a sufficiently strong attained entry. Falsifiers are failure of the inherited actual $H'>0$ domain, a missing $u$ or denominator in (1), or an actual simple loop satisfying the stated inequalities whose transverse integral is nonnegative.
