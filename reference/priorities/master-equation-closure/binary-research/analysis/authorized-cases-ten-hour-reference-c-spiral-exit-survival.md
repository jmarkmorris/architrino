# Independent attained-exit and long-survival reduction

Claim grade: derived compactness reduction within the accepted compatible family and its nonlinear cone construction. This reference is frozen before reading the new attained-exit and survival subjects. It does not construct a new physical history or choose an amplitude.

The accepted cone construction uses a fixed small exit threshold and a fixed time map. At first exit the unstable coordinate lies between that threshold and a fixed multiple of it, while the stable coordinate remains in the cone. All intermediate physical segments stay in one ordinary-root chart with strict speed and separation margins. The exit's distance from the local symmetry orbit has a fixed positive lower bound.

The bounded chart histories have bounded positions, velocities and accelerations. On a generated segment, differentiating the exact logarithmic row uses only bounded source acceleration and ordinary clock denominators; it therefore bounds the receiving jerk uniformly. After a full history width, this gives equicontinuity of acceleration. Arzela–Ascoli makes the exit histories precompact in $C^2$ on each fixed retained window. Their cluster set is attained by sequences of the original members; closing an arbitrary ball of formal histories would not suffice. The strict margins and fixed positive symmetry distance pass to these exit limits.

For every fixed number $j$ of steps before exit, the cone's unstable lower expansion gives a deviation at most a fixed constant times $\gamma^{-j}$, with $\gamma>1$. Exit times tend to infinity as the original amplitude tends to zero. Diagonal extraction therefore supplies a complete backward history through each attained exit cluster, tending to the base spiral in similarity coordinates. This history is nontrivial because its exit distance is retained. It need not have a complete viable future.

A finite strict prefix from such a limit transfers to sufficiently late approximating exit histories by ordinary-root continuous dependence. Any open finite-event condition entered after that prefix therefore transfers as well. In particular the admitted inward-budget condition is an open finite-unit event. The argument does not transfer an event reached only after an unproved singular or nonstrict prefix.

Now take original amplitudes tending to zero whose post-exit strict lifetimes tend to infinity in similarity time; the individual members need not be infinite. On any fixed positive scaled-time window, all sufficiently late members survive past its endpoint by a fixed multiplicative factor. The explicit inward criterion then excludes a near-minus-one radial velocity on that window: if it occurred, its forced endpoint would lie within the assumed surviving extension. The criterion's middle interval is in already generated history. The compact strict exit prefixes cover any earlier sampled pieces.

This yields a uniform positive source factor on each fixed surviving window, because a degenerating source factor would force the excluded inward radial geometry at its source. The positive exit angular momentum and its monotonicity give a uniform radius floor; the complete speed bound gives a radius ceiling. The exact acceleration and its derivative are then bounded on the window and its sampled predecessors. Diagonal extraction produces an entire future curve with speed at most one and the unchanged ordinary partner equation. Positive delay and the complete speed bound preserve the empty self census, with any equality chord excluded by the nonzero partner acceleration and viable unit-touch geometry. Unit touches of the limit remain possible; strict speed is not asserted for the limit.

Joining this future to the backward exit history gives an attained nontrivial complete connection from the spiral. Thus absence of all such attained viable connections forces a uniform upper bound on sufficiently small members' post-exit strict lifetime. This is a compactness implication, not a proof that the connection exists or is absent. A sequence of actual infinite members is a special case and gives compact forward connections directly.

The same mechanisms explain which constants can be uniform across small infinite members: fix the compact exit collection and its strict sampled prefix, then use the universal inward-event threshold and common exit angular/radius bounds. Any later normalized compactness constants must be obtained from those common data and the established radial/torque contradiction, not from each member's arbitrary late cutoff. This reduction supplies uniform bounds only after the common generated-source windows are reached. It does not turn existential family smallness into a certified numerical amplitude.

Falsifiers are failure of generated-segment smoothing in the declared topology, loss of the fixed exit distance, an event transfer across a nonstrict prefix, insufficient future lifetime to apply the inward criterion, or omission of an earlier supplied source in the diagonal limit. No numerical target is launched.
