# Blind compactness corollary for arbitrary near-unit sequences

**Derived before the unit-limit subject is opened.** Work on an actual infinite strict-subfield member of the fixed logarithmic family, using the accepted compact normalized regime. Suppose $t_j$ are actual times with $|v(t_j)|\to1$. Strict speed and continuity on every bounded time interval imply $t_j\to\infty$ after a subsequence. Normalize with the shifted time $w_j=t_j-s_0$ and rotate the current radius to a fixed direction.

The accepted normalized compactness gives a subsequential exact ordinary limiting history on positive scaled times. Its speed is at most one everywhere and equals one at its reception coordinate one. Its radius, tangential speed and source denominator are bounded away from zero. Its source lies in a positive compact time window. The exact equation is differentiable there, so speed has two derivatives even though only $C^2$ position compactness was initially used to obtain the limit.

Let $n$ be its receiving chord direction, $R$ its range, $v$ its receiving velocity, $v_s$ its representative mirror-source velocity, and $D=1+n\cdot v_s$. The logarithmic row is $x''=-n/(RD)$. At a unit-speed maximum, $n\cdot v=0$. Positive angular quantity and the acute lag select $v=Jn$, not its negative. Therefore its radial velocity is strictly positive: the limiting touch is outward.

Differentiating the row at this stationary-speed point gives the already independently derived curvature identity

$$
\left.\frac{d^2|v|}{dt^2}\right|_{|v|=1,\ n\cdot v=0}
=-\frac{(n+v)\cdot v_s}{R^2D^2}.
$$

Since this is a maximum of a speed function bounded above by one on both sides, necessarily $\Lambda=(n+v)\cdot v_s\ge0$. A negative value is excluded; zero is permitted by the second-order test and needs higher information. Strict positivity gives negative curvature and the previously established isolated-touch local behavior for this limiting equation. It does not establish an actual unit event on any strict member.

This conclusion applies to arbitrary actual near-unit times; they need not be local maxima themselves. Stationarity belongs to the attained maximum in the compact limiting history. The limiting source is ordinary, and the complete limiting root census follows from the accepted compact regime. No degenerate source denominator or collapsed radius is being silently admitted.

The result neither constructs a near-unit sequence nor shows that a nonzero departing member has an infinite branch. If such a sequence exists, scalar speed cannot converge to one by the accepted scalar-limit rigidity, so a lower speed subsequence must also occur. It does not follow that the limiting touch has strictly negative curvature or that the entire compact limit set is the spiral. The exact base spiral is a consistency case without a unit sequence. Falsifiers are failure of normalized compactness, a lost source margin, the wrong orientation choice at stationarity, or a sign error in the curvature identity. No computation or new physical preparation was used.
