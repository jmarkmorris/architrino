# Two architrinos below wake speed with no symmetry: is a periodic history possible? (exploration, 2026-10-06)

## Result

**The question is not settled.** No proof was found that two architrinos of opposite polarity, both below wake speed at all times and with no symmetry assumed, have no periodic or relative-periodic history, and no such history was found either. What follows is what was established.

1. **An exact rate (derived).** The relative angular momentum $J=(\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2)\times(\mathbf v_1-\mathbf v_2)$ obeys, for opposite polarity and both members below wake speed, $dJ/dt=r\,[\sin\beta_1/(\tau_1^2D_1)+\sin\beta_2/(\tau_2^2D_2)]$, where $r$ is the present separation, $\tau_i$ and $D_i$ are the causal delay and transmitter factor of the one row member $i$ receives, and $\beta_i$ is the aberration angle at member $i$: the signed angle, counterclockwise positive, from the direction of the partner's delayed image to the direction of the partner's present position. Each $\beta_i$ lies strictly between $-\pi/2$ and $\pi/2$.
2. **A conditional exclusion that contains the mirror-pair theorem as a special case (derived).** If both aberration angles are non-negative at every instant and not both identically zero, $J$ increases over every interval and the history is neither periodic nor periodic in a uniformly rotating frame, with or without a constant drift. The hypothesis says that each member, viewed from its partner's present position, has advanced counterclockwise (net) over the delay window of the row its partner is receiving. For a mirror pair this is exactly the hypothesis of the [mirror-pair statement](mirror-pair-angular-momentum-monotonicity.md) as corrected by its [second reading](mirror-pair-angular-momentum-second-reading-2026-10-05.md), so the mirror symmetry is not needed, only the sign of the two aberration angles. The same holds with all signs reversed, and for like polarity with $J$ decreasing.
3. **A second exact rate with its own conditional exclusion (derived).** The sum $K=K_1+K_2$ of each member's angular momentum about the emission point of the row it receives, $K_i=(\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_j(s_i))\times\mathbf v_i(t)$, obeys $dK_i/dt=\dot s_i\,\mathbf v_i(t)\times\mathbf v_j(s_i)$ with $\dot s_i>0$. No delay or transmitter factor appears other than through $\dot s_i$. If the partner's delayed velocity is never clockwise of the receiver's present velocity, for both members, no history exists that is periodic, periodic in a frame rotating about a fixed centre, or periodic up to a constant drift.
4. **The obstruction to an unconditional proof by either quantity (derived, and measured on solutions).** When the two delay windows are straight and uniform the rate has the closed form $r\,dJ/dt=(v_{1\perp}-v_{2\perp})+v_{1\perp}v_{1\parallel}/S_1+v_{2\perp}v_{2\parallel}/S_2$, in components along and across the present separation, with $S_i=\sqrt{1-v_{i\perp}^2}$. The first bracket is $J/r$. The remaining terms are of second order in speed, do not vanish when $J=0$, and take either sign: a pair with a common velocity oblique to its own axis changes $J$ even with no relative rotation. Because speeds below wake speed are not small in units of wake speed, these terms can exceed the first. Released solutions of the equation with a large common velocity show $dJ/dt$ against the sign of $J$ over whole segments (measured). No pointwise sign for $dJ/dt$ can therefore be proved without a hypothesis; an unconditional proof must be an averaged argument that controls the common velocity.
5. **Necessary features of any counterexample (derived).** In a relative-periodic history, $\sin\beta_1/(\tau_1^2D_1)+\sin\beta_2/(\tau_2^2D_2)$ must take both signs (or vanish identically), so at some instants a member must see its partner's delayed image ahead of the partner's present position in the sense of the pair's turning. If the history has a fixed centre of rotation, each member's row must in addition pass through alignment with that centre at some instant.
6. **Numerical record (measured, on releases, not on whole-past solutions).** In 400 releases of non-mirror pairs from prescribed pasts, every pair either separated or reached wake speed; none stayed bound, and $J$ never changed sign. $J$ moved against its sign at some instant in 11 of the 238 runs with usable segments (15 of 400 when the same sample was extended), and its net change was against its sign in 2 short runs with velocity sum above $1.1$.

**What remains open:** whether a relative-periodic history exists in which the aberration angles change sign; equivalently, whether the second-order common-velocity terms can cancel the first-order gain on average. In polar form about any reference point the hypothesis of item 2 can fail, at moderate angles, only when a member changes its distance from that point while the pair is out of opposition through it (Corollary 2c), so the open cases are breathing, misaligned histories; members on fixed circles turning non-uniformly in one sense within a quarter turn of opposition are excluded. A candidate mechanism is described under [Limits and falsifiers](#limits-and-falsifiers) (guessed).

**Claim grade:** derived for items 1 to 5 by the hand arguments below, with the algebra of items 1 and 4 checked numerically on prescribed histories; measured for item 6.

**Second reading.** A [second reading](two-member-subwake-periodicity-second-reading-2026-10-06.md), which derived the rate of $J$ before reading this note, accepts every item, four of them with corrections. The corrections, to be read into the text below: the proviso about the partner's path passing through the receiver is vacuous; the aberration angles obey the sharper bound $\lvert\sin\beta_i\rvert\le$ the partner's mean speed over the window; in item 2, $J$ never decreases and strictly increases over any interval containing an instant with a positive angle, and item 3 needs the same "not both identically zero" clause; the velocity form of the sufficient condition after Theorem 2 should read $\mathbf n\times\mathbf v_1\ge0$ on $[s_2,t]$ and $\mathbf n\times\mathbf v_2\le0$ on $[s_1,t]$; the statement that no pointwise sign can be proved is derived for the row identity and for prescribed histories, measured on releases, and only inferred for whole-past solutions; item 5 proves only that the two angles are not both non-negative throughout nor both non-positive throughout; and the summary sentence on fixed circles omits two conditions kept in the body. The second reading also adds, for straight windows with a common velocity, that the relative acceleration is exactly a gradient, so the second-order terms of item 4 exchange $J$ back and forth and are not a secular gain or loss, and it suggests seeking the missing monotone quantity as a correction of the associated comparison quantity. The question remains open.

## Setting and notation

Units: wake speed $c_f=1$ and coupling $K=1$. Two architrinos move in a plane at $\mathbf x_1(t)$, $\mathbf x_2(t)$ with velocities $\mathbf v_1$, $\mathbf v_2$, speeds strictly below $1$ at every instant, and polarity product $\sigma=-1$ (opposite) unless stated. The planar cross product is $\mathbf a\times\mathbf b=a_1b_2-a_2b_1$.

**Rows.** Member $1$ at time $t$ receives a row emitted by member $2$ at the time $s_1(t)<t$ with $t-s_1=\lvert\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1)\rvert=\tau_1$. With $\mathbf n_1=(\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1))/\tau_1$ and $D_1=1-\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf v_2(s_1)$, the equation gives the acceleration $\mathbf a_1=-\mathbf n_1/(\tau_1^2D_1)$. Member $2$ likewise has $s_2$, $\tau_2$, $\mathbf n_2=(\mathbf x_2(t)-\mathbf x_1(s_2))/\tau_2$, $D_2=1-\mathbf n_2\cdot\mathbf v_1(s_2)$ and $\mathbf a_2=-\mathbf n_2/(\tau_2^2D_2)$. Write $W_i=1/(\tau_i^3D_i)$.

**Row census (derived).** Below wake speed a member has no root on its own path, because the distance to its own earlier position is less than the elapsed time. The function $g(s)=t-s-\lvert\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_j(s)\rvert$ is strictly decreasing in $s$ and negative at $s=t$, so there is at most one partner root, and $D_i\ge1-\lvert\mathbf v_j\rvert>0$. The root exists when $g\to+\infty$ as $s\to-\infty$. This holds for every history of the form $\mathbf x_i(t)=\mathbf c+\mathbf u\,t+{}$bounded, which covers all the periodic and relative-periodic histories considered here: the mean velocity $\mathbf u$ is a long-time average of velocities whose speeds are below $1$, and the speed is a continuous function on the compact torus of the two phases, so its mean is strictly below $1$ and $\lvert\mathbf u\rvert<1$. For a general history the existence of the root is an assumption of this note.

**Emission-time rate (derived).** Differentiating $t-s_1=\lvert\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1)\rvert$ gives $\dot s_1=(1-\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf v_1(t))/D_1>0$, and the same for member $2$.

**Relative quantities.** $\mathbf r=\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2$, $r=\lvert\mathbf r\rvert$, $\mathbf n=\mathbf r/r$ (pointing from member $2$ to member $1$), $\mathbf w=\mathbf v_1-\mathbf v_2$, $\mathbf P=\mathbf v_1+\mathbf v_2$ (the velocity sum), $J=\mathbf r\times\mathbf w$. For any velocity $\mathbf v$, $v_\parallel=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v$ and $v_\perp=\mathbf n\times\mathbf v$.

**Window displacements.** $\boldsymbol\Delta_1(t)=\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_1(s_2(t))$ is the displacement of member $1$ over the delay window of the row that member $2$ is receiving at $t$, and $\boldsymbol\Delta_2(t)=\mathbf x_2(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1(t))$ likewise. Then $\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1)=\mathbf r+\boldsymbol\Delta_2$ and $\mathbf x_2(t)-\mathbf x_1(s_2)=-\mathbf r+\boldsymbol\Delta_1$.

**Aberration angles.** $\beta_1(t)$ is the signed angle at $\mathbf x_1(t)$ from the direction of $\mathbf x_2(s_1)$ to the direction of $\mathbf x_2(t)$, counterclockwise positive; $\beta_2(t)$ is the signed angle at $\mathbf x_2(t)$ from the direction of $\mathbf x_1(s_2)$ to the direction of $\mathbf x_1(t)$.

**Relative-periodic history.** A history is relative periodic when, in a frame rotating at a constant rate about a point that is fixed or moves at constant velocity, both positions are periodic with a common period $T$. Then $\mathbf r(t+T)$ and $\mathbf w(t+T)$ are $\mathbf r(t)$ and $\mathbf w(t)$ turned through one fixed angle, the same for both. A periodic history is the special case of no rotation and no drift. When the frame both rotates and drifts, the individual velocities at $t+T$ are not a rotation of those at $t$, so only quantities built from $\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf w$ are periodic in that case.

## Exact rates

Every rate in this section is derived from the equation alone, for both members below wake speed and opposite polarity. For like polarity the accelerations change sign, and with them the rates of $J$, $\ell_i$ and $M$; the rate of $K_i$ keeps its form for either polarity, and the comparison quantity becomes $\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf v_i\rvert^2+1/\tau_i$ with the opposite rate.

**Relative angular momentum.** Since $dJ/dt=\mathbf r\times(\mathbf a_1-\mathbf a_2)$, $\mathbf a_1=-(\mathbf r+\boldsymbol\Delta_2)W_1$ and $\mathbf a_2=-(-\mathbf r+\boldsymbol\Delta_1)W_2$,

$$
\frac{dJ}{dt}=\frac{\mathbf r\times\boldsymbol\Delta_1}{\tau_2^3D_2}+\frac{(-\mathbf r)\times\boldsymbol\Delta_2}{\tau_1^3D_1} .
$$

The first numerator is twice the signed area of the triangle $\mathbf x_1(s_2),\mathbf x_1(t),\mathbf x_2(t)$ taken in that order, and the second is twice the signed area of the triangle $\mathbf x_2(s_1),\mathbf x_2(t),\mathbf x_1(t)$. The second triangle has sides $\tau_1$ and $r$ meeting at $\mathbf x_1(t)$ with included angle $\beta_1$, and the first has sides $\tau_2$ and $r$ meeting at $\mathbf x_2(t)$ with included angle $\beta_2$, so

$$
\frac{dJ}{dt}=r\left[\frac{\sin\beta_1}{\tau_1^2D_1}+\frac{\sin\beta_2}{\tau_2^2D_2}\right].
$$

**Bound on the aberration angles.** The partner travels less than $\tau_1$ during the window, so $\mathbf x_2(t)$ lies strictly inside the disc of radius $\tau_1$ about $\mathbf x_2(s_1)$, and $\mathbf x_1(t)$ lies on the boundary of that disc. Every point strictly inside a disc is seen from a boundary point at an angle of less than a quarter turn from the direction of the centre. Hence $\lvert\beta_1\rvert<\pi/2$, and likewise $\lvert\beta_2\rvert<\pi/2$. No winding question arises, because $\beta_i$ is the angle between two directions at one instant. The sign of $\beta_1$ is the sign of the net angle swept by member $2$ about the fixed point $\mathbf x_1(t)$ over the window, and that swept angle is itself less than a quarter turn in magnitude, provided the partner's path does not pass through the point $\mathbf x_1(t)$ during the window (by the first-crossing argument of the second reading applied about $\mathbf x_1(t)$: a half turn about that point would need a path of length at least $\tau_1$).

**Angular momentum about the emission point.** Put $K_1=(\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1))\times\mathbf v_1(t)$. The acceleration $\mathbf a_1$ is parallel to $\mathbf x_1(t)-\mathbf x_2(s_1)$ and drops out, leaving

$$
\frac{dK_1}{dt}=\dot s_1\,\mathbf v_1(t)\times\mathbf v_2(s_1),\qquad\frac{dK_2}{dt}=\dot s_2\,\mathbf v_2(t)\times\mathbf v_1(s_2).
$$

**Comparison quantity.** With $e_i=\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf v_i\rvert^2-1/\tau_i$, using $d\tau_1/dt=1-\dot s_1$,

$$
\frac{de_1}{dt}=-\frac{\mathbf n_1\cdot\mathbf v_2(s_1)}{\tau_1^2D_1}=\frac{D_1-1}{\tau_1^2D_1},
$$

and the same for member $2$. The rate is negative when the partner was moving toward the reception point at emission and positive when it was moving away. It has no sign.

**Angular momentum about a fixed point $O$.** With positions measured from $O$, $\ell_1=\mathbf x_1\times\mathbf v_1$ has $d\ell_1/dt=\mathbf x_1(t)\times\mathbf x_2(s_1)\,W_1$ and $d\ell_2/dt=\mathbf x_2(t)\times\mathbf x_1(s_2)\,W_2$. The cross quantity $M=\mathbf x_1\times\mathbf v_2+\mathbf x_2\times\mathbf v_1$ has

$$
\frac{dM}{dt}=-\,\mathbf x_1(s_2)\times\mathbf x_1(t)\,W_2-\mathbf x_2(s_1)\times\mathbf x_2(t)\,W_1-\mathbf x_1(t)\times\mathbf x_2(t)\,(W_2-W_1),
$$

and $J=\ell_1+\ell_2-M$. The first two terms are the areas swept by each member about $O$; the last is the term that spoils a sign whenever the members are not in line through $O$ and the two weights differ.

**Straight uniform windows (derived; exact for such windows).** If each member moves at constant velocity over the two delay windows, then $\tau_1D_1=r\,S_2$ and $\tau_1=r\,(v_{2\parallel}+S_2)/(1-\lvert\mathbf v_2\rvert^2)$ with $S_2=\sqrt{1-v_{2\perp}^2}$, and the mirror statement for member $2$ with $\mathbf n\to-\mathbf n$. Substituting,

$$
r\,\frac{dJ}{dt}=v_{1\perp}\Bigl(1+\frac{v_{1\parallel}}{S_1}\Bigr)-v_{2\perp}\Bigl(1-\frac{v_{2\parallel}}{S_2}\Bigr)=\frac{J}{r}+\frac{v_{1\perp}v_{1\parallel}}{S_1}+\frac{v_{2\perp}v_{2\parallel}}{S_2}.
$$

For a common velocity $\mathbf V$ with no relative motion, $J=0$ and $r\,dJ/dt=2V_\perp V_\parallel/\sqrt{1-V_\perp^2}$.

**First order in speed (derived as an expansion, error not bounded).** Keeping terms of first order in the speeds, with $1/r$ counted as second order, $\mathbf a_1-\mathbf a_2=-2\mathbf n/r^2+(\mathbf w-2\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf w))/r^2$ and $\mathbf a_1+\mathbf a_2=(2\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf P)-\mathbf P)/r^2$. The relative motion decouples from $\mathbf P$ at this order, $dJ/dt=J/r^2$, and $\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf w\rvert^2-2/r$ changes at the rate $(w_\perp^2-w_\parallel^2)/r^2$. The transverse part of the relative velocity is amplified and the radial part is damped. The component of $\mathbf P$ along the pair's axis grows and the component across it decays, each at the rate $1/r^2$; for an axis that turns uniformly these cancel on average. This agrees with the mirror pair's first-order rate.

## What is proved

**Theorem 1 (exact rate; derived).** For two architrinos of opposite polarity, both below wake speed, each receiving its one partner row, $dJ/dt=r[\sin\beta_1/(\tau_1^2D_1)+\sin\beta_2/(\tau_2^2D_2)]$ with $\lvert\beta_i\rvert<\pi/2$, $\tau_i>0$ and $0<D_i<2$. For like polarity the right side changes sign. Proof: the two paragraphs "Relative angular momentum" and "Bound on the aberration angles" above.

**Theorem 2 (conditional exclusion; derived).** Suppose in addition that $\beta_1(t)\ge0$ and $\beta_2(t)\ge0$ for all $t$, and that they are not both zero at every instant. Then the history is not periodic and not relative periodic, with or without a constant drift. Proof: in a relative-periodic history $\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf w$ at $t+T$ are $\mathbf r$ and $\mathbf w$ at $t$ turned through one common angle, so $J(t+T)=J(t)$; the drift does not enter because $J$ contains only the difference of positions and its rate of change. By Theorem 1 and the hypothesis, $J(t+T)-J(t)=\int_t^{t+T}(dJ/dt)\,du>0$. The same proof with both angles non-positive, or with like polarity, gives the same exclusion.

The hypothesis has three equivalent forms: (i) both aberration angles are non-negative; (ii) both signed triangle areas above are non-negative, that is $\mathbf r\times\boldsymbol\Delta_1\ge0$ and $(-\mathbf r)\times\boldsymbol\Delta_2\ge0$; (iii) the net angle swept by each member about its partner's present position, over the delay window of the row the partner is receiving, is non-negative. A sufficient condition in terms of velocities alone: at every instant $t$, each member's velocity has a non-negative component counterclockwise across the present separation direction $\mathbf n(t)$ throughout that window.

**Corollary 2a (the mirror pair; derived).** For $\mathbf x_2=-\mathbf x_1=-\mathbf x$, $J=4\ell$, $\beta_1=\beta_2=\beta$ and $\tau\sin\beta=\rho(s)\sin(\theta(t)-\theta(s))$, so Theorem 1 reduces to the mirror-pair rate and the hypothesis of Theorem 2 reduces to $\theta(t)\ge\theta(s)$ at every causal pair. The mirror theorem is the special case.

**Corollary 2b (members in line through a fixed centre, unequal distances; derived).** Let $\mathbf x_2(t)=-\lambda(t)\,\mathbf x_1(t)$ with an arbitrary positive function $\lambda$, so the members stay on opposite sides of a fixed centre at unequal and varying distances. Then $\mathbf r\times\boldsymbol\Delta_1=(1+\lambda)\,\mathbf x_1(s_2)\times\mathbf x_1(t)$ and $(-\mathbf r)\times\boldsymbol\Delta_2=(1+1/\lambda)\,\mathbf x_2(s_1)\times\mathbf x_2(t)$. If the common polar angle increases and its advance over each delay window is less than half a turn, both are positive and no relative-periodic history of this class exists. The advance is automatically less than half a turn when the same member is the farther one from the centre at both ends of the window (first-crossing argument applied to that member); otherwise it is an added hypothesis.

**Corollary 2c (the hypothesis in polar form about any reference point; derived, algebra checked numerically).** Choose any point $O$ and write $\mathbf x_i=\rho_i(\cos\theta_i,\sin\theta_i)$ about it. Let $\delta(t)=\theta_2(t)-\theta_1(t)-\pi$ be the angle by which member $2$ leads exact opposition through $O$, and let $\varsigma_2=\theta_2(t)-\theta_2(s_1)$ and $\varsigma_1=\theta_1(t)-\theta_1(s_2)$ be the angles swept about $O$ over the two windows. Then exactly

$$
(-\mathbf r)\times\boldsymbol\Delta_2=\rho_2(s_1)\rho_2(t)\sin\varsigma_2+2\rho_1(t)\rho_2(s_1)\sin\frac{\varsigma_2}{2}\cos\Bigl(\delta-\frac{\varsigma_2}{2}\Bigr)+\rho_1(t)\bigl[\rho_2(t)-\rho_2(s_1)\bigr]\sin\delta ,
$$

$$
\mathbf r\times\boldsymbol\Delta_1=\rho_1(s_2)\rho_1(t)\sin\varsigma_1+2\rho_2(t)\rho_1(s_2)\sin\frac{\varsigma_1}{2}\cos\Bigl(\delta+\frac{\varsigma_1}{2}\Bigr)-\rho_2(t)\bigl[\rho_1(t)-\rho_1(s_2)\bigr]\sin\delta .
$$

The first two terms of each line are positive whenever the member turns counterclockwise about $O$ by less than half a turn in the window and $\lvert\delta\mp\varsigma/2\rvert<\pi/2$, that is, whenever the members are within a quarter turn of opposition through $O$ after allowing half the sweep. Only the last term can then make the hypothesis fail, and it is the product of a change of distance from $O$ over the window and the departure from opposition. Two consequences. First, **two members on fixed circles about a common centre, of equal or unequal radii, turning in one sense at arbitrary non-uniform angular rates, have no relative-periodic history below wake speed** as long as the two conditions on the angles hold; this extends the rigid exclusion to non-uniform turning on fixed circles, though only for arrangements within that distance of opposition, whereas the rigid exclusion needs no such condition. Second, a history that escapes Theorem 2 with moderate angles must breathe and depart from opposition at once, in a definite sense: member $2$ moving inward while it leads opposition (or outward while it lags), or member $1$ moving outward while member $2$ leads (or inward while it lags), by enough to outweigh the swept terms.

**Corollary 2d (three dimensions; derived, not checked numerically).** With vector cross products, $d\mathbf J/dt=\mathbf r\times(\boldsymbol\Delta_1W_2-\boldsymbol\Delta_2W_1)$ exactly. For a history periodic in a frame rotating about a fixed axis direction $\hat{\mathbf k}$, with or without drift, $\hat{\mathbf k}\cdot\mathbf J$ is periodic, so no such history exists if $\hat{\mathbf k}\cdot(\mathbf r\times\boldsymbol\Delta_1)\ge0$ and $\hat{\mathbf k}\cdot(-\mathbf r\times\boldsymbol\Delta_2)\ge0$ throughout and not both identically zero.

**Theorem 3 (second conditional exclusion; derived).** $K=K_1+K_2$ is unchanged when the whole history is turned through a fixed angle and shifted by a fixed vector, so $K(t+T)=K(t)$ whenever $\mathbf x_i(t+T)$ is $\mathbf x_i(t)$ turned and shifted in that way. This covers periodic histories, histories periodic in a frame rotating about a fixed centre, and histories periodic up to a constant drift; it does not cover a rotating frame whose centre itself drifts. For either polarity, $dK/dt=\dot s_1\,\mathbf v_1(t)\times\mathbf v_2(s_1)+\dot s_2\,\mathbf v_2(t)\times\mathbf v_1(s_2)$ with $\dot s_i>0$. If $\mathbf v_1(t)\times\mathbf v_2(s_1)\ge0$ and $\mathbf v_2(t)\times\mathbf v_1(s_2)\ge0$ throughout and not both identically zero, no history of the covered kinds exists. For a mirror pair the condition reads $\mathbf v(s)\times\mathbf v(t)\ge0$: the velocity direction has turned counterclockwise by at most half a turn over the delay. This is a different hypothesis from that of Theorem 2 and neither implies the other.

**Proposition 4 (necessary features of a counterexample; derived).** In any relative-periodic history of two architrinos of either polarity below wake speed, by the periodicity of $J$, $K$, $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$: (a) $\sin\beta_1/(\tau_1^2D_1)+\sin\beta_2/(\tau_2^2D_2)$ is identically zero or takes both signs, so the aberration angles are not of one sign throughout; (b) unless the frame both rotates and drifts, $\dot s_1\,\mathbf v_1(t)\times\mathbf v_2(s_1)+\dot s_2\,\mathbf v_2(t)\times\mathbf v_1(s_2)$ is identically zero or takes both signs; (c) if the centre of rotation $O$ is fixed, $\ell_1$ and $\ell_2$ are separately periodic, so each of $\mathbf x_1(t)\times\mathbf x_2(s_1)$ and $\mathbf x_2(t)\times\mathbf x_1(s_2)$ is identically zero or changes sign: each member's row points exactly along its own line through $O$ at some instant. Rigid rotation is the case in which (c) must hold at every instant, which is what the [rigid exclusion](asymmetric-opposite-pair-closed-form.md) uses.

**Proposition 5 (no unconditional pointwise sign; derived for straight windows, measured on solutions).** By the straight-window closed form, $dJ/dt$ has the sign of $J$ if $\lvert v_{1\perp}-v_{2\perp}\rvert>\lvert v_{1\perp}v_{1\parallel}\rvert/S_1+\lvert v_{2\perp}v_{2\parallel}\rvert/S_2$ and can have the opposite sign otherwise; for a common velocity it is nonzero at $J=0$ with the sign of $V_\perp V_\parallel$. So neither $J$ nor any function of $J$ alone is monotone on all histories below wake speed. That this happens on histories that obey the equation is measured below.

## What was tried and failed

**An unconditional sign for $dJ/dt$.** Fails for the reason in Proposition 5. The weights $1/(\tau_1^2D_1)$ and $1/(\tau_2^2D_2)$ differ when the pair has a velocity sum, and one aberration angle can be negative when one member moves "backwards" across the separation line, which a common velocity produces. The equation is not unchanged when a common velocity is added to both members, so the common velocity cannot be removed by a change of frame; it is a dynamical variable.

**Total angular momentum about the centre of the rotating frame.** $d(\ell_1+\ell_2)/dt=\mathbf x_1(t)\times\mathbf x_2(s_1)W_1+\mathbf x_2(t)\times\mathbf x_1(s_2)W_2$. With the members nearly opposite through the centre and the partner leading opposition by an angle $\delta$, the two terms are proportional to $\sin(\text{sweep}_2-\delta)$ and $\sin(\text{sweep}_1+\delta)$. With equal weights their sum is positive; with unequal weights and an oscillating $\delta$ it has no sign. The identity for $dM/dt$ isolates the offending term as $\mathbf x_1\times\mathbf x_2\,(W_2-W_1)$. The combinations $c_1\ell_1+c_2\ell_2+c_3M$ were considered; each rate is $W_1$ times one cross product plus $W_2$ times another, and no choice other than the one giving $J$ was found in which both cross products are areas with a geometric bound that does not refer to the centre.

**The angular momentum $K$ about the emission points.** Its rate has no $1/\tau^3$ weight, which looked promising, but the cross products of present and delayed velocities change sign as readily as the aberration angles do. On released solutions $K$ decreased on 20 percent of steps, against 1 percent for $J$ (measured).

**The comparison quantities $e_i$.** Their rates $(D_i-1)/(\tau_i^2D_i)$ depend on the radial motion of the partner at emission and average to nothing obvious. No combination of $e_1+e_2$ with $J$ was found whose rate has a sign.

**Pairing the two families of causal links by a change of variable.** Along the links from member $1$ to member $2$, $dt/D_2=ds/(1-\mathbf n_2\cdot\mathbf v_2(t))$, so the transmitter factor is exactly the Jacobian between reception and emission time, and period averages can be written over emission time. But the links from $1$ to $2$ and those from $2$ to $1$ are two different families of causal pairs (reversing time would exchange them, and the equation uses only delayed ones), so the two terms of a period average of $dJ/dt$ or $dK/dt$ are not brought to a common integrand. This is the structural reason no cancellation or pairing argument was found.

**Extending the rigid angle-sum argument.** With $\psi_1(t)=\theta_1(t)-\theta_2(s_1(t))$ and $\psi_2$ likewise (polar angles about the centre), $\psi_1(t)+\psi_2(s_1(t))$ equals the angle swept by member $1$ about the centre over a round trip of two delays. In rigid rotation both $\psi$ are constant multiples of $\pi$ and the round-trip angle is too small unless the outer member moves at $\pi/2$. In a non-rigid history Proposition 4(c) only makes each $\psi_i$ pass through a multiple of $\pi$ at some instant, not at linked instants, and writing $\psi_i=\pi+\delta_i$ the constraints $\delta_1(t)+\delta_2(s_1(t))>0$ and $\delta_2(t)+\delta_1(s_2(t))>0$ are satisfied by lead angles that oscillate out of phase with amplitude larger than half the round-trip sweep. No contradiction follows from the angles alone.

**Corrected quantities suggested by the straight-window form.** Using $d v_{i\parallel}/dt=(w_\perp/r)v_{i\perp}+\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a_i$, the second-order terms can be written partly as a derivative, which suggests $\tfrac12J^2-\tfrac r2(v_{1\parallel}^2+v_{2\parallel}^2)$, the same minus $\ln r$, and $\tfrac12J^2-r(v_{1\parallel}^2+v_{2\parallel}^2)$. All three are far less monotone than $J$ on released solutions: they decrease on 41, 51 and 59 percent of steps (measured). The leftover term $w_\parallel(v_{1\parallel}^2+v_{2\parallel}^2)/2$ is not small when the separation changes.

**A rigorous low-speed theorem.** The first-order rate $dJ/dt=J/r^2$ excludes relative-periodic histories at low speed if the neglected terms are dominated. They are dominated only if the velocity sum is small compared with the square root of the transverse relative speed, which is again a hypothesis of the kind in Theorem 2 and not a consequence of low speed. No error bound was established for curved windows.

## Numerical experiments

**Instruments.** Three session scripts on the shared venv, pure Python floats, written for this note and not retained in the repository. (a) A kinematic checker that solves each causal root by bisection on prescribed histories. (b) A delay integrator: fourth-order Runge–Kutta with fixed step, cubic Hermite interpolation of the stored paths, Newton solution of the single partner root, unchanged equation, opposite polarity. Each run prescribes a past for $t<0$ (two members circling a centre at unequal radii $fR$ and $(1-f)R$ with a phase offset, the centre moving at constant velocity) and lets the equation act from $t=0$. Only samples whose two rows were both emitted after $t=0$ are used. (c) A driver that draws random starts.

**What these can and cannot establish.** Prescribed histories are not solutions; checks on them test algebra and geometry only. Released histories obey the equation for $t>0$ with rows drawn from the released path, but they are not solutions on their whole past and none is periodic. They show what $J$ does on histories that obey the equation; they do not test periodic histories and are not independent evidence for the theorems.

**Controls, run before the samples (measured).** On a wide mirror pair released at separation $20$ and speed $0.166$, the ratio of $dJ/dt$ to the first-order value $J/r^2$ stayed between $0.964$ and $1.047$, the known low-speed case. On every run the finite difference of $J$ along the path agreed with $\mathbf r\times(\mathbf a_1-\mathbf a_2)$ to between $10^{-10}$ and $6\times10^{-5}$ depending on speed, the triangle-area form agreed with it to $3\times10^{-16}$, and the finite difference of $K$ agreed with the Theorem 3 rate to the same accuracy. Halving the step twice changed $J$ near time $40$ in the fifth significant figure (end times matched only to $0.02$).

**Algebra checks on prescribed histories (measured).** On 2000 random pairs of straight uniform histories the straight-window closed form agreed with the directly computed rate to $1.5\times10^{-13}$, and $J\,dJ/dt<0$ in 219 of them. On 1958 samples from curved, drifting, non-mirror prescribed histories the aberration-angle form agreed to a relative $6\times10^{-13}$, the largest $\lvert\beta\rvert$ was $1.07$, below $\pi/2$, and the two aberration angles had opposite signs in 755 samples.

**Released pairs, first sample (measured).** Forty starts with separation $1.3$ to $6$, radius split $0.15$ to $0.85$, common speed up to $0.6$: 34 were separating, with separation above $43$ when the run stopped at separation $60$ or time $120$, and 6 reached wake speed within 8 time units. $J$ rose in each of the 38 runs with usable samples. $dJ/dt$ was negative at some instants in 3 runs, all with a velocity sum of $0.78$ or more. The hypothesis of Theorem 2 failed at some instants in most runs with a common velocity. No pair completed one full turn of its separation direction within its usable segment.

**Released pairs, broad sample (measured).** Four hundred starts with separation $1.2$ to $7$, radius split $0.05$ to $0.95$, common speed up to $0.9$, any phase offset: 141 separated beyond $60$, 255 reached wake speed, 4 were still separating at time $200$ with separation above $20$; none remained bound. Of the 238 with at least 20 usable samples, $J$ moved against its sign at some instant in 11, the hypothesis of Theorem 2 failed somewhere in 160, $J$ never changed sign, and the net change of $J$ was against its sign in 2 runs. Those two were short segments ending at wake speed with velocity sums $1.22$ and $1.15$ and $J\approx0.7$ to $0.8$; in the first, $dJ/dt$ was negative on the whole usable segment. The largest fall of $J$ from a previous maximum was $0.47$, in a run with velocity sum $1.52$ and $J$ rising overall from $4.89$ to $5.98$. An extension of the same sample (same seed, continued until 400 runs had usable segments, so it contains the 238 above) gave $J$ decreasing on $1.1$ percent of steps, in 15 runs; the percentages quoted above for $K$ and for the corrected quantities are from this extension.

**Reading (inferred).** Along histories that obey the equation, $J$ grows in the great majority of cases and the pair separates within about one turn, as the first-order rates predict; the exceptions are tied to a large velocity sum, as the straight-window form predicts. The samples contain no bound history at all, so they say nothing direct about periodic ones.

## Limits and falsifiers

**Limits.** Theorems 2 and 3 are conditional, and their hypotheses fail on most released non-mirror histories that carry a common velocity, so they do not answer the question. They are non-existence statements about classes that may be empty. Planar motion is assumed except in Corollary 2d. Everything assumes both members strictly below wake speed, a continuously differentiable path, and that each member receives its partner row at every instant; a history that reaches wake speed acquires own-path rows and is outside this note. Collinear histories have $J\equiv0$ and are not excluded. The first-order rates are an expansion without an error bound. The numerical samples are float measurements with fixed step on releases from one family of prescribed pasts.

**Why the conjecture is still plausible (inferred).** For a pair whose separation direction turns uniformly and whose velocities are otherwise steady, the second-order terms $v_{i\perp}v_{i\parallel}/S_i$ average to zero over a turn, because reflecting the angle between the axis and the common velocity about a right angle reverses $V_\parallel$ and keeps $V_\perp$; the first-order gain $J/r^2$ does not average away. A proof would need a quantity that removes these terms exactly, or an averaged inequality that controls the velocity sum. Neither was found.

**Candidate mechanism for a counterexample (guessed).** Proposition 4 requires an arrangement in which one member sees its partner's delayed image ahead of the partner. In rotation about a fixed centre this happens when the members are not opposite through the centre and the common velocity turns with the pair's axis at a fixed oblique angle, so that $V_\perp V_\parallel$ keeps one sign instead of averaging out; the [closed-form unequal-circle pair](asymmetric-opposite-pair-closed-form.md), whose inner member leads by $62^\circ$, is of this kind, and there the inner member's term is negative. That pair needs its outer member above wake speed when rigid. A breathing, non-rigid relative of it with both members below wake speed is not excluded by anything in this note, and is where a search for a counterexample should look: unequal distances from the centre, inner member leading, lead angle oscillating with amplitude larger than half the round-trip swept angle, and, by Corollary 2c, a change of distance from the centre correlated with the lead angle in the sense stated there. A second place to look is a pair whose axis swings about its own direction of common travel without turning fully; the straight-window term turns the axis toward that direction, but in exact tandem the members close on each other at the rate $2/r^2$, so this arrangement is expected to end at wake speed (inferred from the straight-window form, and consistent with 255 of 400 releases ending there).

**Falsifiers.** A history of two opposite architrinos below wake speed, each receiving its partner row, at which $dJ/dt$ differs from $r[\sin\beta_1/(\tau_1^2D_1)+\sin\beta_2/(\tau_2^2D_2)]$, or at which an aberration angle reaches a quarter turn, refutes Theorem 1. A relative-periodic history with both aberration angles non-negative throughout and not identically zero refutes Theorem 2. A pair of straight uniform histories below wake speed whose rate differs from the closed form refutes Proposition 5's formula. A proof that $dJ/dt$ has the sign of $J$ on every solution would contradict the measured segments with $dJ/dt<0$; a rerun of the broad sample with a finer step and a different integrator that showed no such segment would withdraw that measurement. Any relative-periodic history of two architrinos below wake speed, found numerically and certified, would answer the question in the negative and should be checked first against Proposition 4.
