# Blind reference on the regular boundary continuation class

Frozen before access to either new regular-boundary or continuous-boundary subject. The unchanged logarithmic mirror equation has attractive partner contribution $-n/(RD)$ and positive self contributions $n_j/(R_j|D_j|)$ at all ordinary self roots. The complete incoming history is strictly subfield, the receiving endpoint has positive separation and unit limiting velocity $v_0$, and its earlier partner source remains regular and strictly before the endpoint. No actual member is asserted to attain this configuration.

## Locally bounded acceleration

Claim grade: derived. Any locally bounded-acceleration continuation remains at speed at most one in a sufficiently small right neighborhood. To prove this, suppose there are superfield times tending to the endpoint. Continuity makes the superfield set open. At each such reception the chord gap is negative for sufficiently small positive delay and positive at an earlier fixed incoming source, so at least one self root exists. Every such self root must approach zero delay as reception approaches the endpoint: a nonzero limiting delay would be an incoming self root, excluded by the complete strict-speed chord inequality. The remote past is excluded uniformly by the fixed complete preparation and its subfield tail.

Uniform continuity of velocity makes all these short self chords point toward $v_0$. Their projections on $v_0$ are eventually at least one half. The source denominator obeys $|D_j|\le1+|v(s_j)|<3$ locally. Thus the sum of self contributions has projection at least $1/(6R_j)$ for any one root. It diverges as the endpoint is approached, whereas the partner contribution stays bounded. Ordinary full roots are required almost everywhere by the stated continuation class. On each open superfield interval this contradicts an essential local acceleration bound. Multiple self roots cannot cancel this estimate because all their projections have the same sign.

With speed at most one, every self chord is strictly shorter than its time span unless the velocity is one constant unit vector almost everywhere on that span. An interval intersecting the strict incoming history cannot have equality. An entirely new unit straight interval cannot solve the partner-only row, whose acceleration is nonzero at positive separation and ordinary source. Therefore there are no self roots in the small continuation neighborhood. All partner sources are still in the fixed incoming past, so the ordinary partner row is a smooth local ordinary differential equation with its original source data.

It follows that a bounded-acceleration continuation exists precisely when this uniquely reconstructed partner-only curve remains at speed at most one on some right neighborhood. If it does, the complete chord argument verifies the unchanged full equation. If it does not, no bounded-acceleration continuation exists. This criterion includes higher-order or flat touches, without inferring a curvature sign. At a strict negative speed curvature it yields the already admitted isolated below-unit touch; a flat crossing requires the full local curve, not a finite derivative test. The strict-domain convention may label the touch terminal even when the inclusive equation admits this continuation.

## Continuous velocity with only positive-time local absolute continuity

Claim grade: partial derivation; the general exclusion is not yet established by this reference. The same root census and common positive projection remain valid. They show that the self contribution has nonnegative projection on $v_0$ and is confined to a narrow cone, while the partner contribution remains bounded. Continuous velocity consequently makes the total positive projected self acceleration integrable up to the endpoint; the cone estimate then gives integrability of its full magnitude. This improves the assumed positive-time local absolute continuity to absolute continuity through the endpoint.

That observation alone does not contradict an unbounded but integrable self contribution. The pointwise estimate $1/(6R_j)$ is insufficient unless one also controls the relation between self delay and reception time or proves a divergent root integral. In particular, replacing $R_j$ by the elapsed receiving time without proof is invalid when the relevant source lies in the incoming past. A complete proof must handle root creation, multiple roots and nonmonotone branches without presupposing a finite-order departure. An integral identity or a direct bound on the smallest root could close this gap; no such identity is asserted here.

Falsifiers for the derived bounded result are a nonvanishing-delay incoming self root, loss of the common short-chord direction, cancellation by another unbounded contribution, or a constant unit straight partner-only interval. The stronger continuous-velocity result remains a separate subject for adjudication. No numerical target or preparation change is used, and only this new reference is written.
