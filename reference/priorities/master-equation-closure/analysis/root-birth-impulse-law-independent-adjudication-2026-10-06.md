## Result

**Verdict: accepted with corrections.** The law itself is right. My blind derivation, written before the note was opened, reproduces the note's three derivatives of $g$, its local system, its first integral, $y_*$, $t_*$, every row of the table "state at the arrival", and both straight-path formulas, symbol for symbol after renaming (derived, independently). My own integrator confirms $t_*$ on a prescribed source and a free receiver to between $10^{-6}$ and $5\times10^{-3}$ in relative terms wherever $\kappa\tau_b^3\le0.01$ and other rows are weak, for both polarity products, on straight, circular and accelerated source paths, with the receiver at rest or moving obliquely (measured; instrument and limits below). The one configuration with strong older rows deviates by $3.3$ percent, which the note's $Bt_*$ term anticipates.

**What is wrong is the stated domain of validity and the stated size of the error.** The note says the description holds "whenever $\tau_b$ is small compared with the natural length $K/c_f^2$, whatever the source's speed and acceleration", with relative error "of order $\tau_b$". Both statements fail. The controlling small quantity is $\kappa\tau_b^3$, which on a straight source path equals $(V^2-1)\tau_b^2=b^2V^2$, together with $\tau_b$ itself; the relative error of $t_*$ is second order in $\tau_b$ at fixed source data, measured at $+0.30\,\kappa\tau_b^3$ (opposite polarities) and $+0.10\,\kappa\tau_b^3$ (like polarities) for a receiver at rest beside a straight source. A direct counterexample to the stated domain: a straight source at $V=100$ passing at $b=0.019$ gives a birth with $\tau_b=0.0190$, and an opposite-polarity receiver at rest never reaches wake speed (maximum speed $0.752$, measured, unchanged between two step sizes). That run meets the conditions of the note's own first falsifier ($\tau_b<0.02$, no other rows, no arrival within $2t_*$) in the prescribed-source setting, and the runs at $V=30$ and $V=100$ meet its second (deviation $5.8\tau_b$ and $12.8\tau_b$). The note's measured releases are not undermined: from its table, the six with $\tau_b\le0.012$ have $(V^2-1)\tau_b^2$ between $0.005$ and $0.014$, inside the corrected domain, and the seventh ($\tau_b=0.1977$, source speed $4.22$) has $0.66$, for which the coefficient measured here, $0.30$, gives an excess of 20 percent against the note's measured $20.5$ percent (inferred: that run has an accelerated source and a moving receiver, for which I measured coefficients between $-0.2$ and $0.9$).

## Blind derivation

Written before reading the note. Grade of everything in this section: derived, at leading order in the small quantities named in the self-consistency paragraph, from the unchanged Master Equation row with a prescribed source path that is twice continuously differentiable near the newborn emission point.

**Setup.** Receiver path $x(t)$, velocity $v$, $|v|<1$; source path $X(s)$, velocity $V$, acceleration $A$; polarity product $\sigma=s_is_j$. Define the root function $G(t,\tau)=\tau-|x(t)-X(t-\tau)|$, so causal roots are the positive zeros of $G(t,\cdot)$. With $R=x(t)-X(t-\tau)$ and $n=R/|R|$, direct differentiation gives $\partial_\tau G=1-n\cdot V=D$, $\partial_t G=1-n\cdot v-D$, and at a root $\partial_\tau^2G=-(|V|^2-(n\cdot V)^2)/\tau+n\cdot A$. A birth at $(t_b,\tau_b)$ has $G=0$ and $D=0$, hence $n_b\cdot V_b=1$ (so $|V_b|>1$), $\partial_tG=1-n_b\cdot v_b>0$, and two roots appear for $t>t_b$ exactly when

$$
\kappa:=-\partial_\tau^2G\big|_b=\frac{|V_b|^2-1}{\tau_b}-n_b\cdot A_b>0 .
$$

The only local property of the source path that enters at leading order is this one number $\kappa$: the newborn delay, the source speed at the newborn emission point, and the component of the source acceleration along $n_b$ there.

**Local expansion.** Write $x(t)=x_b+\xi(t)$, $\delta t=t-t_b$, emission-time offset $\delta s=\delta t-\delta\tau$. Expanding $|R_b+\xi-(X(s)-X(s_b))|$ to second order,

$$
G=y(t)-\tfrac12\kappa\,\delta s^2+\frac{(\xi_\perp\cdot V_\perp)\,\delta s}{\tau_b}-\frac{|\xi_\perp|^2}{2\tau_b}+O(\delta s^3/\tau_b^2),\qquad y(t):=\delta t-n_b\cdot\xi(t)=\int_{t_b}^{t}\bigl(1-n_b\cdot v(t')\bigr)\,dt' .
$$

Here $\perp$ means perpendicular to $n_b$. The cross terms are higher order (see self-consistency), so the two newborn roots sit at $\delta s=\pm\sqrt{2y/\kappa}$, and since $\delta t=O(\delta s^2)$, $\delta\tau=\mp\sqrt{2y/\kappa}$.

**(a) Growth of $|D|$.** $D=\partial_\tau G=\kappa\,\delta s$ at leading order, so on both newborn rows

$$
|D|=\sqrt{2\kappa\,y(t)},\qquad y(t)=\int_{t_b}^{t}(1-n_b\cdot v)\,dt' .
$$

If the receiver's velocity did not change this would be $|D|=\sqrt{2\kappa(1-n_b\cdot v_b)(t-t_b)}$: square-root growth. The receiver's velocity does change by order one over the interval of interest, so the variable that matters is the lag $y$, not $t-t_b$.

**(b) Equations of motion.** The two newborn rows have $n=n_b\mp V_\perp\delta s/\tau_b$, $\tau=\tau_b\mp\delta s$, equal $|D|$ at leading order, so their sum is $2\sigma n_b/(\tau_b^2\sqrt{2\kappa y})$: along $n_b$, with the first-order transverse parts cancelling. With $u:=n_b\cdot v$ and $c:=\sqrt{2/\kappa}/\tau_b^2$,

$$
\dot v_\perp=0,\qquad \dot y=1-u,\qquad \dot u=\frac{\sigma c}{\sqrt y},\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad \ddot y=-\frac{\sigma c}{\sqrt y},\quad y(t_b)=0,\ \dot y(t_b)=m_0:=1-n_b\cdot v_b .
$$

First integral: $\tfrac12\dot y^2+2\sigma c\sqrt y=\tfrac12m_0^2$, that is $(1-u)^2=m_0^2-4\sigma c\sqrt y$.

**(c) Time to speed 1.** The transverse velocity is conserved, so $|v|=1$ when $u=\sigma w$ with $w:=\sqrt{1-|v_{\perp}|^2}$ (like polarities, $\sigma=+1$, drive $u$ up to $+w$; opposite polarities, $\sigma=-1$, drive $u$ down to $-w$). Let $m:=1-u=\dot y$ and $m_*:=1-\sigma w$. From the first integral $\sqrt y=\sigma(m_0^2-m^2)/(4c)$, so $\dot u=4c^2/(m_0^2-m^2)$ and

$$
t_*-t_b=\frac{1}{4c^2}\int_{m_*}^{m_0}(m_0^2-m^2)\,dm=\frac{\kappa\,\tau_b^4}{24}\,(m_0-m_*)^2\,(2m_0+m_*),\qquad m_0=1-n_b\cdot v_b,\quad m_*=1-\sigma\sqrt{1-|v_\perp|^2}.
$$

State at the arrival, same order: $y_*=\kappa\tau_b^4(m_0^2-m_*^2)^2/32$; emission-time half-separation of the newborn pair $q_*=\sqrt{2y_*/\kappa}=\tau_b^2|m_0^2-m_*^2|/4$; $|D|_*=\kappa q_*=\kappa\tau_b^2|m_0^2-m_*^2|/4$; magnitude of the acceleration $|a|_*=2/(\tau_b^2|D|_*)=8/(\kappa\tau_b^4|m_0^2-m_*^2|)$; rate of change of the speed $d|v|/dt=w\,|a|_*$ (positive for both polarities).

Special case, receiver at rest ($m_0=1$, $w=1$): like polarities $m_*=0$, $t_*-t_b=\kappa\tau_b^4/12$, $|D|_*=\kappa\tau_b^2/4$; opposite polarities $m_*=2$, $t_*-t_b=\kappa\tau_b^4/6$, $|D|_*=3\kappa\tau_b^2/4$. Straight source path at constant speed $V>1$, perpendicular distance $b$: $\tau_b=bV/\sqrt{V^2-1}$, $\kappa=(V^2-1)/\tau_b$, $\kappa\tau_b^4=b^3V^3/\sqrt{V^2-1}$, so $t_*-t_b=b^3V^3/(12\sqrt{V^2-1})$ for like and $b^3V^3/(6\sqrt{V^2-1})$ for opposite polarities.

**Continuation past the arrival (like polarities), same local system.** For $\sigma=+1$ and $v_\perp=0$ the arrival is at $\dot y=0$, the maximum of $y$. If the same two rows keep acting, $y$ then decreases back to zero, the pair merges, and the first integral gives $\dot y=-m_0$ at the merger, that is $u=2-n_b\cdot v_b$ (a receiver initially at rest leaves at speed 2 along $n_b$), after total time $\kappa\tau_b^4m_0^3/6$ from the birth. For $\sigma=-1$ no merger follows at this order; $y$ keeps growing.

**Self-consistency.** The treatment keeps only the quadratic term of the source-receiver distance in emission time and freezes $n$, $\tau$ at their birth values. It is self-consistent when (i) $q_*=\tau_b^2|m_0^2-m_*^2|/4$ is small against the emission-time scale $\ell$ over which the second derivative of the source-receiver distance varies, and (ii) $t_*-t_b\ll\tau_b$. For a straight source path $\ell=\min(\tau_b,\ b/V)$ (closest approach is only $\tau_b/V^2$ after the newborn emission point, and the fourth-order coefficient is $3\kappa(5-V^2)/\tau_b^2$), so both conditions read $\tau_b\,|m_0^2-m_*^2|\,V^2/\sqrt{V^2-1}\ll4$: roughly $\tau_b\ll1$ for moderate $V$ and $V\tau_b\ll1$ for large $V$, in units of $K/c_f^2$. For a curved path one also needs the source acceleration to vary slowly over $q_*$. Further hypotheses used: $|v|<1$ up to the arrival; no other root contributes comparably (an older root with delay $\tau_o$ and $|D_o|$ of order one changes the velocity by about $(t_*-t_b)/\tau_o^2$, which must be small against the order-one change produced by the newborn pair); no second birth or merger in the interval; the source path is prescribed (in a mutual problem the source's own response must be shown not to change $\kappa$ at this order).

**Expected size of the correction (inferred, not derived in full).** The summed pair contribution is a symmetric function of the two roots of a near-quadratic, so odd powers of $q$ cancel and the relative correction should start at order $q_*^2/\ell^2$ and $(t_*-t_b)/\tau_b$, that is second order in $\tau_b$ at fixed $V$ and fixed velocity data (with a coefficient growing like $V^2$ for the straight path), not first order. Falsifier: a measured relative difference between the integrated arrival time and the formula that scales linearly in $b$ at fixed $V$.

**The self-consistency statement.** The note compares the displacement of the roots with $\tau_b$ and concludes that $\kappa$, and so the source speed, drops out. The comparison length is wrong for a fast source. For the straight path the symbolic check gives $\partial_\tau^3g=3\kappa/\tau_b$ and $\partial_\tau^4g=3\kappa(V^2-5)/\tau_b^2$ at the birth (derived; `derivative_check.log`), so the quartic term is small against the quadratic one only when $V^2(Q\tau_b/4)^2\ll1$. Independently, $t_*/\tau_b=\kappa\tau_b^3(y_*-y_0)^2(y_*+2y_0)/24$, so the time is short against the delay only when $\kappa\tau_b^3\ll1$; and $\kappa\tau_b^3=(V^2-1)\tau_b^2=b^2V^2$ exactly on a straight path. Physically, a fast source is past the receiver within a time $b/V$ of the newborn emission (closest approach is $\tau_b/V^2$ after it), so the square-root regime ends early. A second, exact statement sets the scale of what the passage supplies: on each root branch $dt=\lvert D\rvert\,ds$ at fixed receiver position, so the time integral of all rows on a receiver held in place equals $\sigma\int\mathbf n\,ds/\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(s)\rvert^2$ along the source path, which for the straight path is $2\sigma/(Vb)$ across the path, for every $V>1$ (derived; confirmed by quadrature of my row sum to five digits at three $(V,b)$, `extra_checks.log`). The leading-order law needs this sum to be large, $Vb\ll1$. It is not a bound once the receiver moves: like-polarity receivers, which move with the arriving wake, reached wake speed at $Vb=3$ in my runs.

**One correction to my own blind text.** My blind self-consistency paragraph gave the comparison scale for the straight path as $\min(\tau_b,b/V)$ and the combined condition as $\tau_b\lvert m_0^2-m_*^2\rvert V^2/\sqrt{V^2-1}\ll4$. The large-$V$ reading, $V\tau_b\ll1$, is right and is what the measurements show. The small-excess reading ($V\to1$), which would demand $\tau_b\ll\sqrt{V^2-1}$, is not supported by the derivatives above, which give the scale $\tau_b/\max(1,V)$; I did not test $V$ close to one. The blind expectation that the correction is second order in $\tau_b$ is confirmed.

**Theorem U.** I read the statement at line 9 and the Scope paragraph of [the obstruction note](curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions.md), not the proofs. (U1) asks for $v<1$ at every earlier time, a continuous velocity up to the event with speed one there, and $1-v(s)\ge a\lvert s\rvert$ on an interval before it. The local law supplies a positive, finite rate of change of the speed at the arrival and continuity of the velocity on the open interval after the birth, which give the last two on any interval $[-\eta,0]$ with $\eta<t_*$; my runs show the rate positive in every arriving case, including those far outside the leading-order regime (measured). It does not supply $v<1$ over the whole earlier history: that is an assumption about the receiver before the birth and must be stated, because it is what removes own-path rows from (U2). (U2) asks that the sum of rows from other architrinos be bounded near the event and continuous at it along the path and its continuation. Bounded holds because $\lvert D_*\rvert=\kappa Q\tau_b^2/4>0$ (derived at leading order; measured within $\pm Q\tau_b/4$ of that value here). Continuous along any continuation holds because a row is a function of the receiver's position and the source's path only, provided no other root degenerates at that instant and the sources' velocities are continuous at the emission points; the note does not say this and should. The theorem's own Scope paragraph limits "no continuation" to the stated class (velocity continuous at the event and absolutely continuous after it, ordinary roots); the note's wording "excludes every continuation with continuous velocity and ordinary roots" and "no continuation exists with velocity continuous at the arrival and absolutely continuous after it, with ordinary roots" respects that. The item "Not covered (4)" of the theorem (a partner root born at the event) does not apply, since the arrival is later than the birth by $t_*>0$. So the use is justified once conditioned as in correction 6, and it inherits the leading-order grade and the corrected domain.

**Other points checked and found sound.** The statement that $y_*$ is reached for both polarities; the sign bookkeeping in $dt=\lvert y^2-y_0^2\rvert\,dy/(4c^2)$; "a like-polarity receiver is driven to wake speed sooner than an opposite one" for the receiver at rest (measured ratio of the two intervals $0.5000$ at small $\kappa\tau_b^3$); the remark that for like polarities and $v_\perp=0$ the arrival is at the instant $dG/dt=0$; $\kappa>0$ at small delay for bounded source acceleration. The point with $V_0=2$, $a=+400$, $\tau_b=0.03$ in my own test set has $\kappa=-100$ and is a merger, as the note's criterion says.

## Numerical test

**Instrument.** `birth_adjudication.py`, written for this adjudication without reading or reusing any other code under `.local-data`. Prescribed source path (straight at constant speed; straight with constant acceleration; circle of radius $0.05$ at speed 3), free receiver in the plane, acceleration equal to the sum of the rows $\sigma\mathbf n/(\tau^2\lvert D\rvert)$ over all causal roots of that one source. Float arithmetic, $K=c_f=1$.

**Birth instant.** The run starts on the birth point itself, constructed in closed form: choose an emission point, a unit direction $\mathbf n$ with $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V=1$ there and a delay $\tau_b$, and place the receiver at $\mathbf X+\tau_b\mathbf n$ at $t_b=0$. Residuals at the birth are $\lvert g\rvert\le2\times10^{-18}$ and $\lvert D\rvert\le3\times10^{-16}$. Because the source is prescribed, the receiver's acceleration is a function of its position and the time only, so its earlier history does not enter; for the straight source arriving from far away there is no root before the birth and a receiver at rest is exactly the state used. For the circle and for the line with $a=+400$ older roots exist and their rows are included after the birth.

**Resolving the square-root growth.** Time variable $s=\sqrt{t-t_b}$, in which the equations of motion are regular; fixed-step fourth-order Runge-Kutta in $s$ with step $\sqrt{t_*}/N$, $N=150,300,600$ (and up to $2400$ on five cases). The first interval $[0,s_0]$, $s_0=\epsilon\sqrt{t_*}$, uses the leading local solution; $\epsilon=10^{-3}$ by default, with $10^{-2}$ and $10^{-4}$ as controls. The arrival $\lvert\mathbf v\rvert=1$ is located by bisection on the fraction of the crossing step.

**Roots.** At every evaluation the critical points of $g$ in $\tau$ are found from sign changes of $D$ on a fixed grid of 3200 delays (geometric and linear) refined by Brent's method, and each root of $g$ is then bracketed between consecutive critical points and solved by Brent's method to relative tolerance $9\times10^{-16}$. The two newborn roots are therefore always separated by the located maximum of $g$.

## Limits

- **Same model family.** The author of the note and this adjudicator are instances of the same model family, working in one research session's lineage. Agreement between us is not independent evidence in the strong sense: we share training, habits of derivation and likely blind spots. A reader should weigh the items below by what each rests on.
- **What is independent of the author's work.** The blind derivation was completed and written to this file before the note was opened; the brief had told me only the row, the definition of a birth, and that a closed formula for the arrival time in terms of $\tau_b$, the receiver's velocity, the polarities and a local property of the source path was wanted. The integrator is new code that shares no line with `release4.py` or `birth_impulse.py`, which I did not open, and it tests a different physical setting (prescribed source, one free receiver) on cases of my choosing, including like polarities, which the note had not measured. Its checks against closed forms (static-source fall, quadratic roots, $2/(Vb)$) do not depend on the note.
- **What is not independent.** The brief disclosed the note's variable names $y_0$, $v_\perp$, $\kappa=-\partial^2g/\partial\tau^2$ and that the note claims the difference scales like $\tau_b$, so the form of the answer was partly signposted.
