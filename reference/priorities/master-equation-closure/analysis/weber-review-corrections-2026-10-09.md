# Weber investigations: corrections and scope boundaries from the 2026-10-09 review

**Status: governing correction addendum, written 2026-10-09.** Codex reviewed the Weber investigations on 2026-10-09 at the operator's direction and returned seven comments, W1 to W7, with one independent calculation. This document records the disposition of each, states the corrected text, and governs wherever it differs from a source it names. The sources themselves are left byte for byte as they were: they are investigation records and comparison documents, several of them one side of an independent comparison, and a correction written into one side would blur what each side originally said. The living summaries that repeated an affected statement were revised on the same date and are listed in Section 9. Nothing here selects an equation, changes a queue rank, or adopts anything into canon. The [canonical Master Equation](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/master-equation.md#the-master-equation-canonical-form) remains the baseline; Sections 9 and 9a of the [equation-variants manuscript](../equation-variants/manuscript.md#9-weber-inspired-relative-motion-response) are research comparisons.

Notation follows that manuscript. $T$ is absolute reception time and $S$ the emission time of a hit; $\mathscr R$ is the delayed range of a hit, $\mathbf n$ its unit line of action from emission point to receiver, $D_t=c_f-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j(S)$ the transmitter factor, $\sigma=\pm1$ the sign of the polarity product and $K>0$ the coupling. For a pair, $r$ is the present separation, $\mathbf e$ the present unit direction from partner to receiver, and $h=\|\mathbf r\times\dot{\mathbf r}\|$ the angular quantity of the relative motion. All numerical instances use $c_f=K=1$.

What the reviewer did and did not do: Codex read the definition and authorization owners and the analyses named below and inspected existing receipts. It did not rerun the large campaigns and did not certify the interval arithmetic of the six-member ring classification. Its one new calculation is recorded in Section 3.

## 1. W1: orbital period of the instantaneous pair circle (accepted)

Source: [frequency treatment](../binary-research/analysis/weber-frequency-continuation.md), Section 2, the sentence after equation (2.4). It prints $P=2\pi/\Omega=\pi\sqrt{d^3/(2K)}$. The balance condition in the same sentence is $\Omega^2=2K/d^3$, so

$$
P=\frac{2\pi}{\Omega}=2\pi\sqrt{\frac{d^3}{2K}} .
$$

The printed closed form is too small by a factor of two. At $K=1$, $d=2$ the balance gives $\Omega=1/2$ and $P=4\pi$; the printed expression gives $2\pi$. Grade: derived, by substitution. The error is confined to that one expression: a fixed-string search for `pi\sqrt{d^3/(2K)}` under `reference/` and `content/` on 2026-10-09 found it only in that sentence, the [independent reference](../binary-research/analysis/weber-frequency-independent-reference.md) states the period as $2\pi/\Omega$, and the tabulated rows and perturbation runs of the treatment were generated from $\Omega$ and the closed forms (2.3), (2.4), (3.2), (4.3) and (4.5), none of which is the faulty expression. Falsifier: a declared primitive-period convention under which half a revolution returns the labeled opposite-polarity configuration to itself; it does not, because the two members differ in polarity.

## 2. W2: two transcription defects in the delayed-pair comparison (accepted)

**Subject document.** In the [pair investigation](../binary-research/analysis/weber-delayed-pair-investigation.md), Section 2, the displayed right-hand side $\mathbf b_i$ of the implicit system $M_i\mathbf A_i=\mathbf b_i$ omits a factor $c_f$. Collecting the terms of Section 9a that do not contain the receiver's present acceleration gives

$$
\mathbf b_i=\sum_{j,S}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K\,c_f\,\mathbf n}{\mathscr R^2|D_t|}\left[1-\frac{(1-p)^2}{2}+\frac{\|\mathbf w_\perp\|^2-\mathscr R\,p^2\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A_j(S)}{c_f D_t}\right],
$$

with $p=dS/dT$ and $\mathbf w_\perp$ as defined there. The block $M_i=I-\sum\sigma_{ij}K\,\mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T}/(\mathscr R|D_t|D_t)$ is printed correctly, and it is consistent only with the prefactor that carries $c_f$. The independent reference has the factor in its Section 1a. Every run used $c_f=1$, so the omission is a symbolic transcription defect and does not bear on any number.

**Reference document.** In the [independent reference](../binary-research/analysis/weber-delayed-pair-independent-reference.md), the last paragraph of Section 1c gives the sum of the two members' first-order terms as $-\sigma K[\dot{\mathbf r}-2\dot r\,\mathbf e]/(r^2c_f)$. That expression is the first-order part of the relative acceleration $\mathbf A_1-\mathbf A_2$, not of the sum. The first-order term on receiver $i$ is $\sigma K[\mathbf v_j-2(\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf v_j)\mathbf e]/(r^2c_f)$; exchanging the members reverses $\mathbf e$, which leaves the bracket's form unchanged, so

$$
\mathbf A_{1\leftarrow2}+\mathbf A_{2\leftarrow1}\Big|_{1/c_f}=\frac{\sigma K}{r^2c_f}\Big[(\mathbf v_1+\mathbf v_2)-2\big(\mathbf e\cdot(\mathbf v_1+\mathbf v_2)\big)\mathbf e\Big],
\qquad
\mathbf A_{1\leftarrow2}-\mathbf A_{2\leftarrow1}\Big|_{1/c_f}=-\frac{\sigma K}{r^2c_f}\Big[\dot{\mathbf r}-2\dot r\,\mathbf e\Big].
$$

The sum vanishes exactly when $\mathbf v_1+\mathbf v_2=\mathbf 0$, as the subject's Corollary 4.3 says, and in particular on every mirror preparation; the reference's printed expression does not vanish there. The defect is in that sentence of prose. The reference's code and its numerical checks are not affected by it, since they evaluate the law directly.

**Consequence for the recorded agreement.** Section 8 of the investigation says the two lanes' derivations "agree in every displayed formula". With the two defects above that sentence is too strong. The supported statement is: the two lanes agree on the range derivatives, the present-acceleration block, the rigid-circle results, and the first- and second-order expansion of the acceleration on one receiver; each lane has one displayed expression that the other states correctly. Grade of both corrections: derived, by direct collection of terms; both were re-derived by the writing session on 2026-10-09 before acceptance. Falsifier: a direct expansion of the Section 9a law that reproduces either printed expression.

## 3. W3: what the first proposal's expansion establishes (accepted), with an independent check of its circle coefficient

Source: the [slow-motion expansion of the first proposed adaptation](../binary-research/analysis/weber-delayed-p1-slow-expansion.md), called P1 there, which aims each hit from the partner's position extrapolated at its emission velocity. Its opening, its verdict table and its closing prose say that the pair "still expands", is not bound, and that no bound class is restored. No history was evolved under P1 and no remainder bound controls the long-time drift.

**Established.** (a) The first-order transverse term vanishes on every history with the regularity stated in Section 4 below (derived, checked numerically by the author on prescribed histories). (b) On the rigid mirror circle the tangential acceleration is strictly positive for every $0<\beta<1$, so no exact subfield circle exists, and its leading value is $+K\beta^3/(3R_0^2)$ (derived). (c) On a slow orbit the leading tangential term is forward, equation (3.1) of the source (derived as a leading-order statement).

**Inferred, and to be read as such wherever it is quoted.** The estimate $d(R_0^3)/dT=K^2/(2c_f^3)$ and the sentence that the pair expands without bound are a formal near-circular inference. It is conditional on the pair continuing to track a slow circle for as long as the estimate is applied. It does not exclude a bounded history that is not nearly circular or not a mirror pair, and it is not a proof that every history disperses. The source's own table grades this row inferred; the unqualified sentences elsewhere in it, and in summaries, are narrowed to this.

**The statement about what a binding law would need.** Section 5 of the source says a binding delayed law must contain an acceleration-driven term across the extrapolated line of action. Its scope is: laws on the canonical causal roots that are radial about the extrapolated position with a scalar factor of the class in Section 5 below, assessed on the slow mirror circle. Within that scope the leading tangential term is fixed and forward. Outside it nothing is claimed.

**The closing recommendation.** The queued [case for closing the delayed Weber family](../binary-research/work-queue.md#review-the-case-for-closing-the-delayed-weber-family-codex) is an agenda recommendation at inferred grade. It is not a proof that every adaptation fails or that no bound class exists. Codex agrees with giving further Weber adaptations low priority on this evidence. The decision remains the operator's.

**Independent check of (b), by Codex, 2026-10-09.** Codex computed the prescribed-circle geometry in a separately written Node calculation that imported no subject instrument. With $c_f=K=R_0=1$ and root half-angle $\xi=\beta\cos\xi$, the receiver is at $(1,0)$, the partner's emission position is $(-\cos2\xi,\sin2\xi)$ and its emission velocity is $(-\beta\sin2\xi,-\beta\cos2\xi)$; the chord and transmitter factor are computed directly and the extrapolated chord normalized. Known case first: on the canonical circle at $\beta=0.01$ it returned $A_\theta/\beta=0.24998333616613028$, within $10^{-4}$ of the known coefficient $1/4$, with root residual zero. Targets: $A_\theta/\beta^3=0.3317940251$, $0.3329471283$, $0.3332366955$, $0.3333091685$ at $\beta=0.04$, $0.02$, $0.01$, $0.005$, with root residual at most $4.5\times10^{-16}$. Codex also gave a separate proof of the sign: multiplying the numerator by $\cos\xi>0$ gives $g(\xi)=\tfrac12\sin2\xi-\xi\cos2\xi$, with $g(0)=0$ and $g'(\xi)=2\xi\sin2\xi>0$ on $0<\xi<\pi/2$; expanding $\xi=\beta-\beta^3/2+O(\beta^5)$ gives the numerator $4\beta^3/3+O(\beta^5)$ and hence the coefficient $+1/3$. That calculation was not rerun by the writing session; the numbers are quoted from Codex's review entry. This raises result (b) from self-reviewed to independently checked for the circle coefficient and its sign. It measures the geometry of a prescribed circle and says nothing about evolution, so the inferred items above stay inferred. Falsifier of (b): a zero of the exact tangential residual on $0<\beta<1$, or a slow mirror circle on which $A_\theta R_0^2/(K\beta^3)$ does not approach $+1/3$.

## 4. W4: hypotheses that the proofs use (accepted)

**Root census, Lemma 2.1 of the pair investigation.** The lemma is stated for members with "speed below $c_f$ on the whole history", and its proof uses a bound $v_{\max}<c_f$ that holds uniformly over the entire past. Pointwise subfield speed is not sufficient. Counterexample in one dimension with $c_f=1$ and reception time $T=0$: the receiver is at rest at the origin and the partner follows $X_j(S)=-S+1+e^{S}$ for $S\le0$. Its speed $|-1+e^{S}|$ is below one at every finite time and the separation is positive, yet the arrival residual $\mathscr R-c_f(T-S)=1+e^{S}$ is positive for every $S<0$, so no partner root exists. The writing session verified the residual directly. The corrected hypothesis is: both members have speeds bounded by one constant $v_{\max}<c_f$ on the whole history up to $T$, with positive separation. Under it the conclusions of Lemma 2.1 and Lemma 2.2 stand as printed. The investigation's Definition 5.1 already imposes a uniform margin $D_t\ge\eta c_f$ on its evolution domain, so no run and no later result depends on the weaker wording. Grade: derived counterexample. A later [audit of root-existence hypotheses](root-census-hypothesis-audit-2026-10-09.md) separates what pointwise speed gives (at most one root, no own-path root) from what existence needs, lists the sufficient conditions, and records that the same defect had been found and corrected in another document on 2026-10-05.

**Remainder in equation (2.1) of the P1 expansion.** The source grades (2.1), with remainder $O(\epsilon^4)$, as derived "on a history with three continuous derivatives". Three continuous derivatives give a remainder $o(\tau^3)$ after expanding position and velocity, not a fourth-order bound. The stated order requires a bounded fourth derivative of the transmitter's position, or a Lipschitz third derivative, with the slow scaling $r^3\|\mathbf X_j''''\|/c_f^4\sim\epsilon^4$. With only three continuous derivatives the remainder in (2.1) is $o(\epsilon^3)$. The numerical check in the source used polynomial histories, for which the stronger hypothesis holds, and its observed fourth-order convergence is consistent with this. Grade: derived, a standard Taylor regularity distinction.

## 5. W5: the class of scalar factors (accepted)

Corollary 4.4 of the pair investigation concerns a law $\sigma Kc_fB\,\mathbf n/(\mathscr R^2|D_t|)$ with a scalar bracket $B=1+O(1/c_f)$. Summaries shortened this to "no scalar bracket, with any coefficients, removes the forward push". The hypothesis on $B$ is part of the statement. If a scalar factor has leading limit $B_0$ on the slow state, the first-order transverse term is multiplied by $B_0$: it is unchanged for $B_0=1$, keeps its sign for $B_0>0$, and is removed or reversed by a factor that vanishes or changes sign there, at the price of no longer having the inverse-square leading response. The corrected summary is: no regular scalar bracket with unit leading limit, placed on the delayed line of action, changes the first-order transverse term.

The same restriction applies to Section 5 of the P1 expansion, which says the third-order tangential term is unchanged "whatever scalar factor multiplies it". That holds for a regular scalar factor with unit leading limit on the circle. A leading limit $B_0$ multiplies the coefficient $+1/3$ by $B_0$. The selected Weber bracket equals one exactly on a rigid circle, so the results for Section 9a and for P1 stand. No other scalar factor is proposed or examined here. Grade: derived, from the form of the law. A separate [exact-circle exclusion](../binary-research/analysis/weber-scalar-multiplier-circle-exclusion.md), written later the same day, shows that for rigid mirror circles at $0<\beta\le1$ no finite scalar multiplier admits the circle. The class restriction of this section therefore applies to the size of the push and not to the existence of an exact circle. That exclusion is derived and was independently reviewed by Codex on 2026-10-09.

## 6. W6: pair diagnostics inside a six-member evolution (accepted)

Source: [binding-sphere investigation](../braid-program/analysis/weber-binding-sphere-investigation.md), Sections R5.1, R5.2 and S5. The runs are classified as "separates into binaries" because, at each run's stop, one or more opposite-polarity pairs have a negative isolated-pair energy-like value $\varepsilon$ and nonzero $h$. Those two quantities are invariants of an isolated pair under Section 9. In a six-member system the other members accelerate each pair, so they are not conserved there. The source's own C2 #2 run shows the limit of the test: two pairs sharing a member both pass it at the stop, so negativity at an endpoint does not by itself define a partition into disjoint binaries.

The statements are therefore to be read as follows. Measured, on the recorded windows: the configurations break within the first period, pairs form, and separations between the groups grow to the recorded values. Diagnostic: "bound by the pair invariants" is an instantaneous bound-pair test evaluated at the stop. Inferred: that the products are isolated binaries of the instantaneous bound class. Raising that to measured would need persistent separation from every other member together with a bound on the differential acceleration the others exert across the pair. Step-capped runs establish their windows only and no later fate. Falsifier of the inferred classification: a diagnosed pair that later separates under the changing external accelerations. No new run is needed to state this boundary, and none was made.

**Why the pair quantities are not conserved, as an identity.** The derivation was supplied by Codex on 2026-10-09 and verified by the writing session by direct differentiation. For an opposite-polarity pair $i,j$ under the instantaneous Section 9 law with the frozen coefficients, let $\mathbf r=\mathbf X_i-\mathbf X_j$, $\mathbf w=\dot{\mathbf r}$ and $\mathbf h=\mathbf r\times\mathbf w$, and let $\mathbf f_{\mathrm{ext}}$ be the acceleration that all other members contribute to $i$ minus the acceleration they contribute to $j$. The pair's own contributions are equal and opposite along $\mathbf e$, so $\ddot{\mathbf r}=-(2K/r^2)\big[1-\dot r^2/(2c_f^2)+r\ddot r/c_f^2\big]\mathbf e+\mathbf f_{\mathrm{ext}}$. With the kinematic relation $\ddot r=\mathbf e\cdot\ddot{\mathbf r}+\|\mathbf h\|^2/r^3$ and $D=1+2K/(c_f^2r)$,

$$
D\ddot r=\frac{\|\mathbf h\|^2}{r^3}-\frac{2K}{r^2}+\frac{K\dot r^2}{c_f^2r^2}+\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf f_{\mathrm{ext}},\qquad
\dot{\mathbf h}=\mathbf r\times\mathbf f_{\mathrm{ext}} .
$$

For $\varepsilon=\tfrac12D\dot r^2+\|\mathbf h\|^2/(2r^2)-2K/r$, differentiating and substituting cancels every isolated-pair term, and the identity $(\mathbf r\times\mathbf w)\cdot(\mathbf r\times\mathbf f)=r^2\,\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf f-r^2\dot r\,\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf f$ leaves

$$
\dot\varepsilon=\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf f_{\mathrm{ext}} .
$$

Setting $\mathbf f_{\mathrm{ext}}=\mathbf 0$ recovers both isolated-pair invariants. Nothing is assumed about the form of $\mathbf f_{\mathrm{ext}}$: it is whatever the other members actually contribute under the same implicit law. Grade: derived. The identity states what a stronger finite-window claim would need. Bounds on $\int\mathbf w\cdot\mathbf f_{\mathrm{ext}}\,dT$ and on $\int\mathbf r\times\mathbf f_{\mathrm{ext}}\,dT$ over a recorded window, if small enough relative to the starting margins of $\varepsilon$ and $\mathbf h$, would keep $\varepsilon\le-\delta<0$ and $\|\mathbf h\|\ge\eta>0$ on that window and so establish persistence of the instantaneous isolated-pair diagnostic there. Because the first two terms of $\varepsilon$ are nonnegative, that persistence bounds the pair's own separation on the window, $\eta^2/(4K)<r\le2K/\delta$. It does not make the pair's motion an isolated-pair trajectory under the six-member law, and separation of the pair from the other members must still be read from the six-member evolution. It cannot turn a value at an endpoint into a statement about all later times, because the external terms after the window are not controlled. The recorded runs report growth of the separations between groups over their windows; "escape" in the sense of an asymptotic fate is not established by them. A later [evaluation on one retained run](../braid-program/analysis/weber-pair-external-acceleration-window.md) applied this identity: it found the diagnostic persistent at every recorded state of a ten-period window and the external term far larger than a static estimate suggests, because the law's acceleration term couples separated pairs at the first power of their distance. Codex reproduced the sampled results independently. Two dense continuations failed a convergence test fixed before launch, so these checks establish no converged window-integral result and no certificate of persistence between recorded states.

## 7. W7: wording of the finite-harmonic lemma (accepted as a clarification)

Source: [great-circle closure](../braid-program/analysis/weber-binding-sphere-great-circle-closure.md), Section 20.1, the proof that a point turning as $\dot{\mathbf X}=\mathbf D\times\mathbf X$ with constant $\mathbf D$ moves "on the circle of the sphere at axial distance $v/\|\mathbf D\|$". The quantity $v/\|\mathbf D\|$ is the radius of that circle, that is, the point's perpendicular distance from the axis. The signed coordinate of the circle's plane along the axis is $\mathbf X\cdot\hat{\mathbf D}=kR/\|\mathbf D\|$, and the two satisfy $(v/\|\mathbf D\|)^2+(kR/\|\mathbf D\|)^2=R^2$. "Axial distance" is used in cylindrical coordinates for the distance from the axis, so the printed phrase is defensible, but it can be read as a position along the axis, which would be wrong. Read it as: the circle of radius $v/\|\mathbf D\|$ about the axis $\hat{\mathbf D}$, in the plane at signed axial coordinate $kR/\|\mathbf D\|$. The degree argument and the constant-$\mathbf D$ proof are unaffected, and the theorem is not in question.
