## 3. Signed leading comparison and an oscillatory corrector

On the leading near-circular comparison put $z=w/H^2$, $P=Hp$, $\omega=H^{-3}$ and $\alpha=H'/H=\gamma H^{-6}$. The centered cubic coefficient has positive sign because

$$

Consequently the leading linear system, with an explicit additive radial input $F$, is

$$
z'=\omega P-2\alpha z,\qquad
P'=-\omega z+5\alpha P+F.

$$
C'=(-i\omega+\tfrac32\alpha)C-\tfrac72\alpha\overline C+iF.
$$

The conjugate term rotates at twice the radial frequency after passing to the oscillating frame. It cannot be replaced pointwise by zero. To remove its leading contribution define

$$
D=C-\frac{7i\alpha}{4\omega}\overline C.
$$

Direct differentiation cancels the leading conjugate coefficient. The residual homogeneous coefficient is $O(\alpha^2/\omega+|(\alpha/\omega)'|)$, and the extra input is $O((\alpha/\omega)F)$. ## 4. Why the accepted norm remainder cannot certify the late seed

The accepted centered error yields an additive normalized radial input of size

$$
|F|\le C\epsilon^4H^{-7}.
$$

Even in the favorable linear comparison, its contribution to the growing-mode transport is bounded by

$$
\left|\int H^{-3/2}e^{i\Theta}F\,ds\right|
\le C\epsilon\int_{H_0}^{H}u^{-7/2}\,du
\le C\epsilon.
$$

The initial transported seed is only $W_0=-i(8/3)\epsilon^3+O(\epsilon^4)$. Thus the already accepted $O(\epsilon)$ norm bound on the forcing integral exceeds the actual seed by two powers of $\epsilon$. The upper bound cannot be subtracted from that seed to obtain a positive lower bound. A constant radial input is nonresonant, but the norm bound does not say the actual delayed remainder is constant in phase.

An elementary control makes this insufficiency explicit. In $w''+w=f(s)$ with zero initial data, $f=b$ gives $w=b(1-\cos s)$, while $f=b\cos s$ gives $w=(b/2)s\sin s$. Substitution verifies both identities. A smooth resonant input of size $O(\epsilon^4)$ lasting $O(\epsilon^{-1})$ can therefore alter a mode of size $O(\epsilon^3)$. Its derivative is also $O(\epsilon^4)$, so imposing the accepted first-derivative remainder bound does not eliminate it. Two adjustable sine and cosine inputs, with smooth finite envelopes, can cancel both transported components in the linear comparison. During this duration $H$ changes only by $O(\epsilon^2)$, so the changing-scale bounds do not resolve the issue either.

This is a witness against a proof from the remainder bounds alone. It is not a counterexample to the exact amplitude-gradient equation: its remainder is determined by the coupled path and cannot be chosen as an external input. No external input has been added to the selected scenario. The proof burden is precisely to establish that the actual remainder has no sufficiently large resonant projection, or to calculate that projection and show it reinforces the seed.

### 6.5 Independent nonlinear cycle coefficient

The nonlinear coefficient is computable without using the small-oscillation approximation. In the central comparison define $E_0=|y'|^2/2-1/r$ and $e^2=1+2E_0h^2$. $$
\frac{h'}h=\frac\gamma{r^3},\qquad

\left[-(r')^2+2\left(\frac hr\right)^2-\frac2r\right].
$$

Integration over one frozen central cycle yields

$$
\Delta\log h=\frac{2\pi\gamma}{h^3},\qquad
\Delta e^2=\frac{6\pi\gamma e^2}{h^3}.

For the second identity the numerator of its angular integrand is $2e\cos\theta+e^2(5\cos^2\theta-1)+e^3\cos\theta(3\cos^2\theta-1)$, whose integral is $3\pi e^2$. No small-$e$ expansion is used. The nonlinear leading cycle ratio is therefore

at first cubic order, uniformly on every fixed compact eccentricity range bounded away from $e=1$. It agrees with the small-amplitude exponent and remains positive throughout that range.
