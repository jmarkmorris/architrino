# Persistence of a lattice arrival under weighted perturbations of the past

## Result

**In words.** For a finite population, the [cross-geometry comparison](saturday-cross-geometry-comparison-2026-10-10.md#theorem-2-an-arrival-at-a-positive-rate-is-stable) proves that an arrival at wake speed with positive incoming rate and positive separation persists: every supplied past close enough to the reference past ends in an arrival of the same kind at a nearby time. Closeness there is uniform in position and velocity. For the infinite alternating lattice that notion of closeness cannot work, because the prescribed lattice sum is not even defined on uniform neighbourhoods of the resting past (the accepted [delayed-summation obstruction](population-delayed-summation.md#ordering-and-partition-scope) and its [sharp form](saturday-cross-geometry-continuation-2026-10-20-sharpness.md)). This note proves the persistence statement for the lattice in a topology that does work: the perturbation of every member's past, together with its velocity and acceleration, must be small and must decay exponentially into the past at a fixed rate $\mu>0$, uniformly over all members.

The mechanism is the one that makes the [fixed-past field theorem](saturday-cross-geometry-continuation-2026-10-20.md#4-theorem-a-the-far-members-act-as-a-fixed-field) work. A member at anchor distance $R$ is seen by the receiver as it was a time of about $R$ ago, so a perturbation that decays like $e^{\mu s}$ into the past reaches the receiver multiplied by $e^{-\mu R}$, and the sum over the lattice converges. The difference between the fields of two histories is then bounded by a constant times the weighted size of their difference (Lemma D). With that, the finite-population proof goes through: continuous dependence up to just before the event, fields on fixed tubes built from the known prefix, comparison paths, and a transverse crossing.

One point is new with infinitely many members, and the coherent staggered branch shows that it is not hypothetical, since there every label arrives at once. Near the event each member moves in a field fixed by the past, so each has a *comparison path*, the solution of its own equation in that fixed field, and each arriving member's comparison path crosses speed one at its own time. The perturbed population is regular up to the infimum of these infinitely many crossing times, and that infimum need not be attained by any member. The actual population is shown to follow the comparison paths only up to the infimum; a later crossing is a crossing of a comparison path, not an established arrival of the population. The theorem shows that the unattained case does not matter for the obstruction: every regular extension of the population past the infimum, however short, contains a positive-rate arrival of some member, at its comparison crossing time, to which [Theorem A′](saturday-cross-geometry-continuation-2026-10-20.md#5-theorem-a-the-obstruction-at-an-arrival) applies.

**Theorem P (persistence).** *Under hypotheses (P1) to (P6) of Section 2 on a reference history, and for every $\mu>0$ allowed by (P2), there are $\varepsilon_0>0$ and $C$ such that every supplied past within $\varepsilon<\varepsilon_0$ of the reference past in the weighted norm $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\mu$ has a unique regular solution on $[0,T^\eta)$, where $T^\eta$ is the infimum over the reference's arriving members of the crossing times of their comparison paths; $\lvert T^\eta-T_*\rvert\le C\varepsilon$; the comparison path of every reference-arriving member crosses speed one exactly once, within $C\varepsilon$ of $T_*$, with incoming rate at least $\beta_0/4$, and the solution follows every comparison path up to $T^\eta$; the comparison path of every other member stays below speed $1-m_0/2$; and no admitted extension of the population beyond $T^\eta$ exists.* **Grade: derived.** No numerical value of $\varepsilon_0$ is obtained.

**Corollary P.1 (the coherent branch).** *The first event of the coherent staggered branch persists in this sense for every $\mu\in(0,\lambda]$, where $\lambda\approx2.7952$ is its growth rate: perturbations of its ancient past that are small in $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\mu$ give a regular evolution up to an infimum of comparison crossing times within $C\varepsilon$ of $t_*$, with the comparison path of every label crossing speed one within $C\varepsilon$ of $t_*$ at an incoming rate above $5.97/4$, and admit no admitted extension beyond that infimum.* **Grade: derived from the accepted certificate bounds.**

Everything is for the unchanged Master Equation with every positive-delay causal root kept, $c_f=1$, the full alternating population with every member dynamical, and the original eight-source block prescription. Supplied pasts are prescribed histories on $(-\infty,0]$, not solutions there, exactly as in the finite theorem. No continuation, update or event rule is selected, and no conserved account is used.

## 1. What this note uses

Read in full before deriving, in this session: the comparison's Theorem 2 and its proof, with the review's finding G2 on the fixed reception tube; the [field theorem](saturday-cross-geometry-continuation-2026-10-20.md) of today, accepted by its Codex review at its stated scope; the [sharpness companion](saturday-cross-geometry-continuation-2026-10-20-sharpness.md), also accepted; the [staggered first event](../lattice-research/analysis/staggered-lattice-first-event.md) and its [adjudication](../lattice-research/analysis/staggered-lattice-first-event-independent-adjudication.md), for Corollary P.1. The notation, the prescription and Lemma R are those of the field theorem's Sections 2 and 3: labels $j\in\mathbb Z^3$, anchors $\mathbf p_j=j$, polarities $\sigma_j$, coupling $g$, row $g\sigma_i\sigma_j\Psi(\mathbf R,\mathbf v)$ with $\Psi=\mathbf n/(r^2D)$, partner field $\mathbf B_i=g\sigma_i[\mathbf S_i+\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_j\mathbf c_j]$, light-cone windows $W_j$, range floors $\rho_j$, and the constants $c_R(w)$, $c_v(w)$.

A *regular solution* from a supplied past $\boldsymbol\eta$ on an interval $[0,T)$ is a family of paths on $(-\infty,T)$ equal to $\boldsymbol\eta$ on $(-\infty,0]$, each continuously differentiable with locally Lipschitz velocity, all speeds below one, every member within a fixed distance $b<1/2$ of its anchor, satisfying at every time $t\in(0,T)$ the hypotheses (H1), (H2) and $\Sigma_0<\infty$ of the field theorem at every member, and with $\mathbf X_i''(t)=\mathbf B_i(t,\mathbf X_i(t))$ for almost every $t\in(0,T)$ and every $i$. Below wake speed there is no own-path root, so this is the unchanged equation.

## 2. Hypotheses on the reference

The reference is a supplied past $\bar{\boldsymbol\eta}$ and its regular solution $\bar{\mathbf X}$ on $[0,T_*)$, with $T_*<\infty$. Write $\bar{\mathbf u}_j=\bar{\mathbf X}_j-\mathbf p_j$.

- **(P1) Anchor ball.** $\lvert\bar{\mathbf u}_j(s)\rvert\le\bar b$ for every $j$ and $s<T_*$, with $\bar b<1/2$.
- **(P2) Exponential envelope of the supplied past.** There are $\bar M$ and $\mu>0$ with $\lvert\bar{\mathbf u}_j(s)\rvert+\lvert\bar{\mathbf u}_j'(s)\rvert+\lvert\bar{\mathbf u}_j''(s)\rvert\le\bar Me^{\mu s}$ for every $j$ and almost every $s\le0$, and the supplied speeds are at most $\bar w_0<1$.
- **(P3) Bounded acceleration after release.** $\lvert\bar{\mathbf X}_j''(s)\rvert\le\bar A$ for every $j$ and almost every $s\in(0,T_*)$.
- **(P4) Uniform positive rate.** The set $\mathcal A$ of members whose speed tends to one at $T_*$ is nonempty, and $\beta_i=\mathbf u_{0,i}\cdot\bar{\mathbf B}_i(T_*,\bar{\mathbf X}_i(T_*))\ge\beta_0>0$ for every $i\in\mathcal A$, where $\mathbf u_{0,i}$ is the limiting unit velocity.
- **(P5) Margin for the others.** $\sup_{k\notin\mathcal A}\sup_{s<T_*}\lvert\bar{\mathbf X}_k'(s)\rvert\le1-m_0$ with $m_0>0$ (vacuous when every member arrives).
- **(P6) Uniform subunit speed before the event.** For every $\kappa>0$ there is $m(\kappa)>0$ with $\lvert\bar{\mathbf X}_j'(s)\rvert\le1-m(\kappa)$ for every $j$ and $s\le T_*-\kappa$.

The *weighted distance* of a supplied past $\boldsymbol\eta$ from $\bar{\boldsymbol\eta}$, with $\mathbf k_j=\boldsymbol\eta_j-\bar{\boldsymbol\eta}_j$, is

$$
\lVert\boldsymbol\eta-\bar{\boldsymbol\eta}\rVert_\mu=\sup_j\ \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s\le0}\ e^{-\mu s}\bigl(\lvert\mathbf k_j(s)\rvert+\lvert\mathbf k_j'(s)\rvert+\lvert\mathbf k_j''(s)\rvert\bigr),
$$

where $\mathbf k_j''$ exists almost everywhere because velocities are Lipschitz. A perturbation small in this distance is small at every member, uniformly, and is smaller still in the remote past; it is not required to be localized in space.

Two consequences of the hypotheses are used. First, the field theorem applies to the reference at every member $i$ and every reception time $t\le T_*+\delta$ for small $\delta$, with constants uniform in $i$ and $t$: (H1) holds by (P1); (H2) holds on emission times up to $t-\gamma$ by (P6), because those times lie a fixed distance before $T_*$; and the light-cone sums are finite with uniform bounds, because the sources whose windows reach after time $0$ have $R_j\le t+c_W$, so they are finitely many with jets bounded by (P3), and all others obey the envelope (P2), so Corollary A.2 bounds their tail. Second, for $i\in\mathcal A$ the speed derivative $\mathbf e_i\cdot\bar{\mathbf B}_i(t,\bar{\mathbf X}_i(t))$ is uniformly continuous in $t$, uniformly in $i$, because the fields are Lipschitz with uniform constants and the velocities are Lipschitz with constant $\bar A$; so there is $\kappa_1>0$ with speed derivative at least $\beta_0/2$ on $[T_*-\kappa_1,T_*)$ for every $i\in\mathcal A$.

> Claim grade: derived from (P1) to (P6) and the field theorem. Falsifier: a reference satisfying (P1) to (P6) at which some member's light-cone sums are infinite, or at which no uniform $\kappa_1$ exists.

## 3. Lemma D: the field of one history against another

**Lemma D.** *Let $(t,\mathbf x)$ be a reception with $\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf p_i\rvert\le\beta$, and let two histories $\mathbf X$, $\mathbf Y$ have the following properties for every $j\ne i$: on an interval $W_j$ both have displacement at most $b$ and speed at most $w<1$; at $(t,\mathbf x)$ each history has exactly one root of source $j$, and both roots lie in $W_j$; and both light-cone sums $\Sigma_0$, formed with these $W_j$ and with $\rho_j=R_j-\beta-b>0$, are finite. Put $\mathbf k_j=\mathbf Y_j-\mathbf X_j$, $H_j=\sup_{W_j}\lvert\mathbf k_j\rvert$, $H_j'=\sup_{W_j}\lvert\mathbf k_j'\rvert$, and let $L_j^X$ be the Lipschitz constant of $\mathbf X_j'$ on $W_j$. Then*

$$
\lvert\mathbf B_i^Y(t,\mathbf x)-\mathbf B_i^X(t,\mathbf x)\rvert\le g\sum_{j\ne i}\Bigl[\frac{c_R(w)\,H_j}{(1-w)\rho_j^{3}}+\frac{L_j^XH_j/(1-w)+H_j'}{(1-w)^{2}\rho_j^{2}}\Bigr].
$$

*Proof.* The static rows are the same for both histories, so the difference of the prescribed sums is the sum of the differences of the actual rows, each absolutely summable. Fix $j\ne i$ and let $s^X$, $s^Y\in W_j$ be the two roots. In the situations used below the roots and windows are those of Theorem A(a), or of Step 2 of the proof of Theorem P. The incidence functions $G^X(s)=t-s-\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert$ and $G^Y$ differ by at most $\lvert\mathbf k_j(s)\rvert\le H_j$ on $W_j$, and $G^Y$ decreases at rate at least $1-w$ there. Since $G^X(s^X)=0$, $\lvert G^Y(s^X)\rvert\le H_j$, so $\lvert s^X-s^Y\rvert\le H_j/(1-w)$. Then

$$
\lvert\mathbf X_j(s^X)-\mathbf Y_j(s^Y)\rvert\le w\lvert s^X-s^Y\rvert+H_j\le\frac{H_j}{1-w},\qquad
\lvert\mathbf X_j'(s^X)-\mathbf Y_j'(s^Y)\rvert\le\frac{L_j^XH_j}{1-w}+H_j' .
$$

On the convex set of Lemma R, where the range is at least $\rho_j$ and the speed at most $w$, $\lVert\partial_{\mathbf R}\Psi\rVert\le c_R(w)/\rho_j^3$ and $\lVert\partial_{\mathbf v}\Psi\rVert\le1/((1-w)^2\rho_j^2)$; the mean-value inequality gives the bound for one source, and summing gives the lemma. $\square$

**Weighted form.** In the situation of Theorem A(a) at $(i,t_0)$, with $W_j$ the windows there and $\lvert t-t_0\rvert<h$, suppose that $\lvert\mathbf k_j(s)\rvert+\lvert\mathbf k_j'(s)\rvert\le Ne^{\mu s}$ for every $j$ and every $s\le t-\gamma$, and that $L_j^X\le\bar L$ for every $j$. Both roots at $(t,\mathbf x)$ satisfy $s\le t-\rho_j\le t-\gamma-h<t-\gamma$, because each delay equals its range, which is at least $\rho_j\ge\gamma+h$. Apply Lemma D on the truncated windows $W_j\cap(-\infty,t-\gamma]$, intervals that contain both roots and the interval between them, so the weighted hypothesis applies on them. There $s\le t_0-R_j+c_W<t-R_j+c_W+h$, so

$$
\lvert\mathbf B_i^Y(t,\mathbf x)-\mathbf B_i^X(t,\mathbf x)\rvert\le gK_\mu Ne^{\mu t},\qquad
K_\mu=26\,e^{\mu(c_W+h)}\sum_{m\ge1}m^{2}e^{-\mu m}\Bigl[\frac{c_R(w)}{(1-w)c_0^{3}m^{3}}+\frac{\bar L/(1-w)+1}{(1-w)^{2}c_0^{2}m^{2}}\Bigr]<\infty,
$$

with $c_0=1-2b-h$, by the shell count of Corollary A.2.

> Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: two histories in the stated class with a reception at which the field difference exceeds the bound; the root-shift estimate $\lvert s^X-s^Y\rvert\le H_j/(1-w)$ and the convexity of the interpolation set are where to look.

## 4. Proof of Theorem P

Fix $r>0$ with $b_P=\bar b+r<1/2$, a window half-width $h_P$ with $\gamma_P=1-2b_P-2h_P>0$, and $w_P<1$ larger than $\bar w_0$ and than $1-m(\kappa)/2$ for the $\kappa$ chosen below. All constants below are uniform over members.

**Choice of the event window.** Choose $\kappa\le\kappa_1$ and $\delta>0$ with $\kappa+\delta<\min\{\gamma_P,1-2b_P\}$, so small that, with $a=T_*-\kappa$ and $b=T_*+\delta$: each member's reference path on $[a,T_*)$, extended to $[T_*,b]$ by solving $\bar{\mathbf y}''=\bar{\mathbf B}_i(t,\bar{\mathbf y})$ with the reference field built from the reference prefix up to $a$, stays within $r/4$ of $\bar{\mathbf X}_i(T_*)$; for $i\in\mathcal A$ the speed of $\bar{\mathbf y}_i$ has derivative at least $\beta_0/2$ on $[a,b]$; and for $k\notin\mathcal A$ the speed of $\bar{\mathbf y}_k$ stays at most $1-3m_0/4$ on $[a,b]$. These are possible uniformly by Section 2, by (P3) and by $\delta\le m_0/(4\bar A')$, where $\bar A'$ bounds the reference fields. The extension $\bar{\mathbf y}_i$ is a comparison path, not a solution after $T_*$.

**Step 1, continuous dependence up to $a$.** Let $\boldsymbol\eta$ have $\lVert\boldsymbol\eta-\bar{\boldsymbol\eta}\rVert_\mu\le\varepsilon$, and write $\mathbf k=\mathbf Y-\bar{\mathbf X}$ for the difference of the solution from $\boldsymbol\eta$ and the reference, and

$$
N(t)=\sup_j\ \operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{s\le t}\ e^{-\mu s}\bigl(\lvert\mathbf k_j(s)\rvert+\lvert\mathbf k_j'(s)\rvert+\lvert\mathbf k_j''(s)\rvert\bigr),\qquad N(0)\le\varepsilon .
$$

Advance by steps of length $\tau<\min\{h_P,\gamma_P\}/2$. On a step $[t_k,t_k+\tau]\subset[0,a]$, as long as $\mathbf Y$ has stayed in the anchor ball $b_P$ with speeds at most $w_P$, every root used at a reception in the step is emitted before $t_k$ (the emission gap $\gamma_P$ exceeds $\tau$), so the members do not interact within the step: each solves its own ordinary differential equation $\mathbf y_i''=\mathbf B_i^Y(t,\mathbf y_i)$ with a field fixed by the past, bounded and Lipschitz in position with constants uniform in $i$ by Theorem A(c) (the velocity-Lipschitz constants of $\mathbf Y$ on the windows are at most those of the reference plus $N(t_k)e^{\mu s}$, so the light-cone sums stay uniformly finite). This gives existence and uniqueness on the step for all members at once. By Lemma D in weighted form and the Lipschitz bound $\Lambda_x$ of the reference field,

$$
\lvert\mathbf k_i''(t)\rvert\le g\Lambda_x\lvert\mathbf k_i(t)\rvert+gK_\mu N(t_k)e^{\mu t}
$$

for almost every $t$ in the step. Integrating twice and using $e^{-\mu t}\le1$ for $t\ge0$ gives $N(t_k+\tau)\le C_{\rm step}N(t_k)$ with $C_{\rm step}$ independent of $k$ and $i$. After at most $\lceil a/\tau\rceil$ steps, $N(a)\le C_1\varepsilon$. For $\varepsilon$ small this keeps $\mathbf Y$ within $\bar b+C_1\varepsilon<b_P$ of the anchors and its speeds below $1-m(\kappa)+C_1\varepsilon\le w_P$ on $[0,a]$, so by a first-exit argument the stepping does reach $a$.

**Step 2, fields on fixed tubes.** For each member $i$ let $Q_i=\{(t,\mathbf x):a\le t\le b,\ \lvert\mathbf x-\bar{\mathbf X}_i(T_*)\rvert\le r/2\}$. For $(t,\mathbf x)\in Q_i$ and $j\ne i$, define the incidence function on $s\le a$ with the known prefix of $\mathbf Y$ only. At $s=a$ the distance from $\mathbf x$ to $\mathbf Y_j(a)$ is at least $1-2b_P$, while $t-a\le\kappa+\delta<1-2b_P$, so the incidence function is negative there; it is decreasing on $(-\infty,a]$ with slope at most $-(1-w_P)$ and tends to $+\infty$. Hence each source has exactly one root, emitted before $a$, and these rows define a field $\mathbf B_i^Y$ on $Q_i$ without reference to the solution after $a$. With $\beta_Q=\bar b+r/2$ and $c_Q=\beta_Q+b_P$, the root lies in $W_j^Q=[a-R_j-c_Q,\ b-R_j+c_Q]\cap(-\infty,a]$, because its delay equals its range, which is within $c_Q$ of $R_j$; the same holds for the reference. Lemma D with these windows, with $\rho_j=R_j-\beta_Q-b_P$, and the shell count of its weighted form (with $e^{\mu(c_W+h)}$ replaced by $e^{\mu(c_Q+b-a)}$) give $\lvert\mathbf B_i^Y-\bar{\mathbf B}_i\rvert\le C_3\varepsilon$ on $Q_i$, uniformly in $i$, from $N(a)\le C_1\varepsilon$. The same light-cone sums show that $\mathbf B_i^Y$ is Lipschitz in position on $Q_i$ with a uniform constant.

**Step 3, comparison paths.** Let $\mathbf z_i$ solve $\mathbf z''=\mathbf B_i^Y(t,\mathbf z)$ on $[a,b]$ from the data of $\mathbf Y_i$ at $a$. While $\mathbf z_i$ stays in $Q_i$, Gronwall's inequality gives $\lvert\mathbf z_i-\bar{\mathbf y}_i\rvert+\lvert\mathbf z_i'-\bar{\mathbf y}_i'\rvert\le C_4\varepsilon$, and then the accelerations differ by at most $C_4'\varepsilon$; since $\bar{\mathbf y}_i$ is within $r/4$ of the tube's centre, $\mathbf z_i$ stays within $r/4+C_4\varepsilon<r/2$ and exists on all of $[a,b]$.

**Step 4, the solution equals the comparison paths.** As long as every member of $\mathbf Y$ stays within $b_P$ of its anchor and below wake speed on $[a,t]$, no root is emitted after $a$ at a reception in a tube, because such a root would need range at least $1-2b_P$ and delay at most $b-a<1-2b_P$. So $\mathbf Y_i''=\mathbf B_i^Y(t,\mathbf Y_i)$ there, and by uniqueness $\mathbf Y_i=\mathbf z_i$ for every $i$ simultaneously. The tube keeps each member within $\bar b+r/2<b_P$ of its anchor.

**Step 5, the crossings.** For $i\in\mathcal A$, the speed of $\mathbf z_i$ is within $C_4\varepsilon$ of that of $\bar{\mathbf y}_i$ and its derivative is at least $\beta_0/2-C_5\varepsilon\ge\beta_0/4$; since the reference speed is at most $1-\beta_0\kappa/2$ at $a$ and at least $1+\beta_0\delta/2$ at $b$, for $C_4\varepsilon<\beta_0\min\{\kappa,\delta\}/4$ the speed of $\mathbf z_i$ equals one exactly once in $[a,b]$, at a time $t_i^\eta$ with $\lvert t_i^\eta-T_*\rvert\le4C_4\varepsilon/\beta_0$. For $k\notin\mathcal A$ the speed stays below $1-3m_0/4+C_4\varepsilon\le1-m_0/2$ once $C_4\varepsilon\le m_0/4$. Put $T^\eta=\inf_{i\in\mathcal A}t_i^\eta$. By Steps 1 and 4 the regular solution from $\boldsymbol\eta$ exists on $[0,T^\eta)$ and is unique among regular solutions that keep every member within $b_P$ of its anchor, and $\lvert T^\eta-T_*\rvert\le4C_4\varepsilon/\beta_0$. Distinct members stay at least $1-2b_P$ apart.

**Step 6, no admitted extension.** An *admitted extension* is a family of paths on $[0,T^\eta+\delta')$, $0<\delta'\le b-T^\eta$, extending the regular solution, with every member within $b_P$ of its anchor, such that each member's path is continuously differentiable with locally absolutely continuous velocity and satisfies the equation almost everywhere on every interval before its first time at speed one, including across $T^\eta$ when no member has arrived there, and continues after that time in one of the two classes for which Theorem A′ proves noncontinuation: the population reading of the continuation class of Theorem U (velocity continuous at the arrival, absolutely continuous afterwards, equation almost everywhere with ordinary roots), or bounded-variation velocity satisfying the population reading of the measure form (M1) to (M5), including the direction rule (M4), imposed across the arrival. Theorem J alone excludes only a jump at the arrival and is not used to exclude a class. Suppose an admitted extension exists. Choose $i\in\mathcal A$ with $t_i^\eta<T^\eta+\delta'$, which the definition of the infimum allows. By the argument of Step 4, which uses only the anchor ball, every root at a reception in the tube is emitted before $a$, so member $i$'s acceleration is $\mathbf B_i^Y(t,\mathbf x_i)$ while its speed is below one, whatever the other members do after $a$. Its path is continuously differentiable with locally absolutely continuous velocity across $T^\eta$, so it solves $\mathbf x''=\mathbf B_i^Y(t,\mathbf x)$ with the bounded Lipschitz fixed field on $[a,t]$ for every $t$ before its first time at speed one, and uniqueness for that ordinary differential equation makes it equal to $\mathbf z_i$ there. Its first time at speed one is therefore $t_i^\eta$, reached with incoming rate at least $\beta_0/4$. At $(i,t_i^\eta)$ the hypotheses of Theorem A′ hold for the extension: the anchor ball (H1) with $b_P$; the speed margin (H2) on emission times up to $t_i^\eta-\gamma_P<a$, from Step 1; finite light-cone sums; and the equation for member $i$ on an interval before $t_i^\eta$. Theorem A′ (through Theorem U for the first class and Theorem BV, conditional on (M4), for the second) excludes a continuation of member $i$ in either class, which contradicts the definition of an admitted extension. If the infimum is attained, the attaining member is obstructed at $T^\eta$ itself; if it is not attained, the regular solution exists on the closed interval $[0,T^\eta]$, with every member below wake speed at $T^\eta$, and still has no admitted extension by any positive time. $\square$

> Claim grade: derived from (P1) to (P6), Lemma D, the field theorem and Theorem A′; the conclusions of Step 6 carry the classes and grades of Theorem A′. Falsifier: a reference satisfying (P1) to (P6) and a sequence of supplied pasts converging to its past in $\lVert\cdot\rVert_\mu$ whose regular solutions last beyond $T_*+\delta$ or end otherwise than as stated; or a failure of the step estimate, of the tube construction or of the transverse crossing. A single perturbed run at finite size is not a counterexample, because no numerical $\varepsilon_0$ is given.

## 5. The coherent branch

The coherent staggered branch $\mathbf X_j(t)=\mathbf p_j+\sigma_jq(t)\mathbf e_3$ of the [first-event note](../lattice-research/analysis/staggered-lattice-first-event.md#1-the-exact-incoming-history) satisfies (P1) to (P6) with its ancient part on $(-\infty,0]$ as the reference supplied past and its certified continuation on $[0,t_*)$ as the reference solution. (P1): $\lvert q\rvert<0.24556$ on the whole incoming history, so $\bar b=0.24556$. (P2): for $s\le0$ the accepted bounds give $\lvert q^{(k)}(s)\rvert\le\lambda^kae^{\lambda s}+(2\lambda)^kK_0a^2e^{2\lambda s}\le(\lambda^ka+(2\lambda)^kK_0a^2)e^{\lambda s}$ for $k\le2$, so the envelope holds with any $\mu\in(0,\lambda]$, and the supplied speeds are at most $\lambda a+2\lambda K_0a^2<10^{-10}$. (P3): the certificate bounds the acceleration on every cell of $[0,t_*]$. (P4): every label arrives at $t_*$, so $\mathcal A$ is the whole lattice; by sublattice symmetry each label's unit velocity is $\pm\mathbf e_3$ and its field projection at the event is $q''(t_*)>5.9711$, the same for all labels, so $\beta_0=5.9711$. (P5) is vacuous. (P6): $q''>0$ on the whole incoming history, so $q'$ is increasing and $\sup_{s\le t_*-\kappa}q'(s)=q'(t_*-\kappa)<1$.

Theorem P then gives Corollary P.1. The anchor ball $b_P$ of the admitted extensions can be any number in $(0.24556,1/2)$, for example the adjudication's $1/3$. The statement concerns perturbations of the supplied ancient past in the weighted norm; it does not concern a localized preparation, whose past is not within a small weighted distance of the coherent past, as Section 6 of the comparison shows for the uniform distance.

> Claim grade: derived from the accepted certificate bounds. Falsifier: a quoted certificate bound that differs from the first-event note or its adjudication.

## 6. Scope

- **What is added to the finite theorem.** The topology is weighted and uniform over members; the perturbation need not be localized. Simultaneous arrival of infinitely many members is handled, including an unattained infimum of comparison crossing times, by the population-level statement of Step 6.
- **What is not shown.** No numerical $\varepsilon_0$; nothing about pasts outside the weighted neighbourhood, in particular nothing about localized preparations; nothing about arrivals at zero rate; nothing about continuations that leave the uniform anchor ball. Whether a solution can end regular evolution with an unattained infimum is not decided; the theorem only shows that the obstruction does not depend on attainment.
- **Relation to the field theorem.** This note uses the field theorem's Theorem A for every history in the class and Theorem A′ at each perturbed arrival. It closes the persistence item listed as open in Sections 7 and 9 of that note, in the weighted topology described here.

No instrument is used; the arithmetic is the shell count of Corollary A.2.
