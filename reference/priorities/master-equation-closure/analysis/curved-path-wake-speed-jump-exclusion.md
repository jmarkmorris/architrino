# Exclusion of velocity jumps at wake speed on curved paths

## Result

**In words.** An architrino on a curved path in two or three dimensions, whose whole past is below wake speed and which reaches wake speed at a positive rate while the rows from every other architrino stay bounded and continuous, cannot continue by a jump in velocity. The equation, read as an equality of measures across the event, would have to supply a concentrated contribution at the single instant of the event. It supplies none: the architrino has never before met its own wake, because a path traversed below wake speed is always shorter than the delay, and each other architrino is received at that instant from isolated emission times only. The velocity is therefore continuous at the event in every continuation of bounded variation, and its right-hand trace has speed one. After that, the rows from the others carry the velocity component along the event direction above one, the receiver overtakes the wake it emitted just before the event, and the forward row from that wake is not integrable in time. The last step needs one property of whatever singular part the acceleration measure has after the event: that it is built from the same row directions as the regular part, so that its component along the event direction is not negative. With that property every continuation of bounded variation is excluded. Without it, the jump at the event and every continuation with absolutely continuous velocity are still excluded, and one case remains, named below.

**Theorem J (no jump at the event).** Assume (U1), (U2) and (P) of the next section, and let the path continue on $[0,\delta)$ with continuous position and velocity of bounded variation, satisfying clauses (M1), (M2), (M3) and (M5) of the measure form on $(-\eta,\delta)$. Then the acceleration measure assigns zero to the instant $\{0\}$, the velocity has no jump at $t=0$, and its right-hand trace equals the event velocity $\mathbf u_0$, of speed one. **Grade: derived**, given the measure form as defined. Of the rules for the singular part, only the atom rule (M3) at the single instant $t=0$ is used; the direction rule (M4) is not.

**Theorem A (no continuation with absolutely continuous velocity).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem J there is no continuation whose velocity is absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of some $(0,\delta')$. Such a continuation is in the continuation class of [Theorem U](curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions.md), which excludes it. **Grade: derived.**

**Theorem S (no continuation with forward singular part).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem J, write the acceleration measure after the event as $\mathbf A\,dt+\boldsymbol\nu$ with $\boldsymbol\nu$ singular. There is no continuation for which $\mathbf u_0\cdot\boldsymbol\nu\ge0$ on some $(0,\delta')$. This contains Theorem A ($\boldsymbol\nu=0$) and is proved directly, without Theorem U. **Grade: derived.**

**Theorem BV (no continuation of bounded variation).** Under the hypotheses of Theorem J and the direction rule (M4), there is no continuation satisfying (M1) to (M5) on any $[0,\delta)$ with continuous position and velocity of bounded variation. In any attempted continuation the proof shows that no own-path root is emitted after the event, so every root is ordinary, the singular part vanishes, and the velocity is absolutely continuous after the event; the contradiction is then that of Theorem S. **Grade: derived conditional on (M4).** The rule (M4) is part of the definition of the measure form and is the same sign statement the one-dimensional adjudication uses when it calls the self measure nonnegative; it is not derived here from a narrower reading of the equation.

**Theorem D-J (downward event).** Under (D1) of Theorem D, the measure form (M1) to (M3), and the extra hypothesis (D0) that the emission times received at the instant of the event with positive delay form a set of zero length for every architrino, the acceleration measure has no atom at the event and the velocity is continuous there in every continuation of bounded variation. Own-path emissions from the recent past contribute nothing to the fibre at the event; (D0) is needed only for the remote own past and for the others. With (M4) and a measure version of (D3), part (a) of Theorem D extends: there is no $\delta'>0$ with speed below one almost everywhere on $(0,\delta')$. **Grade: derived conditional on (M3), (M4), (D0) and the remainder bound**, with (D0) read as corrected below. Part (b) of Theorem D is not extended.

**Review standing and corrections.** This note was written by a delegated analyst from a stated target, and an [adjudication](curved-path-wake-speed-jump-exclusion-independent-adjudication-2026-10-05.md) by a second delegated reviewer in the same session re-derived every step and accepts all results, saying of itself that it worked under a thirty-minute limit and that its agreement is "agreement between two constructions by related instruments, not a fully separate replication". The measure form (M1) to (M5) is a stated formulation, taken to be the intended reading of the equation across an event and not derived from a narrower one. The adjudication accepts all results, with seven corrections to scope, hypotheses and wording; none overturns a conclusion. They are to be read into the statements above and the sections below. (1) The atom rule (M3) is the rule of the length-measure construction; a limiting reading of the delta-function formula agrees with it except at grazing roots, and Theorem J holds under both. (2) The direction rule (M4) follows from the same construction, because the weight on the root set is nonnegative and the row carries $\lvert D\rvert$; Theorem BV's condition is therefore weaker than presented. (3) In Theorem BV, "no continuation at all" means no continuation satisfying (M1) to (M5); continuations with non-ordinary roots on a set of reception times of positive length are excluded by (M2), by definition. (4) Without (M4) the case left open exists only for formulations whose singular part may carry row directions with weights of either sign. (5) In Lemma ZD the interval of emission times is open at its far end. (6) Theorem D-J is derived conditional on (M3), (M4), (D0) and the remainder bound; the own-path measure must be split into a recent part obeying the cone rule and a remainder; and (D0) should read that every root received at the event with positive delay has $D\ne0$, or the fibre has zero length and contains no grazing root. (7) Hypothesis (P) should be stated for a neighbourhood of the event in time and position, with every root of another architrino received there ordinary and emitted before the event; each continuation then has its own $\delta_0$. The adjudication also notes what Theorem J does not say: if the equation is imposed only after the event and not across it, a restart with any velocity below wake speed has no own-path root and solves the equation there, as on the line. Both documents were produced within one working session.

**Not covered.** (1) Without (M4): a continuation of bounded variation whose singular part has a negative component along $\mathbf u_0$ in every interval $(0,\epsilon)$ after the event, either as jumps at times accumulating at the event or as a singular continuous part. Section "Consequences" shows what such a part requires: non-ordinary own-path roots emitted after the event, at emission times where the speed is at least one. (2) Velocities that are not of bounded variation, in particular without a right-hand limit at the event. (3) Tangential arrival, $a=0$, and unbounded or discontinuous rows from the others at the event, exactly as in the parent note. (4) The downward event when (D0) fails, and the no-dip statement (b) of Theorem D for velocities with jumps. (5) Any definition of the acceleration measure by a limiting procedure that does not satisfy (M3).

## Measure form on a curved path

Units are $c_f=1$ and $K=1$. The receiver's path is $\mathbf x(t)\in\mathbb R^d$, $d=2$ or $3$; the event is at $t=0$; $\mathbf u_0$ is the event velocity. For a source $j$ with path $\mathbf X_j$ (the receiver itself included, with $\mathbf X_j=\mathbf x$) put

$$
G_j(t,s)=(t-s)-\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert ,\qquad s<t .
$$

A root of source $j$ at reception time $t$ is an emission time $s<t$ with $G_j(t,s)=0$. Its delay is $\tau=t-s$, its direction is $\mathbf n=(\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf X_j(s))/\tau$, its transmitter factor is $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf X_j'(s)$, and its row is $\sigma_j\,\mathbf n/(\tau^2\lvert D\rvert)$, where $\sigma_j$ is the product of the two polarities and $\sigma_j=+1$ for the receiver's own path. The fibre over $t$ is the set $F_j(t)$ of all roots of source $j$ at reception time $t$. A root is ordinary when $\mathbf X_j$ is differentiable at $s$, $D\ne0$, and $s$ is an isolated point of $F_j(t)$. Since $\partial_sG_j=-D$, the factor $1/\lvert D\rvert$ is the weight with which the line $\{t=\text{const}\}$ crosses the root set $\Gamma_j=\{(t,s):s<t,\ G_j=0\}$ in the plane of reception and emission times.

**Definition (measure form).** Let $I$ be an open interval of reception times. The equation holds in measure form on $I$ when the following hold.

- **(M1) Regularity.** The position $\mathbf x$ is continuous on $I$ and is the integral of a velocity $\mathbf u$ of locally bounded variation. Such a $\mathbf u$ has a left limit $\mathbf u(t^-)$ and a right limit $\mathbf u(t^+)$ at every $t$, and its distributional derivative $D\mathbf u$ is a vector measure with $D\mathbf u(\{t\})=\mathbf u(t^+)-\mathbf u(t^-)$.
- **(M2) Regular part.** For almost every $t\in I$ every root of every source is ordinary and the sum of rows converges absolutely to a vector $\mathbf A(t)$, with $\mathbf A$ locally integrable on $I$.
- **(M3) Atom rule.** There is a vector measure $\boldsymbol\mu$ on $I$, the acceleration measure, of the form $\boldsymbol\mu=\mathbf A\,dt+\boldsymbol\nu$, where $\boldsymbol\nu=\sum_j\boldsymbol\nu_j$ is singular with respect to $dt$ and $\boldsymbol\nu_j$ is concentrated on the set of reception times at which source $j$ has a non-ordinary root. For a single instant $t_0$, $\boldsymbol\nu_j(\{t_0\})\ne0$ only if the fibre $F_j(t_0)$ has positive length.
- **(M4) Direction rule.** For every Borel set $E\subset I$, the value $\boldsymbol\nu_j(E)$ lies in the closed convex cone generated by the vectors $\sigma_j\,\mathbf n(t,s)$ over the non-ordinary roots $s\in F_j(t)$, $t\in E$.
- **(M5) Equality.** $D\mathbf u=\boldsymbol\mu$ on $I$, that is $-\int\mathbf u\,\varphi'\,dt=\int\varphi\,d\boldsymbol\mu$ for every continuously differentiable $\varphi$ with compact support in $I$.

"The equation holds across the event" means that $I$ contains $t=0$, so that test functions with $\varphi(0)\ne0$ are admitted in (M5). A formulation that imposes the equation only on compact subintervals of $(0,\delta)$ admits no such test function and cannot see a jump at the event.

**Where the rules come from.** They are a definition, not a theorem. The row sum is $\mathbf A(t)=\sum_j\int\delta(G_j(t,s))\,\mathbf k_j(t,s)\,ds$ with $\mathbf k_j=\sigma_j(\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf X_j(s))/\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert^3$, so the natural measure in $t$ is the projection onto the $t$ axis of $\mathbf k_j\,\lambda_j$, where $\lambda_j$ is the nonnegative measure "length along $\Gamma_j$ divided by $\lvert\nabla G_j\rvert$". On a branch of ordinary roots $s=s(t)$, $\lvert\nabla G_j\rvert^2=D^2+D_r^2$ with receiver factor $D_r=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u(t)$, and $ds/dt=D_r/D$, so the projection is $dt/\lvert D\rvert$: this is (M2). A single instant $t_0$ receives a nonzero value only from a piece of $\Gamma_j$ of positive length lying in the line $t=t_0$, which is a set of emission times of positive length received at one instant: this is (M3), and it is the sentence used on the line in the [class-level adjudication](../collinear-research/analysis/class-level-first-exit-independent-adjudication-2026-10-03.md). Because $\lambda_j\ge0$, every part of the measure is a superposition of the vectors $\mathbf k_j$ with nonnegative weights: this is (M4), and it is what that adjudication uses when it says a same-polarity contribution along an inward chord is inward, hence the self measure is nonnegative. Theorem J uses (M1), (M3) at $t_0=0$ and (M5). Theorems A and S add (M2). Only Theorem BV uses (M4). **Grade of the definition: a stated formulation, guessed to be the intended reading; the theorems are derived from it.**

**Hypotheses on the past and on the others.**

- **(U1)** The receiver's path is continuously differentiable on $(-\infty,0)$ with $v=\lvert\mathbf x'\rvert<1$ at every time before $0$; the velocity extends continuously to $t=0$ with value $\mathbf u_0$, $\lvert\mathbf u_0\rvert=1$; and $1-v(s)\ge a\lvert s\rvert$ on $[-\eta,0]$ for some $a>0$.
- **(P) Regular partner roots.** There is $\delta_0>0$ such that, for every continuation with continuous position, at every reception time $t\in(-\eta,\delta_0)$ every root of every other architrino is ordinary, their rows sum absolutely to $\mathbf B(t)$, $\lvert\mathbf B\rvert\le B_0$, and $\mathbf B$ is continuous at $t=0$. A row from another architrino depends on the receiver's position and time and not on its velocity, so this is a condition on the others' pasts near their roots, as in H2 of the collinear class-level adjudication.
- **(U2)** The measure form holds on $(-\eta,0)$. By Lemma 0 below and (P) it reads $\mathbf x''=\mathbf B$ there, which is hypothesis (U2) of the parent note.

Notation after the event: $p(t)=\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf u(t)$ is the velocity component along the event direction, and $\omega_+(t)=\sup\lvert\mathbf u(r^\pm)-\mathbf u_0\rvert$ over $0<r<t$, a nondecreasing function. Fix $\theta=1/8$ and $\eta_1\in(0,\eta]$ with $\lvert\mathbf x'(r)-\mathbf u_0\rvert\le\theta$ on $[-\eta_1,0]$. An own-path root at reception time $t>0$ is recent when $s\ge-\eta_1$.

## The zero-atom lemma

**Lemma 0 (no own-path root up to the event).** Under (U1), for every $t\le0$ the receiver has no own-path root: $F_{\text{own}}(t)=\emptyset$. *Proof.* For $s<t\le0$, $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert\le\int_s^tv\,dr<t-s$ because $v<1$ on $(s,t)$ and $v$ is continuous. Hence $G(t,s)>0$. $\square$ **Derived.** This is the curved-path form of Fact 1 of the collinear class-level adjudication: the chord is no longer than the arc, and the arc is shorter than the delay.

**Lemma Z (zero atom).** Under (U1) and (P), for any continuation with continuous position for which an acceleration measure satisfying (M2) and (M3) exists on an interval containing $0$, $\boldsymbol\mu(\{0\})=0$. *Proof.* The part $\mathbf A\,dt$ assigns zero to every single instant. By (M3) the singular part can assign a nonzero value to $\{0\}$ only through a source whose fibre $F_j(0)$ has positive length. The fibre at $t=0$ depends only on $\mathbf x(0)$ and on paths at times before $0$, so it is the same for every continuation. The candidates are as follows. (i) Own-path emissions before the event: by Lemma 0 the fibre is empty. (ii) Own-path emissions at or after the event: they are not earlier than the reception and are not roots; the zero-delay diagonal $s=t$ is excluded by the positive-delay convention of the equation. (iii) Emissions of another architrino: by (P) every root at $t=0$ is ordinary, hence isolated in its fibre; a set of isolated points of the real line is countable and has zero length. No fibre at $t=0$ has positive length. $\square$ **Derived.**

**What could have concentrated there, and why it does not.** A row that is unbounded as $t\downarrow0$ is received at times after the event, not at the event; it contributes to $\boldsymbol\mu((0,\epsilon))$, and if it is not integrable it contradicts bounded variation on $(0,\epsilon)$ instead of producing an atom at $0$. The rows from the others are bounded near the event by (P). The own-path rows do not exist at or before the event by Lemma 0. Nothing else is in the equation.

**Proof of Theorem J.** By (M1) and (M5), $\mathbf u(0^+)-\mathbf u(0^-)=D\mathbf u(\{0\})=\boldsymbol\mu(\{0\})$, which is zero by Lemma Z. By (U1), $\mathbf u(0^-)=\mathbf u_0$. $\square$ Consequently $\omega_+(t)\to0$ as $t\downarrow0$. Fix $\delta_1\le\delta_0$ with $\omega_+(\delta_1)\le\theta$.

## Consequences for arrival from below

**Lemma 2' (recent chords are forward, bounded-variation form).** For $t\in(0,\delta_1)$ and any recent own-path root $s$, ordinary or not, $\lvert\mathbf n-\mathbf u_0\rvert\le\theta$ and $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u_0\ge1-\theta^2/2$; if $\mathbf x$ is differentiable at $s$ then $\lvert D\rvert\le\theta+\theta^2/2<1/7$. *Proof.* $\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)=\int_s^t\mathbf u\,dr$ and $\lvert\mathbf u-\mathbf u_0\rvert\le\theta$ almost everywhere on $[s,t]$, so $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)-\tau\mathbf u_0\rvert\le\tau\theta$; the chord has length $\tau$; the rest is the proof of Lemma 2 of the parent note, with $\lvert\mathbf x'(s)-\mathbf u_0\rvert\le\theta$ at a point of differentiability because $\mathbf x'(s)$ is then a limit of averages of $\mathbf u$. $\square$ **Derived.**

**Lemma 6' (no remote own-path root).** If $t\in(0,\delta_1)$ and $t\,\omega_+(t)<a\eta_1^2/2$, no own-path root has $s<-\eta_1$. *Proof.* As Lemma 6 of the parent note: $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert\le\int_s^t\lvert\mathbf u\rvert\,dr\le(t-s)-a\eta_1^2/2+t\,\omega_+(t)<t-s$. It uses integrals only. $\square$ **Derived.**

**Lemma E (roots emitted before the event are ordinary and unique).** Under (U1), for every $t>0$ the function $s\mapsto G(t,s)$ is strictly decreasing on $(-\infty,0]$. Hence at most one own-path root is emitted before the event; it has $D\ge1-v(s)>0$ and is ordinary unless roots emitted after the event accumulate at it, which cannot happen at a point $s<0$. *Proof.* $s\mapsto\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert$ is locally Lipschitz with derivative of magnitude at most $v(s)<1$, so $G(t,\cdot)$ has derivative at most $-(1-v(s))<0$ almost everywhere. $\square$ **Derived.** Consequently a non-ordinary own-path root at a reception time after the event is emitted at some $s\in[0,t)$, and the singular part $\boldsymbol\nu_{\text{own}}$ is concentrated on reception times that receive such an emission. At a density point of a fibre of positive length the derivative of $G(t,\cdot)$ vanishes, so $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u(s)=1$ and the emission speed is at least one.

**Proof of Theorem S.** Suppose a continuation exists with $\mathbf u_0\cdot\boldsymbol\nu\ge0$ on $(0,\delta')$.

*Step 1.* On $(-\eta,0)$, Lemma 0, (P) and (U2) give $\mathbf x''=\mathbf B$, and the argument of Step 1 of Theorem U gives $\beta=\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B(0)\ge a$. By (P), $\mathbf B$ is continuous at $0$ along any continuation with continuous position. Choose $\delta_2\le\min\{\delta_1,\delta'\}$ with $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B(t)\ge a/2$ on $[0,\delta_2)$, with $\delta_2\,\omega_+(\delta_2)<a\eta_1^2/2$, and with $2\sqrt{t\,\omega_+(t)/a}\le\eta_1$ on $(0,\delta_2)$.

*Step 2, the forward component does not decrease.* By (M5), $Dp=\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf A\,dt+\mathbf u_0\cdot\boldsymbol\nu$ on $(0,\delta_2)$. For almost every $t$ the vector $\mathbf A(t)$ is $\mathbf B(t)$ plus the own-path rows; by Lemma 6' these are all recent, and by Lemma 2' each has $\mathbf u_0$ component $(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u_0)/(\tau^2\lvert D\rvert)>0$. Hence $Dp\ge(a/2)\,dt$ as measures, $p$ is nondecreasing, and with $p(0^+)=1$ from Theorem J,

$$
p(t^\pm)\ \ge\ 1+\frac{a\,t}{2}\qquad(0<t<\delta_2).
$$

In particular $\omega_+(t)>0$ for $t>0$.

*Step 3, no root is emitted after the event, and one is emitted just before it.* For $0\le s<t<\delta_2$, $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert\ge\mathbf u_0\cdot(\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s))=\int_s^tp\,dr>t-s$, so $G(t,s)<0$. Put $\sigma=2\sqrt{t\,\omega_+(t)/a}\le\eta_1$. Then $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(-\sigma)\rvert\le\int_{-\sigma}^0v\,dr+\int_0^t\lvert\mathbf u\rvert\,dr\le\sigma-a\sigma^2/2+t+t\,\omega_+(t)$, so $G(t,-\sigma)\ge a\sigma^2/2-t\,\omega_+(t)=t\,\omega_+(t)>0$. Since $G(t,\cdot)$ is continuous and $G(t,0)<0$, there is a root $s^*\in(-\sigma,0)$. By Lemma E it is the only own-path root, it is ordinary, and its delay satisfies

$$
\tau^2<(t+\sigma)^2\le2t^2+2\sigma^2=2t\Bigl(t+\frac{4\,\omega_+(t)}{a}\Bigr).
$$

*Step 4, the divergence.* By Lemma 2' the root of Step 3 has $\mathbf u_0$ component at least $(1-\theta^2/2)/(\tau^2(\theta+\theta^2/2))\ge5/\tau^2$. Therefore, as measures on $(0,\delta_2)$,

$$
Dp\ \ge\ L(t)\,dt,\qquad L(t)=\frac{5}{2t\,\bigl(t+4\,\omega_+(t)/a\bigr)},
$$

and $tL(t)\to\infty$ as $t\downarrow0$. Choose $\delta_3\le\delta_2$ with $L(t)\ge1/t$ on $(0,\delta_3)$. For $0<t_1<t_2<\delta_3$, $p(t_2^-)-p(t_1^+)\ge\ln(t_2/t_1)$. The left side is at most $2\theta$ and the right side is unbounded as $t_1\downarrow0$. Contradiction. $\square$

No chain rule for the speed of a velocity of bounded variation is needed, because the argument follows the fixed component $p$ and not the speed. The divergence is logarithmic, as in Theorem U.

**Proof of Theorem A.** If the velocity is absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of $(0,\delta')$ then $\boldsymbol\nu=0$ there and Theorem S applies. Alternatively, Theorem J makes the velocity continuous on $[0,\delta')$ with $\mathbf x'(0)=\mathbf u_0$, and (M2) supplies ordinary roots and the equation almost everywhere, so the continuation is in the continuation class of the parent note and Theorem U excludes it. $\square$

**Proof of Theorem BV.** By (P) the others contribute no singular part on $(0,\delta_0)$. By Lemma 6' every own-path root at $t\in(0,\delta_2)$ is recent, and by Lemma 2' every recent root, ordinary or not, has $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u_0>0$. By (M4) with $\sigma_{\text{own}}=+1$, $\mathbf u_0\cdot\boldsymbol\nu_{\text{own}}(E)\ge0$ for every Borel $E\subset(0,\delta_2)$. Theorem S applies. $\square$ Within the proof, Step 3 shows more: no own-path root is emitted after the event, so by Lemma E every root is ordinary, $\boldsymbol\nu=0$ on $(0,\delta_2)$, and the velocity is absolutely continuous there. Jumps at later times and a singular continuous part are therefore absent, not merely harmless, under (M4).

**What remains without (M4).** Let $t^*$ be the supremum of the times $t$ with $\lvert\boldsymbol\nu\rvert((0,t))=0$. If $t^*>0$, Theorem A applies on $(0,t^*)$ and gives a contradiction. So in any continuation of bounded variation the singular part of $D\mathbf u$ charges every interval $(0,\epsilon)$: jump times accumulate at the event, or there is a singular continuous part with the event in its support. By Theorem S its $\mathbf u_0$ component must be negative somewhere in every $(0,\epsilon)$. By Lemma E and (M3) it is carried by non-ordinary own-path roots emitted after the event; a jump at $t_0>0$ needs a set of emission times in $(0,t_0)$ of positive length, all with $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u(s)=1$ and speed at least one, all lying at distance $t_0-s$ from $\mathbf x(t_0)$. Such a fibre is geometrically possible on a curved path: a path that winds toward $\mathbf x(t_0)$ with $\lvert\mathbf x(t_0)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert=t_0-s$ has speed above one and nearly constant direction. On a line it is not possible away from speed exactly one. Whether a solution can sustain it is the open case. **Grade: derived for the necessary conditions; the exclusion of the case itself is open without (M4).**

## The downward event

Here the receiver has been above wake speed, $v(s)-1\ge a\lvert s\rvert$ on $[-\eta,0]$ with velocity Lipschitz of constant $M$ there (hypothesis (D1) of Theorem D), and own-path roots can exist before the event. Lemma 0 fails, and the zero-atom argument must look at the fibre directly.

**Lemma ZD (recent fibre at the event is empty).** Under (D1), no own-path root at reception time $0$ has $s\in[-\eta_2,0)$, where $\eta_2=\min\{\eta,\,3a/M^2\}$ ($\eta_2=\eta$ if $M=0$). *Proof.* By Lemma 3 of the parent note, $\lvert\mathbf x(0)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert\ge\int_s^0(v-\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf x'-\mathbf u_0\rvert^2)\,dr\ge\lvert s\rvert+a s^2/2-M^2\lvert s\rvert^3/6>\lvert s\rvert$ for $0<\lvert s\rvert<3a/M^2$. The chord is longer than the delay, so $G(0,s)<0$. $\square$ **Derived.** The receiver has outrun every wake it emitted in the recent past.

**Obstacle.** The remote own past, $s<-\eta_2$, is not controlled by (D1). A nonzero atom at the event needs a set of remote emission times of positive length received exactly at $t=0$, each with vanishing transmitter factor at a density point. Hypothesis (D3) of Theorem D bounds the remainder after the event and says nothing about this fibre. The condition must be assumed: **(D0)** for every architrino, the receiver included, the fibre at $t=0$ has zero length. It holds when the roots received at the event are ordinary, or finite in number, and in particular when the remote own roots at the event are ordinary.

**Proof of Theorem D-J, continuity.** Under (D0) no fibre at $t=0$ has positive length, so by (M3) $\boldsymbol\mu(\{0\})=0$, and by (M1) and (M5) the velocity has no jump at the event. $\square$ **Derived.** Lemma ZD shows that (D0) is automatic for the recent own past; it is a real hypothesis only for the remote own past and the others.

**Extension of Theorem D(a).** Assume also (M4), and in place of (D3): the acceleration measure minus its recent own-path part, called $\mathbf R$, satisfies $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf R\ge-b\,dt$ on $(0,\delta)$ with $\int_0^\delta b\,dt<\infty$. Then there is no $\delta'>0$ with $\lvert\mathbf u\rvert<1$ almost everywhere on $(0,\delta')$. *Proof.* Suppose there is. For $t<\delta'$ and $s<t$ close to $t$, $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert\le\int_s^t\lvert\mathbf u\rvert\,dr<t-s$, so $G(t,\cdot)>0$ just below $t$; and $\omega_+(t)>0$. The proof of Lemma 4 of the parent note uses only integrals of the velocity on $[0,t]$ and gives, for $t$ so small that $\sigma_D(t)\le\min\{\eta_1,a/M^2\}$, $G(t,-\sigma_D(t))<0$ with $\sigma_D=\sqrt{6t\,\omega_+(t)/a}$, hence a recent root with $\tau^2<2t(t+6\,\omega_+(t)/a)$. By Lemma 2' and (M4) the recent own-path part of the measure, regular and singular, has nonnegative $\mathbf u_0$ component, and for almost every $t$ the newborn root contributes at least $5/\tau^2$. So $Dp\ge(L_D-b)\,dt$ with $L_D(t)=5/(2t(t+6\,\omega_+(t)/a))\ge1/t$ for small $t$, and $p(t_1^-)-p(\epsilon^+)\ge\ln(t_1/\epsilon)-\int_0^{t_1}b\,dt\to\infty$ as $\epsilon\downarrow0$, while $p\le1+\theta$. $\square$ **Derived.** The no-dip statement (b) of Theorem D uses the continuity of the speed at the last time it equals one and is not extended to velocities with later jumps: **open.**

## Limits and falsifiers

| Statement | Grade | What would refute it, and where to look |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Measure form (M1) to (M5) | stated formulation | A derivation from the unchanged equation of a contribution at one reception instant that does not come from a set of emission times of positive length, or of a singular contribution with direction outside the cone of row directions. |

The steps that deserve the closest independent reading are these. First, the measure form is a definition; (M3) is the rule the one-dimensional adjudication states and (M4) is the sign statement it uses, but neither is derived from a more primitive statement of the equation, and a regularized equation that defines an event contribution by a limit is outside the result. Second, Step 2 of Theorem S is the only place where the sign of the singular part enters; everything after it follows from $p\ge1+at/2$. Third, Step 3 uses the projection bound $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert\ge\int_s^tp\,dr$, which replaces the monotonicity of $h$ on the line and is what removes roots emitted after the event. Fourth, hypothesis (P) asserts ordinary partner roots at every reception time near the event, not merely almost every; this is what makes the others' fibre at the event a set of zero length and their singular part zero. Fifth, for the downward event (D0) is assumed and is a condition on the remote past that the hypotheses of Theorem D do not supply.

No numerical instrument was run. The result extends the jump case $-1\le U<1$ of Theorem 1 of the collinear class-level adjudication to curved paths as Theorem J, and its case $U=1$ to velocities of bounded variation as Theorems S and BV. On a curved path a right-hand trace is a vector, and Theorem J excludes every trace other than $\mathbf u_0$ at once, so no separate cases for traces of speed above one are needed.
