## The amplitude-gradient proposal: an exact kinematic control

The useful content of the new proposal is cancellation in a regular affine-source scalar. It does not follow by adding a missing gradient to the canonical law. The [regular receiver-gradient treatment](receiver-wake-gradient-closure.md) already includes the implicit change of emission time when receiver position varies.

Fix absolute reception time $T$, receiver location $\mathbf x$, and a complete affine source $\mathbf X_j(S)=\mathbf X_j(T)+\mathbf w(S-T)$ with constant $\|\mathbf w\|<1$. Define present displacement $\mathbf r=\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(T)$, $r=\|\mathbf r\|>0$, positive delay $\tau=T-S$, delayed range $R=\tau$, emission direction $\mathbf n=(\mathbf r+\mathbf w\tau)/\tau$, and transmitter factor $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf w>0$. The causal equation gives

$$
(1-\|\mathbf w\|^2)\tau^2-2(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf w)\tau-r^2=0,
\qquad
RD=(1-\|\mathbf w\|^2)\tau-\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf w
=L,
$$

where $L=[(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf w)^2+(1-\|\mathbf w\|^2)r^2]^{1/2}>0$. Consequently the proposed scalar and its fixed-time receiver gradient satisfy the exact identities

$$
\Psi=\frac1{R|D|}=\frac1L,
\qquad
-\sigma K\nabla_{\mathbf x}\Psi
=\sigma K\frac{(1-\|\mathbf w\|^2)\mathbf r+(\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf w)\mathbf w}{L^3}.
$$

Here $K>0$ has the inverse-square coupling dimensions and $\sigma$ is the polarity product. Both expressions are even in $\mathbf w$. On a fixed subfield speed-margin chart, their expansion contains no term linear in source velocity. At $\mathbf w=0$, the gradient is exactly $\sigma K\mathbf r/r^3$. This is a **derived kinematic identity, now accepted within the separately adjudicated bounded pair investigation** on prescribed affine histories. It is not a circular solution or a conservation theorem. An independent implicit-root differentiation disagreeing with either identity would falsify it.

The canonical row itself has the branchwise scalar representation $-\nabla(\sigma K\varepsilon/R)$, where $\varepsilon=\operatorname{sgn}D$ is fixed on a regular branch: $\nabla R=\mathbf n/D$ gives $\sigma K\mathbf n/(R^2|D|)$. Thus putting $|D|$ inside the scalar changes the acceleration operator. The derivative in the supplied shorthand must specify whether it holds emission time fixed or follows the causal root; those operations differ.

The scalar $1/(R|D|)$ is an inverse-range-weighted root density. Its identification as the physically accumulated arriving shell amplitude requires a transport derivation. The canonical emitted signed surface measure dilutes as $1/R^2$, and its transmitter-time collapse is not identified with this new scalar merely by naming it an amplitude. No standard electromagnetic law is used as a premise here.

### Why cancellation on an affine source does not settle moving assemblies

For a $C^2$ source on a regular $D>0$ branch, define source velocity $\mathbf v=\mathbf X_j'(S)$, source acceleration $\mathbf a=\mathbf X_j''(S)$ and transverse projector $P=I-\mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T}$. Holding source history and $T$ fixed gives

$$
\nabla S=-\frac{\mathbf n}{D},\qquad
\nabla R=\frac{\mathbf n}{D},\qquad
\nabla D=-\frac{P\mathbf v}{R}
-\frac{\mathbf n\|P\mathbf v\|^2}{RD}
+\frac{\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a)}{D}.
$$

The proposed gradient therefore contains

$$
-\sigma K\nabla\frac1{RD}
=\frac{\sigma K}{R^2D^3}
\left[D(\mathbf n-P\mathbf v)-\mathbf n\|P\mathbf v\|^2
+R\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a)\right].
$$

This **derived regular-chart derivative, now independently adjudicated on the selected compatible finite-pair domain** explicitly introduces delayed source acceleration. Its stationary limit agrees with the preceding control. For a slowly varying near-circle, the condition $R\|\mathbf a\|=O(\|\mathbf v\|^2)$ is needed to classify that term as second order; retaining it on an evolved trajectory needs a bound. In a collective linearization, source acceleration is itself first order in disturbance amplitude and cannot be discarded because an affine source has zero acceleration. The proposal could change the type of delayed evolution problem as well as its velocity coefficient.

The control justifies asking whether the proposed law suppresses leading circular drift or translation residuals under suitable complete-history assumptions. It does not prove either cancellation for an actual accelerated assembly, remove collective instability, define a sum over changing root charts, or supply a value at $D=0$ or $R=0$. Those domains require explicit formulation if the proposal is selected. The scalar derivative above is falsified by an independent fixed-time differentiation with complete implicit source-time variation; affine-only checks would not test its acceleration term.
