# Independent adjudication of the exclusion of velocity jumps at wake speed on curved paths

## Verdict

The subject is [the jump-exclusion note](curved-path-wake-speed-jump-exclusion.md). It builds on [the curved-path obstruction note](curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions.md), whose lemmas are taken here as proved, and on the one-dimensional measure form in [the class-level adjudication](../collinear-research/analysis/class-level-first-exit-independent-adjudication-2026-10-03.md). Units are wake speed $c_f=1$ and coupling $K=1$. Every proof step named below was re-derived by hand from the equation and the stated hypotheses; no step was found to be wrong. Seven corrections are recorded, all to scope, hypotheses or wording; none overturns a conclusion.

- **Measure form (M1) to (M5).** Accept as a faithful curved-path counterpart of the one-dimensional measure form. **Grade: stated formulation**, as the subject says. No clause assumes the conclusion in a circular way, but Theorem J is a two-line corollary of the atom sentence of (M3) together with the requirement that (M5) holds across the event, so the whole content of Theorem J is that reading of the equation. Correction 1 records that the atom sentence of (M3) follows from the length-measure construction but not from the displayed delta-function formula read as a limit, and that Theorem J does not depend on the difference. Correction 2 records that (M4) is a consequence of the same construction and is more robust than (M3), the reverse of the subject's emphasis.
- **Lemma 0.** Accept. **Derived.** Depends on (U1) only.
- **Lemma Z.** Accept. **Derived** given the decomposition in (M3) with locally integrable density (M2) and the atom sentence of (M3) at the single instant $t=0$; uses (P) at $t=0$ only.
- **Theorem J.** Accept. **Derived conditional on the formulation**: (M1), the integrability in (M2), the atom sentence of (M3) at $t=0$, and (M5) across the event. Does not use (M4). It survives the alternative limiting reading of Correction 1.
- **Theorem A.** Accept. **Derived.** Both routes are valid; the continuation class of Theorem U is matched after Theorem J. Depends on what Theorem J depends on, plus (M2) in full.
- **Theorem S.** Accept. **Derived.** Depends on Theorem J, (M1), (M2) including its clause that roots are ordinary at almost every reception time, (M5), and (P) on both sides of the event. No chain rule for functions of bounded variation is used.
- **Theorem BV.** Accept with corrections. **Derived conditional on (M4)** and on the almost-everywhere-ordinary clause of (M2). The words "no continuation at all" must read "no continuation satisfying (M1) to (M5)" (Correction 3). The proof does show that the singular part vanishes on a short interval after the event inside the supposed continuation.
- **The stated gap without (M4).** Accept with corrections. The necessary conditions are correctly derived and the scenario is geometrically coherent. Under the subject's own construction, however, (M4) holds automatically, so the gap is open only for formulations whose singular part can carry weights of either sign (Correction 4).
- **Lemma ZD.** Accept. **Derived.** A closed endpoint in the statement is not covered by the written proof but is true (Correction 5).
- **Theorem D-J, continuity.** Accept with corrections. **Derived conditional on (M3) and (D0)**; (D0) is a real hypothesis. Under the limiting reading of Correction 1, (D0) must be strengthened to exclude grazing roots.
- **Theorem D-J, extension of Theorem D(a).** Accept with corrections. **Derived conditional on (M4), (D0) and the remainder bound**; the measure form as written does not define the "recent own-path part" of the singular measure, and that split must be added as a clause (Correction 6).

**Provenance.** The pre-target reference below was written before the subject's proofs were read. This adjudication and the subject were produced on the same day and the adjudicator worked under a thirty-minute limit, so agreement here is agreement between two constructions by related instruments, not a fully separate replication.

## Pre-target reference

Question: a receiver's whole past is below wake speed, its speed reaches one at time $0$ on a curved path, and the roots in the other architrinos' histories are regular. Which rows could contribute an atom at $t=0$ to the distributional derivative of the velocity, and why does none?

Answer written before reading the proofs. The equation as a relation in the plane of reception time $t$ and emission time $s$ places weight on the root set $\{s<t,\ t-s=\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert\}$. A single reception instant can receive a concentrated value only if a set of emission times of positive length is received at that instant. There are four candidates. (1) Rows from another architrino: if that architrino is below wake speed near its root, $s\mapsto s+\lvert\mathbf x(0)-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert$ has derivative equal to the transmitter factor $D>0$, so the root is a single point; the row is a bounded function of reception time near the event and gives a density, not an atom. (2) Rows from the receiver's own path emitted before the event: the chord is at most the arc, and the arc $\int_s^0v\,dr$ is strictly less than the delay $-s$ because $v<1$ on the whole past, so there is no such root at any reception time up to and including the event. (3) Own emissions at or after the event: not earlier than the reception, so not roots. (4) The zero-delay limit $s\uparrow t$ at the event: excluded by the positive-delay convention; it is a single point in any case. Hence no atom and no velocity jump. Two cautions were recorded at the same time. First, the own-path row received just after the event has delay tending to zero and may fail to be integrable; that is an obstruction to bounded variation after the event, not an atom at the event. Second, the statement "an atom needs a fibre of positive length" is a property of one reading of the equation; a reading that defines the contribution by a limit could behave differently where the weight is singular.

Comparison. The subject's Lemma Z enumerates the same candidates with the same reasons, and its paragraph "What could have concentrated there" states the first caution. The second caution is the subject's limit (5); Correction 1 below makes it specific.

## Item-by-item check

**1. The measure form.** The one-dimensional adjudication states three things: the distributional derivative of velocity equals the acceleration measure; where every root is ordinary the measure is the root sum times $dt$; and a single instant receives a nonzero value only from a set of emission times of positive length. Clauses (M1), (M5), (M2) and the atom sentence of (M3) are these, unchanged in content. The one-dimensional text also uses, in its cases $U=1$ and $U>1$, that the self measure is nonnegative because every self chord points inward; (M4) is that statement for vectors. So the correspondence is faithful.

Is the atom sentence of (M3) justified by the stated construction? Yes for the construction "length measure on the root set $\Gamma_j$ divided by $\lvert\nabla G_j\rvert$, times the kernel, projected to the $t$ axis": a set of zero length carries nothing, so a single instant receives a value only from a vertical piece of $\Gamma_j$ of positive length. It is not justified by the formula $\int\delta(G_j)\,\mathbf k_j\,ds$ read as a limit of narrowing symmetric approximations to $\delta$. Where $G_j$ has a local extremum with value zero at an isolated point $(t_0,s_0)$, which is a wake front that grazes the receiver at one instant with both the transmitter factor $D$ and the receiver factor $D_r=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u(t_0)$ equal to zero, the limit is a finite atom of weight $\pi/\sqrt{\det H}$ times the kernel, $H$ being the second-derivative matrix of $G_j$ there. That atom comes from one emission time, a fibre of zero length. **Derived** (area of the ellipse $\{\tfrac12 z^{\mathsf T}Hz<\epsilon\}$ is $2\pi\epsilon/\sqrt{\det H}$); **measured** by a grid count of $\{\lvert G\rvert<\epsilon\}/(2\epsilon)$ for $H=\begin{pmatrix}1&0.3\\0.3&2\end{pmatrix}$, which returned $2.273$ to $2.274$ against the closed form $2.2732$, after the same instrument returned $0.995$ to $0.999$ on a transversal root whose exact value is $1$; the instrument establishes the planar limit for a smooth $G$, nothing about solutions. At a crossing of two root branches the same count grows without bound, so there is no measure there. The two readings therefore differ exactly at grazing roots. Theorem J is unaffected: at $t=0$ the receiver has no own-path root at all by Lemma 0, and by (P) every root in another architrino's history has $D\ne0$, so neither reading can place an atom at the event; what Theorem J needs is only "no atom at an instant whose fibres are empty or consist of roots with $D\ne0$". The stronger sentence is used in two other places: in "What remains", for jumps at times after the event, and in Theorem D-J through (D0).

Is (M4) a formulation choice or a consequence? It is a consequence of the same construction, because the weight on $\Gamma_j$ is nonnegative under either reading and the row carries $\lvert D\rvert$, so every part of the measure is a superposition of the vectors $\sigma_j\mathbf n/\tau^2$ with nonnegative weights. A formulation in which (M3) holds and (M4) fails would need a singular part that is not a superposition of rows. (M4) is therefore the more robust clause, and Theorem BV is robust to the choice of reading.

Dependence. Theorem J: (M1), integrable density from (M2), atom sentence of (M3) at $t=0$, (M5) across the event. Theorems A and S: these plus all of (M2); the concentration sentence of (M3) is used once, on the past side, to read (U2) as $\mathbf x''=\mathbf B$, and could be replaced by assuming that equation directly as the parent note does. Theorem BV: all of these plus the concentration sentence of (M3) after the event and (M4). The "Where the rules come from" paragraph says Theorem J uses (M1), (M3) and (M5), while the Result paragraph also lists (M2); the Result paragraph is the accurate one.

One clause removes a class by definition and the subject's "Not covered" list omits it: (M2) requires every root to be ordinary at almost every reception time. A receiver that coasts in a straight line at exactly wake speed after the event has the whole interval $[0,t)$ in its own fibre at every $t>0$ and is outside (M2) without proof. See Correction 3.

**2. Lemma Z and Theorem J.** Candidates enumerated independently: rows from another architrino emitted before the event; own-path rows emitted before the event; own-path emissions at or after the event; the zero-delay corner. These are the subject's (i) to (iii). Lemma 0 was re-derived: for $s<t\le0$, $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert\le\int_s^tv\,dr<t-s$ because $1-v$ is continuous and positive on $(s,t)$. On isolation: in the subject's definition an ordinary root is isolated by definition, and the requirement is redundant, because differentiability of $\mathbf X_j$ at $s$ with $D\ne0$ and positive delay gives $G_j(t,s')=-D\,(s'-s)+o(\lvert s'-s\rvert)$, which is nonzero for $s'$ near $s$. A set of real numbers each of which is isolated is countable, since each has a neighbourhood with rational endpoints containing no other point of the set, and a countable set has zero length. The claim is correct.

Hypothesis (P) is used in four places: at $t=0$ only, in Lemma Z; on the past side, to read the equation as $\mathbf x''=\mathbf B$ and obtain $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B(0)\ge a$; on the future side, for $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B\ge a/2$ in Step 2 of Theorem S; and at every reception time after the event, with the concentration sentence of (M3), to give the others no singular part in Theorem BV. It is reasonable when each root in another architrino's history at the event has positive delay and $D\ne0$ on a continuously differentiable stretch of that path, because the roots then persist for receiver times and positions near $(0,\mathbf x(0))$ and are emitted before the event, on a history no continuation can alter. As written, (P) asks for one $\delta_0$ valid for every continuation with continuous position, which regularity of the others' pasts does not give: how soon a continuation leaves the neighbourhood depends on the continuation. The proofs treat one continuation at a time, so a neighbourhood form of (P) suffices (Correction 7).

**3. Theorem A.** The class of Theorem U requires: velocity continuous on $[0,\delta)$ with $\mathbf x'(0)=\mathbf u_0$; velocity absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of $(0,\delta)$; at almost every time every root ordinary, rows absolutely summable, and $\mathbf x''$ equal to the sum; $\mathbf B$ bounded near $0$ and continuous at $0$. After Theorem J the right-hand trace is $\mathbf u_0$, so an absolutely continuous velocity on $(0,\delta')$ is continuous on $[0,\delta')$ and $\mathbf x$ has right derivative $\mathbf u_0$ at $0$. Absolute continuity makes $D\mathbf u$ absolutely continuous on $(0,\delta')$, so by uniqueness of the decomposition $\boldsymbol\nu=0$ there and $\mathbf x''=\mathbf A$ almost everywhere. The subject's notion of ordinary is at least as strong as the parent's. (P) supplies the conditions on $\mathbf B$. The class is matched. The first route, Theorem S with $\boldsymbol\nu=0$, is independent of Theorem U and also valid.

**4. Theorem S.** Lemma 2' was recomputed: $\lvert\mathbf n-\mathbf u_0\rvert\le\theta$, $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u_0\ge1-\theta^2/2=127/128$, $\lvert D\rvert\le17/128$, so the component along $\mathbf u_0$ of a recent own-path row is at least $(127/17)/\tau^2>5/\tau^2$. Lemma 6' was recomputed by splitting $\int_s^t\lvert\mathbf u\rvert$ at $-\eta_1$ and $0$. Step 2: $Dp=\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf A\,dt+\mathbf u_0\cdot\boldsymbol\nu\ge(a/2)\,dt$ follows from $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B\ge a/2$, the positive component of each recent own-path row, and the hypothesis on $\boldsymbol\nu$; with $p(0^+)=1$ from Theorem J this gives $p(t^\pm)\ge1+at/2$. Step 3: for $0\le s<t$, $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert\ge\int_s^tp\,dr>t-s$, so no root is emitted at or after the event; with $\sigma=2\sqrt{t\,\omega_+/a}$, $G(t,-\sigma)\ge a\sigma^2/2-t\,\omega_+=t\,\omega_+>0$, and $\omega_+(t)\ge p-1>0$; the intermediate value theorem gives a root in $(-\sigma,0)$. Lemma E: on $(-\infty,0]$ the derivative of $G(t,\cdot)$ is $-1+\mathbf m\cdot\mathbf x'(s)\le-(1-v(s))<0$, $\mathbf m$ being the unit vector from $\mathbf x(s)$ to $\mathbf x(t)$, so the root is unique, has $D\ge1-v(s)>0$, and is ordinary at every $t$, not merely almost every. Delay: $\tau^2<(t+\sigma)^2\le2t^2+2\sigma^2=2t(t+4\omega_+/a)$. Step 4: $Dp\ge(5/\tau^2)\,dt\ge L\,dt$ with $tL\to\infty$ because $\omega_+\to0$ by Theorem J, so $p(t_2^-)-p(t_1^+)\ge\ln(t_2/t_1)$ while the left side is at most $2\theta$. All steps hold. The only operation applied to the velocity of bounded variation is the fixed linear projection $p=\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf u$, for which $Dp=\mathbf u_0\cdot D\mathbf u$ exactly; no chain rule is used, in contrast with Lemma 1 of the parent note.

**Measured**, as a control on Lemma E and Step 3 only: on prescribed plane paths with speed $1+0.5\,r$ on both sides of the event, a start at low speed in the remote past, and direction turning at rates $0.3$ and $1.0$, a sign-change census of $G(t,\cdot)$ over the whole past found exactly one root at each of $t=0.01,0.03,0.06$, inside $(-\sigma,0)$, with $\tau^2$ between $0.19$ and $0.36$ of the stated bound, $D$ between $0.005$ and $0.031$, and $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u_0\ge0.9999$. The census first reproduced the straight-line control root $s=-t$ of the one-dimensional adjudication. Prescribed paths are not solutions; the instrument tests the geometry of the lemmas, not the theorems.

**5. Theorem BV.** By Lemma 6' every own-path root on $(0,\delta_2)$ is recent, and Lemma 2' holds for non-ordinary roots because its chord estimate uses integrals only. The generators $\mathbf n$ of the cone in (M4) therefore all satisfy $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf n\ge127/128$; a closed convex cone generated by vectors in a closed half-space stays in it; so $\mathbf u_0\cdot\boldsymbol\nu_{\text{own}}(E)\ge0$ for every Borel set $E\subset(0,\delta_2)$, and by (P) with the concentration sentence the others add nothing. The constants defining $\delta_2$ do not depend on the conclusion of Theorem S, so there is no circularity. Theorem S then applies. Inside the supposed continuation, Step 3 holds at every $t\in(0,\delta_2)$, Lemma E makes every own-path root ordinary at every such $t$, and the concentration sentence of (M3) gives $\boldsymbol\nu=0$ on $(0,\delta_2)$. So the proof does show that the singular part vanishes there. Two qualifications: the interval is the short interval $(0,\delta_2)$, not all later times, and the statement is an intermediate step about an object that the theorem then shows not to exist.

**6. The stated gap.** The chain is correct: if $\boldsymbol\nu$ vanished on some $(0,t^*)$ Theorem A would apply, so the singular part charges every interval after the event; by Theorem S its component along $\mathbf u_0$ is not a nonnegative measure on any such interval; by (P) and Lemma E it is concentrated on reception times that have a non-ordinary own-path root emitted at some $s\in[0,t)$, the value $s=0$ included because Lemma E gives $G(t,0)<0$ only inside Theorem S. The geometric scenario is coherent: a path of the form $\mathbf x(s)=\mathbf P+(t_0-s)\,\mathbf e(s)$ with $\mathbf e$ a unit vector has every such $s$ in the fibre of $t_0$ provided it arrives at $\mathbf P$ at $t_0$, with $\mathbf n=-\mathbf e(s)$, $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u(s)=1$ and speed $\sqrt{1+(t_0-s)^2\lvert\mathbf e'\rvert^2}\ge1$. Three qualifications. First, the fibre must stay away from $s=t_0$ or the atom's weight $\int\tau^{-2}ds$ diverges. Second, "all with $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u(s)=1$" holds at almost every point of the fibre, not all. Third, and decisive: by Lemma 2' every such chord has $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf n>0$, so a negative component along $\mathbf u_0$ needs negative weights on row directions. The construction from which the subject obtains (M3) has nonnegative weights. The scenario is thus a description of what a formulation with signed singular weights would have to contain, not a candidate solution of the measure form as constructed. A further necessary condition, not stated in the subject: a root emitted at $s\ge0$ and received at $t$ needs $\int_s^tp\,dr\le t-s$, so the singular part must pull the mean of $p$ over some interval back to at most one against an absolutely continuous part that raises it at rate at least $a/2$. **Derived.**

**7. The downward event.** Lemma ZD: with $v\ge1+a\lvert r\rvert$ and $\lvert\mathbf x'(r)-\mathbf u_0\rvert\le M\lvert r\rvert$, Lemma 3 of the parent gives $\lvert\mathbf x(0)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert\ge\lvert s\rvert+as^2/2-M^2\lvert s\rvert^3/6$, which exceeds $\lvert s\rvert$ exactly when $\lvert s\rvert<3a/M^2$. The proof covers that open range; the statement includes the endpoint $s=-\eta_2$, which can equal $-3a/M^2$. The endpoint is still excluded because the inequality $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf x'\ge v-\tfrac12\lvert\mathbf x'-\mathbf u_0\rvert^2$ inside Lemma 3 is strict wherever $v\ne1$. (D0) is needed: (D1) says nothing about the receiver's path before $-\eta_2$, and a stretch of that path of the form given in item 6 with $\mathbf P=\mathbf x(0)$ and $t_0=0$ is a fibre of positive length at the event. Under (D0) the continuity statement is immediate from (M3). The extension of Theorem D(a): with speed below one almost everywhere on $(0,\delta')$, $G(t,s)>0$ for every $s\in[0,t)$; the estimate of Lemma 4 of the parent uses $\lvert\mathbf u\rvert\ge1-\omega_+$ almost everywhere and integrals only, and gives $G(t,-\sigma_D)\le-t\,\omega_+(1-\omega_+/2)<0$, so a recent root exists with $\tau^2<2t(t+6\omega_+/a)$; it is emitted before the event on a continuously differentiable stretch, so Lemma 2' bounds its $D$. The logarithmic divergence follows as in Theorem S. One defect: the proof subtracts "the recent own-path part" of the acceleration measure, regular and singular, but (M3) and (M4) split the singular part by source only, not by emission window. See Correction 6.

## Corrections

**Correction 1.** Location: "Where the rules come from", and limit (5). Defect: the paragraph presents the delta-function formula and the length-measure construction as one object; they differ at a grazing root, an isolated point of the root set where $D=0$ and $D_r=0$ and $G_j$ has a local extremum, where the limiting reading gives a finite atom from a single emission time. Corrected statement: "(M3) is the rule of the length-measure construction. The limiting reading of the delta-function formula agrees with it except at grazing roots, where it gives an atom from a fibre of zero length. Theorem J needs only that an instant whose fibres are empty or consist of roots with $D\ne0$ receives no atom, which holds under both readings." Conclusions: Theorems J, A, S and BV survive unchanged. "What remains" and (D0) need the additions in Corrections 4 and 6.

**Correction 2.** Location: Result paragraph for Theorem BV, and "Where the rules come from". Defect: (M4) is described as a rule that is "not derived here from a narrower reading", which understates it; the same paragraph derives it from nonnegativity of the weight. Corrected statement: "(M4) follows from the construction, under either reading of Correction 1, because the weight on the root set is nonnegative and the row carries $\lvert D\rvert$. Rejecting (M4) while keeping (M3) requires a singular part that is not a superposition of rows." Conclusion: Theorem BV survives and its condition is weaker than presented.

**Correction 3.** Location: Theorem BV statement and "Not covered". Defect: "no continuation at all" omits that (M2) removes, by definition, continuations with non-ordinary roots on a set of reception times of positive length, such as a straight coast at exactly wake speed. Corrected statement: "no continuation satisfying (M1) to (M5)", with a sixth item under "Not covered" for that class. Addition by this adjudicator, **derived in outline and not written out in full**: if (M4) is stated for the whole own-path measure and not only its singular part, as the construction gives, then $Dp\ge(a/2)\,dt$ holds without the almost-everywhere-ordinary clause, Step 3 follows, and every own-path root is ordinary at every time on $(0,\delta_2)$; the clause then becomes a conclusion and the excluded class is empty near the event. Conclusion: Theorem BV survives with the corrected scope.

**Correction 4.** Location: "What remains without (M4)" and "Not covered" item (1). Defect: the open case is presented as a possible continuation; under the subject's own construction (M4) holds, and the necessary conditions listed can be met only with negative weights on row directions. Also "all with $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u(s)=1$" should read "almost all", the fibre must stay away from zero delay, and under the limiting reading a jump after the event can also be carried by a grazing root. Corrected statement: "The case is open only for formulations whose singular part may carry row directions with weights of either sign." Conclusion: the necessary conditions survive; the gap is narrower than stated.

**Correction 5.** Location: Lemma ZD. Defect: the statement covers $s\in[-\eta_2,0)$ and the proof covers $0<\lvert s\rvert<3a/M^2$. Corrected statement: either open the interval at $-\eta_2$, or add that the projection inequality in Lemma 3 of the parent is strict where $v\ne1$. Conclusion survives.

**Correction 6.** Location: Theorem D-J and "Extension of Theorem D(a)". Defects: (a) the grade "derived" should read "derived conditional on (M3), (M4), (D0) and the remainder bound"; (b) the recent own-path part of the singular measure is not defined by (M3) and (M4), which split by source only; (c) under the limiting reading, zero length of the fibre does not exclude an atom from a grazing remote root. Corrected statements: add a clause that the own-path measure is the sum of a part from emission times in $[-\eta_1,t)$ obeying the cone rule and a remainder; and state (D0) as "every root received at the event with positive delay has $D\ne0$, or the fibre has zero length and contains no grazing root". Conclusions survive under the corrected hypotheses.

**Correction 7.** Location: hypothesis (P). Defect: one $\delta_0$ is asserted for every continuation, and the hypothesis does not say that the roots concerned are emitted before the event. Corrected statement: "There is a neighbourhood of $(0,\mathbf x(0))$ in time and position such that for a receiver at any point of it every root in another architrino's history is ordinary and emitted before the event, and the rows sum absolutely to a bounded $\mathbf B(t,\mathbf x)$ continuous at $(0,\mathbf x(0))$." Each continuation with continuous position then has its own $\delta_0$. Conclusions survive.

## Limits and falsifiers

**What survives, stated once.** Under (U1) and (P), with the equation read as an equality of measures across the event: the velocity has no jump at the event (Theorem J, formulation-conditional but independent of which of the two readings is taken); no continuation with absolutely continuous velocity exists (Theorem A); no continuation whose singular part has nonnegative component along the event velocity exists (Theorem S); and, because nonnegative weights give (M4), no continuation of bounded variation satisfying the measure form exists (Theorem BV). For the downward event, continuity at the event holds under (D0) as corrected, and strict descent below wake speed on an interval is excluded under the corrected hypotheses.

**What Theorem J does not say.** It does not show that the pointwise equation imposed only at times after the event excludes a jump. For any vector $\mathbf U$ with $\lvert\mathbf U\rvert<1$, a continuation with right-hand trace $\mathbf U$ has speed below one near the event, hence no own-path root (chord at most arc, arc less than delay, on both sides), and solves $\mathbf x''=\mathbf B(t,\mathbf x)$ with that initial velocity; existence needs $\mathbf B$ continuous in time and position. **Derived** for the absence of own-path roots; **inferred** for existence, by analogy with part 3 of Theorem 2 of the one-dimensional adjudication and not written out. The family of such punctured continuations fills the open unit ball of traces, and each violates the measure equation only by the missing atom $\mathbf U-\mathbf u_0$ at the event.

**Falsifiers.** For Theorem J: a derivation from the unchanged equation of a nonzero contribution at an instant at which the receiver has no own-path root and every root in another history has $D\ne0$; the only place left to look is the zero-delay corner, which the positive-delay convention excludes. For Correction 1: a symmetric approximation to $\delta$ for which the planar limit at a definite quadratic extremum is not $\pi/\sqrt{\det H}$. For Theorem S: a row with negative component along $\mathbf u_0$ that is neither from another architrino nor a recent own-path row, or a continuation in which $p$ fails to reach $1+at/2$ although $\mathbf u_0\cdot\boldsymbol\nu\ge0$. For Theorem BV: a continuation of bounded variation satisfying (M1) to (M5); or a reading of the equation in which a non-ordinary own-path root contributes against its own chord direction. For the addition in Correction 3: a continuation obeying the cone rule for the whole own-path measure that has non-ordinary own-path roots on a set of positive length arbitrarily soon after the event. For Lemma ZD and (D0): a path satisfying (D1) with a root at the event emitted within $\min\{\eta,3a/M^2\}$ before it.

**Not verified in the time.** The lemmas of the parent note were used as proved; only the arithmetic of Lemmas 2, 3, 4 and 6 as reused here was rechecked. The addition in Correction 3 and the punctured-family existence statement are outlines. Whether a fibre of positive length or a grazing root can occur on an actual solution, in the remote past of the downward event or after the upward event, was not examined. Part (b) of Theorem D for velocities with jumps remains open as the subject says. Tangential arrival ($a=0$), velocities not of bounded variation, and unbounded or discontinuous rows from the others at the event are outside both the subject and this check. The two numerical controls were single short runs of instruments written for this check, each passed on a case with a known answer before use; they are not independent of this adjudicator.
