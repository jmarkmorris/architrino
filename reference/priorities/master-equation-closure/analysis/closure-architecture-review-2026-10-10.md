## How to read this report

The review proceeds in six packets. Packet 0 maps the headline conclusions to their sources. Packets 1 to 5 each read one part of the [parent manuscript](../manuscript.md) against the analyses it rests on. Each packet records what was read, at which version, what was checked and how, and a finite list of findings.

Every reviewed statement receives one of four dispositions.

- **Supported as stated.** The source says what the summary says, at the same scope and grade.
- **Stronger than its source.** The summary says more than the source establishes: a wider domain, a higher grade, or a count the source does not carry.
- **Depends on an unstated hypothesis.** The statement is right only under a condition the summary does not name.
- **Not checkable in this packet.** The dependency was not read; it is named so that a later packet or reader can pick it up.

Each finding states the assumption it concerns, its grade, its consequence, a falsifier, the scope of any earlier review and the check actually performed. The grades are the repository's: derived, measured, inferred, guessed. A grade on a finding of this review describes the review's own check, not the science under review. Most findings here are **measured by reading**: the instrument is a direct comparison of two texts at recorded versions, and its domain is the passages named.

**Independence, stated by construction.** This reviewer had read none of the top-level closure analyses before this campaign, with the exceptions listed under "Prior access" below. A reading of a proof by a second session is an adversarial reading. It becomes an independent check only where this report says that a step was reconstructed without consulting the source's own derivation of that step, and each such place is marked. Agreement between this review and an earlier adjudication that used the same inputs is not counted as a second confirmation.

**One identity reconstructed here.** Manuscript Section 2.4 says that on a connected chart of simple roots where $D$ keeps its sign, the scalar $\Phi=C\,\operatorname{sgn}D/r$ has $-\nabla\Phi=\mathbf a$. Reconstruction, without consulting a source derivation: differentiate the root condition $r=c_f(T-s)$ at fixed $T$ with the transmitter history fixed. Moving the receiver by $\delta\mathbf X$ changes the range by $\mathbf n\cdot\delta\mathbf X-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v\,\delta s$ and must equal $-c_f\,\delta s$, so $\delta s=-\mathbf n\cdot\delta\mathbf X/D$ and $\delta r=c_f\,\mathbf n\cdot\delta\mathbf X/D$. Hence $\nabla r=c_f\mathbf n/D$, and $-\nabla\Phi=C\operatorname{sgn}D\,c_f\,\mathbf n/(r^2D)=C\,c_f\,\mathbf n/(r^2|D|)$, which is the canonical contribution. The manuscript writes $\nabla r=\mathbf n/D$ with $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v$, which is the same at $c_f=1$. **Derived; domain: one simple root, fixed transmitter history, fixed sign of $D$. Supported as stated.** The manuscript's caution that this scalar is not an action or a conserved energy is the correct reading of so local a statement.

### What was reconstructed

Each item was worked from the definitions in manuscript Section 2.1. Where the source's result was visible before the reconstruction, that is said.

1. **Root variation (Section 2.3).** With $c_f=1$ and $G=\|\mathbf X_i+\mathbf b_i-\mathbf X_j(s)-\mathbf b_j\|-(T-s)$, the first variation is $\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf b_i-\mathbf b_j)+D\,\delta s=0$, giving $\delta s=-\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf b_i-\mathbf b_j)/D$. Agrees.
2. **Receiver-position derivative of one contribution (Section 2.3).** Holding the transmitter history fixed and moving the receiver by $\mathbf b$: the separation vector changes by $M\mathbf b$ with $M=I+\mathbf v\otimes\mathbf n/D$; the range by $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf b/D$; the direction by $N\mathbf b$ with $N=(I-\mathbf n\otimes\mathbf n)M/r$; and $D$ by $\mathbf d_D\cdot\mathbf b$ with $\mathbf d_D=-N^{\mathsf T}\mathbf v+(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A_j/D)\mathbf n$. Differentiating $C\mathbf n/(r^2|D|)$ then gives exactly the manuscript's tensor. **Derived; domain: one simple root, fixed transmitter history. Supported as stated.** The remark that a source acceleration which does not decay with distance removes the extra inverse power of range follows from the last term, which is of size $|\mathbf A_j|/(r^2|D|^3)$.
3. **Powers at a wake-speed crossing (Section 2.4).** For the collinear path $x(t)=t+at^2/2$, whose speed passes through wake speed at $t=0$, the own-path root of reception time $T>0$ is $s=-T$, so the delay is $\tau=2T$, the range is $\tau$ and $D=a\tau/2$. The contribution is then of order $\tau^{-3}$; the longitudinal sensitivity, from the terms $1/(r^3D^2)$ and $a/(r^2D^3)$, of order $\tau^{-5}$; the transverse sensitivity, from $N\sim1/r$, of order $\tau^{-4}$. Agrees with all three powers. **Derived; domain: this quadratic path.** The fold powers, inverse square root and inverse three-halves, follow in the same way from $D\propto\sqrt{\delta}$ near an ordinary fold. The sentence about a smoothed selector's amplitude scaling with the inverse square root of width has no linked source in the manuscript; Codex located it in [receiver-wake-gradient-closure.md](receiver-wake-gradient-closure.md), lines 735 to 745, where a substitution gives the scaling $(w\kappa_2)^{-1/2}$ with a coefficient that depends on the profile. I have not read that passage.
4. **The ten-branch census (Section 2.5 and the root-ledger design).** For the two reflected histories with a shelf at $-4$, a ramp of speed two on $[-2,0]$ and rest on $[0,1]$, direct substitution into $|x_i(T)-x_j(s)|=T-s$ on each source piece gives: per own-path bundle, $s=T-4$ and $s=-T$ on $(0,1)$; per partner bundle, $s=T-9/2$ on $(0,1)$ and $s=1/2-T$ and $s=T-1/2$ on $(1/2,1)$. That is ten open branches, four of them own-path. Counting endpoints gives four point events per own-path bundle and five per partner bundle, the two partner branches sharing $(1/2,0)$: eighteen events and twenty half-branch incidences. The transmitter factors are $1,-1$ and $1,-1,1$. 5. **The sign-sector factorization.** $G=(1-\eta v_j)[s-(mT+k)]$ with $m=(1-\eta u_i)/(1-\eta v_j)$ and $k=\eta(d_j-d_i)/(1-\eta v_j)$ follows by expanding the absolute value on one sign sector. ### What the review found, in plain terms

The closure work asks whether the Master Equation, taken exactly as postulated, determines a future for every history it is given. The parent manuscript's answer is that it does on ordinary stretches of motion, that it fails to at specific events, and that several proposed repairs each fix one failure at a stated price. **The central theorem holds, and its conditions matter.** The statement that a member arriving at wake speed from below has no continuation is true for continuations with continuous velocity, when the equation is required to hold across the event. **Three structural observations.** The manuscript's one fidelity review covers a version several revisions old. Five of its eight substantive sections link no source. **Where the science stands, as these documents support it.** Exact finite solutions below wake speed exist in number. None found is stable. A disturbed one almost always runs to the wake-speed event. Whether a persistent solution exists is open. Nothing reviewed supports a stronger statement in either direction.
