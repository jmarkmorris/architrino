# Independent adjudication of the curved-path wake-speed obstructions

Subject: [Wake-speed obstructions on curved paths](curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions.md). Units are wake speed $c_f=1$ and coupling $K=1$. The adjudicator reasoned only from the stated equation: a receiver at position $\mathbf x$ at time $t$ receives, from every emission time $s<t$ with $t-s=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(s)\rvert$ (a causal root with delay $\tau=t-s>0$), the acceleration row $\pm\mathbf n/(\tau^2\lvert D\rvert)$ with $\mathbf n=(\mathbf x-\mathbf X(s))/\tau$ and transmitter factor $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V(s)$; rows from the receiver's own past path carry the sign $+$. The subject was not edited.

## Verdict

**Theorem D (downward crossing): accept with corrections.** Both parts are established at grade **derived**, conditional on hypotheses (D1) to (D3) and on the continuation class as the subject defines it. Part (a): with an integrable opposing remainder, the speed is not below one on any interval $(0,\delta')$. Part (b): with a bounded opposing remainder, the speed is not below one at any time in $[0,\delta_3)$. Every step of Lemmas 1 to 4 and of the proof was re-derived and found correct; the corrections concern wording and one remark, none touches the argument. What is not established, and the subject says so, is the exclusion of a continuation that comes down to wake speed and then keeps speed at or above one. This record adds a derived constraint on that surviving alternative (item 3 below); the constraint has not been reviewed by anyone else.

**Theorem U (arrival from below): accept with corrections.** It is established at grade **derived**, conditional on (U1), (U2) and the continuation class: there is no continuation whose velocity is continuous on $[0,\delta)$, absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of $(0,\delta)$, and which satisfies the equation at almost every time with ordinary roots. Steps 1 to 5 were re-derived and found correct, including the points the brief singled out: Step 1 needs no regularity beyond what (U2) states; Step 3 has no hidden assumption about remote own-path roots, because Lemma 6 removes them; the excess $\varepsilon=v-1$ is positive and absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of $(0,\delta_2)$ exactly where it is used. The one external input is the continuity of the partners' rows at the event along the continuation, which is a stated hypothesis, not a hidden one. The corrections are a quantifier in Step 2 and the scope phrase of the conclusion.

**Asymptotics section: accept the leading-order formulas, correct two subleading coefficients.** The leading formulas for delay, transmitter factor and row are derived and agree with a measured control to four or more digits at $t=10^{-3}$. The stated curvature shift of the emission time is too large by a factor of two, and the stated curvature correction to $D$ omits a term of the same order. Neither enters either theorem.

## Pre-target reference

This derivation was written to a scratch note before the subject file was opened. The brief had already disclosed: the two theorem statements; that a lemma bounds the chord direction, the transmitter factor and a forward component of the form constant$/\tau^2$; the shapes of the two delay bounds ($\tau^2<2t(t+6\omega(t)/a)$ and $\tau^2\le12t\varepsilon/a$); the inequality $(\varepsilon^2)'\ge5a/(6t)$; and the three leading asymptotic formulas. The constants below were therefore not found blind; the mechanisms were derived from the equation.

Notation: path $\mathbf X$, velocity $\mathbf V$, speed $v=\lvert\mathbf V\rvert$, event at time $0$, $\mathbf u_0=\mathbf V(0)$ with $\lvert\mathbf u_0\rvert=1$. For an own-path root with emission time $s$ and reception time $t$ write $F(s,t)=\lvert\mathbf X(t)-\mathbf X(s)\rvert-(t-s)$, so a root is a zero of $F$ with $s<t$.

**(b) Chord direction.** At a root the chord has length $\tau$, so $\mathbf n=\tau^{-1}\int_s^t\mathbf V\,dr$ is the average velocity over the span and is a unit vector. Hence $\mathbf n-\mathbf u_0=\tau^{-1}\int_s^t(\mathbf V-\mathbf u_0)\,dr$ and $\lvert\mathbf n-\mathbf u_0\rvert\le\vartheta$, where $\vartheta=\sup_{[s,t]}\lvert\mathbf V-\mathbf u_0\rvert$. Only continuity of the velocity is used. Both vectors are unit vectors, so $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u_0=1-\lvert\mathbf n-\mathbf u_0\rvert^2/2\ge1-\vartheta^2/2$.

**(c) Transmitter factor.** $D=(1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf u_0)-\mathbf n\cdot(\mathbf V(s)-\mathbf u_0)$ lies in $[-\vartheta,\ \vartheta+\vartheta^2/2]$, so $\lvert D\rvert\le\vartheta+\vartheta^2/2$ for either sign of $D$. No relation between $\lvert D\rvert$ and $\tau$ is available from continuity alone.

**(d) Forward component.** The row is $+\mathbf n/(\tau^2\lvert D\rvert)$. Its component along $\mathbf u_0$ is at least $(1-\vartheta^2/2)/((\vartheta+\vartheta^2/2)\tau^2)$, which exceeds $5/\tau^2$ for $\vartheta\le1/6$. Along the unit velocity at reception, which is within $2\vartheta$ of $\mathbf u_0$, a slightly smaller $\vartheta$ is needed. Every short-delay own-path row is therefore forward, whatever the number of such roots and whichever side of the event the emission lies on.

**(a) Existence.** Since chord $\le$ arc $=\int_s^tv$, a root needs average speed at least one over its span. Downward side (speed $\ge1+a\lvert r\rvert$ before, below one at reception): $F(s,t)<0$ for $s$ just below $t$; projecting the chord on $\mathbf u_0$ gives $F(s,t)\ge\int_s^0(v-1)\,dr-(\text{direction loss before})-t\,\omega(t)$ with $\omega(t)=\sup_{[0,t]}\lvert\mathbf V-\mathbf u_0\rvert$; the gain is at least $as^2/2$ and the loss before the event is cubic in $\lvert s\rvert$ when the incoming velocity is Lipschitz, so $F>0$ once $s^2$ exceeds a multiple of $t\,\omega(t)/a$. The intermediate value theorem in $s$ gives a root with $\tau^2\le2t^2+2s^2$, of the form $2t(t+c\,\omega(t)/a)$. The rate $a>0$ is essential on a curved path: if $v-1=O(\lvert r\rvert^k)$ with $k\ge2$ the gain is $O(\tau^{k+1})$ and can be beaten by the chord defect $\kappa^2\tau^3/24$ of a path with curvature $\kappa$; on a line there is no defect and no rate is needed. Upward side (speed $\le1-a\lvert r\rvert$ before, above one at reception): $F>0$ for $s$ just below $t$, and $F<0$ once the deficit $\int_s^0(1-v)\ge as^2/2$ exceeds the excess $\int_0^t(v-1)\le t\varepsilon(t)$ when $\varepsilon=v-1$ is nondecreasing; so a root exists with $s^2\le2t\varepsilon/a$ or a similar multiple, and with $t\le2\varepsilon/a$ this gives $\tau^2\le12t\varepsilon/a$. Remote own-path roots are absent on the upward side for small $t$, because a wholly sub-wake past leaves a fixed deficit that the vanishing excess after the event cannot repay.

**Consequences expected before reading.** Downward: $v'\ge5/\tau^2-b\ge1/t-b$ at times with $v<1$; on any interval $(t_1,t_2)$ of $v<1$ with $v(t_1)=1$ the speed would be increasing from one, a contradiction that needs only a bounded opposing remainder; with a merely integrable one, only the interval starting at the event is excluded, by the logarithm. Left open: speed at or above one after the event, since $F$ then need not change sign. Upward: before the event there are no own-path roots, so the left rate of the speed equals $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B(0)\ge a$; after it all own-path rows are forward, so $v'\ge a/2$, $\varepsilon\ge at/2$ and $\varepsilon$ is increasing; the newborn root then gives $\varepsilon'\ge5a/(12t\varepsilon)$ and a logarithmic divergence of $\varepsilon^2$. Asymptotics on a prescribed $C^2$ path with $v-1=\alpha_-\lvert r\rvert$ before and $-\alpha_+r$ after: $\int_s^t(v-1)=0$ gives $\lvert s\rvert=\lambda t$ with $\lambda=\sqrt{\alpha_+/\alpha_-}$, $\tau=(1+\lambda)t$, $D\approx1-v(s)=-\sqrt{\alpha_-\alpha_+}\,t$, row $\approx1/((1+\lambda)^2\sqrt{\alpha_-\alpha_+}\,t^3)$, with curvature entering one order higher.

**Comparison.** The subject's Lemma 2 is the same argument as (b) to (d) with $\vartheta=1/8$; its Lemmas 4 to 6 are the same mechanisms as (a) with explicit constants; its two proofs are the two expected consequences. The subject's part (b) of Theorem D coincides with the interval argument expected above. No step of the subject relies on a mechanism the independent derivation did not find.

## Item-by-item check

1. **Forward-chord lemma (Lemma 2). Correct as stated; derived.** The chord direction bound $\lvert\mathbf n-\mathbf u_0\rvert\le\theta=1/8$ uses only that the velocity is continuous on the span and within $\theta$ of $\mathbf u_0$ there, and that the chord has length exactly $\tau$ at a root; nothing more is assumed after the event. The bound $\lvert D\rvert\le\theta+\theta^2/2=17/128<1/7$ holds for both signs, since $D\in[-\theta,\theta+\theta^2/2]$. The bound $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf e(t)\ge(1-\theta-\theta^2/2)/(1+\theta)=111/144>3/4$ is correct, and the product gives $(3/4)\cdot7=5.25\ge5$ (the exact product of the two bounds is $111\cdot128/(144\cdot17)\approx5.80$). The stated angle is $\arccos(3/4)$ from the direction of motion at reception. The lemma needs the reception time inside $(0,\delta_1)$ and the emission time inside $[-\eta_1,t)$; both are in its statement.

2. **Root-existence lemmas. Correct; derived.** Lemma 3: both bounds verified, and the cubic defect $M^2\sigma^3/6$ under a Lipschitz velocity. Lemma 4: $g_t>0$ just below $t$ follows from $v(t)<1$ and continuity of the velocity at $t$; at $s=-\sigma_D(t)$ the gain is at least $a\sigma^2/2-M^2\sigma^3/6\ge a\sigma^2/3=2t\omega_+$ and the loss after the event at most $t\omega_+(1+\omega_+/2)$, so $g_t(-\sigma_D)<0$; the intermediate value theorem applies because $g_t$ is continuous; $\tau^2<(t+\sigma_D)^2\le2t(t+6\omega_+/a)$. Lemma 5: at $s=-\sigma_U$ the deficit $a\sigma_U^2/2=2E$ exceeds the excess $E$, so $g_t(-\sigma_U)\ge E>0$, while $g_t<0$ just below $t$; correct. The Theorem U bound $\tau^2\le2t^2+8t\varepsilon/a\le12t\varepsilon/a$ uses $E\le t\varepsilon$ and $t\le2\varepsilon/a$, both available from Step 3. Lemma 6: correct; it uses $v<1$ on the whole past and $\int_{-\eta_1}^0(1-v)\ge a\eta_1^2/2$. The rate hypothesis enters only where the subject says. The claim that it cannot be dropped on a curved path is confirmed with a refinement (correction 7): a linear rate is sufficient for the birth of the root, not necessary; birth fails for an approach as flat as $v-1=\beta r^2$ with $\beta<\kappa_0^2/8$ on a path of curvature $\kappa_0$ (derived at leading order, and measured below), while it occurs for $\beta>\kappa_0^2/8$.

3. **Theorem D. Correct; derived.** From $v'\ge L(t)-b(t)$ at almost every time with $v(t)<1$ and $L\ge1/t$: part (a) integrates on $[\epsilon,t_1]$ and lets $\epsilon\downarrow0$; the left side is at most $1/8$ because $v(t_1)<1$ and $v(\epsilon)\ge7/8$; correct. Part (b): $t^*$ is the last time in $[0,t_1]$ with $v\ge1$, $v(t^*)=1$, and $v'\ge L-B_0>0$ almost everywhere on $(t^*,t_1]$ because Lemma 4 applies at every time with $v<1$ without assuming $v<1$ earlier; absolute continuity is used only on $[\epsilon,t_1]$ with $\epsilon>t^*\ge0$, which the class supplies; correct. So "the speed does not go below one on $[0,\delta_3)$" is proved under a bounded opposing remainder. What is left open is exactly a continuation with $v(0)=1$ and $v\ge1$ on $[0,\delta_3)$. **Adjudicator's addition (derived here, not reviewed elsewhere).** Take $\eta_1\le a/M^2$. The estimate in Lemma 4 is monotone in $\lvert s\rvert$, so at every $t\in(0,\delta_2)$ every recent own-path root, not only the one found at times with $v<1$, has $s>-\sigma_D(t)$ and therefore forward component above $L(t)\ge1/t$. Let $C$ be the set of times in $(0,\delta_3)$ at which a recent root exists. Then $v'\ge1/t-B_0$ almost everywhere on $C$ and $v'\ge-B_0$ elsewhere, and since $v$ stays in $[7/8,9/8]$, $\int_C dt/t<\infty$: recent roots can be present only on a set of times that is logarithmically thin at the event. If in addition the remainder $\mathbf R$ is continuous at the event, then before the event the acceleration equals $\mathbf R$ (there is no recent root at $t<0$ within $2a/M^2$ of the event, by the same chord estimate), so $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf R(0)\le-a$, and a continuation with $v\ge1$ needs $C$ to have positive length in every $(0,t)$. The surviving alternative is therefore not a smooth rebound: it is a motion in which the remainder lowers the speed at rate about $a$ most of the time and recent own-path rows restore it during brief episodes. Whether such a motion exists is not decided here (**guessed**: no opinion offered).

4. **Theorem U. Correct; derived.** Step 1: $1-v(s)=\int_s^0\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf B\,dr$ needs the speed absolutely continuous on compact subintervals of $(-\eta_1,0)$, its continuity at $0$, and boundedness of $\mathbf B$ near $0$; then the mean of the integrand over $[s,0]$ is at least $a$ and tends to $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B(0)$ by continuity of $\mathbf B$ and of the direction at $0$ from the left. No further regularity is used. Step 3: for almost every $t\in(0,\delta_2)$ the acceleration is $\mathbf B$ plus own-path rows; Lemma 6 shows every own-path root is recent, Lemma 2 shows every recent row has positive forward component, so $v'\ge\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf B\ge a/2$ whatever the sign of $v-1$. The only assumption about $\mathbf B$ along the continuation is the stated one, continuity at $t=0$ from the right. Positivity of $\varepsilon$: integrate on $[t_1,t_2]\subset(0,\delta_2)$ and let $t_1\downarrow0$ using continuity of the speed at $0$; absolute continuity at $0$ is not needed. Absolute continuity of $\varepsilon$ and $\varepsilon^2$ on compact subintervals follows from Lemma 1 because $v\ge7/8$. Step 5: $\varepsilon'\ge a/2+5/\tau^2>5a/(12t\varepsilon)$, so $(\varepsilon^2)'\ge5a/(6t)$ almost everywhere and $\varepsilon(t_2)^2\ge(5a/6)\ln(t_2/t_1)$, unbounded as $t_1\downarrow0$ while $\varepsilon\le1/8$. The conclusion follows for the class. It does not follow for a velocity that is continuous but not absolutely continuous away from the event; that is outside the class by definition (correction 8).

5. **Continuation class. Fair, and no step uses more.** The class is wider than the usual almost-everywhere notion of solution, because absolute continuity is not required up to the event. Uses found: continuity of the velocity at each reception time (Lemmas 4, 5); continuity at the event (Lemma 2 window, limits $\epsilon\downarrow0$); absolute continuity on compact subintervals (Lemma 1 and every integration); the equation almost everywhere with ordinary roots and an absolutely convergent row sum (to discard positive terms). Excluded by definition and not by proof: velocity jumps; a velocity with a singular continuous part; degenerate roots ($D=0$) on a set of reception times of positive length, for example a straight stretch at speed exactly one. Theorem D additionally assumes (D3) along the continuation, and Theorem U assumes the continuity of $\mathbf B$ at the event along the continuation; both are stated.

6. **Asymptotics. Leading order correct (derived, and measured below); two subleading coefficients wrong.** Leading order confirmed: $\tau\approx(1+\lambda)t$, $\lvert D\rvert\approx\sqrt{\alpha_-\alpha_+}\,t$ with $D<0$ on the downward side, row $\approx1/((1+\lambda)^2\sqrt{\alpha_-\alpha_+}\,t^3)$; upward side $\rho\approx t\sqrt{m/a}$, $D\approx+t\sqrt{am}$. From the subject's own root condition $\alpha_-\rho^2/2-\alpha_+t^2/2=\kappa_0^2(t+\rho)^3/24$, linearizing about $\rho=\lambda t$ gives the shift $\kappa_0^2(1+\lambda)^3t^2/(24\lambda\alpha_-)$, not the stated $/(12\lambda\alpha_-)$. The net second-order change of $D$ is the geometric term $\kappa_0^2\tau^2/8$ plus $-\alpha_-$ times that shift, which is $\kappa_0^2(1+\lambda)^2(2\lambda-1)t^2/(24\lambda)$. The transverse-part statement (angle $\kappa_0\tau/2$, component about $\kappa_0/(2\tau\lvert D\rvert)$, directed away from the centre of curvature) and the exponent $p=-1/5$ of the self-consistent scaling were checked by hand only.

7. **Plausibility of the hypotheses in the motivating events. Inferred; the run records were not inspected.** Theorem U, first arrival of any member of an arrangement whose prescribed and computed histories are all below wake speed: (U1) holds for the arriving member; every partner has exactly one root at the receiver with $D\ge1-v_{\text{partner}}>0$, so the partner rows are continuous functions of the receiver's position and time; rows received just after the event were emitted a positive delay earlier, so they depend only on existing histories, and this remains true when symmetric partners reach wake speed at the same instant. The rate hypothesis is equivalent to $\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B(0)>0$, which is the generic case and fails only for tangential arrival. So the hypotheses of Theorem U plausibly hold at such events, with the measured rate $a$ as the item to check against resolution. Theorem D needs a member already above wake speed. In a run started below wake speed that state is reached only past an event of the Theorem U kind, beyond which no continuation in the class exists; the Lipschitz incoming velocity and the bounded remainder, including remote own-path rows, are then properties to be checked on the numerical record and are not consequences of the equation. For prescribed super-wake histories the hypotheses are plausible away from folds of remote own-path roots.

## Corrections

1. **Asymptotics, downward, curvature shift of $\rho$.** Defect: the coefficient is twice too large. Corrected statement: curvature shifts $\rho$ by $\kappa_0^2(1+\lambda)^3t^2/(24\lambda\alpha_-)$. The leading-order formulas and both theorems are unaffected.

2. **Asymptotics, downward, curvature correction to $D$.** Defect: "adds $\kappa_0^2\tau^2/8$ to $D$" gives only the geometric term; the shift of $\rho$ contributes at the same order. Corrected statement: $D=-\sqrt{\alpha_-\alpha_+}\,t+\kappa_0^2(1+\lambda)^2(2\lambda-1)t^2/(24\lambda)+o(t^2)$. Unaffected conclusions as in correction 1.

3. **Lemma 4, last sentence.** Defect: "every recent root lies in $(-\eta_1,0)$" excludes the endpoint without reason. Corrected statement: every recent root lies in $[-\eta_1,0)$. Not used elsewhere; conclusion survives.

4. **Theorem D(b), "explicit interval $[0,\delta_3)$".** Defect: $\delta_3$ depends on the continuation through its direction modulus $\omega_+$, so it is not a number fixed by the incoming data. Corrected statement: for each continuation there is $\delta_3>0$, determined by $a$, $M$, $\eta_1$, $B_0$ and that continuation's $\omega_+$, with $v\ge1$ on $[0,\delta_3)$. Conclusion survives.

5. **Theorem U, Step 2.** Defect: the displayed condition $\delta_2\omega_+(\delta_2)<a\eta_1^2/2$ serves Lemma 6 but does not give $\sigma_U\le\eta_1$; the text covers this only by "so small that". Corrected statement: choose $\delta_2$ with $t\,\omega_+(t)\le a\eta_1^2/4$ on $(0,\delta_2)$; then $E(t)\le t\omega_+(t)$ gives $\sigma_U(t)=2\sqrt{E/a}\le\eta_1$, and Lemma 6 holds as well. Conclusion survives.

6. **Remark after the proof of Theorem D (Lipschitz hypothesis replaced by $\lvert\mathbf x'(r)-\mathbf u_0\rvert^2\le a\lvert r\rvert$).** Defect: graded inferred with "adjusted constants". Corrected statement: the same $\sigma_D(t)=\sqrt{6t\omega_+(t)/a}$ works with no change and without the condition $\sigma_D\le a/M^2$, because the gain is then at least $a\sigma_D^2/4=\tfrac32t\omega_+>t\omega_+(1+\omega_+/2)$; the remark can be graded derived. Conclusion survives and is slightly strengthened.

7. **"Not covered" (2) and "Relation to the one-dimensional results": role of the linear rate.** Defect: "Theorem D needs the linear rate" reads as necessity. Corrected statement: the proof uses a linear rate; the birth of the root fails only for approaches at least as flat as quadratic with coefficient below $\kappa_0^2/8$; for approach exponents strictly between one and two a root is born, but the delay bound that makes the forward row non-integrable under mere continuity of the outgoing velocity is not established. Conclusion survives.

8. **Scope phrases.** Defect: "no continuation with continuous velocity" and "cannot pass through wake speed with continuous velocity" are broader than what is proved. Corrected statements: Theorem U excludes every continuation in the stated class (continuous velocity, absolutely continuous away from the event, equation almost everywhere with ordinary roots); Theorem D excludes going below wake speed and leaves open a descent to wake speed followed by speed at or above one. Conclusions survive at that scope.

## Measured controls

Instrument: a single-process script written for this adjudication (shared venv Python, SciPy adaptive quadrature for chords with a breakpoint at the event, a scan of $1200$ geometrically spaced delays from $10^{-3}t$ to $t+\eta_1$ for sign changes of $g_t$, then bracketing root refinement); it ran in about 15 seconds and is not retained in the repository. Its reach: two roots closer together than the scan spacing would be missed, so "one root" means one sign change on that scan. ## Limits and falsifiers

This adjudication is one reader's derivation plus a control on prescribed paths. The pre-target derivation was not blind to the constants and inequality shapes the brief disclosed. Agreement between the adjudicator's derivation and the subject is agreement between two constructions from the same stated equation; it is not evidence that the equation, the continuation class, or the hypotheses describe any computed event.
