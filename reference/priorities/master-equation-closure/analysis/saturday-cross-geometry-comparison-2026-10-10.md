# Ring, mirror pair and staggered lattice: what is shared and what is not

## Result

**In words.** The three geometries are not limited by the same kind of boundary, and the known obstruction belongs to only one kind.

The mirror pair and the staggered lattice both end at the same event: a member reaches wake speed while it is still well separated from every other member. This note proves, for a finite population whose pasts have Lipschitz velocities and stay below wake speed with a margin (the class is defined in Section 4), that at such an event the hypotheses which the existing wake-speed theorems place on the other members follow from two numbers: the member's distance to its nearest neighbour and the rate at which its speed is rising. If the distance is positive, every wake the member receives from the others for a short time afterwards was emitted before the event, so the others act on it as a fixed field, bounded and Lipschitz in the time and place of reception, and the question of how to continue becomes a question about one member and its own wake. If the rate is also positive, the existing theorems apply, each in its own class of continuations. This note also proves that such an event persists under small changes of a supplied past: every past close enough, in the sense made precise in Theorem 2, ends the same way. No numerical size of "close enough" is obtained. The mirror pair's obstruction is therefore not produced by its symmetry or by its motion on a line.

The six-member ring is different. Its members move well above wake speed, their own earlier wakes reach them at a positive delay as ordinary contributions, and the equation is regular in a neighbourhood of the ring. A different boundary lies close to the ring along one direction: if the exact circle is shrunk at fixed angular rate by $0.87$ percent (the 48-contribution ring) or $0.088$ percent (the 72-contribution ring), two wakes received from another member merge (a fold). That gives upper bounds for the neighbourhood sizes that the two ring theorems leave unspecified. It does not show that this fold is the nearest boundary among all pasts, that a perturbed ring reaches a fold before anything else, or that the ring would pass through one regularly: along the shrinking direction the fold is not of the transverse kind that the existing fold control covers, and no solution through a fold has been constructed.

For the lattice, the obstruction at an arrival carries over to a spatially localized preparation only conditionally: an arrival must happen there, and the complete field from the other members must be a fixed, bounded, Lipschitz field near it. Whether an arrival happens is not settled by the coherent result. At every time a localized history differs from the coherent branch, at some member, by at least the coherent displacement, so it is not inside the narrow enclosure that the coherent certificate uses near its event. And the staggered pattern is not the only growing linear pattern, nor by measurement the fastest: its rate is $2.795$, against a measured $3.028$ for the pattern in which whole planes move against each other. What a localized preparation meets first is open.

Notation. A *member* is one architrino. The row received by member $i$ from an emission of member $j$ at time $s$, at reception time $t$ and position $\mathbf x$, is

$$
g\,\sigma_{ij}\,\frac{\mathbf n}{r^2\,\lvert D\rvert},\qquad r=t-s=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert,\quad \mathbf n=\frac{\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s)}{r},\quad D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf X_j'(s),
$$

with $\sigma_{ij}=+1$ for equal polarities and $-1$ for opposite ones; this is the row of the [canonical equation](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/master-equation.md#the-master-equation-canonical-form), and the equation gives acceleration directly. A *root* is such an emission time; it is *ordinary* when $D\ne0$ and it is isolated. An *own-path* root has $j=i$ and positive delay. *Below wake speed* means speed less than one.

## 3. Three kinds of boundary and the two controls

A regular solution can be advanced by steps shorter than the smallest delay of any root, as long as every root stays ordinary; the ring theorem lists the ways this can stop. Sorted by which quantity fails, there are three.

| | Arrival at wake speed (W) | Contact of two members (C) | Fold at positive range (F) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What goes to zero | the delay of an own-path root, born at zero delay | the range between two different members | $D$ of one root, at positive range and delay |
| Needs | the receiver at speed one | two members at one place | the source at or above speed one at emission, moving along the line of sight |
| Number of roots involved | one new root per member | new roots in the two cross channels | two roots merge or appear together |
| Row near the event | forward part at least $5/\tau^2$ with the delay $\tau\to0$; $t^{-3}$ on the model path | bounded in the control | $\lvert h\rvert^{-1/2}$ in the time $h$ from the event |
| Integrable in time | no | yes in the control | yes |
| Fixed by the past | no: the row depends on the member's own continuation | no: each member's row depends on the other's continuation | yes, when the emission is earlier than the reception by a positive delay |
| Status under the unchanged equation | no continuation in the stated classes | no rule; nothing derived | the two rows have a finite time integral when the fold is transverse ($a_f\ne0$, $b_f\ne0$); no continuation through a fold has been constructed |

**Control 1, the four-member contact.** In the [accepted construction](mec-008-independent-adjudication.md) two target members are *supplied* a past that brings them to speed $2$ at release, a hundredth apart; all rows they receive come from resting earlier positions at range above $1.6$, the acceleration stays below $2\times10^{-6}$, and they meet at $0.0049999999875<T_*<0.005$. No own-path root comes near zero delay. Neither member has received anything the other emitted while approaching, because each outruns the other's recent wake. Reconstructed here in exact rational arithmetic: the supplied polynomial, its end values, the factorization that excludes roots on the joining segment, the row bound and the lower end of the bracket (instrument C). What it rules out in a proposed universal repair: (a) any rule that treats equal positions of two members as the birth of an own-path root, since the channels are different and the own-path channels are uniformly regular there; (b) completeness of any rule that is triggered only by an unbounded row, since nothing diverges before this contact and regular evolution still ends; (c) the claim that contact can never come first, which is proved only when the approaching members are below wake speed and receive each other's wakes (the mirror class and the coherent lattice).

**Control 2, the fold at positive range.** In the [accepted conditional result](mec-008-self-complement-independent-adjudication.md) two roots of one channel merge at range $r_f>0$ with $D=0$, and the two rows add to

$$
\frac{\sqrt2\,\sigma K}{r_f^{2}\sqrt{\lvert a_fb_f\rvert}}\;\frac{\mathbf n_f}{\sqrt{\lvert h\rvert}},\qquad a_f=\mathbf n_f\cdot\mathbf V_r-1,\quad b_f=\frac{\lvert\mathbf V_t\rvert^{2}-1}{r_f}-\mathbf n_f\cdot\mathbf A_t ,
$$

whose time integral over a span $\epsilon$ is finite, of order $\sqrt\epsilon$. Reconstructed here on two prescribed examples: a source on a line, where the two-row sum is exact algebra and tends to the coefficient (ratio $0.999999992$ at $h=10^{-8}$), and a source on a circle with the curvature term active (ratios $0.9999999937$ and $1.000000024$ at two folds), with roots on one side of the event only and $D$ of opposite signs (instrument C). No actual solution passing through a fold has been constructed, there or here, and the control does not prove existence or uniqueness of a continuation through one. What the control establishes for a proposed universal repair: (a) a vanishing $D$, the sign of $D$, or a large row does not by itself distinguish an arrival from a transverse fold, since the fold has all three while its two rows, under the control's hypotheses, have a finite time integral; (b) the fold's finite integral cannot be carried over to an arrival, since at an arrival the range itself goes to zero, there is one new root and not two, and the integral diverges; (c) the two merging rows have opposite signs of $D$ and the same direction, and T02 itself contains an ordinary root with $D=-0.1955$, so a proposal that claims to keep the unchanged regular equation must keep ordinary rows of either sign.

Classification is not an update. Nothing above says what follows a contact or an arrival.

## 4. The arrival obstruction is local, needs two numbers, and is stable

### Setting

A finite population of $N$ members with polarities $\pm1$ and common coupling $g>0$. A *supplied past* is a family of paths $\boldsymbol\eta_j$ on $(-\infty,0]$ that are continuously differentiable with locally Lipschitz velocity, with speeds at most some $w_0<1$ on the whole of $(-\infty,0]$, and with distinct positions at time $0$. Equivalently the velocity is locally absolutely continuous with a locally essentially bounded derivative: the acceleration may jump at joins, but the velocity has no singular part. A bound on the classical second derivative almost everywhere would not be enough; a velocity given by a continuous Cantor function has zero derivative almost everywhere and is not Lipschitz, and the factor $D$ of a moving root would inherit that.

The *regular solution* from a supplied past is the solution of the unchanged equation for $t>0$, with position and velocity continuous at release, as long as every speed is below one and all members are apart. On that interval no own-path root exists (a chord is shorter than the delay when the speed is below one), each ordered pair has exactly one root, $D\ge1-\lvert\mathbf X_j'(s)\rvert>0$, and every delay is at least half the smallest separation, so the solution is unique, is obtained by steps, and again has locally Lipschitz velocity. Let $T_*\le\infty$ be the end of that interval. An all-past solution below wake speed is a special case when it is cut at a time before which its speeds have a common margin below one.

For a member $i$ whose position has a limit at $T_*$ write $d_i$ for its distance at $T_*$ to the nearest other member, and

$$
N_i=\{(t,\mathbf x):\ \lvert t-T_*\rvert<d_i/4,\ \lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf x_i(T_*)\rvert<d_i/4\}.
$$

### Lemma 1 (the others act as a fixed field)

*Assume $T_*<\infty$ and $d_i>0$.* (a) For every $(t,\mathbf x)\in N_i$ and every $j\ne i$ there is exactly one time $s_j(t,\mathbf x)<T_*$ with $t-s=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert$; it satisfies $s_j\le T_*-d_i/4$ and $r\ge d_i/4$; the corresponding rows add to a field $\mathbf B_i(t,\mathbf x)$ that is bounded by $16g(N-1)/(d_i^{2}(1-w_i))$ and Lipschitz on $N_i$, where $w_i<1$ bounds the speeds of the others on the compact set of emission times used. $\mathbf B_i$ is determined by the paths before $T_*-d_i/4$ alone. (b) Let the paths be continued beyond $T_*$ in any way that keeps every position continuous. Then there is $\delta_i>0$ such that for $T_*\le t<T_*+\delta_i$ the roots of member $i$ in the other members' paths are exactly the $s_j(t,\mathbf x_i(t))$, and the sum of their rows is $\mathbf B_i(t,\mathbf x_i(t))$.

*Proof.* Positions have limits at $T_*$ because speeds are below one. Put $G_j(s)=(t-s)-\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert$ for $s<T_*$. Its derivative is $-1+\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf X_j'(s)\le-(1-\lvert\mathbf X_j'(s)\rvert)<0$, so $G_j$ is strictly decreasing. It tends to $+\infty$ as $s\to-\infty$, because the speed is at most $w_0$ on the supplied part. As $s\uparrow T_*$ it tends to $(t-T_*)-\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(T_*)\rvert\le d_i/4-3d_i/4<0$. So there is exactly one root. Write $\theta=T_*-s_j>0$. Then $r=\theta+(t-T_*)\le\theta+d_i/4$, and $r=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s_j)\rvert\ge3d_i/4-\theta$ because the source moves less than $\theta$ in time $\theta$. Hence $\theta\ge d_i/4$, and $r\ge\max\{3d_i/4-\theta,\ \theta-d_i/4\}\ge d_i/4$. The emission times used over the closure of $N_i$ form a compact set of times at or before $T_*-d_i/4$, bounded below because $G_j>0$ at some fixed early time for all of $N_i$; on it the continuous speeds, each below one, have a maximum $w_i<1$, so $D\ge1-w_i$. The row is then at most $g/(r^2D)\le16g/(d_i^{2}(1-w_i))$. The implicit-function theorem with $\partial_sG_j=-D$ makes $s_j$ Lipschitz in $(t,\mathbf x)$, and the velocities of the sources, which are Lipschitz on that compact set of times, make $\mathbf X_j'(s_j)$, hence the row, Lipschitz. The field is Lipschitz and need not be smoother. This is (a). For (b), choose $\delta_i\le d_i/4$ so small that no member has moved more than $d_i/4$ from its position at $T_*$ before $T_*+\delta_i$; this uses only continuity and the finite number of members. A root emitted at $s\in[T_*,t)$ would need $\lvert\mathbf x_i(t)-\mathbf x_j(s)\rvert=t-s<d_i/4$, while that distance is at least $d_i-d_i/4-d_i/4$. So no root is emitted at or after $T_*$, and $(t,\mathbf x_i(t))\in N_i$, so (a) gives the rest. $\square$

Part (b) asks nothing of the other members after $T_*$ except continuous positions. They may reach wake speed at the same instant, later, or never; two of them may even be in contact with each other at $T_*$. The speed of member $i$ after the event is not restricted either. The collinear two-member form of this observation is hypothesis H2 and Fact 2 of the [class-level adjudication](../collinear-research/analysis/class-level-first-exit-independent-adjudication-2026-10-03.md), and the lattice adjudication proves the corresponding statement for the coherent branch; the curved-path theorems take it as their hypothesis (P). What is added here is that for a finite population in the class above it follows from $d_i>0$.

### Theorem 1 (two numbers suffice)

*Assume $T_*<\infty$, $d_i>0$, and that the speed of member $i$ tends to one at $T_*$.* Then its velocity has a limit $\mathbf u_0$ there, and $\beta_i=\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B_i(T_*,\mathbf x_i(T_*))\ge0$. *If $\beta_i>0$*, hypotheses (U1), (U2) of the [curved-path obstruction](curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions.md) and (P) of the [jump exclusion](curved-path-wake-speed-jump-exclusion.md), in the corrected form given by its adjudication, hold for member $i$ at $T_*$. Consequently, whatever the other members do with continuous positions: there is no continuation in which the velocity of member $i$ is continuous at $T_*$, absolutely continuous afterwards and satisfies the equation almost everywhere with ordinary roots (Theorem U there); in the measure form the velocity of member $i$ cannot jump at $T_*$ (Theorem J); and there is no continuation in which it has bounded variation and satisfies the measure form (Theorem BV, with that note's direction rule).

*Proof.* For $T_*-d_i/4<t<T_*$ the regular solution has $\mathbf x_i''=\mathbf B_i(t,\mathbf x_i(t))$ by Lemma 1(a), since below wake speed there is no own-path row and each other member has one root. The right side is bounded and continuous up to $T_*$, so the velocity has a limit and the speed is continuously differentiable up to $T_*$ with derivative $\beta_i$ there; $\beta_i\ge0$ because the speed was below one. If $\beta_i>0$ then $1-v(s)\ge(\beta_i/2)\lvert s-T_*\rvert$ near $T_*$, which with the whole past below wake speed is (U1). (U2) is the equation $\mathbf x_i''=\mathbf B_i$ with $\mathbf B_i$ bounded and continuous at the event along any continuation, which is Lemma 1. (P) asks that near the event in time and position every root in another member's path be ordinary and emitted before the event, with rows summing to a bounded field continuous at the event; that is Lemma 1 again. $\square$

The grades of the conclusions are those of the cited theorems: derived, each with an adjudication made in the same session as its subject, the bounded-variation statement conditional on the measure form stated there. The case $\beta_i=0$ is not covered by them and is not covered here.

For the mirror pair the two numbers are $d=0.103013$ and $\beta=6.389528$; the partner emission precedes the event by $0.2913$, far outside the window $d/4=0.0258$. For the coherent lattice they are $d>0.5089$ and $\beta>5.9711$, with cross emissions earlier than $8.5909$ against $t_*>9.3046$; there the population is infinite and Lemma 1 is replaced by the adjudication's own argument (Section 6).

### Proposition 2 (how regular evolution can end)

*If $T_*<\infty$, then either the smallest distance between two members tends to zero at $T_*$, or all distances have positive limits and at least one member's speed tends to one.* *Proof.* Positions have limits. If all distances stay positive, Lemma 1(a) applies to every member with a common $d$, so every acceleration is bounded near $T_*$ and every velocity has a limit. If every limiting speed were below one, the solution could be advanced beyond $T_*$ by one more step, since delays are at least $d/2$ and the rows are Lipschitz, contradicting the definition of $T_*$. $\square$

So for pasts below wake speed there are three outcomes: regular for all time, contact, or arrival. For two opposite members in mirror symmetry, [initiative 1](../binary-research/analysis/saturday-two-member-subwake-periodicity-2026-10-10.md) shows at its reviewed scope that the first outcome cannot be a periodic or relative-periodic motion; it does not exclude dispersal or other bounded motion, and without mirror symmetry the question is open. Folds are absent from this class because a fold needs a source at or above speed one. Which outcome occurs is not decided here. That contact cannot come before an arrival is proved for the mirror class and for the coherent lattice by their owners; for a general finite population below wake speed it is not proved (the reciprocal-delay identity gives an infinite integral of the magnitude of the approaching pair's row, but in two or three dimensions its direction is not known to settle).

### Theorem 2 (an arrival at a positive rate is stable)

*Let a supplied past $\bar{\boldsymbol\eta}$ have $T_*<\infty$ with all distances positive on $[0,T_*]$, smallest value $\bar d$, and let every member that reaches speed one at $T_*$ have $\beta_i>0$. Fix $S$ so large that every emission time used by the reference, at receptions on $[0,T_*]$ and at receptions within $\bar d/4$ of the event in time and position, is later than $-S+1$. Then for each $w_0<1$ and each $C_2$ there are $\varepsilon>0$ and $C$ such that every supplied past $\boldsymbol\eta$ with speeds at most $w_0$ on $(-\infty,0]$, velocity Lipschitz with constant at most $C_2$ on $[-S,0]$, and*

$$
\sup_{[-S,0]}\bigl(\lvert\boldsymbol\eta-\bar{\boldsymbol\eta}\rvert+\lvert\boldsymbol\eta'-\bar{\boldsymbol\eta}'\rvert\bigr)<\varepsilon
$$

*has a regular solution that ends at a time $T_*(\boldsymbol\eta)$ with $\lvert T_*(\boldsymbol\eta)-T_*\rvert\le C\varepsilon$, all distances positive, and at least one member at speed one with $\beta>0$. By Theorem 1 that member has no continuation in the classes named there.* Nothing is assumed about $\boldsymbol\eta$ before $-S$ except the speed bound, and no value of $\varepsilon$ is obtained.

*Proof.* Take $w_0$ and $C_2$ at least as large as the reference's own bounds. Let $\bar{\mathbf y}_i$ be the reference solution on $[0,T_*)$ extended to $[T_*,T_*+\delta]$ by solving $\mathbf y''=\bar{\mathbf B}_i(t,\mathbf y)$ with the field of Lemma 1. This extension is a comparison path, not a solution of the equation after $T_*$; its speed $\bar\sigma_i=\lvert\bar{\mathbf y}_i'\rvert$ is continuously differentiable near $T_*$. Let $A$ be the set of members with $\bar\sigma_i(T_*)=1$. Choose $\kappa,\delta>0$ with $\kappa+\delta\le\bar d/8$ so small that, with $a=T_*-\kappa$ and $b=T_*+\delta$, on $[a,b]$ one has $\bar\sigma_i'\ge\beta_i/2$ for $i\in A$, $\bar\sigma_k\le1-m$ for $k\notin A$ with some $m>0$, and every $\bar{\mathbf y}_i$ stays within $\bar d/6$ of $\bar{\mathbf x}_i(T_*)$.

*Step 1, up to $a$.* On $[0,a]$ the reference has speeds at most $1-m'$ on the evolved part and at most $w_0$ on the supplied part, so every root it uses has $D\ge m_D=\min\{m',1-w_0\}$; distances are at least $\bar d$ and delays at least $\bar d/2$. *Old emissions are excluded by a sign.* For each reception the reference root is later than $-S+1$ and the incidence function decreases at rate at least $m_D$ in the emission time, so at emission time $-S$ it is at least $m_D$; changing the receiver and source positions by less than $m_D/4$ each keeps it positive, and it is strictly decreasing along any past with speeds below one, so the perturbed root is later than $-S$ whatever $\boldsymbol\eta$ does earlier. *Continuous dependence.* On each step shorter than $\bar d/4$ the equation is an ordinary differential equation whose right side is Lipschitz in the receiver's position and in the earlier paths on $[-S,\cdot]$, measured in position and velocity: a root moves by at most the position change divided by the floor of $D$, and the source velocity at the moved root changes by at most the velocity change plus the Lipschitz constant of the velocity times the root's displacement. Gronwall's inequality over finitely many steps gives a solution from $\boldsymbol\eta$ on $[0,a]$ within $C_1\varepsilon$ of the reference in position and velocity, with speeds at most $1-m'/2$, $D\ge m_D/2$ and distances above $\bar d-2C_1\varepsilon$; it exists up to $a$ because it cannot leave the region where these margins hold. Call this solution with its supplied past the *known prefix* $\mathbf y^{\boldsymbol\eta}$ on $(-\infty,a]$.

*Step 2, fields on a fixed tube.* For each member $i$ let $Q_i$ be the set of receptions $(t,\mathbf x)$ with $a\le t\le b$ and $\lvert\mathbf x-\bar{\mathbf x}_i(T_*)\rvert\le\bar d/4$. For $(t,\mathbf x)\in Q_i$, $j\ne i$ and $s\le a$ put $G_j(s)=(t-s)-\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf y_j^{\boldsymbol\eta}(s)\rvert$; this uses the known prefix only. At $s=a$ the distance is at least $\bar d-\bar d/4-\kappa-C_1\varepsilon$, while $t-a\le\bar d/8$, so $G_j(a)<0$ for small $\varepsilon$. At $s=-S$ it is positive as in Step 1. It is strictly decreasing. Hence there is exactly one root in $(-S,a)$, with $D\ge m_D/2$; and with $\theta=a-s$, the range satisfies $r\ge\theta$ and $r\ge5\bar d/8-C_1\varepsilon-\theta$, so $r\ge\bar d/4$. The rows add to a field $\mathbf B_i^{\boldsymbol\eta}$ on $Q_i$ that is bounded and Lipschitz, is constructed without reference to any solution after $a$, and for $\varepsilon=0$ is the reference field. By the two mechanisms of Step 1 it differs from $\bar{\mathbf B}_i$ by at most $C_3\varepsilon$ on $Q_i$.

*Step 3, comparison paths stay in the tube.* Let $\mathbf z_i$ solve $\mathbf z''=\mathbf B_i^{\boldsymbol\eta}(t,\mathbf z)$ from the data of the known prefix at $a$. As long as $\mathbf z_i$ is in $Q_i$, Gronwall's inequality gives $\mathbf z_i$ within $C_4\varepsilon$ of $\bar{\mathbf y}_i$ in position and velocity, and then in acceleration; since $\bar{\mathbf y}_i$ is within $\bar d/6$ of the tube's centre, $\mathbf z_i$ is within $\bar d/6+C_4\varepsilon<\bar d/4$, so it cannot reach the edge of the tube before $b$. Hence $\mathbf z_i$ exists on all of $[a,b]$ with these bounds.

*Step 4, the actual solution.* While the solution from $\boldsymbol\eta$ is regular on $[a,b]$, each member moves less than $\bar d/8$, so it is in its tube, and two members are at least $\bar d/2>b-a$ apart at any two times of $[a,b]$; no root is emitted after $a$, the roots are those of Step 2, and the solution obeys $\mathbf y_i''=\mathbf B_i^{\boldsymbol\eta}(t,\mathbf y_i)$. By uniqueness it equals $\mathbf z$ until the first time some $\lvert\mathbf z_i'\rvert$ equals one, and it cannot end earlier, because distances stay positive and speeds below one.

*Step 5, the crossing.* For $i\in A$ the speed $\lvert\mathbf z_i'\rvert$ is within $C_4\varepsilon$ of $\bar\sigma_i$ together with its derivative, so for $C_4\varepsilon<\min_i\beta_i\min\{\kappa,\delta,1\}/4$ it is below one at $a$, above one at $b$, increasing at rate at least $\beta_i/4$, and equals one exactly once, at a time $t_i$ with $\lvert t_i-T_*\rvert\le4C_4\varepsilon/\beta_i$. For $k\notin A$ the speed stays below one. Hence $T_*(\boldsymbol\eta)=\min_{i\in A}t_i$; at that time all distances exceed $\bar d/2$, and the member attaining the minimum has speed one with rate at least $\beta_i/4>0$. $\square$

The members need not arrive together in the perturbed solution, and the perturbed motion need not be on a line or symmetric. The theorem does not say that pasts far from the reference reach an arrival, it gives no numerical radius, and it does not cover a reference with a member arriving at zero rate or with a contact at $T_*$.

**Corollary for the mirror pair.** Every supplied two-member past close enough to the resting one in the sense above has a regular solution ending at a positive-rate arrival of at least one member at positive separation, with no continuation in the classes of Theorem 1. *Grade: derived, as an application of Theorem 2 to the accepted mirror ledger.*

## 5. The ring: a fold lies close along the rigid shrinking direction

Instrument B rebuilds the ring ledger from its own derivation of the root condition $\beta\sin v-v=m\pi/6$ (given in its header) at 40 digits. For T02 it finds 8 roots per receiver, by source offset $1,3,1,1,1,1$, tangential sum $2\times10^{-33}$, $R=0.9759764318016906968$, all eight delays and signed $D$ equal to the documented table, smallest $\lvert D\rvert$ $0.1955478193808212$. For T04 it finds 12 per receiver, by offset $1,3,3,3,1,1$, tangential sum $8\times10^{-18}$ at the documented 19-digit speed, $R=0.56173170007129022$, smallest delay $0.1479033789896324$, largest $1.0863003770930462$, smallest $\lvert D\rvert$ $0.11680602873629687$, all equal to the documented margins. *Grade: measured; agreement of a separately derived and separately coded ledger with the certificates.* The balance and the censuses themselves remain the certificates' results.

Three facts follow from the ledger.

1. **The ring keeps ordinary own-path rows.** Each member receives its own earlier wake at delay $1.907$ (T02) or $0.858$ (T04) with $D=1.388$ or $2.919$. Without that row the tangential sum would be $0.0401$ (T02) or $0.0947$ (T04) in units of $K/R^2$ instead of zero, and the circle would not be a solution.
2. **Most of the inward acceleration comes from a pair of roots close to merging.** The two roots of the top level, from the adjacent member in T02 and from the opposite member in T04, supply $93$ and $91$ percent of the radial sum, with $D$ of opposite signs and magnitudes near $0.2$ and $0.12$.
3. **That pair merges nearby in the rigid family.** For a rigid ring at speed $\beta$ the pair exists only above the speed $\beta_q$ with $\sqrt{\beta_q^{2}-1}-\arccos(1/\beta_q)=q\pi/6$; the ladder evidence records these thresholds as the fold boundaries between root-count classes. Evaluated here: $\beta_1=1.81045123826829$, which is $0.875$ percent below T02, and $\beta_3=2.9716938707138$, which is $0.0879$ percent below T04. The next pair would appear $31.8$ and $18.4$ percent above. Wake speed, where the own-path root would be at zero delay, is $45$ and $66$ percent below.

**Consequence: upper bounds on two unspecified radii.** The [ring theorem](../braid-program/evidence/2026-09-01-planar-three-binary-local-history-flow-well-posedness.md) asserts a radius $\varepsilon_*$ such that every supplied past on $[-H,0]$ closer than $\varepsilon_*$ to the exact T04 circle keeps the same 72 roots, all simple; its norm is the maximum over the six members of the supremum norms of position, velocity and acceleration. Shrink the exact circle about its centre by the factor $1-\delta$ at the same angular rate. This is a supplied past in that space. Position, velocity and acceleration all scale by $1-\delta$, so its distance from the exact circle is exactly

$$
\delta\,\max\{R,\ \beta,\ \Omega^{2}R\}=\delta\,\Omega^{2}R .
$$

Its roots at release are those of a rigid ring at speed $(1-\delta)\beta$, and at $\delta_3=1-\beta_3/\beta$ the two roots of the top level have merged into one root with $D=0$. A ball in which all 72 roots stay simple cannot contain that past, so

$$
\varepsilon_*\le\delta_3\,\Omega^{2}R=8.78627\times10^{-4}\times15.748627=0.0138372\ldots,
$$

which rounds up to $0.0139$. If the norm were the sum of the three parts the same argument would give $\delta_3(R+\beta+\Omega^2R)=0.0169440\ldots$, rounded up to $0.0170$. In the same way the [T02 root-neighbourhood argument](../braid-program/analysis/t02-nonlinear-history-independent-adjudication-2026-10-03.md) keeps all 48 roots simple for complete pasts within $b$ of the circle in position and in velocity; the shrunk T02 circle differs by $\delta R$ in position and $\delta\beta$ in velocity, and at $\delta_1=1-\beta_1/\beta$ its top pair has merged, so $b\le\delta_1\beta=\beta-\beta_1=0.0159797\ldots$ if the neighbourhood is open and $b<0.0159797\ldots$ if it is closed; either way $b$ is at most $0.0160$ after rounding up. *Grade: derived from the measured ledger and the theorems' own statements. These are upper bounds obtained from one direction; other directions may be more restrictive, and no lower bound is obtained.* Falsifier: a statement of either theorem with a certified radius above these values, or a recomputation of $\beta_1$, $\beta_3$ that differs.

**What this does and does not show.** It shows that a fold of the top root pair lies close to each ring reference along the rigid shrinking direction, much closer than wake speed or a contact lies along that direction. It does not show that this fold is the nearest boundary in the full space of pasts, and it does not show what a perturbed ring meets first. It also does not show that a ring could pass through the fold regularly. The fold control requires $a_f\ne0$, and along the rigid family the source and receiver factors are equal, so $a_f=0$ at the merging: the transverse result does not apply to the family, and for a perturbed ring it could apply only through the perturbation, which has not been computed. No solution of the unchanged equation through a fold has been constructed, here or in the control. What can be said from the ledger alone is that the merging pair carries about nine tenths of the radial sum, so the balance beyond the fold, whatever the motion there, is not a small change of the ring's. *Grade: the shares are measured; the last remark is inferred.*

## 6. The lattice: what carries over to a localized preparation

The [localized preparation](../lattice-research/analysis/saturday-localized-nonlinear-preparation-2026-10-10.md) of this campaign gives exact solutions at $g=16$ on $T\le0$ that are small, decay in space faster than any power, and are built on the band of wavevectors around the staggered one. They are defined up to time zero with speeds at most $3/16$. Nothing in that result follows them to an event.

**What carries over, as a condition on the full field.** The per-member argument of Section 4 needs no symmetry, but for infinitely many members Lemma 1 is not available and its conclusion has to be supplied. A sufficient condition, at an event where member $i$ reaches speed one at time $T_*$ with its whole past below wake speed, is the following.

- **(F1) Old, ordinary roots only.** There are a neighbourhood $N$ of the event in reception time and position and a number $\rho>0$ such that, for every outgoing continuation that is admitted (continuous positions, in the class of the obstruction being applied), every root of member $i$ in another member's path at a reception in $N$ has range at least $\rho$, is ordinary, and is emitted before $T_*$ by a gap bounded below. For infinitely many members this needs a bound, common to all members, on how far each moves during the window; continuity of each position separately does not give one.
- **(F2) The full prescribed sum is a fixed, bounded, Lipschitz field.** The complete sum of the other members' rows, taken with the lattice's prescription, that is, the original eight-source block sum for the resting part evaluated at the actual receiver positions in $N$, together with the changed-source correction, converges and defines a function $\mathbf B_i(t,\mathbf x)$ on $N$ that is bounded, Lipschitz, and determined by the paths before $T_*$. Both parts matter: the resting block field must be regular at the receiver positions that actually occur, and the corrections and their Lipschitz constants must be summable.
- **(F3) Regular histories.** The paths have Lipschitz velocities, with a common margin below wake speed on the emission times used.

*If (F1) to (F3) hold and $\beta_i=\mathbf u_0\cdot\mathbf B_i>0$,* the verification in the proof of Theorem 1 goes through unchanged, and the cited conclusions hold for member $i$ in their own classes: the continuous-velocity class of the curved-path obstruction, and the measure form with its direction rule for the jump exclusion. *Grade: derived, conditional on (F1) to (F3).*

In the coherent case the lattice adjudication proves this condition for continuations that keep every member within $1/3$ of its anchor: ranges are then at least $1/3$, emissions precede the event by at least $1/6$, the resting block field is regular in that receiver ball, and the corrections and their derivatives are summable because the old motion is exponentially small. For a localized solution continued forward, none of this is inherited. The event would be at a different place with a different receiver neighbourhood, the regularity of the resting block field there has to be shown, and the summability needs the far members' motion to stay small up to the event. Verifying (F1) to (F3) for a localized forward solution is open. A failure of one of them would be a failure of this sufficient condition and would not show that a localized preparation fails to reach an obstruction.

**What does not carry over, and what is not settled.**

1. *Distance from the coherent branch.* A localized history has displacements $\mathbf u_j(t)$ tending to zero far away at each time, while the coherent branch displaces member $j$ by $\sigma_jq(t)\mathbf e_3$. Hence $\sup_j\lvert\mathbf u_j(t)-\sigma_jq(t)\mathbf e_3\rvert\ge\lvert q(t)\rvert$ at every time. A localized history therefore lies outside every uniform ball of radius less than $\lvert q(t)\rvert$ about the coherent positions at that time. Larger balls are not excluded, and at early times, where $q(t)$ is small, the absolute distance can be small. Near the coherent event $q$ is between $0.22$ and $0.25$, while the certificate encloses every member within an error below $0.005$ of one common scalar motion; a localized history is not inside that enclosure, so the certificate says nothing about it. *Grade: derived.*
2. *Another growing pattern, measured to be faster.* For displacements $\boldsymbol\phi\,e^{i\mathbf k\cdot i+\lambda T}$ the linear equation reads $\lambda^2\boldsymbol\phi=g\,M(\mathbf k,\lambda)\boldsymbol\phi$. At $\mathbf k=(0,0,\pi)$ the matrix is diagonal, and instrument D evaluates $\lambda^2-gM_{33}$ in floating point as $-1.7456$ at $\lambda=2.9$ and $+5.9922$ at $\lambda=3.5$. The part of the lattice sum left out of the cube of radius 14 is bounded by a derived expression that is below $10^{-15}$ here. The instrument has no bound on its rounding error; the summed terms are a few tens of thousands of numbers of order one or smaller, so an error near the size of the values $1.7$ and $6$ would be surprising, but that is a judgment and not an enclosure. If the two signs are right, continuity gives a real root in $(2.9,3.5)$, above the staggered rate; the instrument's bisection puts one at $3.027972$. At $(0,\pi,\pi)$ it finds $2.929052$. Its downward scan over $(0.3,6)$ at three wavevectors is not a search for the largest root of the whole equation. The known case is that the same floating function changes sign between $2.7952306900$ and $2.7952306902$, in agreement at that precision with the separately certified staggered enclosure. *Grade: endpoint signs and roots measured; tail bound derived; the existence of a root above $2.9$ derived conditional on the measured signs.* The pattern at $(0,0,\pi)$ moves whole planes against each other, $\mathbf X_i=i+(-1)^{i_3}q\,\mathbf e_3$. This family is invariant under the unchanged equation, as the staggered family is: horizontal unit translations with all polarities exchanged, quarter turns about the vertical line through a member, and reflection in a horizontal plane followed by a unit vertical translation map the configuration to itself, so every acceleration is the same vertical scalar up to the sign $(-1)^{i_3}$ (*derived*). No exact history on it has been constructed.
3. *What the rates do and do not say.* They compare linear components of given amplitudes: a layered component gains on a staggered one by $e^{0.2327\,T}$ while both are small, a factor of about ten over the time a staggered seed of amplitude $2^{-40}$ takes to reach order one. They do not say how much of each a given localized preparation contains, they say nothing about the nonlinear stage, and they do not determine which pattern, if any, leads a localized preparation to its first event; no statement about generic preparations is made. They also do not rule out stability estimates about the coherent branch that are uniform in the width of an envelope on a fixed finite interval. They are a reason to examine other linear components before assuming that the staggered pattern leads. A preparation built on a narrow band around the staggered wavevector produces layered components only at high order, since a sum of $n$ wavevectors within $\rho$ of $(\pi,\pi,\pi)$ lies within $n\rho$ of it or of zero.
4. *Simultaneity.* In the coherent branch every member arrives at once. In a localized solution several members may still arrive together. Arrival of every member at once would contradict localization of the velocities only if that localization were known to last up to the event, which the initiative-2 result, valid for $T\le0$, does not provide.

**The missing estimates, stated exactly.** To prove that some localized preparation reaches an arrival to which the obstruction applies one needs: (E1) a forward continuation of the initiative-2 solutions from small amplitude to order one in a spatially weighted norm, on the finite interval up to the first event, which the small-amplitude contraction does not give; (E2) a statement of which boundary is reached first, for which the coherent branch's contact barrier (one approaching partner per member) is not available in a localized configuration; (E3) $\beta>0$ at a first arriving member; (E4) conditions (F1) to (F3) at that event. None of these is attempted here, and none is shown to be impossible.

## 7. What an update would have to preserve or supply

No update is proposed, adopted or tested. The following are what the results above require of, or leave open for, any rule offered for all geometries at once. Items 1, 2 and 4 are consequences of results cited or proved here; items 3 and 5 say what the controls do and do not decide. Each is checkable against a named history.

1. **It must act at an arrival, and there it can be a rule for one member.** By Lemma 1 the others are a fixed, bounded, Lipschitz field for a time of order $d$ after the event, for finite populations in the class of Section 4. A rule therefore needs as input only the arriving member's own path and that field, and must return that member's motion on the window. If it restarts the member below wake speed with some velocity, then while the speed stays below one there is no own-path root, the member obeys $\mathbf x''=\mathbf B_i(t,\mathbf x)$ with the Lipschitz field of Lemma 1, and its motion on the window is unique. What such a rule supplies is therefore exactly one outgoing velocity per arriving member. This is Theorem 2 part 3 of the class-level adjudication on a line, and the jump-exclusion adjudication notes it on curved paths. That those restarts violate the equation at the event itself is the content of the jump exclusion. Test histories: the mirror release, its perturbations in Section 4, the coherent lattice.
2. **It must leave ordinary own-path rows alone.** The ring's balance contains one per member. A rule that removes, caps or reweights own-path rows at positive delay, or that confines speeds to wake speed or below, changes the regular equation and the ring references are no longer solutions (tangential sums $0.040$ and $0.095$).
3. **$D\to0$, the sign of $D$, or a large row is not by itself the mark of an arrival.** A transverse fold has all three, and under the control's hypotheses its rows have a finite time integral; the regular ring has ordinary roots of both signs of $D$, within one percent in speed of a merging. So a rule keyed to those indicators alone would also act where the unchanged equation is regular or has only an integrable singularity, and a proposal that claims to keep the unchanged regular equation must keep ordinary rows of either sign. The control does not construct a continuation through a fold, so nothing here says whether a rule may or must act at one.
4. **It must not identify contact with own-path birth, and a one-member rule cannot be complete at a contact.** In the four-member control nothing diverges, no own-path root is near, and after the contact each member's rows depend on the other's continuation at vanishing delay. Lemma 1 fails there because $d=0$.
5. **Continuity across nearly simultaneous arrivals is a design requirement to declare, not a derived property.** By Theorem 2, arrivals at nearby pasts occur at nearby times with nearby $d$ and $\beta$, and in the measured table members that arrive together in the symmetric history arrive separately in its neighbours (lags from $10^{-6}$ to $10^{-2}$). A rule whose outcome changed discontinuously between simultaneous and nearly simultaneous arrivals would make the continued motion depend discontinuously on the past. Whether to require continuity is a choice for whoever proposes a rule; it is stated here so that the choice is explicit.

What is *not* supplied by any of this: a conserved account. The fixed-sign identities at the mirror event, the cancellation of the resting lattice field, and the ring's instability are statements about rows and histories. None is an energy, momentum or angular-momentum balance on an update, and the account obligation stays with its owner.

Explicitly selected comparison laws are outside this note. The field-speed ceiling is an authorized research variation with its own records; the queue reports that on released balances it continues past wake speed and most runs then end at a contact that its definition does not resolve. That report was not examined here and carries no weight in this note.
