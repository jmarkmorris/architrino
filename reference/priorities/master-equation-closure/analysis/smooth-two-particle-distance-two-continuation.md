# Distance-two continuation with overlapping reception of the fixed smooth disturbance

## Result and scope

The unchanged two-target smooth history has an EOM continuation through $T=113\ell/128$ for every $0<G/\ell\le16$. Every distance-$2\ell$ pulse finishes before that horizon, while the distance-$\sqrt5\ell$ pulses have started and remain unfinished. Exactly 76 environmental labels have nonconstant future histories. The two targets remain stationary and separated by $\ell$.

The overlap is required by the causal geometry: the distance-$\sqrt5\ell$ support starts before the distance-$2\ell$ support ends, even allowing for receiver displacement. The proof therefore solves all affected receivers together in one finite system, preserving the infinite stationary background under its fixed block sum. No environmental future is prescribed. Nevertheless every received emission remains before release, with $s\le-59\ell/512<0$, so the emissions generated by the environmental response have not yet arrived.

Claim grade: derived, submitted for independent reconstruction. This is an extension of the [accepted second-shell control](smooth-two-particle-second-shell-independent-adjudication.md) for one fixed complete past, not an invariance theorem for arbitrary perturbations of the [original history class](population-history-class.md). The coupling range, summation convention, source history and class ceilings remain unchanged. The accepted large-coupling obstruction and the unclassified intermediate range remain as stated in the [first-pulse adjudication](smooth-two-particle-pulse-independent-adjudication.md). No contact, genericity, selected physical domain, global existence or unrestricted uniqueness follows.

## 1. Complete input and receiver equation

Set $c_f=1$. Anchors are $\mathbf z_i=\ell i$ for $i\in\mathbb Z^3$, polarities are $\sigma_i=(-1)^{i_1+i_2+i_3}$, and $G=\kappa q_0^2>0$. The changed labels are $E=\{0,e_1\}$ with $e_1=(1,0,0)$; all other labels are environment. On their complete supplied pasts,

$$
\mathbf X_j(s)=\ell j+\varepsilon\ell\mathbf e\,
\psi\big(8(s/\ell+5/4)\big),\quad j\in E,\ s\le0,
\qquad \mathbf e=(0,0,1),\quad\varepsilon=2^{-16},
$$

where $\psi(v)=v(1-v^2)^4$ on $|v|<1$ and is zero elsewhere. Every other supplied past is stationary. The prescribed support is $[-11\ell/8,-9\ell/8]$, with endpoint derivatives through third order zero. These pasts are input data, not asserted past EOM solutions. The accepted exact derivative estimate gives input speed $\nu=1/8192$, displacement at most $\varepsilon\ell$, acceleration at most $3/(8\ell)$ and jerk at most $27/(2\ell^2)$.

Use dimensionless time $t=T/\ell$, coupling $g=G/\ell$ and receiver displacement $\mathbf y_i(t)=(\mathbf X_i(\ell t)-\ell i)/\ell$. Put

$$
b=1/1024,\qquad H=113/128.
$$

The ball of radius $b$ is an estimate for this solution, not a change to the history class. The fixed stationary eight-source block sum, with the receiver's own source omitted, defines $\mathbf S(\mathbf y)$. Its accepted cubic cancellation estimate is

$$
\|\mathbf S(\mathbf y)\|\le C_b\|\mathbf y\|^3,
\qquad \|D\mathbf S(\mathbf y)\|\le3C_b\|\mathbf y\|^2,
\qquad C_b=1309/(1-b)^5<1400.
$$

This uses exact stationary equilibrium and cube symmetry, not a linearization at an unbalanced configuration. Summation remains the original absolutely convergent complete-block prescription. Finite source corrections do not change its distant tail.

For $j\in E\setminus\{i\}$ write $\mathbf k=i-j$, $u=s/\ell+11/8$ and $p(u)=(-1+8u)u^4(1-4u)^4$ on $[0,1/4]$, zero elsewhere. The correction to the stationary source row is evaluated at its unique source root:

$$
\begin{gathered}
\mathbf R=\mathbf k+\mathbf y-p(u)\mathbf e,\quad
r=\|\mathbf R\|,\quad\mathbf n=\mathbf R/r,\quad D=1-n_3p'(u),\\
t+11/8-u=r,\qquad
\mathbf Q_{\mathbf k}(t,\mathbf y)
=\frac{\mathbf K(\mathbf R)}D-\mathbf K(\mathbf k+\mathbf y),
\quad\mathbf K(\mathbf x)=\mathbf x/\|\mathbf x\|^3,\\
\mathbf y_i''=g\left[\mathbf S(\mathbf y_i)+
\sum_{j\in E\setminus\{i\}}\sigma_i\sigma_j
\mathbf Q_{i-j}(t,\mathbf y_i)\right].
\end{gathered}
$$

There are at most two corrections, whatever the number of receiving shells. Their signs are $\sigma_i\sigma_j=(-1)^{\|i-j\|^2}$. This is the transmitter-weighted [Master Equation](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/master-equation.md); receiver playback does not multiply the acceleration.

For $\|\mathbf y\|\le b$ and $t\le H$, the delay residual at source time zero is at most $H-(1-b)<0$. Toward the remote past it increases with delay at least at rate $1-\nu$ and eventually becomes positive. Thus the source function has exactly one root in the supplied past. An auxiliary stationary extension beyond zero can define this function but supplies no received source value. Section 5 proves identification with the full evolved population rather than assuming it.

## 2. A range estimate that closes the longer interval

Distance-one pulses have already ended: their support-end reception is at most $-1/8+b<0$. For every possibly active correction, the anchor distance is at least $\sqrt2$. Both its range and the whole straight subtraction segment obey

$$
r\ge\sqrt2-b-\varepsilon>7/5.
$$

The exact kernel bounds on that segment are $\|\mathbf K\|<(5/7)^2$ and $\|D\mathbf K\|<2(5/7)^3$. Subtracting the stationary contribution before estimating gives

$$
\|\mathbf Q\|\le Q_*=
\frac{2\varepsilon(5/7)^3+\nu(5/7)^2}{1-\nu},
\qquad
C_bb^3+2Q_*<\frac1{6400}.
$$

The last inequality holds even with $C_b$ replaced by $1400$ and is a rational comparison. Using the actual range, rather than replacing it by one, supplies the extra time without reducing $g$. Integrating from zero displacement and velocity at release, up to any first displacement exit, yields

$$
\|\mathbf y_i''(t)\|<g/6400,\qquad
\|\mathbf y_i'(t)\|\le gt/6400,\qquad
\|\mathbf y_i(t)\|\le gt^2/12800.
$$

For $g\le16$ the decisive exact comparisons are

$$
\frac{16H^2}{12800}=\frac{12769}{13107200}
<\frac{12800}{13107200}=b,
\qquad
\frac{16H}{6400}=\frac{113}{51200}<\frac1{400},
\qquad \frac g{6400}\le\frac1{400}.
$$

The equations are at least $C^2$ in receiver position and time on a slightly larger ball: the source is $C^3$, ranges stay positive and implicit-root denominators have a uniform positive floor. The finite receiving system specified below has a locally unique classical solution. A first exit contradicts the strict displacement comparison, and bounded position and velocity on this smooth domain give continuation through $H$. All other labels are appended as stationary futures. The old solution is recovered on the earlier interval by uniqueness.

For an explicit uniqueness bound, implicit differentiation gives $\|u_{\mathbf y}\|\le(1-\nu)^{-1}$. With $r>1$, the accepted input acceleration bound gives

$$
\|D_{\mathbf y}\mathbf Q\|
\le\frac2{(1-\nu)^2}+\frac{\nu+3/8}{(1-\nu)^3}+2<5.
$$

Thus the full receiver acceleration is Lipschitz with constant less than $11g\le176$. This also establishes uniqueness among classical continuations sharing the exact full past and satisfying complete future displacement at most $b\ell$ and speed at most $1/4$: Section 5 reduces every such competitor to these same equations. No singular or unbounded comparison class is included.

The complete derivative check retains source acceleration when differentiating a root. Along a receiver set $\mathbf v=\mathbf y_i'$, $\mathbf W=p'(u)\mathbf e$, $\mathbf A=p''(u)\mathbf e$; then

$$
u'=\frac{1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v}{D},\quad
\mathbf R'=\mathbf v-\mathbf W u',\quad
\mathbf n'=\frac{(I-\mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T})\mathbf R'}r,
\quad D'=-\mathbf W\cdot\mathbf n'-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A\,u'.
$$

The conservative bounds $\|\mathbf v\|\le1/4$, $\|\mathbf W\|\le5/512$, $\|\mathbf A\|\le3/8$ and $r>1$ give $|u'|<4/3$, $\|\mathbf R'\|<17/64$, $|D'|<33/64$ and $D>63/64$. Including the stationary subtraction derivative gives $\|\mathbf Q'\|<34/63+2112/3969+1/2<2$. The background derivative contributes less than one, since $3C_bb^2<1/256$. Hence $\|\mathbf y_i'''\|<5g\le80$. Endpoint flatness preserves continuous acceleration and jerk at all entries and exits, including simultaneous receptions at different receivers. The histories join as $C^3$ paths at release and throughout this interval.

## 3. Complete reception census, including the overlap

At a support endpoint the source is at its anchor. For a pair with anchor distance $d=\|i-j\|$, the entry and exit residuals are

$$
F^a_{ij}(t)=t+11/8-\|i-j+\mathbf y_i(t)\|,
\qquad F^b_{ij}(t)=t+9/8-\|i-j+\mathbf y_i(t)\|.
$$

Their derivatives are at least $399/400$, and each differs from its anchor residual by at most $b$. When its zero lies on the constructed interval it is unique, with entry in $[d-11/8-b,d-11/8+b]$ and exit in $[d-9/8-b,d-9/8+b]$. The source-root derivative $u'=(1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf y_i')/D$ is positive, so the support is traversed in order without reentry.

The inequalities that settle the new census are

$$
\begin{gathered}
7/8+b<H,\qquad
\sqrt5-11/8+b<H<\sqrt5-9/8-b,\\
H<\sqrt6-11/8-b,\qquad
\sqrt5-11/8+b<7/8-b.
\end{gathered}
$$

They prove, respectively, complete distance-two reception, started but unfinished distance-$\sqrt5$ reception, exclusion of every distance at least $\sqrt6$, and unavoidable overlap between the new shell entries and the distance-two exits. They are exact comparisons, verified by squaring positive rational bounds when a square root occurs. Earlier shells have already ended. No inactive cross root is deleted: its correction vanishes only because it samples the stationary part of its complete source history.

The finite affected set is therefore

$$
\mathcal A=\bigcup_{j\in E}\{i:\|i-j\|^2\in\{2,3,4,5\}\}.
$$

The individual shell counts come from the signed permutations of $(1,1,0)$, $(1,1,1)$, $(2,0,0)$ and $(2,1,0)$, respectively:

| Squared anchor distance | Pairs from both sources | Reception by $H$ |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| $2$ | $24$ | Complete |
| $3$ | $16$ | Complete |
| $4$ | $12$ | Complete |
| $5$ | $48$ | Started, unfinished |

To count distinct labels analytically, put $m=\|i\|^2$, $n=\|i-e_1\|^2$. A double receiver satisfies $i_1=(m-n+1)/2$ and $i_2^2+i_3^2=m-i_1^2$. With $m,n\in\{2,3,4,5\}$, the only nonempty cases are $(m,n)=(2,3),(3,2),(2,5),(5,2),(4,5),(5,4)$, each containing four labels. The candidate cases $(3,4),(4,3)$ require a sum of two squares equal to three and contain none. Equal-parity cases require a noninteger first coordinate. Thus there are 100 entered ordered pairs, 24 double receivers and $100-24=76$ distinct labels. Of these pairs, 52 are completed and 48 unfinished.

The 12 distance-two receivers are disjoint from the previous 32-label set. Of the 48 distance-$\sqrt5$ pairs, eight reach earlier distance-$\sqrt2$ receivers and eight reach distance-two receivers; the other 32 labels are new. Equal distances to the two centers are impossible, so none of those 32 starts with two simultaneous source pulses. Both targets lie outside $\mathcal A$. The zero path solves the equation for every label outside $\mathcal A$ and is unique there. Counting a receiving set alone does not prove nonconstant motion; the next section supplies that missing implication.

## 4. Fifth- and sixth-order first responses

Every newly affected label first receives at the exact anchor time $t_d=d-11/8$, since its future is stationary until that instant. Here $d=2$ for 12 new labels and $d=\sqrt5$ for 32 new labels. For some distance-two labels a distance-one pulse passed before release, during the supplied stationary receiver past; it is absent from the future equation. The other changed source has no active pulse near the new label's first future reception.

Set $\theta=t-t_d>0$, $\mathbf k=i-j$ relative to the first receiving source, and $\sigma=\sigma_i\sigma_j=(-1)^{d^2}$. The exact input has $p(u)=-u^4+O(u^5)$ and $p'(u)=-4u^3+O(u^4)$. At the stationary anchor the root gives $u=\theta+O(\theta^4)$. If $k_3\ne0$, expansion of the transmitter denominator gives

$$
\mathbf Q_{\mathbf k}(t_d+\theta,0)
=-\frac{4\mathbf k k_3}{d^4}\theta^3+O(\theta^4),
\qquad
\mathbf y_i(t_d+\theta)
=-\frac{\sigma g\mathbf k k_3}{5d^4}\theta^5+O_g(\theta^6).
$$

The receiver feedback cannot change the leading term: the zero-data integral equation and its uniform Lipschitz bound first give $\mathbf y_i=O_g(\theta^5)$, and inserting this estimate changes acceleration only at fifth or higher order. Integrating the cubic term twice contributes the factor $1/20$.

The transverse cases $k_3=0$ need a separate argument. Their leading cubic term vanishes, so the previous fifth-order formula does not prove motion. At the anchor, $n_3=-p(u)/r=O(u^4)$, whence $D-1=O(u^7)$. The leading correction instead comes from displacement in the kernel:

$$
-p(u)D\mathbf K(\mathbf k)\mathbf e
=\frac{\mathbf e}{d^3}u^4+O(u^5),
\qquad
\mathbf y_i(t_d+\theta)
=\frac{\sigma g\mathbf e}{30d^3}\theta^6+O_g(\theta^7).
$$

Here $D\mathbf K(\mathbf k)\mathbf e=\mathbf e/d^3$ because $\mathbf k\cdot\mathbf e=0$. The same integral argument now gives $\mathbf y_i=O_g(\theta^6)$, and the quartic acceleration integrates with factor $1/30$. Every displayed leading coefficient is nonzero for $g>0$.

In the distance-two set, four labels have $k_3\ne0$ and eight have $k_3=0$. Among the 32 new distance-$\sqrt5$ labels, 24 have $k_3\ne0$ and eight have $k_3=0$: from each source the eight transverse permutations of $(2,1,0)$ lose two earlier distance-two and two earlier distance-$\sqrt2$ labels. Thus all 44 new histories are nonconstant, 28 with fifth-order first displacement and 16 with sixth-order first displacement. Together with the accepted 32 nonconstant histories and the stationary complement, this proves exactly 76. It makes no assertion about nonzero velocity at every instant or permanent motion after a pulse ends.

## 5. Full evolved roots and preservation of the admitted class

The complete supplied-and-evolved histories have displacement less than $b\ell$ and speed at most $1/400$, since $\nu<1/400$. Every cross range at any reception through $H\ell$ and any earlier emission satisfies

$$
r_{ij}(T,s)\ge\ell\|i-j\|-2b\ell\ge511\ell/512.
$$

The delay residual $f_{ij,T}(\tau)=\tau-\|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(T-\tau)\|$ increases at least $399/400$ times any delay increment. It starts negative for a cross channel and becomes positive toward the remote past. Thus every cross channel has exactly one positive-delay root, globally over the entire supplied history. The original half-width $w=\ell/256$ root tube lies in positive delay, retains range at least $511\ell/512$ and transmitter factor at least $399/400$, and has complement gap at least $399w/400>w/4$.

For each self channel, the complete speed bound gives $f_{ii,T}(\tau)\ge399\tau/400$. There are no positive-delay self roots. Both the normalized near-diagonal margin and the ordinary complement gap for $\tau\ge2w$ exceed the original class thresholds. The exact zero-delay diagonal is not assigned an acceleration value.

The received emission bound is

$$
s=T-r\le\ell\left(\frac{113}{128}-\frac{511}{512}\right)
=-\frac{59\ell}{512}<0.
$$

Every retained cross row therefore samples the supplied past, proving that the receiver equations are the full EOM on the constructed interval. The environmental response is genuinely generated by those equations; its generated emissions are causally absent from the received updates. For the uniqueness comparison class, displacement at most $b\ell$ gives the same negative-emission bound and speed at most $1/4$ still excludes self roots, so the reduction applies to every such competitor.

At every cut through $H\ell$, the complete acceleration and jerk satisfy

$$
\sup\|\ddot{\mathbf X}_i\|\le\frac{\max(3/8,g/6400)}\ell
\le\frac3{8\ell},\qquad
\sup\|\mathbf X_i^{(3)}\|\le\frac{\max(27/2,5g)}{\ell^2}
\le\frac{80}{\ell^2}.
$$

These are below the original ceilings $256/\ell$ and $65536/\ell^2$. The stronger displacement and speed estimates retain the original environmental and target envelopes, equal-time separation exceeds $\ell/8$, and the unchanged anchor-cube volume argument retains the same infinite density bounds. All histories remain $C^3$. Thus the full original history class and root certificate conditions are preserved for this one continuation.

Falsifiers are an incorrect rational bound in Section 2; an omitted lattice pair or failed endpoint inequality in Section 3; a vanishing or differently signed leading onset coefficient in Section 4; a second cross root, positive self root or received nonnegative emission despite Section 5's inequalities; or an original class ceiling violated by the displayed bounds. Changing the fixed input or taking $g$ outside the stated range is outside the claim, rather than a counterexample.

## 6. Remaining mathematical boundary

This interval completes the requested distance-two shell and includes the overlapping next-shell entries. The distance-$\sqrt5\ell$ exits occur later; no value is assigned to those exits beyond the constructed horizon. The next distance-$\sqrt6\ell$ entries also lie later. A continuation to either requires a fresh bound covering the relevant receiving geometry. Once emitted environmental motion actually reaches another label, the source futures enter the acceleration and the present fixed-past reduction must be replaced by a coupled-history argument.

The two targets remain at their anchors throughout this theorem, separated by $\ell$. Their stationary response is an exact property of the specified reception interval and supplied history. Neither the mere presence of an infinite population nor this finite disturbance proves eventual coincidence, exclusion under arbitrary perturbations, or an EOM-invariant population domain.
