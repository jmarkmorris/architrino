# Passage through a transverse fold when the folding source is in the fixed past

## Result

**In words.** A *fold* is the event in which two causal roots of one source merge and disappear, or appear together, at positive range: the receiver crosses the edge of the region that the source's wake covers twice. Each of the two merging rows grows like the inverse square root of the time from the event, because the transmitter factor $D$ of each root shrinks like that square root. The [cross-geometry comparison](saturday-cross-geometry-comparison-2026-10-10.md#3-three-kinds-of-boundary-and-the-two-controls) records, from the accepted [transverse-fold reconstruction](mec-008-self-complement-independent-adjudication.md#independent-transverse-fold-reconstruction), that the two rows have a finite time integral along a prescribed path, and that no solution passing through a fold had been constructed. This note constructs the passage locally. If, for a short window, every root the receiver can meet was emitted earlier than the window (so the folding source and all others act on it as a fixed field), and if the receiver crosses the fold *transversally*, then the unchanged equation has exactly one continuation through the fold. The velocity stays continuous, the acceleration grows at most like the inverse square root of the time from the crossing, and the velocity changes by an amount of order the square root of the elapsed time.

The mechanism is a weighted contraction. The singular field is of size $\lvert H\rvert^{-1/2}$, where $H$ measures how far the reception point is from the fold surface in reception time and position, and its gradient is of size $\lvert H\rvert^{-3/2}$. Along a transverse crossing $H$ grows linearly in the time $\tau$ since the crossing, so two candidate paths that start together differ after time $\tau$ by at most a multiple of $\tau^{3/2}$, while the field difference this produces is at most $\tau^{-3/2}$ times their separation. Integrating twice returns a multiple of $\tau^{1/2}$ times the weighted separation, which is a contraction for small $\tau$. The singular gradient is not integrable, but it is integrable against the separation that two such paths can actually have.

This settles the local half of the second open obligation of the comparison, the evolved passage through a fold, under explicit conditions. It does not show that an evolved six-member ring reaches a fold, or that it reaches one transversally. Along the rigid shrinking direction the comparison found the transversality number $a_f$ equal to zero, so the rigid fold is exactly the case not covered. The contrast with an arrival at wake speed is sharp: there the accepted obstruction excludes every continuation in the named classes, while a transverse fold in a fixed-past window is passable, with a unique continuation.

**Proposition F.** *Under the window, old-field, fold and transversality hypotheses (W1) to (W4) of Section 2, a receiver path that solves the equation on an incoming interval and reaches the fold surface transversally has exactly one continuation through the crossing in the kinematic class $\mathcal C$ of Section 2 that solves the unchanged equation almost everywhere. If the fold pair exists after the crossing, the continuation is the fixed point of Section 4 and satisfies $\lvert\mathbf x''(T)\rvert\le C(T-T_c)^{-1/2}$; if the pair exists before the crossing, the incoming velocity has a limit and the continuation is the ordinary solution without the pair. The equation holds almost everywhere through the crossing; no acceleration is assigned at the crossing instant itself.* **Grade: derived.**

Everything is for the unchanged Master Equation with every positive-delay root kept and $c_f=1$; no regularization, finite-width routing or event rule is used, and the result is a statement about the sharp equation. The population is finite, with the old regular rows summing to a bounded Lipschitz field as hypothesis (W2) requires. For the infinite alternating lattice the statement applies to an old self-fold, with the partner field supplied by the [field theorem](saturday-cross-geometry-continuation-2026-10-20.md#4-theorem-a-the-far-members-act-as-a-fixed-field) and its block prescription; a partner fold in the lattice is not covered, because the field theorem's speed margin (H2) excludes a partner source at wake speed along the line of sight, and isolating finitely many such sources is not written out here.

## 1. What this note uses

Read in full before deriving, in this session: the comparison and its review (Section 3 controls, Section 5 ring, final disposition); the verdict table and the section "Independent transverse-fold reconstruction" of the [self-complement adjudication](mec-008-self-complement-independent-adjudication.md); the canonical [Master Equation](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/master-equation.md#the-master-equation-canonical-form). Lemma R and the convexity device of the [field theorem](saturday-cross-geometry-continuation-2026-10-20.md#3-one-changed-source-row) are reused for the regular rows. The other sections of the self-complement adjudication (entrance classification, self-delay floor, endpoint velocity) are not used.

The row of source $j$ at a root is $\sigma K\,\mathbf n/(r^2\lvert D\rvert)$, with $K>0$ the channel coupling, $\sigma$ the polarity sign, $\mathbf n$ the unit vector from the emission point to the receiver, $r$ the range and $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j(s)$. For a reception at time $T$ and position $\mathbf x$, the *residual* of source $j$ is

$$
F(T,\mathbf x,s)=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert-(T-s),
$$

the residual of the accepted reconstruction with the receiver position made a free variable. Its zeros in $s<T$ are the roots. Its partial derivatives are

$$
F_s=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j(s)=D,\qquad F_T=-1,\qquad \nabla_{\mathbf x}F=\mathbf n^{\mathsf T},\qquad F_{ss}=\frac{\lvert\mathbf V_j\rvert^2-(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j)^2}{r}-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A_j(s),
$$

with $\mathbf A_j=\mathbf X_j''$. The last formula is the reconstruction's $F_{ss}$.

## 2. Hypotheses

Fix a receiver $i$, a crossing time $T_c$ and position $\mathbf x_c$, and a window $U=\{(T,\mathbf x):\lvert T-T_c\rvert<\tau_0,\ \lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf x_c\rvert<\rho_0\}$.

- **(W1) Old emissions only along the class.** There is $\gamma_0>\tau_0$ such that, for every path of the kinematic class $\mathcal C$ defined below and every reception $(T,\mathbf x(T))\in U$ on it, every root of every source, the receiver's own path included, is emitted at or before $T_c-\gamma_0$, for every *considered continuation* of the other members: continuous positions that stay farther than $\gamma_0+\tau_0$ from the spatial ball of $U$ during $(T_c-\gamma_0,T_c+\tau_0)$. This is a statement about paths of a kinematic class, not about every point of $U$: off the receiver's path, points of $U$ near the receiver's recent positions do receive recent own-path emissions.
- **(W2) Regular part of the old field.** Apart from the fold pair of (W3), every root emitted at or before $T_c-\gamma_0$ and received in $U$ is ordinary with $\lvert D\rvert\ge D_0>0$ and range at least $r_0>0$, its source has Lipschitz velocity near the emission, and the sum of these rows, $\mathbf F_{\rm reg}(T,\mathbf x)$, is a bounded Lipschitz function on $U$. Finiteness of the population alone does not give this, since finitely many sources can have infinitely many old roots; it is a hypothesis.
- **(W3) Fold.** For one source $j$ (possibly the receiver itself, with an old emission) and one emission $s_f\le T_c-\gamma_0-\sigma_0$: $F=F_s=0$ at $(T_c,\mathbf x_c,s_f)$, the range $r_f=T_c-s_f$ is positive, $\mathbf X_j$ is twice continuously differentiable near $s_f$, and $b_f=F_{ss}(T_c,\mathbf x_c,s_f)\ne0$. On $U\times[s_f-\sigma_0,s_f+\sigma_0]$ the sign of $F_{ss}$ is that of $b_f$, with $\lvert b_f\rvert/2\le\lvert F_{ss}\rvert\le2\lvert b_f\rvert$, and the range is at least $r_0$. The *fold pair* is the set of roots of source $j$ with emission in $[s_f-\sigma_0,s_f+\sigma_0]$; Lemma F1 counts them.
- **(W4) Transversality and speed.** The incoming velocity has the limit $\mathbf V_c$ at $T_c$, with $a_f=\mathbf n_f\cdot\mathbf V_c-1\ne0$, where $\mathbf n_f=(\mathbf x_c-\mathbf X_j(s_f))/r_f$; $\bigl\lvert\lvert\mathbf V_c\rvert-1\bigr\rvert\ge m>0$; and the incoming velocity stays within $\min\{m,\lvert a_f\rvert\}/4$ of $\mathbf V_c$ on $(T_c-\gamma_0,T_c)$.

**The kinematic class.** $\mathcal C$ consists of paths that equal the incoming path before $T_c$ and, on $[T_c,T_c+\tau]$, are continuously differentiable, with $\mathbf x(T_c)=\mathbf x_c$, $\mathbf x'(T_c)=\mathbf V_c$ and $\lvert\mathbf x'-\mathbf V_c\rvert\le\min\{m,\lvert a_f\rvert\}/4$, and velocity absolutely continuous on compact subintervals away from $T_c$. (For case (ii) below the same definition is used on $[T_c-\tau,T_c+\tau]$.) Membership does not require solving any equation, so (W1) can be verified before a solution is known. This is the curved-path continuation class with its velocity bound made explicit.

The number $a_f$ is the reconstruction's reception derivative of the residual along the receiver path, $\frac{d}{dT}F(T,\mathbf x(T),s_f)=-1+\mathbf n_f\cdot\mathbf V_c$, to be distinguished from the partial derivative $F_T=-1$ at fixed position; it is minus the receiver factor $D_r=1-\mathbf n_f\cdot\mathbf V_c$. Transversality says that the receiver does not move along the line of sight at wake speed. Hypothesis (W1) is verified in the usual way along paths of $\mathcal C$. A partner root emitted after $T_c-\gamma_0$ at a reception in $U$ would need a range below $\gamma_0+\tau_0$, which the separation of a considered continuation excludes. An own-path root emitted after $T_c-\gamma_0$ would need a chord of the receiver's path equal to its time span, with both ends in $(T_c-\gamma_0,T]$; along a path of $\mathcal C$, whose velocity stays within $m/4$ of $\mathbf V_c$ on that whole interval by (W4) and the definition, every such chord has length at least $(\lvert\mathbf V_c\rvert-m/4)$ times the span when $\lvert\mathbf V_c\rvert\ge1+m$ (project on the direction of $\mathbf V_c$), or at most $(\lvert\mathbf V_c\rvert+m/4)$ times the span when $\lvert\mathbf V_c\rvert\le1-m$, and in neither case equal to the span. Own-path roots emitted before $T_c-\gamma_0$ are part of the old field.

## 3. The local structure of the field

By the implicit-function theorem applied to $F_s=0$, which is continuously differentiable in $(T,\mathbf x,s)$ because $\mathbf X_j$ is twice continuously differentiable and $F_{ss}\ne0$, there is a unique critical emission $s_c(T,\mathbf x)$ near $s_f$ for $(T,\mathbf x)$ near $(T_c,\mathbf x_c)$. Define the *fold function* $H(T,\mathbf x)=F(T,\mathbf x,s_c(T,\mathbf x))$. Because $F_s=0$ at $s_c$, $H$ is continuously differentiable with $\partial_TH=-1$ and $\nabla_{\mathbf x}H=\mathbf n(T,\mathbf x,s_c)^{\mathsf T}$, a unit vector. So $H=0$ is a smooth hypersurface through $(T_c,\mathbf x_c)$ in reception time and position, and $H$ is comparable to the distance from it.

**Lemma F1.** *Shrinking $U$ if necessary and putting $\beta=\lvert b_f\rvert$: where $b_fH>0$, source $j$ has no root with emission in $[s_f-\sigma_0,s_f+\sigma_0]$; where $b_fH<0$, it has exactly two there, $s_\pm(T,\mathbf x)$, with*

$$
\sqrt{\lvert H\rvert/\beta}\le\lvert s_\pm-s_c\rvert\le2\sqrt{\lvert H\rvert/\beta},\qquad
\tfrac12\sqrt{\beta\lvert H\rvert}\le\lvert D_\pm\rvert\le4\sqrt{\beta\lvert H\rvert}.
$$

*The sum $\mathbf S(T,\mathbf x)$ of their two rows (zero where there are none) satisfies $\lvert\mathbf S\rvert\le C_0\lvert H\rvert^{-1/2}$ and, where $b_fH<0$, is continuously differentiable with $\lVert\nabla_{(T,\mathbf x)}\mathbf S\rVert\le C_1\lvert H\rvert^{-3/2}$, with $C_0=4K/(r_0^2\sqrt\beta)$ and $C_1$ depending only on $K$, $r_0$, $\beta$ and bounds on the source's speed and acceleration near $s_f$.*

*Proof.* Shrink $U$ so that $\lvert s_c-s_f\rvert<\sigma_0/2$ and $\lvert H\rvert<\beta\sigma_0^2/16$ on it. Fix $(T,\mathbf x)$ and put $g(s)=F(T,\mathbf x,s)$, so $g(s_c)=H$ and $g'(s_c)=0$. On $[s_f-\sigma_0,s_f+\sigma_0]$, which contains $[s_c-\sigma_0/2,s_c+\sigma_0/2]$, $g'$ is strictly monotone because $F_{ss}$ has a fixed sign, so $g$ is strictly monotone on each side of $s_c$ and has at most one zero on each side. With $\delta=s-s_c$, Taylor's formula and the bounds on $F_{ss}$ give $(\beta/4)\delta^2\le\lvert g(s)-H\rvert\le\beta\delta^2$, with $g-H$ of the sign of $b_f$. If $b_fH>0$ there is no zero. If $b_fH<0$, at $s_c\pm\sigma_0/2$ the value $g$ has the sign of $b_f$, because $\lvert g-H\rvert\ge\beta\sigma_0^2/16>\lvert H\rvert$ there, so there is exactly one zero on each side, inside $[s_c-\sigma_0/2,s_c+\sigma_0/2]$, at a distance $\delta_\pm$ with $(\beta/4)\delta_\pm^2\le\lvert H\rvert\le\beta\delta_\pm^2$. Then $\lvert D_\pm\rvert=\lvert g'(s_\pm)\rvert=\lvert\int_{s_c}^{s_\pm}F_{ss}\rvert$ lies between $(\beta/2)\delta_\pm$ and $2\beta\delta_\pm$, which gives the displayed bounds. Each row has magnitude $K/(r^2\lvert D_\pm\rvert)\le2K/(r_0^2\sqrt{\beta\lvert H\rvert})$, which gives $C_0$.

For the gradient, differentiating $F(T,\mathbf x,s_\pm)=0$ gives $ds_\pm=(dT-\mathbf n\cdot d\mathbf x)/D_\pm$, so $\lvert ds_\pm\rvert\le(\lvert dT\rvert+\lvert d\mathbf x\rvert)/\lvert D_\pm\rvert$. A row is $\sigma K\tilde\Psi(\mathbf R,\mathbf v)$ with $\tilde\Psi=\mathbf n/(r^2\lvert D\rvert)$, $\mathbf R=\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s_\pm)$, $\mathbf v=\mathbf V_j(s_\pm)$, so its differential is $\partial_{\mathbf R}\tilde\Psi\,(d\mathbf x-\mathbf v\,ds_\pm)+\partial_{\mathbf v}\tilde\Psi\,\mathbf A_j\,ds_\pm$. The derivative formulas of the field theorem's Section 3, with $\lvert D\rvert$ in place of $D$, give $\lVert\partial_{\mathbf R}\tilde\Psi\rVert\le c/(r^3D^2)$ with $c$ depending on the speed bound, and $\lVert\partial_{\mathbf v}\tilde\Psi\rVert=1/(r^2D^2)$. With $\lvert D_\pm\rvert\ge\frac12\sqrt{\beta\lvert H\rvert}$ every term is at most a constant times $\lvert D_\pm\rvert^{-3}$, hence times $\lvert H\rvert^{-3/2}$. $\square$

The leading coefficient agrees with the reconstruction: with $\delta_\pm\approx\sqrt{2\lvert H\rvert/\beta}$ and $\lvert D_\pm\rvert\approx\sqrt{2\beta\lvert H\rvert}$, the two rows add to $\sqrt2\,\sigma K\mathbf n_f/(r_f^2\sqrt{\beta\lvert H\rvert})$, and along a path with $H\approx a_fh$ this is the boxed coefficient $\sqrt2\sigma K\mathbf n_f/(r_f^2\sqrt{\lvert a_fb_f\rvert}\sqrt{\lvert h\rvert})$ there.

> Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: a source satisfying (W3) at a reception with $b_fH<0$ whose transmitter factors violate the displayed two-sided bound, or a second pair of roots in the emission interval.

**The old-emission field.** On $U$ define $\mathbf F^{\rm old}(T,\mathbf x)=\mathbf F_{\rm reg}(T,\mathbf x)+\mathbf S(T,\mathbf x)$, the sum of the rows of all roots emitted at or before $T_c-\gamma_0$, with the fold pair written separately as $\mathbf S$. It is defined at every reception point of $U$ off the fold surface, that is on $U\setminus\{H=0\}$, from the paths before $T_c-\gamma_0$; on the fold surface itself $D=0$ and no canonical value is assigned, and the passage below uses only integrals of the integrable one-sided field. It is a mathematical extension off the receiver's path, not a replacement of the equation, and (W1) says that along paths of $\mathcal C$ it is the whole unchanged sum.

**Transversality along every path of the class.** For $\mathbf x\in\mathcal C$, $\frac{d}{dT}H(T,\mathbf x(T))=-1+\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf x'(T)$. At $T_c$ this is $a_f$. The direction $\mathbf n$ at the critical emission is continuous and equals $\mathbf n_f$ at the crossing, and $\lvert\mathbf x'-\mathbf V_c\rvert\le\lvert a_f\rvert/4$, so after shrinking the window the derivative stays between $a_f/2$ and $3a_f/2$ in sign and size. Hence

$$
\tfrac12\lvert a_f\rvert\,\lvert T-T_c\rvert\le\lvert H(T,\mathbf x(T))\rvert\le\tfrac32\lvert a_f\rvert\,\lvert T-T_c\rvert ,
$$

with the sign of $a_f(T-T_c)$, for every path of the class. The fold pair exists after the crossing when $a_fb_f<0$ and before it when $a_fb_f>0$.

## 4. Proof of Proposition F

Throughout this section time is translated so that $T_c=0$. Then $\tau=T$ is the time elapsed since the crossing, the integration variable $\sigma$ is also an elapsed time, paths and fields are evaluated at $T=\tau$ or $T=\sigma$, and $T_c$ in the formulas below stands for $0$.

**Case (i): the pair exists after the crossing ($a_fb_f<0$).** Write $\tau=T-T_c\ge0$. Along any path of $\mathcal C$ whose acceleration is $\mathbf F^{\rm old}$, Lemma F1 and the transversality bound give $\lvert\mathbf S\rvert\le C_0(\lvert a_f\rvert\tau/2)^{-1/2}$, so

$$
\lvert\mathbf x''(T)\rvert\le M_0+C_2\tau^{-1/2},\qquad \lvert\mathbf x(T)-\mathbf x_c-\mathbf V_c\tau\rvert\le\tfrac12M_0\tau^2+\tfrac43C_2\tau^{3/2},
$$

with $M_0=\sup\lvert\mathbf F_{\rm reg}\rvert$ and $C_2=C_0\sqrt{2/\lvert a_f\rvert}$. Let $X_\tau$ be the set of continuous paths on $[T_c,T_c+\tau_1]$ with $\mathbf x(T_c)=\mathbf x_c$ and $\lvert\mathbf x(T)-\mathbf x_c-\mathbf V_c\tau\rvert\le3C_2\tau^{3/2}$, with the distance $\lVert\mathbf e\rVert_*=\sup_{0<\tau\le\tau_1}\lvert\mathbf e(T_c+\tau)\rvert/\tau^{3/2}$. This is a complete metric space. On it define

$$
\Phi(\mathbf x)(T)=\mathbf x_c+\mathbf V_c\tau+\int_{T_c}^{T}(T-\sigma)\,\mathbf F^{\rm old}(\sigma,\mathbf x(\sigma))\,d\sigma .
$$

For $\tau_1$ small, a path of $X_\tau$ lies on the root side with $\lvert H\rvert\ge\lvert a_f\rvert\tau/4$, so that along it $\lvert\mathbf S\rvert\le\sqrt2\,C_2\tau^{-1/2}$: its position differs from the straight line by $O(\tau^{3/2})$, and $H$ is continuously differentiable with $\partial_TH=-1$ and unit spatial gradient, so $H(T,\mathbf x(T))=a_f\tau+O(\tau^{3/2})+o(\tau)$. Since $\int_0^\tau(\tau-\sigma)\sigma^{-1/2}d\sigma=\tfrac43\tau^{3/2}$ and $\tfrac43\sqrt2<2$, the displacement of $\Phi(\mathbf x)$ from the line is at most $\tfrac12M_0\tau^2+2C_2\tau^{3/2}\le3C_2\tau^{3/2}$ for small $\tau_1$, so $\Phi$ maps $X_\tau$ into itself, and that $\Phi(\mathbf x)$ is continuously differentiable with $\Phi(\mathbf x)'(T_c)=\mathbf V_c$. For two paths of $X_\tau$, every point of the segment joining $\mathbf x_1(\sigma)$ and $\mathbf x_2(\sigma)$ is within $6C_2\sigma^{3/2}$ of each, so it also has $\lvert H\rvert\ge\lvert a_f\rvert\sigma/8$ for small $\tau_1$; the mean-value inequality with Lemma F1 and the Lipschitz constant $L_{\rm reg}$ of $\mathbf F_{\rm reg}$ gives

$$
\lvert\mathbf F^{\rm old}(\sigma,\mathbf x_1)-\mathbf F^{\rm old}(\sigma,\mathbf x_2)\rvert\le\Bigl(L_{\rm reg}+C_1\bigl(\tfrac{\lvert a_f\rvert\sigma}{8}\bigr)^{-3/2}\Bigr)\lVert\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2\rVert_*\,\sigma^{3/2}=\bigl(L_{\rm reg}\sigma^{3/2}+C_3\bigr)\lVert\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2\rVert_* ,
$$

with $C_3=C_1(8/\lvert a_f\rvert)^{3/2}$. Integrating against $(T-\sigma)$ gives $\lvert\Phi(\mathbf x_1)-\Phi(\mathbf x_2)\rvert(T)\le(L_{\rm reg}\tau^{7/2}/2+C_3\tau^2/2)\lVert\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2\rVert_*$, so

$$
\lVert\Phi(\mathbf x_1)-\Phi(\mathbf x_2)\rVert_*\le\Bigl(\tfrac12L_{\rm reg}\tau_1^{2}+\tfrac12C_3\tau_1^{1/2}\Bigr)\lVert\mathbf x_1-\mathbf x_2\rVert_* .
$$

For $\tau_1$ small the factor is below one, and the contraction mapping theorem gives exactly one fixed point of $\Phi$ in $X_\tau$, a solution of $\mathbf x''=\mathbf F^{\rm old}(T,\mathbf x)$. The fixed point is continuously differentiable, its acceleration is continuous for $T>T_c$ and bounded by $M_0+\sqrt2C_2\tau^{-1/2}$, so its velocity is continuous at $T_c$ and $\lvert\mathbf x'(T)-\mathbf V_c\rvert\le M_0\tau+2\sqrt2\,C_2\tau^{1/2}$, which keeps it in $\mathcal C$ for small $\tau_1$. By (W1), along this path of $\mathcal C$ every root of the unchanged sum is emitted at or before $T_c-\gamma_0$, so the unchanged sum along it equals $\mathbf F^{\rm old}$ and the fixed point solves the Master Equation almost everywhere. Conversely, a path of $\mathcal C$ that solves the unchanged equation almost everywhere has, by (W1) again, acceleration $\mathbf F^{\rm old}(T,\mathbf x(T))$; it satisfies the displayed acceleration bound, the integral equation from $T_c$ (its velocity is continuous at $T_c$ and absolutely continuous away from it, with an integrable acceleration), hence lies in $X_\tau$ and is a fixed point of $\Phi$; so it equals the one constructed. Beyond $T_c+\tau_1$ uniqueness holds locally while $H\ne0$ and the path stays in $U$, until another boundary is met.

**Case (ii): the pair exists before the crossing ($a_fb_f>0$).** On $[T_c-\tau_1,T_c)$ every path of the class is on the root side with $\lvert H\rvert\ge\lvert a_f\rvert(T_c-T)/2$, the field $\mathbf F^{\rm old}$ is locally Lipschitz there, and the incoming solution is unique given its data at $T_c-\tau_1$. Its acceleration is bounded by $M_0+C_2(T_c-T)^{-1/2}$, which is integrable, so its velocity and position have limits; this is consistent with the limit $\mathbf V_c$ assumed in (W4) rather than an independent derivation of it. For $T>T_c$ the path is on the side without the pair, where $\mathbf F^{\rm old}=\mathbf F_{\rm reg}$ is Lipschitz, and the continuation from $(\mathbf x_c,\mathbf V_c)$ is the unique solution of $\mathbf x''=\mathbf F_{\rm reg}$ in $\mathcal C$, which by (W1) is the unique solution of the unchanged equation in $\mathcal C$. The acceleration drops from the singular two-row value to the regular field at the crossing; the velocity is continuous. $\square$

> Claim grade: derived from (W1) to (W4), Lemma F1 and the contraction mapping theorem. Falsifier: a configuration satisfying (W1) to (W4) with two distinct continuations in $\mathcal C$, or none; the places to look are the transversality bound along the class, the segment estimate $\lvert H\rvert\ge\lvert a_f\rvert\sigma/8$, and the integral $\int_0^\tau(\tau-\sigma)\sigma^{-3/2}\sigma^{3/2}\,d\sigma=\tau^2/2$.

## 5. What this says about the ring and what it does not

The six-member ring references move at speeds $1.826$ (T02) and $2.974$ (T04), so the speed condition of (W4) holds with $m=0.826$ for paths near the reference. Their smallest positive delays are $0.3609$ (T02) and $0.1479$ (T04), from the comparison's retained ring-ledger output, so for histories near a reference a sufficiently small window has fixed past for the partner channels; the window sizes $\tau_0$, $\gamma_0$, $\rho_0$ and the separation in (W1) must be checked for the actual history, and the delay floor alone does not certify any particular window. The comparison found that the top root pair, which carries about nine tenths of the radial sum, merges $0.875$ percent (T02) or $0.088$ percent (T04) below the reference speed along the rigid shrinking direction. Along that rigid direction the source and receiver factors are equal at the merging, so $a_f=0$, and Proposition F does not apply: the rigid fold is a nontransverse fold.

What Proposition F adds is the following conditional statement, for one receiver. If an evolved ring history reaches the merging of its top pair at a reception where $a_f\ne0$, with $b_f\ne0$ and the other conditions of (W1) to (W3), then the unchanged equation continues that receiver uniquely through the merging, given the other members' paths in the considered class; in the direction in which the pair disappears (case (ii)) the acceleration loses the pair's contribution at the crossing while the velocity stays continuous. A complete six-member continuation needs the corresponding control for every member, including members whose own windows contain no fold, and is not written out. The remaining open steps are therefore precise: (1) an evolved ring history that reaches the merging; (2) transversality $a_f\ne0$ there, which the rigid family fails; (3) the evolution after the passage, which Proposition F gives only on a short window; (4) the simultaneous control of all six members. None is attempted here. In particular no claim is made that perturbed rings reach a fold before any other boundary.

The comparison's Section 7 noted that a vanishing $D$, the sign of $D$ or a large row does not distinguish an arrival from a transverse fold. Proposition F strengthens the distinction from integrability on prescribed paths to evolution: in a fixed-past window, a transverse fold has a unique continuation under the unchanged equation, while a positive-rate arrival has none in the named classes. A rule that intervened at every root with small $D$ would therefore act where the unchanged equation already has a unique continuation.

> Claim grade: derived for the conditional statement; the ring numbers are measured ledger values of the comparison's instrument B (the Codex review of this note recomputed them separately in floating point). Falsifier: a reference ledger value that differs, or an evolved ring history satisfying (W1) to (W4) at its top-pair merging with no continuation of the receiver in $\mathcal C$ or two.

## 6. Scope

- **Shown.** Local existence and uniqueness, in the kinematic class $\mathcal C$, of an evolved passage of one receiver through a transverse fold whose window satisfies (W1), for the sharp unchanged equation, holding almost everywhere through the crossing.
- **Not shown.** Nontransverse folds ($a_f=0$, as on the rigid ring family) or degenerate folds ($b_f=0$); folds whose source emits within the window, so that the folding source responds during the passage; several simultaneous folds at one reception, which add finitely many singular terms of the same type and are expected to be handled by the same estimate but are not written out; partner folds in the infinite lattice; any statement about whether or when an evolved history reaches a fold; continuation of the other members; global continuation.
- **Relation to the solver.** The [evolution contract](../../app-solver/contracts/evolution-contract-v1.md#regularization-and-caustics) of the EOM solver routes folds through a finite-width calculation. Proposition F concerns the sharp equation and is not evidence about that routed computation, or the reverse.

No instrument is used.
