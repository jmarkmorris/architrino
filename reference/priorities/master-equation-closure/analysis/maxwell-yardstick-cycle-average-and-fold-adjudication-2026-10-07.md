The second, the fold obstacle of the [response assessment, Section 6](maxwell-derived-response-assessment-2026-10-03.md#6-obstacles-specific-to-this-proposal), is confirmed as stated and its open clause is now examined: whether the two branches born at a fold cancel depends on a sign convention that the rows do not yet have above the field speed, and under the convention of the canonical law they do not cancel. ## 1. Notation

A receiver at position $\mathbf x$ and absolute time $T$ admits a causal root at emission time $S<T$ from a transmitter with path $\mathbf X(S)$ when $R=\|\mathbf x-\mathbf X(S)\|=T-S$. Write $\mathbf n=(\mathbf x-\mathbf X(S))/R$, $\mathbf v=\mathbf X'(S)$, $\mathbf a=\mathbf X''(S)$, $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v$ and $P=I-\mathbf n\mathbf n^{\mathsf T}$, the projector across the line of action. The rows of the [assessment, Section 2](maxwell-derived-response-assessment-2026-10-03.md#2-the-three-response-rows) are, per hit,

$$
\text{canonical: }\frac{\mathbf n}{R^2D},\qquad
\mathbf G=\frac{(1-\|\mathbf v\|^2)\mathbf n-D\mathbf v+R\,\mathbf n(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a)}{R^2D^3},\qquad
\mathbf E=\frac{(1-\|\mathbf v\|^2)(\mathbf n-\mathbf v)+R\big((\mathbf n-\mathbf v)(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a)-D\mathbf a\big)}{R^2D^3},
$$

where $\mathbf G=-\nabla_{\mathbf x}\Psi$ with $\Psi=1/(RD)$ and $\mathbf E=\mathbf G+\mathbf W$ with $\mathbf W=-\partial_T(\Psi\mathbf v)$, both derivatives taken at a fixed receiver point following the root. For a receiver at rest the arrival time of the hit emitted at $S$ is $T(S)=S+R(S)$, and $dT/dS=D$.

## 2. Part A: the cycle-average identity

**Claim (ledger, Section 3.6).** For a probe at rest at a point with positive clearance from a periodic host whose path is twice continuously differentiable and stays strictly below the field speed, the averages over one host period of the canonical row, the G row and the E row are equal, and equal the static inverse-square pull of the host's time-averaged distribution, $\langle\mathbf A\rangle=(\sigma K/P_h)\int_0^{P_h}(\mathbf x-\mathbf X(S))/\|\mathbf x-\mathbf X(S)\|^3\,dS$, with $P_h$ the host period.

**Proof, re-derived.** Take one host member; the statement for a host of several members follows by summing with their polarity signs. Because the member is strictly subfield, $D>0$ everywhere, so $T(S)$ is strictly increasing and each reception time has exactly one root; because the path has period $P_h$ and the probe is at rest, $T(S+P_h)=T(S)+P_h$, so one period of reception time corresponds to one period of emission time. (i) Canonical row: $\int\mathbf n/(R^2D)\,dT=\int\mathbf n/(R^2D)\,D\,dS=\int\mathbf n/R^2\,dS$, which is the static integral. (ii) G row: the probe point enters $\Psi(\mathbf x,T)$ as a parameter and the period in $T$ is $P_h$ at every point, so the gradient commutes with the average: $\langle\mathbf G\rangle=-\nabla_{\mathbf x}\langle\Psi\rangle$. The same change of variable gives $\langle\Psi\rangle=P_h^{-1}\int(RD)^{-1}D\,dS=P_h^{-1}\int R^{-1}\,dS$, an integral of a periodic integrand over a full period whose value does not depend on where the period starts, and $-\nabla_{\mathbf x}(1/R)=\mathbf n/R^2$ at fixed $S$. (iii) E row: $\mathbf W=-\partial_T(\Psi\mathbf v)$ is the reception-time derivative, at a fixed point, of a quantity with period $P_h$ in $T$, so its average over a period vanishes and $\langle\mathbf E\rangle=\langle\mathbf G\rangle$. $\square$

The hypotheses are all used: positive clearance keeps $R>0$; strict subfield motion gives one root and the positive weight; periodicity closes the window; and the probe is at rest, so that $dT=D\,dS$ and the receiver-velocity response is absent. Strict subfield motion is sufficient and more than the proof uses: it needs only $D>0$ on the whole period as seen from the probe point. The argument does not need circular motion, a planar host or commensurate member rates beyond the common period.

**Numerical check (measured).** The script first passed its known cases: all rows equal $\mathbf n/R^2$ for a source at rest ($3\times10^{-17}$); the closed forms of $\mathbf G$ and $\mathbf E$ agree with central differences of $\Psi$ and $\Psi\mathbf v$ on an accelerated non-planar path ($8.6\times10^{-12}$); and $\mathbf E$ for a uniformly moving source equals the closed form about the source's present position, $(1-v^2)\mathbf r/(r^3(1-v^2\sin^2\theta)^{3/2})$, to $4\times10^{-16}$, which is a reference independent of the row algebra. Then three hosts were averaged at three probe points each, by the periodic trapezoid rule in reception time with a root solve at every node: two members on circles of different radii and rates with opposite polarity; one member on a non-circular, non-planar accelerated path; and three members combining both, where the fastest reaches $D=0.197$.

| Host | Largest difference among the three averages and the static integral, 2048 nodes | Largest instantaneous difference between canonical and E rows |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Two circles, opposite polarity | $3.2\times10^{-15}$ | $1.9$ |
| Non-circular accelerated path | $1.2\times10^{-15}$ | $1.7$ |
| Three members, fastest at $D=0.197$ | $4.3\times10^{-15}$ | $24$ |

For the third host the difference is $1.0\times10^{-4}$ at 256 nodes and $1.4\times10^{-9}$ at 512, the geometric convergence expected of the trapezoid rule on a sharply peaked periodic integrand, and reaches rounding at 2048. As a negative control, averaging over half a period gives $\|\langle\mathbf E\rangle-\langle\text{canonical}\rangle\|=0.127$: the agreement is a property of the full period, not of the rows.

> Claim grade: derived, and independently confirmed: the author's derivation, this separate derivation and a separately written numerical check agree. Falsifier: a periodic strictly subfield host and a probe at rest with positive clearance for which two of the three period averages differ beyond quadrature error.

**What it does and does not carry.** The identity is exact and is the reason the ledger's reading in its Section 4 is narrow: what a canonical hit gets wrong relative to the yardstick, on a body at rest near a bound periodic host, averages to zero over a cycle. It says nothing for a probe in motion, where the receiver-velocity response enters and $dT\ne D\,dS$; nothing about times shorter than a period, where the rows differ by amounts of order one or more; and nothing about whether any periodic host exists under the canonical law.

## 3. Part B: the rows at a transmitter fold

**Claim (assessment, Section 6).** Rows G, V and E carry $1/D^3$, which scales as $|T-T_\ast|^{-3/2}$ on each branch near an ordinary fold and is not integrable in reception time, whereas the canonical weight $1/|D|$ scales as $|T-T_\ast|^{-1/2}$ and is; unless the two branches born at a fold cancel to an integrable remainder, which had not been examined, these rows are defined only on strictly subfield histories.

**Fold kinematics.** An ordinary fold seen from a fixed point $\mathbf x$ is an emission time $S_\ast$ with $D(S_\ast)=0$ and $D'(S_\ast)\ne0$. There $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v=1$, so the transmitter is at or above the field speed, and $T(S)$ has a simple extremum $T_\ast$. With $s=S-S_\ast$ and $\tau=|T-T_\ast|$ on the side where roots exist, $T-T_\ast=\tfrac12D's^2+O(s^3)$, so two roots $s_\pm=\pm\sqrt{2\tau/|D'|}+O(\tau)$ appear or disappear together, with $D(s_\pm)=D's_\pm+O(\tau)$ of opposite signs and $D^2=2|D'|\tau+O(\tau^{3/2})$. Differentiating $\mathbf n$ along the path, $d\mathbf n/dS=-P\mathbf v/R$, gives

$$
D'=\frac{\|P\mathbf v\|^2}{R}-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a .
$$

**Lemma B1 (single branch).** At an ordinary fold the numerators of the G, E and W rows take the values $-RD'\,\mathbf n$, $-RD'\,(\mathbf n-\mathbf v)$ and $+RD'\,\mathbf v$, so that $\|\mathbf W\|\,\tau^{3/2}\to\|\mathbf v\|/(2^{3/2}R|D'|^{1/2})$, which cannot vanish because $\|\mathbf v\|\ge1$ at a fold. Hence on each branch

$$
\mathbf G\simeq-\frac{D'\,\mathbf n}{R\,D^3},\qquad \mathbf E\simeq-\frac{D'\,(\mathbf n-\mathbf v)}{R\,D^3},\qquad \|\mathbf G\|\,\tau^{3/2}\to\frac{1}{2^{3/2}R\,|D'|^{1/2}},\qquad \|\mathbf E\|\,\tau^{3/2}\to\frac{\|P\mathbf v\|}{2^{3/2}R\,|D'|^{1/2}} .
$$

*Proof.* At $D=0$ the G numerator is $\mathbf n[(1-\|\mathbf v\|^2)+R\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a]$. Since $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf v=1$, $\|\mathbf v\|^2=1+\|P\mathbf v\|^2$, so the bracket is $-\|P\mathbf v\|^2+R\,\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a=-RD'$. The E numerator at $D=0$ is $(\mathbf n-\mathbf v)$ times the same bracket, and the W numerator, $D\mathbf v-(1-\|\mathbf v\|^2)\mathbf v-R\mathbf v(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a)-RD\mathbf a$, is $-\mathbf v$ times it. At the fold $\mathbf n-\mathbf v=-P\mathbf v$. The limits follow from $|D|^3=(2|D'|\tau)^{3/2}(1+O(\tau^{1/2}))$. $\square$

So the G and W rows are non-integrable at every ordinary fold, because their leading coefficients cannot vanish while $D'\ne0$. The E row is non-integrable at an ordinary fold exactly when the transmitter's velocity has a component across the line of action there. On an exactly collinear history, where the transmitter moves straight at the receiver, the E row reduces identically to $(1+v_n)$ times the canonical row (the ledger's Section 3.3) and is integrable like it. At a fold where the transverse velocity vanishes at that instant only, on a history that is not collinear, the E numerator vanishes to second order in $s$: with $\mathbf w=\mathbf n-\mathbf v$ it is $(2D-\|\mathbf w\|^2)\mathbf w+R\big(\mathbf w(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a)-D\mathbf a\big)$, and there $\mathbf w=-\mathbf a\,s+O(s^2)$ and $D=-(\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf a)s+O(s^2)$, so its first-order term cancels identically. The single-branch E row then scales as $\tau^{-1/2}$ and is integrable like the canonical row, while the G and W rows are unaffected. This last case was derived by the referee and measured on one history (Section 5); the first version of this paragraph wrongly expected $1/\tau$ there.

**Lemma B2 (two branches).** The rows are defined in the assessment on branches with $D>0$. A fold always involves one branch with $D>0$ and one with $D<0$, so summing the two needs a definition on the second. Two readings are possible and they give opposite answers.

1. *Magnitude weight.* The scalar is $\Psi=1/(R|D|)$ on every branch, in line with the nonnegative weight $1/|D|$ of the canonical law, on which its finite-impulse result at a fold rests. Each row then carries $1/|D|^3$, the two leading terms of Lemma B1 have the same sign, and the sum is twice the single-branch term to leading order: $\|\mathbf G_++\mathbf G_-\|\,\tau^{3/2}\to2/(2^{3/2}R|D'|^{1/2})$. The two branches add; nothing cancels; the sum is not integrable through the fold, for the G and W rows always and for the E row wherever the transverse velocity is not zero at the fold.
2. *Signed weight.* The scalar is $\Psi=1/(RD)$ with its sign on every branch. Then, for a path that is real-analytic in $S$ near $S_\ast$, the two-branch sum is not merely integrable but bounded and analytic at the fold. *Proof.* With $F(S;\mathbf x,T)=S+R(\mathbf x,S)-T$, whose zeros are the roots and whose derivative in $S$ is $D$, the sum over the two roots near the fold is $\sum h(S_k)/\partial_SF(S_k)=(2\pi i)^{-1}\oint h(S)/F(S;\mathbf x,T)\,dS$ on a fixed small contour around $S_\ast$, with $h=1/R$ for $\Psi$ and $h=\mathbf v/R$ for $\Psi\mathbf v$. The integrand is analytic in $(\mathbf x,T)$ for $(\mathbf x,T)$ near $(\mathbf x,T_\ast)$, so the sum is an analytic function of the receiver point and time there, on both sides of $T_\ast$. The closed-form rows on a branch with $D\ne0$ of either sign are the derivatives $-\nabla_{\mathbf x}$ and $-\partial_T$ of that branch's signed scalar and vector, since the implicit differentiation that produces them does not use the sign of $D$. So the signed two-branch sums of $\mathbf G$, $\mathbf W$ and $\mathbf E$ are derivatives of analytic functions and are bounded. On the side of $T_\ast$ without real roots there are no hits, so the pair contributes a bounded jump at $T_\ast$, which is integrable. $\square$ For a path that is only finitely smooth the contour argument does not apply as written; the referee's argument by a smooth change of variable (Section 5) gives boundedness with the sum as smooth as the path allows. The statement concerns the sum of the per-hit rows. It is not the derivative of the truncated two-branch wake: writing the signed pair scalar as an analytic function times a step at $T_\ast(\mathbf x)$, its derivatives carry in addition surface terms concentrated at the fold, with a finite impulse.

**Numerical check (measured, 40 digits).** A transmitter on a uniform circle at speed $1.5$ seen from a fixed off-plane point has one birth fold and one annihilation fold per turn ($D'=2.228$ and $-1.335$). For $\tau$ from $10^{-2}$ to $10^{-8}$, with one root solved on each side of the fold:

| Quantity | $\tau=10^{-2}$ | $\tau=10^{-4}$ | $\tau=10^{-8}$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Single-branch $\|\mathbf G\|\tau^{3/2}$ over the value of Lemma B1 (birth fold, two branches) | $0.98888$, $0.98543$ | $0.99988$, $0.99987$ | $1.00000$, $1.00000$ |
| Single-branch $\|\mathbf E\|\tau^{3/2}$ over the value of Lemma B1 | $1.01081$, $1.01547$ | $1.00012$, $1.00013$ | $1.00000$, $1.00000$ |
| Magnitude-weight sum of $\mathbf G$, times $\tau^{3/2}$, over twice the single-branch value | $0.987152$ | $0.999875$ | $1.000000$ |
| Signed-weight sum, $\|\mathbf G_++\mathbf G_-\|$ | $0.369035$ | $0.353623$ | $0.353472$ |
| Signed-weight sum, $\|\mathbf E_++\mathbf E_-\|$ | $0.561884$ | $0.541628$ | $0.541430$ |

The annihilation fold behaves the same way (signed sums tending to $0.363475$ and $0.644785$). For a uniformly accelerated transmitter moving straight at the receiver, with its fold at speed one, the single-branch G row still follows Lemma B1 (ratio $1.00000$), while the E row times $\tau^{1/2}$ tends to $0.125$, twice the canonical row's $0.0625$: the factor $1+v_n=2$ and the integrable exponent of the collinear case.

> Claim grade. Lemma B1 and both parts of Lemma B2: derived in this adjudication, checked numerically at 40 digits on the three folds listed, and independently confirmed by the referee on a different history (Section 5). The original claim's first sentence is confirmed for the G and W rows at every ordinary fold and for the E row at every ordinary fold with transverse transmitter velocity, and is sharpened by the two exceptions for the E row. Its open clause is answered: under magnitude weights the branches add and the rows are not integrable through a fold; under signed weights they cancel to an analytic remainder. Falsifiers: an ordinary fold at which a single-branch $\|\mathbf G\|\tau^{3/2}$ does not tend to $1/(2^{3/2}R|D'|^{1/2})$; or a fold at which the signed two-branch sum of $\mathbf G$ is unbounded as $\tau\to0$.

**What follows, and what does not.** The assessment's practical conclusion stands under the convention the canonical law uses: with nonnegative weights the G and W rows cannot be carried through any ordinary fold, nor the E row through one with transverse transmitter velocity, so they are confined to receiver points that see $D>0$ throughout, which strictly subfield histories guarantee for every receiver, or need a ceiling. The signed reading is recorded as a mathematical observation and is not a proposal. It would be a new specification of the rows on branches with $D<0$, giving the reversed-order branch a contribution of opposite sign, and under the [approval rule](../equation-variants/README.md#operator-approval-for-deviations) it could be examined only on an explicit operator selection. Not examined here: a receiver in motion and the receiver-velocity response at a fold; folds with $D'=0$; self hits; and any evolution.
