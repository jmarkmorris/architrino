# Elongated-history fate of the amplitude-gradient mirror pair

The selected amplitude-gradient pair admits an all-future dispersal result in the sufficiently slow compatible preparation class of the accepted signed-mode theorem. The useful scalar is a decaying mathematical account, rather than the moving-center norm used on compact eccentric charts. Its derivative is positive while speed is bounded by a fixed multiple of the inverse square root of radius. If the account never becomes positive, the same complete history continues for all future time, separation tends to infinity and speed tends to zero. If it becomes positive, a controlled pericenter passage puts the actual pair on an outward branch; that branch reaches a radial escape cone and has a nonzero terminal velocity. These are derived analytical conclusions in this new treatment, awaiting separate mathematical adjudication. No numerical speed threshold, conserved physical account or arbitrary-history fate is claimed.

The new estimates do not use a bounded radial period. They weight the six actual derivatives by the current radius, and use the actual polar angle only for one passage through pericenter. An elongated outgoing leg may take arbitrarily long in absolute time. Its delay nevertheless occupies a small fraction of the local evolution scale, and its source clock keeps increasing. The complete past and all propagated derivative seams are retained.

## 3. A decaying account with a pointwise sign

Define

$$
E_0=\frac12\|\mathbf v\|^2-\frac1r,\qquad
\mathcal E=E_0-\frac{\epsilon^2h^2}{2r^3}-\frac{\gamma p}{r^2}.
\tag{9}
$$

Every correction tends to zero on a parabolic outgoing leg. This account is a mathematical comparison of speed and separation. It is neither conserved nor a claim about physical energy. The moving-center potential in the compact signed-mode treatment subtracts its value of the radial derivative at the center; that subtraction includes a term linear in the outer radius unless its coefficient vanishes. No such uniform vanishing is assumed here. Equation (9) avoids carrying that coordinate correction to infinity.

Two direct controls explain the sign. First,

$$
\mathbf v\cdot F_2=-\frac{3h^2p}{2r^4}.
$$

Second, differentiating the explicit central vector gives

$$
F_3=-\frac43\frac d{ds}F_0,\qquad
\mathbf v\cdot F_0=-\frac p{r^2}.
\tag{10}
$$

Substitution of (7) in the derivative of (9) cancels both total derivatives exactly and yields the actual identity

$$
\mathcal E'=\gamma\mathbf y''\cdot F_0
-\frac{\epsilon^2hh'}{r^3}+\mathbf v\cdot Q_4.
\tag{11}
$$

On (3), $h^2\le V_*^2r$ and $|p|\le V_*/\sqrt r$. Equations (7)–(8) bound the last two terms and the noncentral part of the first by $C\epsilon^4r^{-9/2}$. In particular the quadratic part of $h'$ contributes $\epsilon^4h^2p/r^5$, whose magnitude has exactly that bound. Thus

$$
\boxed{\quad
\mathcal E'=\frac\gamma{r^4}
\left[1+O\!\left(\frac\epsilon{\sqrt r}\right)\right]>0.
\quad}
\tag{12}
$$

The positive coefficient is uniform for fixed $V_*$ and $r\ge r_*$. It applies at arbitrary phase, including the long outer excursion. It requires no orbit average. On any controlled interval $I$, its signed integrated error obeys

$$
\left|\int_I\left(\mathcal E'-\frac\gamma{r^4}\right)ds\right|
\le C\frac\epsilon{\sqrt{r_*}}\int_I\frac\gamma{r^4}\,ds.
\tag{12a}
$$

Thus an arbitrarily long duration does not by itself accumulate an error larger than the positive comparison work. The radius weights, rather than a compact period, control that accumulation.

As a separate known comparison, on the central paths $r=h^2/(1+e\cos\theta)$, $p=e\sin\theta/h$ and $ds=r^2d\theta/h$, the uncorrected cubic work is

$$
\frac{dE_0}{d\theta}
=\frac\gamma{h^5}
\left[(1+e\cos\theta)^3-2e^2\sin^2\theta(1+e\cos\theta)\right].
\tag{13}
$$

Its full integral is $\pi\gamma(2+e^2)/h^5>0$. At $e=1$ its outbound half integral is $3\pi\gamma/(2h^5)$. This follows by integrating the polynomial in sine and cosine; it is a prescribed central comparison, not an exact delayed ellipse. The account (9) makes its positive remainder pointwise and is the estimate actually used.

## 5. The last near-parabolic passage after a positive crossing

It remains to show that a finite crossing of $\mathcal E=0$ leads to an actual outward leg; a positive speed/separation scalar alone is not a radial escape condition. If $w\le1$ and $\mathcal E\ge0$, (18) implies $u^2\ge c w$ for small $\delta$, with a fixed $c>0$; the term linear in $u$ is absorbed by its square and by $C\delta^6w^4$. ### Incoming part close to the outer end

Suppose a zero crossing occurs with $u<0$ and $w<1$. Equations (17)–(18) give $w_\theta\ge c\sqrt w$ up to the section $w=1$. Also $|u|\le C\sqrt w$ as long as $h^2\mathcal E\le c_0w$. These estimates are closed, rather than assumed: (12) gives

Here subscript $c$ means the crossing state. These estimates improve $A_E\le c_0w$ for small $\delta_c$. They also prove finite angle to $w=1$, even when the starting $w$ is arbitrarily small. Since $ds/d\theta=h^3/w^2$, the incoming duration is at most $C h_c^3 w_c^{-3/2}$, finite for every $w_c>0$ but unbounded as $w_c\downarrow0$. Radius is decreasing on this segment and bounded by its starting value. No cutoff tending to a radial-period singularity is used.

On $w\ge1$, or if the zero crossing starts there, (18) puts the state within $O(\delta_c^2)$ of the fixed compact parabolic curve. ## 6. From a positive outward account to a radial escape cone

Choose $C_A$ from Section 2 first, and then a fixed $M>4C_A$. Enlarge the parabolic speed inventory once to $V_*^2=4(M+1)$ and lower-radius margin $h_*^2/[4(M+1)]$. All constants and the sufficiently-small parameter are chosen for this entire enlargement. On

$$

## 7. All-future theorem and its limits

**Theorem (derived; awaiting independent adjudication).** Fix the compatible complete preparation family and any fixed compact entry level of the accepted signed-mode theorem. For sufficiently small initial speed ratio $\epsilon$, the selected ordinary-root mirror amplitude-gradient solution continues on that same history for all future time. It remains separated and uniformly subfield, with one partner root and no positive-delay self root. Its separation tends to infinity. There are two possibilities:

1. If the decaying account $\mathcal E$ never becomes positive after entry, speed tends to zero, and $\mathcal E\to0$.
2. If $\mathcal E$ becomes positive at a finite time, the actual pericenter/outward-section argument and escape cone apply; velocity tends to a nonzero vector.

The proof is the exhaustion in Sections 4–6. A zero crossing cannot be a singular wake-speed event: its velocity, separation, jets and root margins are controlled, and (12) makes the crossing transversal. A long near-parabolic interval cannot be mistaken for a bounded radial period. Either its nonpositive account gives all-future dispersal directly, or its positive crossing gives the finite actual outward section, a signed account-scale hit and the exact tail estimate. Radius cannot diverge in finite time under either inventory.

The theorem does not decide which terminal-speed branch the inherited preparation selects, determine its terminal velocity, give an explicit useful numerical $\epsilon$ threshold, or apply to arbitrary compatible histories. It does not infer a conservation law, make the current canonical equation equal to the candidate, or establish stability of an assembly. The resulting isolated pair disperses under the selected variant in this declared slow class.

The principal new mathematical burden for independent review is the uniform spatial jet induction and the actual last-passage estimate. The algebraic sign in (12) alone would be insufficient without those two continuation steps. If either fails, the independently checkable conditional result remains: on the declared spatial inventory, a nonpositive account has the all-future zero-speed dispersal consequence; a positive outward account with the radial cone margin has the exact all-future tail consequence. A loss of an upper estimate alone is not an exact instability or late exit.
