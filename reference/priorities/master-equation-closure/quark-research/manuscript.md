# Quark Candidates: Geometry and Complete Histories

## 1. The physical question

A quark-candidate drawing assigns architrinos to a host braid and an accessory arrangement. The dynamical question is whether those constituents can follow a mutually consistent history under the Master Equation while preserving that arrangement. Each architrino responds to earlier source positions, so a snapshot does not specify the state needed to answer the question.

The [published quark chapter](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/assemblies/fermions/quarks.md) owns the candidate catalog and its counting argument. The [published color chapter](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/assemblies/fermions/color-charge-su3.md) owns axis exceptionality and the alternative down-type patterns. Those constructions identify possibilities; their association with physical quarks remains guessed until retained dynamics and response are established.

## 2. Distinct hosts and polarity arrangements

The catalog's three-binary first-generation scaffold and the eight-member F6c host are distinct candidate constructions. Adding six accessories to the latter gives a fourteen-member experiment. Its result could not automatically validate the catalog's different constituent history. The [shared F6c capture analysis](../braid-program/analysis/f6c-geometry.md#are-there-neutral-volumes-that-could-capture-six-architrinos) supplies an octahedral reference arrangement and distinguishes an axial pair from a transverse quartet; it does not demonstrate capture.

Changing the polarities on the same six geometric sites changes the interacting histories. In particular, a result for six same-polarity accessories cannot establish retention for a mixed-polarity quark candidate. Opposite-polarity accessories introduce attractive contributions, while the host response depends on delayed separations, phases and source velocities. The [quark accessory analysis](analysis/accessory-geometry.md) gathers these placement and polarity questions from the shared research.

## 3. What would make the geometry testable

A proposed experiment needs labeled constituents and polarities, a chosen host, complete relevant pasts and a declared surrounding population. Its specification must say whether host motion is prescribed or evolved, and whether accessories influence each other and the host. Fixed-host probes can identify candidate regions of residence. They cannot establish mutual retention of the full assembly.

The decisive dynamical object is a coupled history with complete causal-root accounting on a declared interval. Numerical instantiations use $c_f=1$. Replacing delayed interactions with a fitted confining response would test that replacement, so an unchanged-Master-Equation experiment must obtain every acceleration contribution from the declared history and law. A failed equation residual, an omitted source contribution or loss of the declared candidate identity rejects that realization.

Even a successful finite-interval calculation would have a bounded conclusion. Long-time persistence, stability under changes of preparation and identification with observed quarks require further evidence. A stability calculation around an equilibrium first requires that equilibrium to exist.

## 4. From assembly motion to observer comparisons

The [vortex-coupling target](analysis/quark-vortex-coupling-target.md) proposes studying interactions among quark-like assemblies and comparing the resulting behavior with confinement and gluon response. Its color-singlet and octet constructions are comparison requirements, not additional substrate laws. No completed simulation is supplied by that proposal.

Mass, flavor mixing and effective confinement calculations remain in the [Standard Model geometry program](../../mapping-standard-model/analysis/geometry-first-program.md). They require branch-derived inputs: quantities extracted from an actual retained history. Selecting such inputs to reproduce a desired observer value would not establish that the Master Equation produces them. Quark research supplies the candidate dynamics; the mapping work must then show how those same dynamics produce the observable response.
