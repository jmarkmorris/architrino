## 1. Setting

**Law.** A receiver at $\mathbf x$ at time $T$ receives from source $j$ at every causal emission time $s<T$ with $T-s=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert$ the acceleration $\sigma_{ij}K(\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s))/(\tau^3\lvert D\rvert)$, with $\tau=T-s$, $\mathbf n=(\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s))/\tau$, $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j(s)$ and $\sigma_{ij}=+1$ for like and $-1$ for unlike polarities. Contributions add over all sources and all causal roots, including any positive-delay root from the receiver's own past. The emission at $s=T$ is excluded. Nothing is added to the law.

**Unit ladder.** Lengths in units of $R$, times in units of $R/c_f$. With $c_f=1$ the member speed equals the angular rate, $\varepsilon=\omega R$; the spacing is $\delta=d/R$; the law acquires the coupling $g=K/R=10^{-3}$. Growth exponents in these units are $R$ times physical ones, so $\lambda_*/\varepsilon=\lambda_{\rm phys}/\omega$. The reference ladder has, for every $k\in\mathbb Z$, a positive member at $Q(\varepsilon t+k\pi)\mathbf e_1+k\delta\,\mathbf e_z$ and a negative member at $Q(\varepsilon t+k\pi+\pi)\mathbf e_1+k\delta\,\mathbf e_z$, where $Q(\alpha)$ is the rotation by $\alpha$ about $\mathbf e_z$ and $J$ its generator, $J\mathbf e_1=\mathbf e_2$, $J\mathbf e_2=-\mathbf e_1$, $J\mathbf e_z=0$. The balance is $\sum_j\mathbf a_j=-\varepsilon^2\mathbf e_1$ at the positive member of rung $0$ at $t=0$, equivalent (existence proof, Lemma 2, [small-speed existence](rotating-ladder-small-speed-existence.md)) to the ladder solving the law at every member and time.

**The history class $\mathcal C$.** For twice continuously differentiable in-plane displacement histories $\mathbf u_0,\mathbf u_1:(-\infty,T_1]\to\mathbb R^2$,

$$
\mathbf X^{+}_k(t)=Q(\varepsilon t+k\pi)\bigl[\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_{k\bmod2}(t)\bigr]+k\delta\,\mathbf e_z,\qquad
\mathbf X^{-}_k(t)=Q(\varepsilon t+k\pi+\pi)\bigl[\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_{k\bmod2}(t)\bigr]+k\delta\,\mathbf e_z .
$$

Each member stays in the plane of its rung, its in-plane displacement in its own rotating frame depends only on the parity of its rung, and the two members of a rung carry the same displacement in their own frames. This is the span of $q\in\{0,\pi\}$ in the sector $p=+1$, in-plane: $q=0$ is $\mathbf u_1=\mathbf u_0$ and $q=\pi$ is $\mathbf u_1=-\mathbf u_0$. Write $\mathbf z=(\mathbf u_0,\mathbf u_1)$ and $\mathbf z_\pm=(\mathbf u_0\pm\mathbf u_1)/2$.

**Sources.** For the receiver at the positive member of rung $r\in\{0,1\}$, sources are $j=(\nu,m)$, $\nu=\pm1$, $m\in\mathbb Z$, $(\nu,m)\ne(+1,0)$: the member of rung $r+m$ whose reference in-plane position is $\nu$ times the receiver's. Its polarity product is $\sigma_j=\nu(-1)^m$ and its displacement history is $\mathbf u_{k'}$, $k'=(r+m)\bmod2$. In the receiver's rotating frame at reception time $t$, an emission at delay $\tau$ has

$$
\mathbf r_j=\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_r(t)-\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\bigl[\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_{k'}(t-\tau)\bigr]-m\delta\,\mathbf e_z,\qquad
\mathbf V_j=\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\bigl[\varepsilon J(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_{k'}(t-\tau))+\mathbf u_{k'}'(t-\tau)\bigr].
$$

Reference delay $\tau_j$, reference factor $D_j\in[1-\varepsilon^2,1+\varepsilon^2]$, and $\tau_j\ge\ell_j$ with $\ell_{(+1,m)}=\lvert m\rvert\delta$, $\ell_{(-1,m)}=\sqrt{(m^2\delta^2+4)/(1+\varepsilon^2)}$ (existence proof, Lemma 1(c)). For $\delta\ge2$ the smallest is $\tau_{\min}=2/\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}$.

**Reduced equation.**

$$
\mathbf u_r''(t)+2\varepsilon J\mathbf u_r'(t)-\varepsilon^2\mathbf u_r(t)=P_\perp\Bigl[\mathcal A_r(\mathbf z)(t)-\mathcal A_r(0)\Bigr],\qquad r=0,1,\tag{1.1}
$$

with $\mathcal A_r(\mathbf z)(t)=\sum_jg\sigma_j\mathbf r_j/(\tau^3D)$ at the receiver's causal roots, $P_\perp$ the in-plane projection and $\mathcal A_r(0)=-\varepsilon^2\mathbf e_1$. The equation is autonomous.

**History norm.** $\mathsf N_t(\mathbf z)=\sup_{s\le t}\max_{r}\bigl(\lvert\mathbf u_r(s)\rvert+\varepsilon^{-1}\lvert\mathbf u_r'(s)\rvert\bigr)$: position in units of $R$ and rotating-frame velocity in units of the member speed, over the whole past. The phase family $\mathcal O$ consists of $\mathbf u_0=\mathbf u_1=Q(\varphi)\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_1$, constant; each has $\mathbf u'\equiv0$.

**Coefficient space.** $\mathcal W$ is the Wiener algebra of complex series $f=\sum_{n\ge0}f_nw^n$ with $\lVert f\rVert=\sum_n\lvert f_n\rvert$, and $\mathcal W_0$ the ideal $f_0=0$. Vector and matrix series use the complex Euclidean norm of each coefficient and the operator norm of each matrix coefficient. **Dot products of complex vectors are the complex bilinear extension $\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b=\sum_ia_ib_i$ of the Euclidean dot product, not a Hermitian product**; this is required for holomorphy, and $\lvert\mathbf a\cdot\mathbf b\rvert\le\lvert\mathbf a\rvert\lvert\mathbf b\rvert$ still holds. The unknown is $\mathbf p=(\mathbf p_0,\mathbf p_1)\in\mathcal W_0^2\times\mathcal W_0^2$ with $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert=\max_r\lVert\mathbf p_r\rVert$, and the history is $\mathbf u_r(t)=\mathbf p_r(e^{\lambda_*t})$, $t\le0$. The Euler operator is $\mathcal D\mathbf p=\lambda_*w\mathbf p'(w)$. For series with coefficients of order $n\ge2$ only, the weighted norms are

$$
\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(k)}=\max_{r=0,1}\sum_{n\ge2}n^k\lvert\mathbf v_{r,n}\rvert,\qquad k=0,1,2 .
$$

## 2. Analytic lemmas

**Lemma A1 (reduction; derived).** A class history solves the Master Equation at every member and every time $t\le T_1$ if and only if $\mathbf z$ solves (1.1) for $r=0,1$ and $t\le T_1$, all row sums converging absolutely.

*Proof.* Translation by $2\delta\,\mathbf e_z$ (rung $k\to k+2$, same parity and polarity), the half turn $Q(\pi)$ with global polarity reversal (the members of each rung exchanged, all polarity products preserved), and the reflection $z\to-z$ (rung $k\to-k$, same parity since $Q(k\pi)=Q(-k\pi)$, same polarity, in-plane vectors unchanged) map a class history to itself. The law is equivariant under isometries and global polarity reversal, causal roots being mapped bijectively, and absolute convergence (Lemma A5) allows reordering. The first two operations carry the positive members of rungs $0$ and $1$ to every member. The reflection in $z=0$ fixes the positive member of rung $0$ and pairs source offsets $m$ and $-m$ with identical parity histories; the reflection in $z=\delta$ (composition with the $2\delta$ translation) does the same for rung $1$. Paired rows have equal in-plane and opposite axial components, so each receiver gets zero axial acceleration, which is its actual axial acceleration; the in-plane components are (1.1). This proves a consistent reduction for this ansatz; it does not assume a symmetry-preserving general flow. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma A2 (causal roots in subwake histories; derived).** Let a source path $\mathbf X$ be Lipschitz on $(-\infty,t]$ with speed at most $\bar V<1$, and $\rho_0=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(t)\rvert>0$. Then $f(\tau)=\tau-\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(t-\tau)\rvert$ has exactly one zero $\tau_c\in(0,\infty)$, with $\rho_0/(1+\bar V)\le\tau_c\le\rho_0/(1-\bar V)$, and if $\mathbf X$ is differentiable at $t-\tau_c$ then $f'(\tau_c)=D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf X'(t-\tau_c)\ge1-\bar V$. If $\mathbf x=\mathbf X(t)$, then $f(\tau)\ge(1-\bar V)\tau>0$ for $\tau>0$: no self root.

*Proof.* The distance $\tau\mapsto\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(t-\tau)\rvert$ is Lipschitz with constant $\bar V$, so $f(\tau_2)-f(\tau_1)\ge(1-\bar V)(\tau_2-\tau_1)$, and $f(0)=-\rho_0$; this gives existence, uniqueness and the bounds. At the zero the distance equals $\tau_c>0$, hence is differentiable with $d\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(t-\tau)\rvert/d\tau=+\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf X'(t-\tau)$ (since $d(\mathbf x-\mathbf X(t-\tau))/d\tau=+\mathbf X'(t-\tau)$), so $f'(\tau_c)=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf X'=D$. The self case is the same inequality with $\rho_0=0$. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma A3 (Wiener algebra; derived).** (a) $\mathcal W$ is a commutative unital Banach algebra; evaluation at $\lvert w\rvert\le1$ is a continuous character. (b) If $F$ is analytic in a disk of radius $\varrho$ about $a$ and $\lVert x\rVert<\varrho$, then $\lVert F(a+x)-F(a)\rVert\le\sum_{k\ge1}\lvert F^{(k)}(a)\rvert\lVert x\rVert^k/k!$; in particular $\lVert e^x\rVert\le e^{\lVert x\rVert}$, $\lVert\cos x-1\rVert\le\cosh\lVert x\rVert-1$, $\lVert\sin x\rVert\le\sinh\lVert x\rVert$, $\lVert(a+x)^{-k}\rVert\le(\lvert a\rvert-\lVert x\rVert)^{-k}$. (c) If $\mathbf p,\varphi\in\mathcal W_0$ with $\lVert\varphi\rVert\le q<1$, then $\mathbf p\circ\varphi=\sum_n\mathbf p_n\varphi^n\in\mathcal W_0$, $\lVert\mathbf p\circ\varphi\rVert\le q\lVert\mathbf p\rVert$, and the delayed Euler derivative satisfies $\lVert(\mathcal D\mathbf p)\circ\varphi\rVert\le\lambda_*\sup_{n\ge1}(nq^n)\lVert\mathbf p\rVert$. The maps $(\mathbf p,\varphi)\mapsto\mathbf p\circ\varphi$ and $(\mathbf p,\varphi)\mapsto(\mathcal D\mathbf p)\circ\varphi$ are jointly holomorphic on $\mathcal W_0\times\{\lVert\varphi\rVert<1\}$ (on each smaller ball $\lVert\varphi\rVert\le q'<1$ the series and all fixed-order derivatives converge normally); the derivative of $\mathbf p\circ\varphi$ in $\varphi$ is multiplication by $\mathbf p'\circ\varphi$. This is not a claim that the undelayed operator $\mathcal D$ is bounded on $\mathcal W$. (d) If $q=e^{-\lambda_*a}$, $a>0$, then $\lambda_*\sup_nnq^n\le1/(ea)$. (e) Real inputs give real outputs; $t\mapsto\mathbf p(e^{\lambda_*t})$ is smooth on $t<0$ and $C^2$ on $t\le0$ when $\sum_nn^2\lvert\mathbf p_{r,n}\rvert<\infty$ for each $r$.

*Proof.* Absolute convergence and $\lVert fg\rVert\le\lVert f\rVert\lVert g\rVert$ give (a) to (c); (d) is $\sup_{x>0}xe^{-\lambda_*ax}=1/(e\lambda_*a)$; (e) is termwise differentiation. $\blacksquare$

Part (c) removes the loss of derivatives caused by state-dependent delays: a delayed evaluation $\mathbf u(t-\tau)=\mathbf p(we^{-\lambda_*\tau})$ lies strictly inside the disk, where the delayed velocity is bounded by the position norm. The source-velocity formula involves only this delayed derivative.

**Lemma A4 (the delay map, uniform over all sources; derived).** Fix $r_p>0$, $0<\bar\vartheta<\tau_{\min}$, and put

$$
e_1=e^{\varepsilon\bar\vartheta},\quad \sigma_*=\frac1{e(\tau_{\min}-\bar\vartheta)},\quad \beta_b=1+e_1,\quad \beta_v=e_1(\varepsilon+\sigma_*),\quad D_{\min}=1-\varepsilon^2,
$$

$$
K_1=\frac{\bar\vartheta^2(1+\varepsilon^2e_1)+2(1+e_1)\beta_br_p+\beta_b^2r_p^2}{2\tau_{\min}D_{\min}},\qquad
K_2=\frac{2\bar\vartheta(1+\varepsilon^2e_1)+2\varepsilon e_1\beta_br_p+2(1+e_1)\beta_vr_p+2\beta_b\beta_vr_p^2}{2\tau_{\min}D_{\min}} .
$$

If $K_1\le\bar\vartheta$ and $K_2\le\frac12$ (condition R1), then for every source $j$ and every $\mathbf p$ with $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert<r_p$ there is exactly one $\vartheta_j(\mathbf p)\in\mathcal W_0$ with $\lVert\vartheta_j\rVert\le\bar\vartheta$ solving the squared causal equation $\mathbf r_j\cdot\mathbf r_j=(\tau_j+\vartheta)^2$ (bilinear dot product), in which $\mathbf u_r(t)\to\mathbf p_r(w)$, $\mathbf u_{k'}(t-\tau)\to\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)$, $\mathbf u_{k'}'(t-\tau)\to(\mathcal D\mathbf p_{k'})(\varphi_j)$, $\varphi_j=we^{-\lambda_*(\tau_j+\vartheta)}$. The map $\mathbf p\mapsto\vartheta_j(\mathbf p)$ is holomorphic, and all bounds are uniform in $j$.

*Proof.* Write $\tau=\tau_j+\vartheta$ and $\mathbf r_j=\mathbf r^0_j(\tau)+\mathbf b_j$ with $\mathbf r^0_j(\tau)=\mathbf e_1-\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\mathbf e_1-m\delta\,\mathbf e_z$ and $\mathbf b_j=\mathbf p_r(w)-\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)$, in-plane. For $\lVert\vartheta\rVert\le\bar\vartheta$: $\lVert\varphi_j\rVert\le e^{-\lambda_*(\tau_{\min}-\bar\vartheta)}<1$ uniformly, so by Lemma A3(c,d) $\lVert\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)\rVert\le\lVert\mathbf p\rVert$ and $\lVert(\mathcal D\mathbf p_{k'})(\varphi_j)\rVert\le\sigma_*\lVert\mathbf p\rVert$; and $\lVert Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\rVert\le e_1$. Hence $\lVert\mathbf b_j\rVert\le\beta_br_p$ and, from $\partial_\vartheta\mathbf b_j=\nu\varepsilon JQ(-\varepsilon\tau)\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)+\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)(\mathcal D\mathbf p_{k'})(\varphi_j)$, $\lVert\partial_\vartheta\mathbf b_j\rVert\le\beta_vr_p$. The in-plane part of $\mathbf r^0_j(\tau)$ has norm at most $1+e_1$ and $\vartheta$-derivative $\mathbf V^0_j(\tau)=\nu\varepsilon Q(-\varepsilon\tau)J\mathbf e_1$ of norm at most $\varepsilon e_1$; the axial part of $\mathbf r^0_j$ never meets an in-plane vector in a dot product, which keeps the constants uniform in $m$. With $h_j(\vartheta)=\mathbf r^0_j\cdot\mathbf r^0_j-\tau^2=-\Phi_\nu(\tau)$, $\Phi_\nu(\tau)=\tau^2-m^2\delta^2-2+2\nu\cos\varepsilon\tau$, one has $h_j(0)=0$, $h_j'(0)=-2\tau_jD_j$, and the squared equation is $\vartheta=\mathcal K_j(\vartheta)=[h_j(\vartheta)+2\tau_jD_j\vartheta+2\mathbf r^0_j\cdot\mathbf b_j+\mathbf b_j\cdot\mathbf b_j]/(2\tau_jD_j)$. Here $h_j+2\tau_jD_j\vartheta=-\vartheta^2-2\nu[\cos\varepsilon\tau_j(\cos\varepsilon\vartheta-1)-\sin\varepsilon\tau_j(\sin\varepsilon\vartheta-\varepsilon\vartheta)]$ has norm at most $\bar\vartheta^2(1+\varepsilon^2e_1)$ and $\vartheta$-derivative of norm at most $2\bar\vartheta(1+\varepsilon^2e_1)$; the other two terms are bounded by $2(1+e_1)\beta_br_p$ and $\beta_b^2r_p^2$, and their derivatives by $2\varepsilon e_1\beta_br_p+2(1+e_1)\beta_vr_p$ and $2\beta_b\beta_vr_p^2$. With $\tau_jD_j\ge\tau_{\min}D_{\min}$, $\mathcal K_j$ maps the ball $\lVert\vartheta\rVert\le\bar\vartheta$ of $\mathcal W_0$ into itself with Lipschitz constant at most $K_2\le\frac12$. The fixed point is unique, and as a uniform limit of holomorphic iterates it is holomorphic in $\mathbf p$. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma A5 (rows and the lattice sum; derived).** Under R1, put

$$
K_x=\bar\vartheta+\varepsilon(e_1-1)+(1+e_1)\beta_vr_p+\varepsilon e_1\beta_br_p+\beta_b\beta_vr_p^2,\qquad
\Gamma=\frac{\tau_{\min}^2\,(\tau_{\min}+e_1-1+\beta_br_p)}{(\tau_{\min}-\bar\vartheta)^2\,(\tau_{\min}D_{\min}-K_x)},
$$

$$
Z=\sum_j\ell_j^{-2}=\frac{\pi^2}{3\delta^2}+(1+\varepsilon^2)\frac{\pi}{2\delta}\coth\frac{2\pi}{\delta},\qquad M=2g\,\Gamma Z,
$$

and assume $K_x<\tau_{\min}D_{\min}$ (condition R2). Then each row map $\mathbf a_j(\mathbf p)=g\sigma_j\mathbf r_j(\tau_j+\vartheta_j)^{-2}\kappa_j^{-1}$, $\kappa_j=\tau_j+\vartheta_j-\mathbf r_j\cdot\mathbf V_j$, is holomorphic on $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert<r_p$ with $\lVert\mathbf a_j(\mathbf p)\rVert\le g\Gamma/\ell_j^2$; $\mathcal F_r(\mathbf p)=P_\perp\sum_j\mathbf a_j(\mathbf p)$ converges absolutely and uniformly; $\mathcal F=(\mathcal F_0,\mathcal F_1)$ is holomorphic and $\sup_{\lVert\mathbf p\rVert<r_p}\lVert\mathcal F(\mathbf p)-\mathcal F(0)\rVert\le M$.

*Proof.* $\mathbf V_j=\mathbf V^0_j(\tau)+\boldsymbol\beta_j$ with $\boldsymbol\beta_j=\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)[\varepsilon J\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)+(\mathcal D\mathbf p_{k'})(\varphi_j)]$, $\lVert\boldsymbol\beta_j\rVert\le\beta_vr_p$. The identity $\mathbf r^0_j(\tau)\cdot\mathbf V^0_j(\tau)=\nu\varepsilon\sin\varepsilon\tau$ holds for complex $\tau$, and $\tau_jD_j=\tau_j-\nu\varepsilon\sin\varepsilon\tau_j$; so $\kappa_j-\tau_jD_j=\vartheta_j-\nu\varepsilon(\sin\varepsilon\tau-\sin\varepsilon\tau_j)-\mathbf r^0_{j\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol\beta_j-\mathbf b_j\cdot\mathbf V^0_j-\mathbf b_j\cdot\boldsymbol\beta_j$, of norm at most $K_x$. By Lemma A3(b), $\lVert\kappa_j^{-1}\rVert\le(\tau_jD_{\min}-K_x)^{-1}$ (R2 keeps the series away from its pole), $\lVert(\tau_j+\vartheta_j)^{-2}\rVert\le(\tau_j-\bar\vartheta)^{-2}$, $\lVert\mathbf r_j\rVert\le\tau_j+c$ with $c=e_1-1+\beta_br_p$. The normalized majorant $(\tau/(\tau-\bar\vartheta))^2(\tau+c)/(\tau D_{\min}-K_x)$ is a product of two positive decreasing factors, so $\lVert\mathbf a_j\rVert\le g\Gamma/\tau_j^2\le g\Gamma/\ell_j^2$; also $\lvert\mathbf a_j(0)\rvert\le g/(\tau_j^2D_{\min})\le g\Gamma/\ell_j^2$. The two lattice sums are classical. Uniform absolute convergence of holomorphic maps gives a holomorphic sum. $\blacksquare$

The image sums converge because rows decay like the inverse square of the delay on a one-dimensional lattice, uniformly in history depth; the exponential factors control delayed velocities (Lemma A3(c)). Fixed-order derivatives of the rows in $\mathbf p$, on a strictly smaller coefficient ball, keep an $\ell_j^{-2}$ majorant, with constants that depend on the order of the derivative and on the radius margin (Cauchy estimates); they are not all bounded by the zero-order constant $g\Gamma/\ell_j^2$. On the real constructed history, where $D>0$ is proved (Section 3), $\kappa/\tau=D=\lvert D\rvert$, so the complex row is the holomorphic extension of the regular real branch, not a complex version of the absolute value.

**Lemma A5′ (optional sharpened row-difference bound; derived, self-reviewed only).** Under R1 and R2, with $\Delta_r=e_1-1+\beta_br_p$ and

$$
\Gamma'=\tau_{\min}^3\Bigl[\frac{\Delta_r}{(\tau_{\min}-\bar\vartheta)^2(\tau_{\min}D_{\min}-K_x)}+\frac{\tau_{\min}\bigl((\tau_{\min}-\bar\vartheta)^{-2}-\tau_{\min}^{-2}\bigr)}{\tau_{\min}D_{\min}-K_x}+\frac{K_x}{\tau_{\min}D_{\min}(\tau_{\min}D_{\min}-K_x)}\Bigr],\qquad Z_3=\sum_j\ell_j^{-3},
$$

and provided $D_{\min}\bar\vartheta\le2K_x$, one has $\sup_{\lVert\mathbf p\rVert<r_p}\lVert\mathcal F(\mathbf p)-\mathcal F(0)\rVert\le M':=g\Gamma'Z_3$, which may replace $M$ everywhere.

*Proof.* With $\mathbf r_{j0}$, $T_0=\tau_j^{-2}$, $K_0=(\tau_jD_j)^{-1}$ the reference values, $\mathbf a_j-\mathbf a_j(0)=g\sigma_j[(\mathbf r_j-\mathbf r_{j0})TK+\mathbf r_{j0}(T-T_0)K+\mathbf r_{j0}T_0(K-K_0)]$ with $\lVert\mathbf r_j-\mathbf r_{j0}\rVert\le\Delta_r$, $\lVert T-T_0\rVert\le(\tau_j-\bar\vartheta)^{-2}-\tau_j^{-2}$ (majorant series), $\lVert K-K_0\rVert\le K_x/(\tau_jD_{\min}(\tau_jD_{\min}-K_x))$, $\lvert\mathbf r_{j0}\rvert=\tau_j$. Multiplied by $\tau_j^3$, the three terms are decreasing in $\tau_j$: the first and third as products of positive decreasing factors; the second equals $\tau^2(2\tau\bar\vartheta-\bar\vartheta^2)/((\tau-\bar\vartheta)^2(\tau D_{\min}-K_x))$, whose second factor has derivative of the sign of $\bar\vartheta(D_{\min}\bar\vartheta-2K_x)\le0$. Hence $\lVert\mathbf a_j-\mathbf a_j(0)\rVert\le g\Gamma'/\ell_j^3$. $Z_3$ is enclosed as $2\zeta(3)/\delta^3$ plus the other-rail sum, both with explicit tails. $\blacksquare$

This is not the direct second-derivative bound that would sharpen Lemma A6 itself; that bound was not derived here. Lemma A5′ improves the supremum that enters A6.

**Lemma A6 (quadratic remainder; derived).** With $\mathcal N(\mathbf p)=\mathcal F(\mathbf p)-\mathcal F(0)-D\mathcal F(0)\mathbf p$ and $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert,\lVert\tilde{\mathbf p}\rVert\le\rho<r_p$,

$$
\lVert\mathcal N(\mathbf p)-\mathcal N(\tilde{\mathbf p})\rVert\le\frac{4M\rho}{(r_p-\rho)^2}\lVert\mathbf p-\tilde{\mathbf p}\rVert,\qquad\lVert\mathcal N(\mathbf p)\rVert\le\frac{2M\rho^2}{(r_p-\rho)^2},
$$

and $\mathcal N(\mathbf p)$ has no coefficient of order $0$ or $1$ in $w$.

*Proof.* For $\lVert\boldsymbol\xi\rVert\le\rho$ and unit $\mathbf a,\mathbf b$, $(\zeta_1,\zeta_2)\mapsto\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\xi+\zeta_1\mathbf a+\zeta_2\mathbf b)-\mathcal F(0)$ is holomorphic and bounded by $M$ on the polydisk of radii $(r_p-\rho)/2$; its mixed coefficient is $D^2\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\xi)[\mathbf a,\mathbf b]$, so $\lVert D^2\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\xi)[\mathbf a,\mathbf b]\rVert\le4M/(r_p-\rho)^2$. Integrating from $0$ to $\boldsymbol\xi$ and then along the argument segment gives both bounds. The order statement uses the filtration of this construction: multiplication adds $w$-orders, $\mathcal D$ preserves order, every delayed argument carries a factor $w$, and the delay series starts at positive order; hence every nonlinear term has order at least two. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma A7 (linear part; derived given the adjudicated first variation).** For $\mathbf c\in\mathbb C^2\times\mathbb C^2$ and $n\ge1$, $(n\lambda_*+\varepsilon J)^2\mathbf c\,w^n-D\mathcal F(0)[\mathbf c\,w^n]=\Delta_{\mathcal C}(n\lambda_*)\mathbf c\,w^n$, where $\Delta_{\mathcal C}(\mu)$ acts as $\Delta_0(\mu)$ on $\mathbf c_+$ and $\Delta_\pi(\mu)$ on $\mathbf c_-$, and $\Delta_q(\mu)$ is the in-plane block of $\Delta(\mu;q,+1)=(\mu I+\varepsilon J)^2-\sum_jL_{u,j}+\sum_j\phi_{q,m}e^{-\mu\tau_j}[L_{u,j}Q_j-L_{w,j}Q_j(\mu I+\varepsilon J)]$, with $\phi_{0,m}=1$, $\phi_{\pi,m}=(-1)^m$, $Q_j=\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau_j)$ on the plane, and $L_{u,j},L_{w,j}$ the adjudication's matrices times $g$. The coefficient equations of (1.1) are

$$
\Delta_{\mathcal C}(n\lambda_*)\,\mathbf p_n=[\mathcal N(\mathbf p)]_n,\qquad n\ge1. \tag{2.1}
$$

*Proof.* At $\mathbf p=0$, the composite $\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)$ has derivative $\mathbf c\,w^n\mapsto e^{-n\lambda_*\tau_j}\mathbf c\,w^n$, the delayed derivative $\mathbf c\,w^n\mapsto n\lambda_*e^{-n\lambda_*\tau_j}\mathbf c\,w^n$, and $D\vartheta_j(0)$ is the root shift $\rho=\mathbf n_j\cdot\mathbf u/D_j$, $\mathbf u=\delta\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\xi$. The variations $\delta\tau=\rho$, $\delta\mathbf r=\mathbf u+\rho\mathbf V$, $\delta\mathbf V=\mathbf w-\rho\mathbf A$, $\delta\kappa=\rho(1-\lvert\mathbf V\rvert^2+\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf A)-\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf V-\mathbf r\cdot\mathbf w$ are those of the adjudication's pre-target reference and yield $L_u$, $L_w$; for a mode of exponent $\mu$, $\boldsymbol\xi=Q_je^{-\mu\tau_j}\mathbf c_{k'}$ and $\mathbf w=Q_je^{-\mu\tau_j}(\mu I+\varepsilon J)\mathbf c_{k'}$, which gives the signs shown. The screw operation (translation by $\delta$ with a half turn) maps $(\mathbf u_0,\mathbf u_1)$ to $(\mathbf u_1,\mathbf u_0)$, so the linear part is $\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B&A\end{pmatrix}$ and acts as $A\pm B$ on $\mathbf c_\pm$, the $q=0$ and $q=\pi$ blocks. The in-plane block is closed at $q\in\{0,\pi\}$ because the reflection maps $(\nu,m)$ to $(\nu,-m)$ with the same phase. Both parity blocks are needed, since nonlinear coefficients need not stay in $q=\pi$. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma A8 (inverse on the real half-line; derived).** For real $\mu\ge0$ let $b_0(\mu)=\lVert(\sum_jL_{u,j})_\perp\rVert+\sum_je^{-\mu\tau_j}\lVert(L_{u,j}Q_j-\varepsilon L_{w,j}Q_jJ)_\perp\rVert$ and $b_1(\mu)=\sum_je^{-\mu\tau_j}\lVert(L_{w,j}Q_j)_\perp\rVert$ (spectral norms, in-plane blocks). Let $\mu_0>0$, $\bar b_0\ge b_0(\mu_0)$, $\bar b_1\ge b_1(\mu_0)$, **$\bar b_0\ge\varepsilon^2$**, and $h_0=\mu_0^2+\varepsilon^2-\bar b_0-\bar b_1\mu_0>0$. Put $h(\mu)=\mu^2+\varepsilon^2-\bar b_0-\bar b_1\mu$. Then for real $\mu\ge\mu_0$: $h(\mu)\ge(\mu/\mu_0)^2h_0>0$ and $\lVert\Delta_q(\mu)^{-1}\rVert\le1/h(\mu)$, $q\in\{0,\pi\}$. If $\mu_0=2\lambda_{\rm lo}$ with $0<\lambda_{\rm lo}\le\lambda_*$, then for every $n\ge2$, $\lVert\Delta_q(n\lambda_*)^{-1}\rVert\le4/(n^2h_0)$, and the operator $\mathcal R$ that sends a series $\mathbf f$ with coefficients of order $\ge2$ to the series with coefficients $\Delta_{\mathcal C}(n\lambda_*)^{-1}\mathbf f_n$ satisfies

$$
\lVert\mathcal R\mathbf f\rVert_{(k)}\le\frac{2^{k+1}}{h_0}\lVert\mathbf f\rVert,\qquad k=0,1,2 .
$$

*Proof.* For real $\mu$ the matrix $\mu I+\varepsilon J$ is normal with eigenvalues $\mu\pm i\varepsilon$, so the smallest singular value of $(\mu I+\varepsilon J)^2$ is $\mu^2+\varepsilon^2$; each block differs from it by at most $b_0(\mu)+b_1(\mu)\mu\le\bar b_0+\bar b_1\mu$ for $\mu\ge\mu_0$, since $e^{-\mu\tau_j}$ decreases. Next, $\frac{d}{d\mu}\frac{h(\mu)}{\mu^2}=\frac{\bar b_1}{\mu^2}+\frac{2(\bar b_0-\varepsilon^2)}{\mu^3}\ge0$, so $h(\mu)\ge(\mu/\mu_0)^2h_0>0$ and the Neumann series gives $\lVert\Delta_q(\mu)^{-1}\rVert\le1/h(\mu)\le\mu_0^2/(\mu^2h_0)$. At $\mu=n\lambda_*\ge2\lambda_{\rm lo}=\mu_0$ this is at most $4\lambda_{\rm lo}^2/(n^2\lambda_*^2h_0)\le4/(n^2h_0)$. Write $D_{q,n}=\Delta_q(n\lambda_*)^{-1}$, $A_n=(D_{0,n}+D_{\pi,n})/2$, $B_n=(D_{0,n}-D_{\pi,n})/2$, so $\lVert A_n\rVert,\lVert B_n\rVert\le4/(n^2h_0)$ and $(\mathcal R\mathbf f)_{0,n}=A_n\mathbf f_{0,n}+B_n\mathbf f_{1,n}$, $(\mathcal R\mathbf f)_{1,n}=B_n\mathbf f_{0,n}+A_n\mathbf f_{1,n}$. Then $\sum_{n\ge2}n^k\lvert(\mathcal R\mathbf f)_{r,n}\rvert\le\sum_{n\ge2}\frac{4n^{k-2}}{h_0}(\lvert\mathbf f_{0,n}\rvert+\lvert\mathbf f_{1,n}\rvert)\le\frac{2^k}{h_0}(\lVert\mathbf f_0\rVert+\lVert\mathbf f_1\rVert)\le\frac{2^{k+1}}{h_0}\lVert\mathbf f\rVert$, using $n^{k-2}\le2^{k-2}$ for $n\ge2$, $k\le2$. $\blacksquare$

The condition $\bar b_0\ge\varepsilon^2$ cannot be dropped from this argument: positivity of $h_0$ alone does not imply positivity of $h$ on $\mu\ge\mu_0$ (Codex's algebraic example: $\varepsilon=0.1$, $\bar b_0=0.001$, $\bar b_1=0.2$, $\mu_0=0.01$ gives $h(\mu_0)=0.0071$ and $h(0.1)=-0.001$). Enlarging $\bar b_0$ to reach $\varepsilon^2$ is allowed only if $h_0$ is rechecked afterwards. No inverse off the real axis is needed by this construction. For comparison only: along a vertical line $\operatorname{Re}\mu=a$ the smallest singular value of $(\mu I+\varepsilon J)^2$ is $\min\{a^2+(y\pm\varepsilon)^2\}$, whose minimum is $a^2\approx3.90\,\varepsilon^2$ at $a=2\lambda_*$; whether a Neumann comparison would close on that line, where $b_1\lvert\mu\rvert$ grows, was not examined and is not a premise.

## 3. The theorem

**Theorem (ancient departure from the rotating ladder at $R=1000$).** Let $(\delta,\varepsilon)\in P$ be the balance of B0, $g=10^{-3}$, $\lambda_*\in(\lambda_{\rm lo},\lambda_{\rm hi})$ a selected positive real root of $\det\Delta_\pi$ furnished by B1, and $\mathbf c\in\mathbb R^2$ a real unit vector with $\Delta_\pi(\lambda_*)\mathbf c=0$. Let $r_p=0.21$, $\bar\vartheta=0.546$, $M\le5.5154\times10^{-3}$ (Lemma A5 over $P$), $h_0\ge3.9397016552\times10^{-4}$ (B3), and

$$
\eta_0=\min\Bigl(\frac{r_p}4,\ \frac{h_0r_p^2}{256M}\Bigr)\ \ge\ 1.2305\times10^{-5}.
$$

For every $\eta\in(0,\eta_0]$ there is a unique series $\mathbf v$ with coefficients of order $n\ge2$ only and $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(0)}\le\eta$ such that $\mathbf p=\eta w(\mathbf c,-\mathbf c)+\mathbf v$ solves (2.1) for all $n$. Its coefficients are real, and $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(0)}\le\eta/4$, $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(1)}\le\eta/2$, $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(2)}\le\eta$. The class history $\mathbf u_r(t)=\mathbf p_r(e^{\lambda_*t})$, $t\le0$, has the following properties.

1. **Exact solution.** It solves the unchanged Master Equation at every member and every time $t\le0$, with absolutely convergent sums over all sources, exactly one causal root per source and no self root.
2. **Approach along the selected root.** $\mathbf u_0=\eta e^{\lambda_*t}\mathbf c+\mathbf y_0$, $\mathbf u_1=-\eta e^{\lambda_*t}\mathbf c+\mathbf y_1$, with $\lvert\mathbf y_r(t)\rvert\le\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(0)}e^{2\lambda_*t}$, $\lvert\mathbf y_r'(t)\rvert\le\lambda_*\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(1)}e^{2\lambda_*t}$, $\lvert\mathbf y_r''(t)\rvert\le\lambda_*^2\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(2)}e^{2\lambda_*t}$, and $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(k)}\le2^{k+1}\cdot32M\eta^2/(h_0r_p^2)$.
3. **Admissibility.** For $t\le0$: $\lvert\mathbf u_r\rvert\le\frac54\eta$, $\lvert\mathbf u_r'\rvert\le\frac32\lambda_*\eta$, member speeds at most $\varepsilon(1+2\eta)+2\lambda_*\eta<1$, equal-time separations of distinct members at least $\min(2,\delta)-3\eta>0$, $D\ge1-\varepsilon(1+2\eta)-2\lambda_*\eta$ at every causal root.
4. **Departure.** For $L>0$ let $\mathbf z_L(t)=\mathbf z(t-L)$, $t\le L$, with components $\mathbf u_{r,L}$. It is an exact complete-history solution in $\mathcal C$, with $\mathsf N_0(\mathbf z_L)\le4\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}$, and $\varepsilon^{-1}\lvert\mathbf u_{0,L}'(L)\rvert=\varepsilon^{-1}\lvert\mathbf u_0'(0)\rvert\ge\lambda_*\eta/(2\varepsilon)$. Hence $\mathsf N_L(\mathbf z_L-\mathbf o)\ge\lambda_*\eta/(2\varepsilon)$ for every $\mathbf o\in\mathcal O$. At $\eta=\eta_0$ the distance is at least $d_*=\lambda_{\rm lo}\eta_0/(2\varepsilon_{\rm hi})\ge6.075\times10^{-6}$, while the initial whole-past distance $4\eta_0e^{-\lambda_*L}$ is as small as desired.

The same statement holds with $M$ replaced by $M'\le8.7655\times10^{-4}$ of Lemma A5′ at $r_p=0.17$, $\bar\vartheta=0.4165$, giving $\eta_0\ge5.0739\times10^{-5}$ and $d_*\ge2.5050\times10^{-5}$ (optional, self-reviewed).

*Proof.* **Step 1 (coefficient fixed point).** Put $\mathbf c_{\mathcal C}=(\mathbf c,-\mathbf c)$; then $\lVert\eta w\mathbf c_{\mathcal C}\rVert=\eta$ and $\Delta_{\mathcal C}(\lambda_*)\mathbf c_{\mathcal C}=0$ (here $\mathbf c_-=\mathbf c$, $\mathbf c_+=0$). By Lemma A6 the order-one equation of (2.1) holds, and the rest is $\mathbf v=\mathcal T(\mathbf v):=\mathcal R\,\mathcal N(\eta w\mathbf c_{\mathcal C}+\mathbf v)$. For $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(0)}\le\eta$, $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert\le\rho:=2\eta\le r_p/2$, so $(r_p-\rho)^2\ge r_p^2/4$ and Lemma A6 gives $\lVert\mathcal N\rVert\le32M\eta^2/r_p^2$ and Lipschitz constant at most $32M\eta/r_p^2$. Lemma A8 with $k=0$ gives $\lVert\mathcal T(\mathbf v)\rVert_{(0)}\le64M\eta^2/(h_0r_p^2)\le\eta/4$ and contraction factor at most $64M\eta/(h_0r_p^2)\le\frac14$, since $\eta\le h_0r_p^2/(256M)$. The fixed point exists and is unique in the ball. With $k=1,2$: $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(1)}\le128M\eta^2/(h_0r_p^2)\le\eta/2$ and $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(2)}\le256M\eta^2/(h_0r_p^2)\le\eta$. All data are real at real points, so the conjugate of the fixed point is a fixed point in the same ball and equals it. **Step 2 (derivative bounds of the real history, without the equation).** By Lemma A3(e) the history is $C^2$ on $t\le0$; for $t\le0$, $\lvert\mathbf u_r\rvert\le\eta+\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(0)}\le\frac54\eta$ and $\lvert\mathbf u_r'\rvert\le\lambda_*(\eta+\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(1)})\le\frac32\lambda_*\eta$. A member's inertial velocity is $Q(\cdot)[\mathbf u'+\varepsilon J(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u)]$, of size at most $\varepsilon(1+2\eta)+2\lambda_*\eta\le\frac12(1+\frac{4}{16})<1$, using $\eta\le r_p/4<1/16$ and $\lambda_*<\varepsilon\le\frac12$. Distinct members of one rung are $2\lvert\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u\rvert\ge2-3\eta$ apart; members of different rungs are at least $\delta$ apart, since heights are unchanged. **Step 3 (Lemma A2).** With these bounds every source has exactly one causal root at every $t\le0$, with $D\ge1-\bar V>0$, and no member has a self root. **Step 4 (identification of the canonical rows).** At real $w=e^{\lambda_*t}\in(0,1]$ the series $\tau_j+\vartheta_j(w)$ is real and positive (it differs from $\tau_j\ge\tau_{\min}$ by at most $\bar\vartheta<\tau_{\min}$), and the squared equation with the true delayed positions $\mathbf u_{k'}(t-\tau)=\mathbf p_{k'}(we^{-\lambda_*\tau})$ then implies the unsquared causal equation; by Step 3 it is the canonical root. The evaluated $\kappa_j$ is $\tau D=\tau\lvert D\rvert$. The row sum converges absolutely (Lemma A5). **Step 5 (the law).** Evaluating (2.1) at $w=e^{\lambda_*t}$, legitimate because $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(2)}<\infty$, gives (1.1) at every $t\le0$, and Lemma A1 gives the full law. Item 2 follows from $\lvert\mathbf y^{(i)}_r(t)\rvert\le\lambda_*^i\sum_{n\ge2}n^i\lvert\mathbf v_{r,n}\rvert e^{n\lambda_*t}$. **Step 6 (departure).** The reduced equation is autonomous; physically $\mathbf z_L$ is the original history shifted in time and rotated about the axis. For $s\le0$, $\lvert\mathbf u_{r,L}(s)\rvert\le e^{-\lambda_*L}\lVert\mathbf p\rVert\le2\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}$ and $\lvert\mathbf u_{r,L}'(s)\rvert\le\frac32\lambda_*\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}$, so $\mathsf N_0(\mathbf z_L)\le(2+\frac32)\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}\le4\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}$. At time $L$, $\lvert\mathbf u_{0,L}'(L)\rvert=\lvert\mathbf u_0'(0)\rvert\ge\lambda_*(\eta-\lVert\mathbf v\rVert_{(1)})\ge\lambda_*\eta/2$, and every element of $\mathcal O$ has zero rotating-frame velocity. $\blacksquare$

**Physical reading of the departure.** The bound is in the whole-past norm with rotating-frame velocity. The inertial velocity offset from a phase-rotated balance $\mathbf o$ is $Q[\mathbf u'+\varepsilon J(\mathbf u-\mathbf o)]$ and can have cancellations, so the lower bound is not claimed for that quantity alone; a combined position and inertial-velocity distance does keep it, since $\lvert\mathbf u-\mathbf o\rvert+\varepsilon^{-1}\lvert\mathbf u'+\varepsilon J(\mathbf u-\mathbf o)\rvert\ge\varepsilon^{-1}\lvert\mathbf u'\rvert$. The amplitude $\eta_0$ is dimensionless; in physical lengths the position scale is $R\eta_0\ge1.23\times10^{-2}$ (units $K/c_f^2$).

## 7. What is not shown, and falsifiers

**Not shown.** Anything outside $\mathcal C$; any ordering of $\lambda_*$ among the characteristic roots (faster roots that are not resonances $n\lambda_*$ do not obstruct this construction); an unstable manifold or all departing histories; uniqueness outside the coefficient ball; anything after the departure time or about the eventual fate; finite segments; other radii; optimality of $\eta_0$; a direct second-derivative sharpening of Lemma A6.

**Falsifiers.**
- A subwake history with positive separation in which a source has two causal roots, or a member has a positive-delay self root, would refute Lemma A2.
- A real $\mu\ge\mu_0$ at which a block is singular although B3 holds with $\bar b_0\ge\varepsilon^2$ would refute Lemma A8; `class-spectrum-float-N20000.json` holds float inverse norms on that half-line for comparison.
- A nonvanishing in-plane to axial entry of $\Delta(\mu;q,+1)$ at $q\in\{0,\pi\}$ would refute the block reduction of Lemma A7.
- Failure of R1, R2, the interval checks of Section 5, or the unit cross-check under a rerun of the scripts on the same receipt would void the certified constants.
- A first-variation discrepancy between the subject matrix and independently solved perturbed rows that persists as the perturbation amplitude decreases (Codex's `packet-3-first-variation.py` records falling discrepancies down to $10^{-10}$) would refute Lemma A7's input.
- Operator-checkable consistency test, not evidence: computing $\mathbf v_n$ for $n\le N$ by $\mathbf v_n=\Delta_{\mathcal C}(n\lambda_*)^{-1}[\mathcal N(\mathbf p)]_n$ and evaluating the exact law on the truncated history at $t\le0$ should give a residual falling like $\eta^{N+1}e^{(N+1)\lambda_*t}$.
