# The localized lattice family beyond its evolved-history endpoint: the seed's forward envelope, a later barrier, and the signed structure of the limiting term (2026-10-14)

## Result

**In words.** The [evolved-history note](saturday-localized-evolved-history-continuation-2026-10-12.md) followed the localized disturbance of the alternating lattice, together with its complete nonlinear past, to a time $T_E(\varepsilon)$ at which the linear seed's size bound reaches $0.018$ of a lattice spacing. It did so by showing that the actual nonlinear correction cannot cross an explicit barrier. Its estimate bounds the seed at every delayed source by the seed's upper envelope for times before the origin, which falls into the past at the slow rate $a$. This note adds two things. First, for times after the origin the accepted upper envelope rises at the fast rate $\lambda_+=a+0.2$; measured against the seed-size bound $\Delta(t)$, which itself rises at a rate above $\lambda_+$, the envelope at a delayed source, which is always seen at least $1-2\rho$ time units back, is smaller than the earlier bound by the factor $\mu=e^{-0.2(1-2\rho)}$, about $16$ percent smaller. This is a statement about the upper envelope, not about the seed's actual rate of growth, which is a superposition of rates between $a$ and $\lambda_+$. With that single change the same barrier argument continues the same solution to seed size $0.0211$, a further guaranteed time of at least $0.0548$. Second, the term that limits the present absolute-value estimate, the correction at a member multiplied by the seed at its delayed sources, has a sign structure that the absolute-value bound discards. The coefficient tensors of all second-order terms are odd under reflection of the source offset, while the seed is a smooth envelope times the alternating sign pattern. Pairing each source with its mirror image across the receiver therefore cancels these terms except for the envelope's variation across the pair and a remainder of third order. An orientation evaluation, which omits the source-difference Lipschitz factors and the third-order remainder, puts the receiver-source part of the limiting coefficient at about $1$ times the seed size after pairing, against about $17.4$ before, on the declared chart; it is not a bound on the assembled barrier polynomial. What is not yet done is an explicit, uniformly summed bound on that third-order remainder and its assembly with all other terms; it is stated precisely as the remaining estimate.

1. **The seed's forward envelope (derived).** At every time $t\ge1-2\rho$ after the seed's origin, the seed at a source sampled at least $1-2\rho$ time units in the past is bounded by the factor $\mu=e^{-0.2(1-2\rho)}$ times the earlier bound, where $0.2$ is exactly the width of the contour's real interval of growth rates and $\rho$ is the chart radius (Lemma S.1). The proof uses only the accepted upper envelope.
2. **A later barrier for the same solution (derived; numbers by exact arithmetic on declared values).** For every amplitude $0<\varepsilon\le6.46\times10^{-3}$ the continued history exists at least to
   $$
   T_S(\varepsilon)=\frac1{2.8953}\ln\frac{0.0211}{0.1144\,\varepsilon}\ \ge\ T_E(\varepsilon)+0.0548,
   $$
   and for $T_E\le T\le T_S$ it differs from the linear seed by less than $32\,\Delta(T)^2$, where $\Delta(T)=0.1144\,\varepsilon\,e^{2.8953T}\le0.0211$. Over the whole of $(-\infty,T_S]$ every displacement is at most $0.035347$, every speed at most $0.13367$, every separation at least $0.929306$, every transmitter factor at least $0.86633$; each ordered pair of members has exactly one causal root and no member has one on its own path (Theorem S.2). For $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$: $T_S\ge2.597$, against $T_E\le2.543$.
3. **The limiting term has a sign structure (derived).** For each receiver the sources at offsets $\ell$ and $-\ell$ combine into an exact sum of three parts: two differences in the source histories, whose seed part is only the envelope's variation across the pair, at most $r/20$ of the seed bound for a source at distance $r$; and the odd part of a single row, whose Taylor expansion along a smooth pure-seed amplitude line has no constant, linear or quadratic term (Proposition S.4). The variation bound comes from the seed's Fourier integral over wavevectors within $1/20$ of the staggered wavevector (Lemma S.3).
4. **The remaining estimate (stated, not proved).** An explicit bound on the odd part of a row, uniform and summed over the sources, which needs third derivatives of the row, a second-order expansion of the causal delay and the seed's third time derivative (Section 5), followed by its assembly with the source-difference Lipschitz terms and all other terms and a check at a declared later endpoint. Whether that gives a barrier beyond $T_S$, and by how much, is not known; no such barrier is claimed.
5. **What is not obtained.** The central-member growth lower bound is empty at $T_S$, as it was at $T_E$: the solution is shown to exist and stay small, not to keep growing. No ceiling of any estimate, order-one evolution, physical ending, wake-speed arrival or growth uniform in $\varepsilon$ is established.

Claim grade: derived for items 1 to 3, by the hand arguments below, with the numbers of item 2 checked by a session instrument in exact rational arithmetic after known cases; item 4 is a stated open estimate and its payoff an orientation figure, not a bound. The lattice sums and the contour and seed constants are the declared values of the accepted notes and keep their interval-library conditionality. Nothing is measured on solutions: no history, correction or evolution is computed. Falsifiers are listed in Section 8.

Everything is for the unchanged Master Equation at $g=16$, unit spacing, $c_f=1$, the full alternating population with every member dynamical, every causal root retained, the original eight-source block summation, the contour of radius $1/10$, the accepted normalized seed class, and the complete-past family $u^\varepsilon=\varepsilon\phi+v^\varepsilon$. A run started at a finite time from the linear seed, a fixed exterior, a finite population or a copied coherent trajectory is a different preparation.

## 2. The seed's forward profile

The seed $\phi$ is a superposition of modes with growth rates between $a$ and $\lambda_+$. The accepted seed bound (quantification note, Proposition Q6(3)) reads $\lvert\phi_j^{(n)}(s)\rvert\le a^n\bar\Lambda_0e^{as}$ for $s\le0$ and $\le a^n\bar\Lambda_0e^{\lambda_+s}$ for $s\ge0$. The evolved-history estimate used, at every source, a bound equivalent to the first form continued past the origin, an envelope falling into the past at the slow rate $a$ throughout. For times after the origin the accepted envelope is the second form, which rises at most at the fast rate $\lambda_+$. Measured against $\Delta(t)$, which rises at the rate $2.8953>\lambda_+$, the second form is smaller at every source time at least $1-2\rho$ before $t$. Both forms are upper envelopes: the seed is a superposition of modes with rates between $a$ and $\lambda_+$, and nothing here asserts that its actual growth or decay proceeds at either rate.

**Lemma S.1 (derived).** Let $0<\rho\le1/16$ and $t\ge1-2\rho$, and put $\mu=e^{-0.2(1-2\rho)}$. For every real seed $\phi$ of the accepted class, every member $j$, every $T'\le-(1-2\rho)$ and $n=0,1,2$,

$$
\lvert\varepsilon\phi_j^{(n)}(t+T')\rvert\le a^n\,\mu\,\Delta(t)\,e^{aT'} .
$$

*Proof.* If $t+T'\ge0$, the accepted bound gives $\lvert\varepsilon\phi^{(n)}_j(t+T')\rvert\le a^n\varepsilon\bar\Lambda_0e^{\lambda_+(t+T')}$. Since $\lambda_+<2.8953$, $e^{\lambda_+t}\le e^{2.8953t}$, and since $\lambda_+=a+0.2$, $e^{\lambda_+T'}=e^{aT'}e^{0.2T'}\le e^{aT'}e^{-0.2(1-2\rho)}$. If $t+T'<0$, the bound gives $a^n\varepsilon\bar\Lambda_0e^{a(t+T')}=a^n\Delta(t)e^{aT'}e^{(a-2.8953)t}$, and $a-2.8953<\lambda_+-0.2-2.8953<-0.2$, so the last factor is below $e^{-0.2t}\le e^{-0.2(1-2\rho)}$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

In Lemma E.2 every source is sampled within $2\rho$ of the time $T-r$, with $T\le0$ and $r\ge1$, so at a relative time $T'\le-(1-2\rho)$. The receiver is sampled at $T$, which may be $0$, and keeps the old bound. Inserting Lemma S.1 into the proof of Lemma E.2 multiplies the seed at every source by $\mu$ and changes nothing else:

**Corollary S.1a (derived).** Under the hypotheses of Lemma E.2 with, in addition, the seed part at every source time $T'\le-(1-2\rho)$ bounded by $a^n\mu\,d\,e^{aT'}$, $\lVert\mathcal N[\psi+v]\rVert_{0,2a}\le F_\mu(d,y)$, where $F_\mu$ is $F$ with $d\eta S_p(a)$ replaced by $d\mu\eta S_p(a)$ in the receiver-source products and with $\bar p=d\mu+y\mathsf s_1$, $\bar v=d\mu+2y\mathsf s_1$, $\bar w=d\mu+4y\mathsf s_1$; the receiver factor $A=d+y$ and the cubic receiver term are unchanged. $F_\mu$ is a polynomial in $(d,y)$ with nonnegative coefficients and no term of degree below two, and $F_1=F$.

*Proof.* In the proof of Lemma E.2 the source factors are $\mathfrak p=X(d\,\mathsf s+yX\mathsf s^2)$ and its two derivatives, where the term $d\,\mathsf s$ comes from the seed at the source; it becomes $d\mu\,\mathsf s$. Every other step is unchanged. $\qquad\blacksquare$

## 3. A later barrier

**Theorem S.2 (derived, relying on the accepted notes).** Let $\rho_3=1/20$, $\delta_3=0.0211$, $K_3=32$, $y_3=K_3\delta_3^2=0.01424672$, $\mu\le0.83528$, and let $F_\mu$ be the polynomial of Corollary S.1a for the chart $\rho_3$, whose constants are $\bar a\rho_3=0.134765$, $\kappa=0.9$, $\eta\le1.30935$, $\mathsf s_1\le0.08842$, $c_{20}\le8.3344$, $c_{11}\le4.032$, $c_{02}\le3.0877$, $c_{E3}\le53.672$. Then, in exact arithmetic on these declared values,

$$
\text{(a)}\ \ \delta_3+2y_3=0.04959344\le\rho_3,\qquad
\text{(b)}\ \ C_LF_\mu(\delta_3,y_3)\le0.0142448<y_3,\qquad
\text{(c)}\ \ \delta_2+2K_2\delta_2^2=0.036144\le\rho_3\ \text{and}\ K_3\ge K_2 .
$$

Consequently, for every $0<\varepsilon\le6.46\times10^{-3}$, with $T_S(\varepsilon)=\ln(\delta_3/(\bar\Lambda_0\varepsilon))/2.8953\ge T_E(\varepsilon)+0.0548$:

1. the unique charted continuation of $u^\varepsilon$ exists on $(-\infty,T_S]$; it is the same solution, with the same past, and every emission it receives lies on it;
2. for $T_E\le T\le T_S$ and every member, $\lvert u_j(T)-\varepsilon\phi_j(T)\rvert<32\,\Delta(T)^2$, and the first two derivatives of the difference are below $2a$ and $(2a)^2$ times that;
3. on the whole of $(-\infty,T_S]$: displacements are at most $0.035347$, speeds at most $0.13367$, accelerations at most $0.56728$, transmitter factors at least $0.86633$, separations at least $0.929306$, and each delay is within $0.070694$ of the anchor distance; each ordered pair of distinct members has exactly one causal root at every reception time, and no member has a root on its own path;
4. the disturbance has finite spatially weighted size for every weight exponent at each time up to $T_S$ (forward note, Proposition 4; no constant is evaluated).

*Proof.* The argument is that of Theorem E.3, started at $T_E$ instead of $T_B$. *Validity of Lemma S.1 on the interval.* $T_E(\varepsilon)\ge T_E(6.46\times10^{-3})=\ln(0.018/(0.1144\times0.00646))/2.8953>1.1>1-2\rho_3$. *Scaling.* $F_\mu(d,K_3d^2)/d^2$ is nondecreasing in $d$, because every monomial of $F_\mu$ has total degree at least two and nonnegative coefficient; so (b) gives $C_LF_\mu(d,K_3d^2)<K_3d^2$ for every $0<d\le\delta_3$. *The inequality along the solution.* At a time $t\ge T_E$ up to which the solution exists with $\Delta(t)+2y(t)\le\rho_3$, Lemma E.1, Lemma S.1 and Corollary S.1a give $y(t)\le C_LF_\mu(\Delta(t),y(t))$; Lemma E.1's pointwise chart holds because $\rho_3\le1/16$ and $\bar a\rho_3\le3/16$. *Start.* By Theorem E.3, $y(T_E)<K_2\delta_2^2\le K_3\Delta(T_E)^2$, and $\Delta(T_E)+2y(T_E)\le0.036144\le\rho_3$ by (c). *Barrier.* Let $\mathcal T$ be the set of $t\in[T_E,T_S]$ with $t<T_{\max}$ and $y(s)<K_3\Delta(s)^2$ on $[T_E,t]$. On $\mathcal T$ the solution lies in the chart of radius $\delta_3+y_3$ with speeds at most $\bar a(\delta_3+2y_3)$ and bounded accelerations, so by the forward note's remark on maximal continuation the supremum $t_0$ of $\mathcal T$ is attained with $t_0<T_{\max}$; by continuity $y(t_0)\le K_3\Delta(t_0)^2$, so $(*)$ holds there, and equality would give $K_3\Delta^2\le C_LF_\mu(\Delta,K_3\Delta^2)<K_3\Delta^2$. So the strict inequality persists past $t_0$ unless $t_0=T_S$, and $T_{\max}>T_S$. *Items.* As in Theorem E.3: displacement at most $\Delta+y\le\delta_3+y_3$, speed at most $\bar a(\Delta+2y)$, acceleration at most $\bar a^2(\Delta+4y)$, separations at least $1-2(\delta_3+y_3)$, transmitter factor at least $1-\bar a(\delta_3+2y_3)$, delay within $2(\delta_3+y_3)$ of the anchor distance; before $T_E$ the evolved-history note's smaller bounds hold. The gain is $\ln(\delta_3/\delta_2)/2.8953\ge0.0548$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

*Growth.* The central-member lower bound $\varepsilon p_0e^{aT}-K_3\Delta(T)^2$ is not positive at $T_S$ for $\varepsilon=10^{-3},\dots,10^{-6}$ (instrument table), as at $T_E$. The theorem gives existence and upper bounds, not continued growth.

*What kind of result this is.* It continues the same solution beyond $T_E$ by a quantified interval, using the accepted upper envelope of the seed over the elapsed time, where the previous estimate used the envelope that falls at the slow rate $a$. The gain is modest, $0.0548$ in time and $17$ percent in seed size, because the factor $\mu$ acts only on the seed at the sources while the correction coefficient $K_3=32$ grows.

## 4. The signed structure of the limiting term

The evolved-history note identified the term that limits its barrier: the part of $C_LF$ linear in the correction, whose leading coefficient on its chart is about $17.7$ times the seed size, of which about $15.1$ is the correction at a receiver multiplied by the seed at the sources. On the chart $1/20$ the same receiver-correction, source-seed coefficient is about $17.4$ times the seed size (float evaluation, orientation only). Lemma E.2 bounds it source by source in absolute value. This section shows that the sources cancel this term in pairs, up to the seed's spatial variation and a third-order remainder.

**Lemma S.3 (mirror symmetry of a row and the seed's envelope; derived).** (i) For a receiver displacement $\beta$ and a source history $Y$ let

$$
f_\ell(\beta,Y)=\Psi\bigl(\ell+\beta-Y(T-\tau),\,Y'(T-\tau)\bigr)-\Psi(\ell+\beta,0)-T_\ell\,Y(T-r)-B_\ell\,Y'(T-r),\qquad\tau=\lvert\ell+\beta-Y(T-\tau)\rvert,
$$

with $T_\ell=(3nn^{\mathsf T}-I)/r^3$ and $B_\ell=nn^{\mathsf T}/r^2$, so that the remainder of the equation is $\mathcal N_i=g\,E(u_i)+g\sum_{\ell\ne0}\sigma_\ell f_\ell(u_i(T),u_{i-\ell})$. Then $f_{-\ell}(\beta,Y)=-f_\ell(-\beta,-Y)$, $f_\ell(\beta,0)=0$ for every $\beta$, and $\sigma_{-\ell}=\sigma_\ell$.

(ii) The seed has the form $\varepsilon\phi_j(T)=\sigma_j\,\psi(j,T)$, with $\psi(x,T)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int_{\lvert q\rvert\le1/20}e^{iq\cdot x}\,\varepsilon\hat\phi(Q+q,T)\,dq$ in the local torus coordinate $q=k-Q$. For every real $x,x'$ and $n=0,1,2,3$,
$$
\lvert\partial_T^n\psi(x,T)-\partial_T^n\psi(x',T)\rvert\le\tfrac1{20}\lvert x-x'\rvert\,M_n(T),\qquad M_n(T)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int\lvert\partial_T^n\varepsilon\hat\phi(k,T)\rvert\,dk,
$$
and $\sup_z\lvert\partial_T^n\psi(z,T)\rvert\le M_n(T)$. For $n\le2$, $M_n$ obeys the accepted seed bound of Proposition Q6(3) with the constant $\bar\Lambda_0=0.1144$. For $n=3$ the same proof gives the constant $\bar\Lambda_3=(\varrho_\Gamma/m_F)(\lambda_+/a)^3\le\bar\Lambda_0\,(\lambda_+/a)\le0.1144\times2.8953/2.6952<0.1229$, a factor at most $1.0743$ relative to the two-derivative constant.

*Proof.* (i) $\Psi(-R,-v)=-\Psi(R,v)$. The root equation for $(-\ell,\beta,Y)$ is $\tau=\lvert\ell-\beta+Y(T-\tau)\rvert$, the root equation for $(\ell,-\beta,-Y)$. $T_\ell$ and $B_\ell$ are even in $\ell$. So $f_{-\ell}(\beta,Y)=-\Psi(\ell-\beta+Y(s),-Y'(s))+\Psi(\ell-\beta,0)-T_\ell Y(T-r)-B_\ell Y'(T-r)=-f_\ell(-\beta,-Y)$. With $Y=0$ the root is $r'=\lvert\ell+\beta\rvert$ and $f_\ell(\beta,0)=\Psi(\ell+\beta,0)-\Psi(\ell+\beta,0)=0$. (ii) The seed is $\varepsilon\phi_j(T)=(2\pi)^{-3}\int e^{ik\cdot j}\varepsilon\hat\phi(k,T)\,dk$ with $\hat\phi(\cdot,T)$ supported in $\lvert k-Q\rvert\le1/20$, $Q=(\pi,\pi,\pi)$, and $e^{iQ\cdot j}=\sigma_j$; so in the local coordinate $q=k-Q$ on the supported ball, $\psi$ is the displayed integral and agrees with the seed's envelope at lattice points. For $\lvert q\rvert\le1/20$, $\lvert e^{iq\cdot x}-e^{iq\cdot x'}\rvert\le\lvert q\rvert\,\lvert x-x'\rvert\le\lvert x-x'\rvert/20$; integrating against $\lvert\partial_T^n\varepsilon\hat\phi\rvert$ gives the difference bound, and dropping the difference gives $\sup_z\lvert\partial_T^n\psi\rvert\le M_n$. The quantification note's proof of Proposition Q6(3) bounds $M_n$ for $n\le2$: $\lvert\partial_T^n\hat\phi(k,T+t)\rvert\le\varrho_\Gamma\lambda_+^ne^{aT}e^{\lambda_+t}b(k)/m_F$ and $\varrho_\Gamma(\lambda_+/a)^2/m_F\le\bar\Lambda_0$. For $n=3$ the same integral carries $\lambda_+^3$, so the constant is $\varrho_\Gamma(\lambda_+/a)^3/m_F\le\bar\Lambda_0(\lambda_+/a)$, and $\lambda_+/a<2.8953/2.6952<1.0743$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

**Proposition S.4 (exact pair decomposition; derived).** Fix a receiver $i$ and a reception time, and pair each offset $\ell$ with $-\ell$. Write the two source histories as $u_{i\mp\ell}=\Phi_{i\mp\ell}+c_{i\mp\ell}$, seed plus correction, and put $\sigma'=\sigma_{i-\ell}=\sigma_{i+\ell}$, $\bar\Phi=\tfrac12\sigma'[\psi(i-\ell,\cdot)+\psi(i+\ell,\cdot)]$, $D_\ell=\tfrac12\sigma'[\psi(i-\ell,\cdot)-\psi(i+\ell,\cdot)]$, so that $\Phi_{i\mp\ell}=\bar\Phi\pm D_\ell$. With $\alpha=u_i(T)$, the contribution of the pair to $\mathcal N_i/g$ is exactly

$$
\sigma_\ell\Bigl\{\bigl[f_\ell(\alpha,\bar\Phi+D_\ell+c_{i-\ell})-f_\ell(\alpha,\bar\Phi)\bigr]-\bigl[f_\ell(-\alpha,-\bar\Phi+D_\ell-c_{i+\ell})-f_\ell(-\alpha,-\bar\Phi)\bigr]+\bigl[f_\ell(\alpha,\bar\Phi)-f_\ell(-\alpha,-\bar\Phi)\bigr]\Bigr\},
$$

and $\lvert D_\ell^{(n)}\rvert\le\tfrac r{20}M_n$ at every time, with $M_n$ as in Lemma S.3(ii): the accepted seed bound for $n\le2$, and that bound times $\lambda_+/a\le1.0743$ for $n=3$.

*Proof.* The pair contributes $\sigma_\ell[f_\ell(\alpha,u_{i-\ell})+f_{-\ell}(\alpha,u_{i+\ell})]=\sigma_\ell[f_\ell(\alpha,\bar\Phi+D_\ell+c_{i-\ell})-f_\ell(-\alpha,-\bar\Phi+D_\ell-c_{i+\ell})]$ by Lemma S.3(i). Add and subtract $f_\ell(\alpha,\bar\Phi)$ and $f_\ell(-\alpha,-\bar\Phi)$. The bound on $D_\ell$ is Lemma S.3(ii) with $\lvert(i-\ell)-(i+\ell)\rvert=2r$. $\qquad\blacksquare$

*What the decomposition does.* The first two brackets are differences between two source histories at a fixed receiver displacement. They are bounded exactly as in the accepted Lipschitz estimate (quantification note, Proposition Q4, with the receiver displacement held fixed), by a coefficient that is first order in $\lvert\alpha\rvert$ and the source size, times the size of the history difference. That difference is $D_\ell$ plus a correction; its seed part $D_\ell$ is at most $r/20$ of the seed bound. So in the products of the receiver displacement with the seed at the sources, which carry the limiting coefficient, the sum $\sum_\ell e^{-ar}/r^p$ is replaced by $\tfrac1{20}\sum_\ell e^{-ar}/r^{p-1}$. For orientation only: on the chart $1/20$ the float expression $C_Lg\eta\mu[c_{20}\kappa^{-4}S_3(a)+(\bar ac_{11}\kappa^{-3}+2\bar a)S_2(a)+\bar a^2S_1(a)]/20$, with $S_1(a)\le0.69831$ from the quantification note, is about $0.81$ times the seed size, or about $0.98$ without $\mu$, against about $17.4$ for the corresponding unpaired expression. This omits the extra factors of the Lipschitz form of the brackets and the whole third bracket. It proves neither an eighteenfold reduction of the assembled barrier polynomial nor which condition would then limit the barrier. The products of two seed factors at the sources are reduced in the same way. The products involving the correction at the sources are not reduced; they are the second line of the evolved-history note's $B_2$ and its $C_2$, about $2.6$ times the seed size on that chart. The third bracket is the odd part of one row with a pure-seed source. Along the amplitude line $s\mapsto(s\alpha,s\bar\Phi)$, for fixed smooth pure-seed data on the regular chart, the root and the row are smooth in $s$; $f_\ell$ vanishes at $s=0$; its first $s$-derivative there, $-T_\ell(\alpha-\bar\Phi(T-r))+B_\ell\bar\Phi'(T-r)$ from the row, is removed exactly by the receiver and linear-source subtractions; and its second-order term is even under $s\mapsto-s$. So the odd part starts at third order locally along that line. This is a local Taylor statement, not a uniform bound summed over the sources.

The mechanism, in words: every second-order effect of moving one member relative to another reverses when the source is reflected through the receiver, because the derivatives of the row at rest are odd in the offset. The seed is the alternating pattern times a slowly varying envelope, so the source and its mirror image carry nearly the same seed with the same sign pattern, and their second-order effects nearly cancel. What survives is the envelope's change across the pair and the next order. The absolute-value bound adds the two instead.

## 5. The remaining estimate

To turn Proposition S.4 into a barrier, one more bound is needed, followed by its assembly with the source-difference Lipschitz terms and all other terms and a check at a declared later endpoint. The bound: for a pure-seed source path $\bar\Phi$ with $\lvert\bar\Phi^{(n)}(T')\rvert\le a^n\mu\,d\,e^{aT'}$ for $n\le2$ and $\le1.0743\,a^3\mu\,d\,e^{aT'}$ for $n=3$ (Lemma S.3(ii)), and $\lvert\alpha\rvert\le(d+y)e^{aT}$,

$$
\sum_{\ell}\bigl\lvert f_\ell(\alpha,\bar\Phi)-f_\ell(-\alpha,-\bar\Phi)\bigr\rvert\le e^{2aT}\,\mathcal O(d,y),
$$

with $\mathcal O$ an explicit polynomial of degree three in $(d,y)$ with nonnegative coefficients and every monomial containing $d$. The route is known and was not completed in this checkpoint. Write $f_\ell=\mathcal F(\alpha,p,v)+T_\ell[Y(s)-Y(T-r)]+B_\ell[Y'(s)-Y'(T-r)]$ with $\mathcal F(\alpha,p,v)=\Psi(\ell+\alpha-p,v)-\Psi(\ell+\alpha,0)-T_\ell p-B_\ell v$ and $(p,v)$ the sampled source values. Since $\mathcal F(\alpha,0,0)=0$, the odd part of $\mathcal F$ is an integral over the source factor of a central second difference of $\partial\Psi$, which needs the third derivatives of $\Psi$ on the chart. The two roots of $(\alpha,\bar\Phi)$ and $(-\alpha,-\bar\Phi)$ differ to first order, but the sum of their delay shifts is of second order, so the delay brackets combine into second differences in time; their remainder needs the seed's third time derivative. Every term so obtained has three small factors, at least one of them a source factor carrying $e^{-ar}$, so the sum over $\ell$ converges with lattice sums $S_p(a)$. The pure-receiver cubic is not part of this bound; it is the separately bounded $E$. The local Taylor statement of Section 4 shows that the odd part starts at third order along each smooth amplitude line; it does not supply the uniform constants or their sum over the sources, which are what $\mathcal O$ requires.
