## Release symmetry, periodic motion and static derivatives

For the rigid seed, same-sublattice positions and velocities initially agree up to translation. Let $\lambda_A$ be its normalized release acceleration split. Taylor expansion then gives $\lambda_{\rm sym}(T)=\lambda_A T^2/2+O(T^3)$ while the histories and roots are regular. The recorded $N=2$ split near $0.2556023$ predicts the leading leakage near $1.27801\times10^{-5}$ at $T=0.01$; the recorded value is $1.277705\times10^{-5}$. This is a comparison of retained values, not an independent evolution reference. The two observations reject the same finite symmetry preparation at different levels; their agreement is scientifically informative but is not evidence of two unrelated failure mechanisms.

In a well-defined translation-covariant periodic model, same-sublattice translations give the same acceleration identically. A symmetry-split test is then automatically satisfied. The prescribed circle still needs its mean acceleration to equal $-\rho\omega^2$ times its radial unit direction, with complete received history and a proved sum. No periodic orbit follows merely from translation symmetry.

For stationary sources away from coincidence, the receiver derivative of each signed inverse-square vector row is proportional to $(I-3nn^{\mathsf T})/r^3$, with trace zero. Any finite sum has trace zero. An infinite differentiated sum has the same property only when its declared convergence justifies that derivative. A symmetric matrix with trace zero cannot have all eigenvalues strictly negative, so it cannot provide strict restoring acceleration in every receiver direction. Static defects can change directional responses without escaping this constraint. Motion-dependent histories and moving retained assemblies remain possible routes requiring their own derivation. This statement is derived from the current kernel; the classical electrostatic comparison is not used as a premise.

For nondecaying periodically moving populations, the ancient branch's exponential-past convergence proof is unavailable. Its linear velocity terms scale as delayed velocity over $r^2$; their absolute sum diverges for a sustained common oscillatory amplitude. That fact and a nonconvergent continuum radial integral do not determine conditional convergence of the exact discrete signed sum. A prescribed periodic image operator must state and prove its own summation, derivative and exhaustion properties. No replacement Abel summation is selected by this integration.
