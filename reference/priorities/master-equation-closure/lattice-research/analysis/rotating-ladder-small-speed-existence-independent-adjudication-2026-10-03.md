# Independent adjudication of the small-speed existence proof for the rotating alternating ladder

**Verdict: accept with corrections.** The theorem of the [subject](rotating-ladder-small-speed-existence.md) is proved as stated, in all four parts: for the law written in its Assumptions section, and for every compact spacing window $I$ containing $\delta_*$, there is a positive speed $\varepsilon_0(I)$ below which the rigidly rotating alternating ladder satisfies the delayed acceleration law exactly at exactly one spacing ratio $\delta(\varepsilon)\in I$, with $\delta$ continuously differentiable on $[0,\varepsilon_0)$, $\delta(0)=\delta_*$, $\delta'(0)=0$ and $\delta(\varepsilon)=\delta_*-\varepsilon^2/(6\beta_*)+O(\varepsilon^3)$. Every lemma and every inequality was rechecked by hand against a reference written before the proofs were read, and no gap was found in the argument. One stated decimal is wrong in its last digit: $1/(6\beta_*)$ is $1.277966$, not $1.277967$. One expository sentence about which estimates the proof uses does not match Section 9 as written. Neither affects the theorem. The scope is small speed only, with no numerical value for $\varepsilon_0$, within the family of rigid ladders whose two rails share one radius and one speed, and it carries no statement about stability.

**Claim grade.** Derived, for the acceptance of the theorem: the adjudicator reconstructed the causal-root lemma, the reduction to two scalar conditions, the exact identity for the tangential sum, the alternating-sum bound and the first-order bracket independently, and then verified each step of the subject's proof line by line. This is a hand check by one adjudicator, not a machine-checked proof. Measured, for every decimal quoted below: by the adjudicator's own three-vector evaluator in float arithmetic and in 50-digit arithmetic, with $c_f=1$ and $K=1$, validated first on a stationary source and on a uniformly moving source; these measurements establish agreement at the sampled points and nothing about other points. Inferred, for the sharper form of the tangential sum discussed in the last numerical item; the adjudication adds measurements in its favour and no proof.

## Notation

Units are $c_f=1$ and $K=1$. A ladder has pair radius $R$, rung spacing $d$ and angular rate $\omega$; $\delta=d/R$ is the spacing ratio and $\varepsilon=\omega R$ the member speed. After scaling lengths and times by $R$, the receiver is the positive member of rung $0$ at $\mathbf x=(1,0,0)$ at time $0$, and the source on rail $\nu=\pm1$ at rung $k$ has path $(\nu\cos\varepsilon t,\nu\sin\varepsilon t,k\delta)$ and polarity product $\sigma=\nu(-1)^k$ with the receiver. The delay of a source is $\tau$, fixed by $\tau=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(-\tau)\rvert$ with $\tau>0$; the delay angle is $\theta=\varepsilon\tau$. The source contributes the acceleration $\sigma(\mathbf x-\mathbf X)/(\tau^3\lvert1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V\rvert)$ with $\mathbf n=(\mathbf x-\mathbf X)/\tau$ and the source position $\mathbf X$ and velocity $\mathbf V$ taken at time $-\tau$. The elementary functions are $p(\theta)=\sin\theta/\theta$, $q(\theta)=p(\theta/2)^2$, $\varphi=(p-1)/\theta^2$ and $\chi=(1-q)/\theta^2$. $G(\delta,\varepsilon)$ is the tangential acceleration of the receiver divided by $\varepsilon$, $S(\delta,\varepsilon)$ is its inward radial acceleration, $B(\delta)=G(\delta,0)$, $y=2\pi/\delta$, $y_*$ is the positive root of $y\sinh y=6$, $\delta_*=2\pi/y_*$ and $\beta_*=B'(\delta_*)$.

## Pre-target reference

This section was written before Sections 1 to 10 of the subject were read. At that point the adjudicator had read only the subject's Result and Assumptions sections, which state the theorem, the value of $\beta_*$ and a two-paragraph outline of the method, and the commissioning brief, which named the identity $G=B+\varepsilon^2E$ and the bound $\lvert E-\tfrac16\rvert\le C\varepsilon$. The reference was therefore blind to the formula for the kernel, to every lemma statement and to every constant, but not to the overall strategy. The working copy, with its file time of 18:33 EDT on 2026-10-03, is retained as `pre-target-reference.md` in the directory named under What was run.

**(a) Causal root (derived).** Put $f(\tau)=\tau-\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(-\tau)\rvert$. Then $f(0)\le0$, $f'\ge1-\varepsilon>0$ because the source speed is $\varepsilon<1$, and $f\to\infty$; so there is exactly one root $\tau\ge0$, and it is positive unless the source is the receiver itself, whose only root $\tau=0$ is excluded by $\tau>0$. The squared distance is $2-2\nu\cos\theta+k^2\delta^2$, and $2-2\cos\theta=\theta^2q(\theta)$, so the root satisfies $\tau^2(1-\varepsilon^2q)=k^2\delta^2$ on the receiver's rail and $\tau^2(1+\varepsilon^2q)=4+k^2\delta^2$ on the other rail. The $\tau$-derivative of $\tau^2-2+2\nu\cos(\varepsilon\tau)-k^2\delta^2$ is $2\tau(1-\nu\varepsilon^2p(\theta))\ge2\tau(1-\varepsilon^2)>0$, so by the analytic implicit-function theorem $\tau$ is real-analytic in $(\delta,\varepsilon)$ for $\lvert\varepsilon\rvert<1$ and $\delta>0$, and even in $\varepsilon$. The factor $1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V$ equals $D_\nu=1-\nu\varepsilon^2p(\theta)\in[1-\varepsilon^2,1+\varepsilon^2]$. The bounds are $\lvert k\rvert\delta\le\tau_+\le\lvert k\rvert\delta/\sqrt{1-\varepsilon^2}$ and $\sqrt{(4+k^2\delta^2)/(1+\varepsilon^2)}\le\tau_-\le\sqrt{4+k^2\delta^2}$, and on the receiver's rail $\partial_\delta\theta=\theta(1-\varepsilon^2q)/(\delta(1-\varepsilon^2p))$.

**(b) Uniform bounds on each rung (derived).** The radial, tangential and axial components of one row are $\sigma(1-\nu\cos\theta)/(\tau^3D_\nu)$, $\sigma\nu\sin\theta/(\tau^3D_\nu)$ and $-\sigma k\delta/(\tau^3D_\nu)$, and $\sigma\nu=(-1)^k$ on both rails. The tangential row divided by $\varepsilon$ is $(-1)^kp/(\tau^2D_\nu)$, of size at most $1/(k^2\delta^2(1-\varepsilon^2))$ on the receiver's rail and $(1+\varepsilon^2)/((4+k^2\delta^2)(1-\varepsilon^2))$ on the other: a bound $C/(1+k^2)$ uniform in $0\le\varepsilon\le\tfrac12$ and $\delta\ge\delta_a$. For the $\delta$-derivative write the own-rail row as $T=g(\theta)/(k^2\delta^2)$ with $g=p(1-\varepsilon^2q)/(1-\varepsilon^2p)$; then $\partial_\delta T=-2T/\delta+g'(\theta)\,\partial_\delta\theta/(k^2\delta^2)$, and $\theta g'(\theta)$ is bounded because $\theta p'=\cos\theta-p$ and $\theta q'$ are bounded, so $\lvert\partial_\delta T\rvert\le C/k^2$ uniformly, and the other rail is the same. Hence $G$ and $\partial_\delta G$ are continuous on $I\times[0,\tfrac12]$, zero speed included, by dominated convergence. The radial rows are $\sigma\varepsilon^2q/(2\tau D_+)$ on the receiver's rail, bounded by $C/\lvert k\rvert^3$ uniformly in $\varepsilon$ because $q\le\min(1,4/\theta^2)$, and $-(-1)^k(2-\varepsilon^2\tau^2q/2)/(\tau^3D_-)$ on the other, bounded by $C/(1+\lvert k\rvert^3)$. The axial rows of $k$ and $-k$ cancel exactly because the root depends on $k^2$, and they are absolutely summable.

**(c) The limit and its slope (derived).** $B(\delta)=\sum_{k\ne0}(-1)^k/(k^2\delta^2)+\sum_k(-1)^k/(4+k^2\delta^2)=-\pi^2/(6\delta^2)+\pi/(2\delta\sinh(2\pi/\delta))=y(6-y\sinh y)/(24\sinh y)$. Since $dB/dy=-y/12+(\sinh y-y\cosh y)/(4\sinh^2y)<0$ for every $y>0$, $B$ is strictly increasing in $\delta$ on all of $\delta>0$ with the single zero $y\sinh y=6$. At the zero $dB/dy=-y/24-y^3\cosh y/144$ and $dy/d\delta=-y^2/(2\pi)$, so $B'(\delta_*)=y_*^3/(48\pi)+y_*^5\cosh y_*/(288\pi)$, which is the subject's $\beta_*$. The exact identity is obtained by subtracting the static row: the own-rail numerator is $(p-1)+\varepsilon^2p(1-q)=\theta^2(\varphi+\varepsilon^2p\chi)$ with $\theta^2=\varepsilon^2k^2\delta^2/(1-\varepsilon^2q)$, and the other rail is the same with $\nu=-1$, giving $G=B+\varepsilon^2E$ with $E=\sum(-1)^k(\varphi+\nu\varepsilon^2p\chi)/((1-\nu\varepsilon^2q)(1-\nu\varepsilon^2p))$ evaluated at the delay angles. An alternating sum over all integers of a smooth decaying function $F$ obeys $\lvert\sum_k(-1)^kF(k)\rvert\le\tfrac12\int_{\mathbb R}\lvert F''\rvert$, which is of order $\varepsilon$ here; the receiver's rail lacks $k=0$, so it contributes $-\varphi(0)=\tfrac16$ and the other rail contributes nothing in the limit. Hence $E\to\tfrac16$ and the coefficient of $\varepsilon^2$ in $\delta(\varepsilon)$ is $-1/(6\beta_*)$. The reference also predicted that the true remainder is of order $\varepsilon^4$, because $\varphi$ has a Fourier transform supported in $[-1,1]$ and the alternating sum of its samples at step $\varepsilon\delta<\pi$ vanishes identically.

**(d) The speed derivative (derived).** $\partial_\varepsilon G=2\varepsilon E+\varepsilon^2\partial_\varepsilon E$, and the derivative of one row of $E$ is about $(1/\varepsilon)\,\theta f'(\theta)$ at the delay angle, because $\theta$ is nearly proportional to $\varepsilon$. The sum of absolute values is of order $1/\varepsilon^2$, so $\varepsilon^2$ times it is of order one and does not vanish with the speed. The function $\theta\varphi'(\theta)$ vanishes at $0$ and decays like $\theta^{-2}$, so its alternating sum is bounded, in fact of order $\varepsilon$, and $\partial_\varepsilon G=\varepsilon/3+O(\varepsilon^2)$. Three things are required: the delay angle as a twice differentiable function of a real rung variable, smooth through rung $0$ on the receiver's rail; integrable derivatives of the summand in that variable; and termwise differentiation at positive speed, justified by a bound $C\varepsilon/(1+\varepsilon\lvert k\rvert\delta)^2$ that is summable locally uniformly in $\varepsilon>0$. Continuous differentiability at zero speed then follows from $\lvert\delta-\delta_*\rvert\le C\varepsilon^2$ and $\lvert\delta'\rvert\le C\varepsilon$, with no derivative of any root taken at $\varepsilon=0$ through the infinite sum.

The relation $\tau^2(1-\nu\varepsilon^2q)=z^2+2(1-\nu)$, both pairs of bounds, and $\tau_-\ge2\cos\tfrac12>\tfrac74$ for $\varepsilon\le\tfrac12$ were rederived; $2\cos\tfrac12=1.7552$. The derivative formulas were rederived by differentiating the root equation: $\partial_z\tau=z/(\tau D_\nu)$, $\partial_\varepsilon(\varepsilon\tau)=\tau/D_\nu$, and the numerator of $\partial_z^2\tau$, where expanding $(1-\nu\varepsilon^2p)^2-(1-\nu\varepsilon^2q)(1-\nu\varepsilon^2\cos\theta)$ gives $\varepsilon^2N_\nu$ exactly as stated. Both brackets of $N_\nu$ vanish to second order at $\theta=0$, the first as $\theta^2/4$, so $\lvert N_\nu\rvert\le C\min(\theta^2,1)$. Part (e) holds because $Z^2=\tau^2-2+2\cos\varepsilon\tau$ has derivative $2\tau D_+$. Measured: the formula for $\partial_z^2\tau$ agrees with central differences to $5\times10^{-8}$ at thirty sampled points on both rails, by `adj_estimate.py`.

2. **Lemma 2, reduction to two conditions: correct (derived).** The three components agree with reference (b). The constants $\tfrac43$ and $\tfrac83$ follow from $D_\nu\ge\tfrac34$, $\tau\ge\lvert k\rvert\delta$, $\lvert p\rvert\le1$ and $\lvert1-\nu\cos\theta\rvert\le2$. The symmetry argument is complete: a time shift with the matching rotation, a shift by $d$ with half a turn, and half a turn with reversal of every polarity each map the full history to itself, and together they carry the receiver at time $0$ to every member at every time. Balance at one instant therefore gives balance at all times, on the stated assumption that the rigid rotation has held for all earlier time. Axial balance is exact, by the pairing of rungs $k$ and $-k$. Measured: the full three-vector acceleration at the negative member of rung $3$ at time $1.7$, the positive member of rung $-2$ at time $-23.4$ and the positive member of rung $5$ at time $0.6$, at the balanced spacing for speeds $0.1$ and $0.3$, has the same tangential and radial components to $4\times10^{-15}$, tangential residual $4\times10^{-13}$ and $1.8\times10^{-12}$ (the size expected from a spacing rounded to ten decimals), axial residual below $10^{-16}$, and radial component equal to $-S$ to the nine digits available, by `adj_member.py` with the generic bracketing evaluator.

3. **Lemma 3, the exact identity: verified (derived).** The identity $g_\nu(z,\varepsilon)=g_\nu(z,0)+\varepsilon^2\Psi_\nu(\varepsilon\tau_\nu,\varepsilon)$, with $\Psi_\nu=(\varphi+\nu\varepsilon^2p\chi)/((1-\nu\varepsilon^2p)(1-\nu\varepsilon^2q))$, is identical to the one obtained in reference (c), and the subject's two-line algebra is correct. Measured: with the delay found from the three-vector root equation at 50 digits, the largest relative defect of the row identity over both rails, four heights from $0.37$ to $977$ and speeds $0.01$, $0.3$ and $0.9$ is $8.9\times10^{-49}$, by `adj_mp.py`.

## Hidden assumptions examined

- **Interchange of limit and infinite sum.** The limit $\varepsilon\to0$ of $G$ and of $\partial_\delta G$ is taken under domination by $C/k^2$ that is uniform in the speed down to zero. The limit of $\partial_\varepsilon G$ is never taken through the sum; only the bound $C\varepsilon$ on its value at positive speed is used. No unjustified interchange was found.
- **Uniformity down to zero speed.** Every constant of Proposition 6 depends only on $I$. The delay angles $\theta_\nu(k)$ are not uniformly small in $k$ at any positive speed, and the proof never assumes that they are: it works with the exact kernel at all angles.
- **Differentiability of roots at zero speed.** Each root is analytic in $\varepsilon$ at $\varepsilon=0$, but not uniformly in $k$. The proof uses this only row by row, in the Weierstrass test.
- **Endpoints of the spacing window.** All bounds hold on the closed interval. They degenerate as $\delta_a\to0$, through $\delta_a^{-3}$, and as $\delta_b\to\infty$, through the factor $\delta$ in $\int\lvert F_\nu''\rvert\le C\varepsilon\delta$; the subject says so.
- **All time from one time.** This follows from the rotation and time-shift symmetry, and rests on the stated assumption that every member has moved on its circle for all earlier time. The theorem is a statement that this eternal history is self-consistent under the law. It is not a statement about an initial-value problem.
- **Axial balance.** Exact, by reflection of the rung index; no limit is involved.
- **The law.** The adjudication takes the law as written in the subject's Assumptions section. Its agreement with the corpus Master Equation was not audited beyond noting that the [corpus collapse of the causal constraint](../../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/master-equation.md) carries the same transmitter-side root denominator, by reading the passage that defines $D_t$.

## What is not established

- **Stability.** Nothing in the subject or in this adjudication bears on whether the ladder persists under perturbation. The configuration document measures growing modes at every speed it examined; that measurement was not reviewed here.
- **A size for $\varepsilon_0$.** The proof and this check give a positive threshold without a number. The branch at speeds $0.1$ to $0.5$ in the tables is measured, in float arithmetic, without interval enclosure; the branch beyond $0.5$ was not examined here.
- **Uniqueness outside the window and outside the family.** For each compact $I$ the zero is unique in $I$ below $\varepsilon_0(I)$. Measured only, by `adj_target.py` with rungs $\lvert k\rvert\le2\times10^4$: on a grid of 177 spacings from $0.6$ to $40$, $G(\cdot,\varepsilon)$ changes sign once, between $3.30$ and $3.35$, at speeds $0.02$ and $0.1$, and is increasing on $0.6$ to $6$; a grid cannot exclude a closely spaced pair of zeros. Ladders with unequal rails, twisted or translating structures, finite segments and non-rigid motions are outside the statement.
- **Monotonicity of the radius in the speed.** Part 4 gives at least one balanced ladder for each large radius, not exactly one.
- **The sharper form and the $\varepsilon^4$ remainder.** Both are supported by measurement and neither is proved.
- **Conformity of the stated law with the corpus Master Equation in every particular.** Not audited here.
- **Dynamical meaning.** The theorem shows that one prescribed eternal history satisfies the law. It does not show that the history is reached from any other, or that the delayed initial-value problem near it is well posed.
- **Independence of this check.** The proof check is by one adjudicator in the same working session as the subject. The numerical instrument was written without reading the subject's scripts, from the stated law alone, and it shares that law with them.

## Falsifiers

- A point $(\nu,z,\varepsilon)$ with speed below $1$ at which $g_\nu(z,\varepsilon)-g_\nu(z,0)-\varepsilon^2\Psi_\nu$ is nonzero beyond rounding, with the delay taken from the three-vector root equation, would overturn item 3. Look in `adj_mp.py`, function `row_vec`, and its recorded defect.
- A value of $1/(6\beta_*)$ computed from $\beta_*=y_*^3/(48\pi)+y_*^5\cosh y_*/(288\pi)$ at the root of $y\sinh y=6$ that rounds to $1.277967$ would overturn correction 1. The check is one line of 15-digit arithmetic.
- A balanced spacing at small speed for which $(\delta-\delta_*+\varepsilon^2/(6\beta_*))/\varepsilon^3$ grows without bound as $\varepsilon\to0$ would refute Part 3 and this acceptance. The 50-digit values in `adj_mp_result.json` show it tending to zero.
- An explicit family of even functions satisfying the hypotheses of Lemma 5 and violating (5.2) would break the proof at its central step.
- A nonzero axial or tangential acceleration, or a radial acceleration different from $-S$, at any member and time other than the reference receiver, at the balanced spacing and beyond the rounding of that spacing, would overturn item 2. Look in `adj_member_result.json`.
- A 50-digit value of $E-e_0(\varepsilon)$ at speed $0.05$ that is not below $10^{-40}$ would contradict item 15.

## What was run

The subject's scripts in the neighbouring `ladder-existence/` directory were neither read nor run.
