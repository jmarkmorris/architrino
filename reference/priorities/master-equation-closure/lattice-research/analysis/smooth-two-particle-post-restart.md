## 15. Rigid sublattice motion and its relation to collinear geometry

The two polarity sublattices preserve their internal shapes while moving oppositely. This geometric fact does not require the same-polarity acceleration to vanish. It requires equal accelerations for all members of each group. The exact translation and reflection symmetries in the [incoming-history derivation](smooth-two-particle-incoming-reachability.md#2-an-exact-two-sublattice-reduction) give those equal accelerations without treating either group as a constrained rigid body.

### 15.1 Equal-time spacing does not determine received acceleration

For a positive-polarity receiver at anchor $i$ and a positive-polarity source at anchor $j$, the simultaneous separation is always $i-j$. The common vertical displacement cancels because both positions are evaluated at the same time. A received contribution uses an earlier source time $s<t$ instead:

$$
\mathbf X_i(t)-\mathbf X_j(t)=i-j,
\qquad
\mathbf X_i(t)-\mathbf X_j(s)=i-j+[q(t)-q(s)]e_3.
$$

Here $q$ is the common positive-group displacement and $e_3$ is the upward unit vector. During the certified increasing motion, $q(t)-q(s)>0$. The received separation therefore includes a vertical offset even between neighbors that are currently at the same height. Its range, direction and source-velocity weight determine the acceleration. Current spacing alone does not specify any of these received quantities.

A finite example makes the distinction explicit. Put the positive receiver at anchor zero and choose its two same-polarity neighbors at anchors $(1,1,0)$ and $(-1,-1,0)$. Reflection gives a common emission time $s$, range $r$ and vertical offset $z$ satisfying

$$
z=q(t)-q(s)>0,\qquad r=t-s=\sqrt{2+z^2},
\qquad D=1-\frac{z}{r}q'(s)>0.
$$

Their horizontal acceleration components cancel. Their vertical components add to

$$
A_{\mathrm{pair},3}(t)=\frac{2gz}{r^3D}>0.
$$

The coupling $g=16$ is positive and wake speed is $c_f=1$. Both earlier source positions lie below the receiver's present height, so both repelling contributions have an upward component. This finite pair disproves pairwise cancellation based solely on current symmetry; it does not establish the sign of the complete same-polarity sum by itself.

### 15.2 The complete first-order contribution is also nonzero

The accepted small-displacement calculation supplies a stronger check. Let $h(t)$ be a small vertical displacement history that decays exponentially into the remote past. Define $C_+[q]$ and $C_-[q]$ by restricting the absolutely convergent changed-source correction in the [exact scalar equation](staggered-lattice-first-event.md#2-full-scalar-equation-and-source-orbits) to same-polarity and opposite-polarity offsets, respectively. The stationary block contribution remains intact. This split does not assign independently reordered bare infinite sums to the two charged sublattices.

For an offset $d\ne0$, let $r_d=|d|$, $n_{d,3}=d_3/r_d$ and $\sigma_d=(-1)^{d_1+d_2+d_3}$. At the stationary history the first-order correction from either polarity is

$$
g\left[-\frac{1-3n_{d,3}^2}{r_d^3}h(t-r_d)
+\frac{n_{d,3}^2}{r_d^2}h'(t-r_d)\right].
$$

The first term comes from the earlier source position; the second comes from the source-velocity weight. The source displacement changes sign with its polarity, canceling the polarity factor in each term. The stationary receiver contribution has zero first derivative, as established in the incoming-history proof.

Every set of lattice offsets at a fixed distance is invariant under coordinate permutations and reflections. Moreover, $d_1+d_2+d_3$ and $|d|^2$ have the same parity, so an entire fixed-distance shell belongs to one polarity subset. On a shell containing $N_r$ offsets, the sum of $n_{d,3}^2$ is $N_r/3$. The position terms therefore cancel shell by shell, while the velocity terms yield

$$
DC_\pm[0]h(t)=\frac g3
\sum_{\substack{d\ne0\\\sigma_d=\pm1}}
\frac{h'(t-|d|)}{|d|^2}.
$$

The notation $DC_\pm[0]h$ denotes the first-order change of the corresponding correction at the stationary history. Exponential decay makes these sums absolutely convergent. For the growing mode $h(t)=e^{\lambda t}$ with $\lambda>0$, both expressions are strictly positive. Thus even the complete same-polarity delayed correction participates in the initial collective growth.
