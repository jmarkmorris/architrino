# Rotating ladder: a conditional nonlinear departure theorem in a symmetric history class

## Result

This note proves, up to three named numerical certificates, that the balanced rotating alternating ladder at pair radius $R=1000$ (units $K=c_f=1$) is left by exact solutions of the unchanged Master Equation. The solutions are complete histories: every member is defined for all past time, every rung $k\in\mathbb Z$ and both polarities are retained, and the law holds exactly at every member and every time up to the departure time. In the far past each solution tends to the balanced ladder along the leading real growing mode $\lambda_*\approx0.98743\,\omega$ (wavenumber $q=\pi$, sector $p=+1$); at a finite time it has moved a fixed, explicitly computable distance from every phase-rotated copy of the balance. Time-shifted copies start arbitrarily close to the balance in a history norm that takes the supremum of position and velocity over the entire past.

The construction is an analytic series in $w=e^{\lambda_*T}$, in the manner of the [six-member ring construction](../../braid-program/analysis/t02-admissible-nonlinear-history-connection.md), carried out in a class of histories that are invariant under axial translation by $2d$, under the half turn with polarity exchange, and under reflection $z\to-z$. In that class the infinite population reduces to two independent in-plane members with lattice-image sums. The two obstacles particular to the ladder are handled as follows. Infinite memory and infinite population: every source row and every perturbation of it is bounded by a constant times the inverse square of its delay, which is summable over a one-dimensional lattice, and every delayed evaluation of a history lies strictly inside the disk of convergence of the series, uniformly over all sources; this is what absorbs the loss of derivatives that state-dependent delays otherwise cause. The linear inverse: the correction at order $w^n$ is obtained by inverting the characteristic matrix at the single real point $n\lambda_*$, so the only spectral information required is invertibility, with a bound, of two real two-by-two matrices on the real half-line $\mu\ge2\lambda_*$; a Neumann-series comparison with the inertial term $(\mu+\omega J)^2$ supplies this from three absolutely convergent lattice sums, with no root search.

**Claim grade.** Derived, conditional on certificates B0 (the balance at $R=1000$), B1 (the real root $\lambda_*$) and B3 (two lattice-sum bounds), for the theorem of Section 3 and Lemmas A1 to A8; each lemma's proof is complete as written here and self-reviewed, not independently adjudicated. The first-variation formula that Lemma A7 identifies with the linear part is the one derived and adjudicated in the [independent adjudication](rotating-ladder-delayed-stability-independent-adjudication-2026-10-03.md) and is used, not re-derived. Measured, by the float instrument `class_spectrum_float.py` written for this note: that the leading real root in the class lies in the in-plane block at $q=\pi$, at $0.98742972\,\omega$; that the certificate inequality B3 holds with margin in float arithmetic; illustrative values of the constants. The explicit constants of Lemmas A4 and A5 are evaluated with outward-rounded interval arithmetic by `departure_constants.py` on the parameter box of the existing float balance; the departure amplitude $\eta_0\ge1.19\times10^{-5}R$ that it reports is illustrative until B0 to B3 are certified, because one of its inputs is a float measurement. No numerical evolution was run and none is used.

**Claim boundary.** The statement is nonlinear only within the declared symmetric class, the declared history norm and the declared analytic series class, and is conditional on the named certificates. It says nothing about generic perturbations, about perturbations outside the class, about finite segments of ladder, about uniqueness of departing histories outside a small coefficient ball, or about the eventual fate of the departing solutions.

## 1. Setting

**Law.** A receiver at $\mathbf x$ at time $T$ receives from source $j$ at every causal emission time $s<T$ with $T-s=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s)\rvert$ the acceleration $\sigma_{ij}K(\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s))/(\tau^3\lvert D\rvert)$, with $\tau=T-s$, $\mathbf n=(\mathbf x-\mathbf X_j(s))/\tau$, $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j(s)$ and $\sigma_{ij}=+1$ for like and $-1$ for unlike polarities. Contributions add over all sources and all causal roots, including any positive-delay root from the receiver's own past. The emission at $s=T$ is excluded. Nothing is added to the law.

**Unit ladder.** Measure lengths in units of $R$ and times in units of $R/c_f$. With $c_f=1$ the member speed equals the angular rate, written $\varepsilon=\omega R$; the spacing is $\delta=d/R$; and the law acquires the coupling $g=K/R$ (so $g=10^{-3}$ at $R=1000$). The reference ladder has, for every $k\in\mathbb Z$, a positive member at $Q(\varepsilon t+k\pi)\mathbf e_1+k\delta\,\mathbf e_z$ and a negative member at $Q(\varepsilon t+k\pi+\pi)\mathbf e_1+k\delta\,\mathbf e_z$, where $Q(\alpha)$ is the rotation by $\alpha$ about the axis $\mathbf e_z$ and $J$ its generator, $J\mathbf e_1=\mathbf e_2$, $J\mathbf e_2=-\mathbf e_1$, $J\mathbf e_z=0$. The balance is $\sum_j\mathbf a_j=-\varepsilon^2\mathbf e_1$ at the positive member of rung $0$ at $t=0$, which by the existence proof's Lemma 2 ([small-speed existence](rotating-ladder-small-speed-existence.md)) is equivalent to the ladder solving the law at every member and time. At $R=1000$ the adjudicated float balance is $\delta=3.345035293$, $\varepsilon=0.01384081428$ (adjudication, Controls, Balance); it is not certified, which is why B0 below is needed.

**The history class $\mathcal C$.** Let $\mathbf u_0,\mathbf u_1:(-\infty,T_1]\to\mathbb R^2$ be twice continuously differentiable in-plane displacement histories. The class history is

$$
\mathbf X^{+}_k(t)=Q(\varepsilon t+k\pi)\bigl[\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_{k\bmod2}(t)\bigr]+k\delta\,\mathbf e_z,\qquad
\mathbf X^{-}_k(t)=Q(\varepsilon t+k\pi+\pi)\bigl[\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_{k\bmod2}(t)\bigr]+k\delta\,\mathbf e_z .
$$

Every member stays in the plane of its own rung, its in-plane displacement in its own rotating frame depends only on the parity of its rung, and the two members of a rung carry the same displacement in their own frames. In the notation of the adjudication this is the span of the wavenumbers $q\in\{0,\pi\}$ in the sector $p=+1$, restricted to in-plane displacements: $q=0$ is $\mathbf u_1=\mathbf u_0$, and $q=\pi$ is $\mathbf u_1=-\mathbf u_0$. Write $\mathbf z=(\mathbf u_0,\mathbf u_1)$ and $\mathbf z_\pm=(\mathbf u_0\pm\mathbf u_1)/2$.

**Sources seen by a receiver.** Take the receiver to be the positive member of rung $r\in\{0,1\}$. Its sources are indexed by $j=(\nu,m)$ with rail sign $\nu=\pm1$ and rung offset $m\in\mathbb Z$, excluding $(\nu,m)=(+1,0)$, which is the receiver itself. Source $(\nu,m)$ is the member of rung $r+m$ whose reference position at a common time is $\nu$ times the receiver's in-plane reference position; its polarity product with the receiver is $\sigma_j=\nu(-1)^m$, and its displacement history is $\mathbf u_{k'}$ with $k'=(r+m)\bmod2$. In the receiver's rotating frame at reception time $t$, a source emission at delay $\tau$ has relative vector and velocity

$$
\mathbf r_j=\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_r(t)-\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\bigl[\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_{k'}(t-\tau)\bigr]-m\delta\,\mathbf e_z,\qquad
\mathbf V_j=\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\bigl[\varepsilon J(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_{k'}(t-\tau))+\mathbf u_{k'}'(t-\tau)\bigr].
$$

The reference values ($\mathbf u\equiv0$) are those of the existence proof's Lemma 2(a): reference delay $\tau_j$, reference transmitter factor $D_j\in[1-\varepsilon^2,1+\varepsilon^2]$, and (Lemma 1(c) there) $\tau_j\ge\ell_j$ with $\ell_{(+1,m)}=\lvert m\rvert\delta$ and $\ell_{(-1,m)}=\sqrt{(m^2\delta^2+4)/(1+\varepsilon^2)}$. For $\delta\ge2$ the smallest of these is $\tau_{\min}=2/\sqrt{1+\varepsilon^2}$, the rung partner.

**Reduced equation.** The receiver's acceleration in its own rotating frame is $\mathbf u_r''+2\varepsilon J\mathbf u_r'-\varepsilon^2(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u_r)$. The reduced law is

$$
\mathbf u_r''(t)+2\varepsilon J\mathbf u_r'(t)-\varepsilon^2\mathbf u_r(t)=P_\perp\Bigl[\mathcal A_r(\mathbf z)(t)-\mathcal A_r(0)\Bigr],\qquad r=0,1,\tag{1.1}
$$

where $\mathcal A_r(\mathbf z)(t)=\sum_j g\sigma_j\mathbf r_j/(\tau^3D)$ is the complete row sum at the receiver's causal roots, $P_\perp$ is the in-plane projection, and the balance $\mathcal A_r(0)=-\varepsilon^2\mathbf e_1$ has been used. The equation is autonomous because the law is invariant under a time shift combined with the matching rotation.

**History norm.** For a class history defined on $(-\infty,t]$,

$$
\mathsf N_t(\mathbf z)=\sup_{s\le t}\ \max_{r=0,1}\Bigl(\lvert\mathbf u_r(s)\rvert+\varepsilon^{-1}\lvert\mathbf u_r'(s)\rvert\Bigr).
$$

It measures position in units of $R$ and velocity in units of the reference member speed, over the whole past. Rigid phase rotations of the balance, $\mathbf u_0=\mathbf u_1=Q(\varphi)\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_1$ constant, form the phase family $\mathcal O$; every element of $\mathcal O$ has $\mathbf u'\equiv0$.

**Coefficient space.** Let $\mathcal W$ be the Wiener algebra of complex power series $f(w)=\sum_{n\ge0}f_nw^n$ with $\lVert f\rVert=\sum_n\lvert f_n\rvert<\infty$, and $\mathcal W_0$ its ideal of series with $f_0=0$. Vector and matrix series use the Euclidean norm of each coefficient and the operator norm of each matrix coefficient respectively. The unknown is $\mathbf p=(\mathbf p_0,\mathbf p_1)\in\mathcal W_0^2\times\mathcal W_0^2$ with norm $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert=\max_r\lVert\mathbf p_r\rVert$, and the history is $\mathbf u_r(t)=\mathbf p_r(e^{\lambda_*t})$ for $t\le0$. The Euler operator is $\mathcal D\mathbf p=\lambda_*w\,\mathbf p'(w)$, so that $\mathcal D\mathbf p$ evaluated at $w=e^{\lambda_*t}$ is $\mathbf u'(t)$.

## 2. Analytic lemmas

**Lemma A1 (reduction; derived, proof complete).** A class history solves the Master Equation at every member and every time $t\le T_1$ if and only if $\mathbf z$ solves (1.1) for $r=0,1$ and $t\le T_1$, all row sums converging absolutely.

*Proof.* Three isometries, each possibly combined with reversal of all polarities, map a class history to itself: translation by $2\delta\,\mathbf e_z$ (rung $k\to k+2$, same parity, same polarity), the half turn $Q(\pi)$ with polarity reversal (positive member of rung $k\to$ negative member of rung $k$), and the reflection $z\to-z$ (rung $k\to-k$, same parity because $Q(k\pi)=Q(-k\pi)$, same polarity, in-plane vectors unchanged). The law is equivariant under isometries and under global polarity reversal, since only products of polarities enter and causal roots are mapped bijectively, provided the sums converge absolutely so that reordering is allowed (Lemma A5). The first two operations carry the positive members of rungs $0$ and $1$ to every member, so the law holds everywhere if and only if it holds at those two. The reflection fixes the positive member of rung $0$, and the reflection in the plane $z=\delta$ (reflection composed with the $2\delta$ translation) fixes the positive member of rung $1$; hence each receives an acceleration with zero axial component, which is the axial component of its actual acceleration. The in-plane components are (1.1). $\blacksquare$

**Lemma A2 (causal roots in subwake histories; derived, proof complete).** Let a source path $\mathbf X$ be defined and Lipschitz on $(-\infty,t]$ with speed at most $\bar V<1$, and let $\rho_0=\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(t)\rvert>0$. Then $f(\tau)=\tau-\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(t-\tau)\rvert$ has exactly one zero $\tau_c$ in $(0,\infty)$, with $\rho_0/(1+\bar V)\le\tau_c\le\rho_0/(1-\bar V)$, and if $\mathbf X$ is differentiable at $t-\tau_c$ then $D=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf X'(t-\tau_c)\ge1-\bar V$. If instead $\mathbf x=\mathbf X(t)$ (the receiver's own path), $f(\tau)\ge(1-\bar V)\tau>0$ for every $\tau>0$: there is no self root.

*Proof.* $\tau\mapsto\lvert\mathbf x-\mathbf X(t-\tau)\rvert$ is Lipschitz with constant $\bar V$, so $f(\tau_2)-f(\tau_1)\ge(1-\bar V)(\tau_2-\tau_1)$ for $\tau_2>\tau_1$, and $f(0)=-\rho_0<0$. Strict increase and $-\rho_0+(1-\bar V)\tau\le f(\tau)\le-\rho_0+(1+\bar V)\tau$ give the zero and its bounds. At the zero the distance equals $\tau_c>0$, so the distance is differentiable there with derivative $-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf X'$, and $f'(\tau_c)=D\ge1-\bar V$. The self case is the same inequality with $\rho_0=0$. $\blacksquare$

The lemma uses only the speed bound and the equal-time separation, over the complete past; it needs no circular reference path.

**Lemma A3 (Wiener algebra; derived, proof complete).** (a) $\mathcal W$ is a commutative unital Banach algebra, and evaluation at any $\lvert w\rvert\le1$ is a continuous character. (b) If $F$ is analytic in a disk of radius $\varrho$ about $a$ and $\lVert x\rVert<\varrho$ then $F(a+x)\in\mathcal W$ by the Taylor series, with $\lVert F(a+x)-F(a)\rVert\le\sum_{k\ge1}\lvert F^{(k)}(a)\rvert\lVert x\rVert^k/k!$; in particular $\lVert e^x\rVert\le e^{\lVert x\rVert}$, $\lVert\cos x-1\rVert\le\cosh\lVert x\rVert-1$, $\lVert\sin x\rVert\le\sinh\lVert x\rVert$, $\lVert(a+x)^{-k}\rVert\le(\lvert a\rvert-\lVert x\rVert)^{-k}$. (c) If $\mathbf p\in\mathcal W_0$ and $\varphi\in\mathcal W_0$ with $\lVert\varphi\rVert\le q<1$, the composite $\mathbf p\circ\varphi=\sum_n\mathbf p_n\varphi^n\in\mathcal W_0$ satisfies $\lVert\mathbf p\circ\varphi\rVert\le q\lVert\mathbf p\rVert$ and $\lVert(\mathcal D\mathbf p)\circ\varphi\rVert\le\lambda_*\sup_{n\ge1}(nq^n)\lVert\mathbf p\rVert$; the map $(\mathbf p,\varphi)\mapsto\mathbf p\circ\varphi$ is analytic on $\mathcal W_0\times\{\lVert\varphi\rVert<1\}$, with derivative in $\varphi$ equal to multiplication by $\mathbf p'\circ\varphi$. (d) If $q=e^{-\lambda_*a}$ with $a>0$ then $\lambda_*\sup_n nq^n\le1/(ea)$. (e) If all inputs have real coefficients, so do all outputs; for $w\in(0,1]$ the function $t\mapsto\mathbf p(e^{\lambda_*t})$ is smooth on $t<0$ and continuously twice differentiable on $t\le0$ when $\sum_nn^2\lvert\mathbf p_n\rvert<\infty$.

*Proof.* (a) to (c) are the elementary properties of absolutely convergent coefficient series: $\lVert fg\rVert\le\lVert f\rVert\lVert g\rVert$ by the Cauchy product, and $\lVert\varphi^n\rVert\le q^n$. (d) is $\sup_{x>0}xe^{-\lambda_*ax}=1/(e\lambda_*a)$. (e) is termwise differentiation of a series whose differentiated coefficients are absolutely summable. $\blacksquare$

Part (c) is the mechanism that removes the loss of derivatives. A delayed evaluation $\mathbf u(t-\tau)$ is $\mathbf p(we^{-\lambda_*\tau})$, an evaluation strictly inside the disk; there the derivative $\mathcal D\mathbf p$ is bounded by the position norm alone, so no derivative of the unknown beyond the one at the receiver's own time is ever needed.

**Lemma A4 (the delay map, uniform over all sources; derived, proof complete).** Fix $r_p>0$ and $\bar\vartheta>0$ with $\bar\vartheta<\tau_{\min}$, and define

$$
e_1=e^{\varepsilon\bar\vartheta},\quad \sigma_*=\frac1{e(\tau_{\min}-\bar\vartheta)},\quad \beta_b=1+e_1,\quad \beta_v=e_1(\varepsilon+\sigma_*),\quad D_{\min}=1-\varepsilon^2,
$$

$$
K_1=\frac{\bar\vartheta^2(1+\varepsilon^2e_1)+2(1+e_1)\beta_br_p+\beta_b^2r_p^2}{2\tau_{\min}D_{\min}},\qquad
K_2=\frac{2\bar\vartheta(1+\varepsilon^2e_1)+2\varepsilon e_1\beta_br_p+2(1+e_1)\beta_vr_p+2\beta_b\beta_vr_p^2}{2\tau_{\min}D_{\min}} .
$$

If $K_1\le\bar\vartheta$ and $K_2\le\tfrac12$ (condition R1), then for every source $j$ and every $\mathbf p$ with $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert<r_p$ there is exactly one $\vartheta_j(\mathbf p)\in\mathcal W_0$ with $\lVert\vartheta_j\rVert\le\bar\vartheta$ solving the squared causal equation $\mathbf r_j\cdot\mathbf r_j=(\tau_j+\vartheta)^2$, in which every displacement is replaced by its series, $\mathbf u_r(t)\to\mathbf p_r(w)$, $\mathbf u_{k'}(t-\tau)\to\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)$ and $\mathbf u'_{k'}(t-\tau)\to(\mathcal D\mathbf p_{k'})(\varphi_j)$, with $\varphi_j=we^{-\lambda_*(\tau_j+\vartheta)}$. The map $\mathbf p\mapsto\vartheta_j(\mathbf p)$ is analytic, and the bounds are uniform in $j$.

*Proof.* Write $\tau=\tau_j+\vartheta$, $\mathbf r_j=\mathbf r^0_j(\tau)+\mathbf b_j$ with the reference relative vector $\mathbf r^0_j(\tau)=\mathbf e_1-\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\mathbf e_1-m\delta\,\mathbf e_z$ and the in-plane perturbation $\mathbf b_j=\mathbf p_r(w)-\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)$. For $\lVert\vartheta\rVert\le\bar\vartheta$: $\lVert\varphi_j\rVert\le e^{-\lambda_*(\tau_j-\bar\vartheta)}<1$, so by Lemma A3(c,d), $\lVert\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)\rVert\le\lVert\mathbf p\rVert$ and $\lVert(\mathcal D\mathbf p_{k'})(\varphi_j)\rVert\le\sigma_*\lVert\mathbf p\rVert$; and $\lVert Q(-\varepsilon\tau)\rVert\le e_1$ because $Q(-\varepsilon\tau_j)$ is orthogonal and $\lVert Q(-\varepsilon\vartheta)\rVert\le\lVert\cos\varepsilon\vartheta\rVert+\lVert\sin\varepsilon\vartheta\rVert\le e_1$. Hence $\lVert\mathbf b_j\rVert\le\beta_br_p$ and, since $\partial_\vartheta\mathbf b_j=\nu\varepsilon JQ(-\varepsilon\tau)\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)+\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)(\mathcal D\mathbf p_{k'})(\varphi_j)$, $\lVert\partial_\vartheta\mathbf b_j\rVert\le\beta_vr_p$. The in-plane part of $\mathbf r^0_j(\tau)$ has norm at most $1+e_1$ (its reference value has length $\sqrt{2-2\nu\cos\varepsilon\tau_j}\le2$, and its deviation is at most $\lVert Q(-\varepsilon\vartheta)-I\rVert\le e_1-1$), and its $\vartheta$-derivative is the reference source velocity $\mathbf V^0_j(\tau)=\nu\varepsilon Q(-\varepsilon\tau)J\mathbf e_1$, of norm at most $\varepsilon e_1$. Only the in-plane part of $\mathbf r^0_j$ meets $\mathbf b_j$ in a dot product. With $h_j(\vartheta)=\mathbf r^0_j\cdot\mathbf r^0_j-\tau^2=-\Phi_\nu(\tau)$, where $\Phi_\nu(\tau)=\tau^2-m^2\delta^2-2+2\nu\cos\varepsilon\tau$ is the root function of the existence proof's Lemma 1, $h_j(0)=0$ and $h_j'(0)=-2\tau_jD_j$. The squared equation is $G_j=h_j(\vartheta)+2\mathbf r^0_j\cdot\mathbf b_j+\mathbf b_j\cdot\mathbf b_j=0$, equivalently $\vartheta=\mathcal K_j(\vartheta)=[h_j(\vartheta)+2\tau_jD_j\vartheta+2\mathbf r^0_j\cdot\mathbf b_j+\mathbf b_j\cdot\mathbf b_j]/(2\tau_jD_j)$. Now $h_j(\vartheta)+2\tau_jD_j\vartheta=-\vartheta^2-2\nu[\cos(\varepsilon\tau_j+\varepsilon\vartheta)-\cos\varepsilon\tau_j+\varepsilon\vartheta\sin\varepsilon\tau_j]$, and the bracket is $\cos\varepsilon\tau_j(\cos\varepsilon\vartheta-1)-\sin\varepsilon\tau_j(\sin\varepsilon\vartheta-\varepsilon\vartheta)$, of norm at most $e^{\varepsilon\bar\vartheta}-1-\varepsilon\bar\vartheta\le\tfrac12\varepsilon^2\bar\vartheta^2e_1$; its $\vartheta$-derivative has norm at most $2\bar\vartheta(1+\varepsilon^2e_1)$. Using $\tau_jD_j\ge\tau_{\min}D_{\min}$, $\lVert\mathcal K_j(\vartheta)\rVert\le K_1\le\bar\vartheta$ and $\lVert\partial_\vartheta\mathcal K_j\rVert\le K_2\le\frac12$ on the ball, so $\mathcal K_j$ is a contraction of the closed ball $\lVert\vartheta\rVert\le\bar\vartheta$ in $\mathcal W_0$ (it preserves $\mathcal W_0$ because every term vanishes at $w=0$). The fixed point is unique, and as the uniform limit of iterates that are analytic in $\mathbf p$ it is analytic in $\mathbf p$. None of the bounds depends on $j$. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma A5 (rows and the lattice sum; derived, proof complete).** Under R1, put

$$
K_x=\bar\vartheta+\varepsilon(e_1-1)+(1+e_1)\beta_vr_p+\varepsilon e_1\beta_br_p+\beta_b\beta_vr_p^2,\qquad
\Gamma=\frac{\tau_{\min}^2\,(\tau_{\min}+e_1-1+\beta_br_p)}{(\tau_{\min}-\bar\vartheta)^2\,(\tau_{\min}D_{\min}-K_x)},
$$

$$
Z=\sum_j\ell_j^{-2}=\frac{\pi^2}{3\delta^2}+(1+\varepsilon^2)\frac{\pi}{2\delta}\coth\frac{2\pi}{\delta},\qquad M=2g\,\Gamma Z,
$$

and assume $K_x<\tau_{\min}D_{\min}$ (condition R2). Then each row map $\mathbf a_j(\mathbf p)=g\sigma_j\mathbf r_j\,(\tau_j+\vartheta_j)^{-2}\kappa_j^{-1}$, with $\kappa_j=\tau_j+\vartheta_j-\mathbf r_j\cdot\mathbf V_j$, is analytic on $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert<r_p$ with $\lVert\mathbf a_j(\mathbf p)\rVert\le g\Gamma/\ell_j^2$. The series $\mathcal F_r(\mathbf p)=P_\perp\sum_j\mathbf a_j(\mathbf p)$ converges absolutely and uniformly there, $\mathcal F=(\mathcal F_0,\mathcal F_1)$ is analytic, and $\sup_{\lVert\mathbf p\rVert<r_p}\lVert\mathcal F(\mathbf p)-\mathcal F(0)\rVert\le M$.

*Proof.* The source velocity is $\mathbf V_j=\mathbf V^0_j(\tau)+\boldsymbol\beta_j$ with $\boldsymbol\beta_j=\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau)[\varepsilon J\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)+(\mathcal D\mathbf p_{k'})(\varphi_j)]$, $\lVert\boldsymbol\beta_j\rVert\le\beta_vr_p$, both in-plane. The reference product is $\mathbf r^0_j(\tau)\cdot\mathbf V^0_j(\tau)=\nu\varepsilon\sin\varepsilon\tau$ (a polynomial identity in $\cos\varepsilon\tau$, $\sin\varepsilon\tau$, valid for complex $\tau$), and $\tau_jD_j=\tau_j-\nu\varepsilon\sin\varepsilon\tau_j$. Hence $\kappa_j-\tau_jD_j=\vartheta_j-\nu\varepsilon[\sin\varepsilon\tau-\sin\varepsilon\tau_j]-\mathbf r^0_{j\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol\beta_j-\mathbf b_j\cdot\mathbf V^0_j-\mathbf b_j\cdot\boldsymbol\beta_j$, of norm at most $K_x$. By Lemma A3(b), $\lVert\kappa_j^{-1}\rVert\le(\tau_jD_{\min}-K_x)^{-1}$, $\lVert(\tau_j+\vartheta_j)^{-2}\rVert\le(\tau_j-\bar\vartheta)^{-2}$, and $\lVert\mathbf r_j\rVert\le\tau_j+e_1-1+\beta_br_p$. The product bound $\tau^2(\tau+c)/((\tau-\bar\vartheta)^2(\tau D_{\min}-K_x))$ is decreasing in $\tau$ (each factor $\tau/(\tau-\bar\vartheta)$ and $(\tau+c)/(\tau D_{\min}-K_x)$ is), so $\lVert\mathbf a_j\rVert\le g\Gamma/\tau_j^2\le g\Gamma/\ell_j^2$. The same bound holds for $\mathbf a_j(0)$, since $\Gamma\ge1/D_{\min}$. The sums over the own rail, $\sum_{m\ne0}(m\delta)^{-2}=\pi^2/(3\delta^2)$, and over the other rail, $(1+\varepsilon^2)\sum_m(m^2\delta^2+4)^{-1}=(1+\varepsilon^2)\frac{\pi}{2\delta}\coth\frac{2\pi}\delta$, are classical. Uniform absolute convergence of analytic maps on a ball gives an analytic sum. $\blacksquare$

The convergence of the image sums uses only that the rows decay like the inverse square of the delay and that the lattice is one-dimensional. It does not use the alternation of the polarities, and it does not use the exponential factors $e^{-n\lambda_*\tau_j}$; those factors are used in Lemma A3(c) to control delayed velocities. This is the answer to the infinite-memory obstacle: in the coefficient norm, rung $m$ samples the history at depth $\tau_j\approx\lvert m\rvert\delta$, and the contribution of that sampling, together with all its derivatives with respect to the history, is bounded by $g\Gamma/\ell_j^2$ uniformly in the depth.

**Lemma A6 (quadratic remainder; derived, proof complete).** Let $\mathcal N(\mathbf p)=\mathcal F(\mathbf p)-\mathcal F(0)-D\mathcal F(0)\mathbf p$. For $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert,\lVert\tilde{\mathbf p}\rVert\le\rho<r_p$,

$$
\lVert\mathcal N(\mathbf p)-\mathcal N(\tilde{\mathbf p})\rVert\le\frac{4M\rho}{(r_p-\rho)^2}\lVert\mathbf p-\tilde{\mathbf p}\rVert,\qquad\lVert\mathcal N(\mathbf p)\rVert\le\frac{2M\rho^2}{(r_p-\rho)^2}.
$$

Moreover $\mathcal N(\mathbf p)$ has no coefficient of order $0$ or $1$ in $w$.

*Proof.* For $\lVert\boldsymbol\xi\rVert\le\rho$ and unit vectors $\mathbf a,\mathbf b$, the function $(\zeta_1,\zeta_2)\mapsto\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\xi+\zeta_1\mathbf a+\zeta_2\mathbf b)-\mathcal F(0)$ is analytic and bounded by $M$ on the polydisk $\lvert\zeta_i\rvert<(r_p-\rho)/2$; the two-variable Cauchy estimate gives $\lVert D^2\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\xi)(\mathbf a,\mathbf b)\rVert\le4M/(r_p-\rho)^2$. Then $\lVert D\mathcal F(\boldsymbol\xi)-D\mathcal F(0)\rVert\le4M\rho/(r_p-\rho)^2$, and both estimates follow by integrating along segments. Every map in the construction sends $w=0$ to the reference, and every nonlinear term is a product of at least two factors in $\mathcal W_0$, so it starts at order $w^2$. $\blacksquare$

**Lemma A7 (the linear part is the characteristic matrix at real points; derived, proof complete given the adjudicated first variation).** For $\mathbf c\in\mathbb C^2\times\mathbb C^2$ and $n\ge1$,

$$
(n\lambda_*+\varepsilon J)^2\mathbf c\,w^n-D\mathcal F(0)[\mathbf c\,w^n]=\Delta_{\mathcal C}(n\lambda_*)\,\mathbf c\,w^n,
$$

where $\Delta_{\mathcal C}(\mu)$ acts as $\Delta_0(\mu)$ on $\mathbf c_+$ and as $\Delta_\pi(\mu)$ on $\mathbf c_-$, and $\Delta_q(\mu)$ is the in-plane two-by-two block of the adjudication's $\Delta(\mu;q,+1)=(\mu I+\varepsilon J)^2-\sum_jL_{u,j}+\sum_j(\pm1)^me^{-\mu\tau_j}[L_{u,j}Q_j-L_{w,j}Q_j(\mu I+\varepsilon J)]$ (sign $+$ for $q=0$, $(-1)^m$ for $q=\pi$; $Q_j=\nu Q(-\varepsilon\tau_j)$ on the plane; $L_{u,j}$, $L_{w,j}$ with the factor $g$). The coefficient equations of (1.1) are therefore

$$
\Delta_{\mathcal C}(n\lambda_*)\,\mathbf p_n=[\mathcal N(\mathbf p)]_n,\qquad n\ge1. \tag{2.1}
$$

*Proof.* At $\mathbf p=0$ the composite $\mathbf p_{k'}(\varphi_j)$ has derivative $\mathbf c\,w^n\mapsto e^{-n\lambda_*\tau_j}\mathbf c\,w^n$, the delayed derivative has derivative $\mathbf c\,w^n\mapsto n\lambda_*e^{-n\lambda_*\tau_j}\mathbf c\,w^n$, and $D\vartheta_j(0)$ is the root shift $\mathbf n_j\cdot(\delta\mathbf x-\boldsymbol\xi)/D_j$ obtained by differentiating $G_j=0$. The chain rule then produces, coefficient by coefficient, exactly the scalar differentiation recorded in the adjudication's pre-target reference, whose result is $L_{u,j}$, $L_{w,j}$; the source frame rotation contributes $Q_j$ and the factor $(\mu I+\varepsilon J)$ in the velocity. Hence the derivative is diagonal in $n$ with the stated matrix. In class coordinates the linear part has the form $\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\B&A\end{pmatrix}$, because the screw operation (translation by $\delta$ with a half turn) maps a class history with $(\mathbf u_0,\mathbf u_1)$ to the class history with $(\mathbf u_1,\mathbf u_0)$ and the law is equivariant under it; so it acts as $A\pm B$ on $\mathbf c_\pm$, and $A\pm B$ are the $q=0$ and $q=\pi$ blocks. The in-plane block is closed under the linear map at $q\in\{0,\pi\}$ because the reflection $z\to-z$ maps source $(\nu,m)$ to $(\nu,-m)$ with the same phase $(\pm1)^m$, so $\Delta(\mu;q,+1)$ commutes with $\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1)$. $\blacksquare$

Measured support for the block structure: in `class_spectrum_float.py` the in-plane to axial entries of $\Delta(0.9\varepsilon;q,+1)$ are below $1.5\times10^{-17}\varepsilon^2$ at both wavenumbers.

**Lemma A8 (inverse on the real half-line; derived, proof complete).** For real $\mu\ge0$ define $b_0(\mu)=\lVert(\sum_jL_{u,j})_{\perp}\rVert+\sum_je^{-\mu\tau_j}\lVert(L_{u,j}Q_j-\varepsilon L_{w,j}Q_jJ)_{\perp}\rVert$ and $b_1(\mu)=\sum_je^{-\mu\tau_j}\lVert(L_{w,j}Q_j)_\perp\rVert$, where $(\cdot)_\perp$ is the in-plane block and $\lVert\cdot\rVert$ the spectral norm. Let $\mu_0>0$, $\bar b_0\ge b_0(\mu_0)$, $\bar b_1\ge b_1(\mu_0)$, and $h_0=\mu_0^2+\varepsilon^2-\bar b_0-\bar b_1\mu_0>0$. Then for every real $\mu\ge\mu_0$, both blocks are invertible and

$$
\lVert\Delta_q(\mu)^{-1}\rVert\le\frac1{\mu^2+\varepsilon^2-\bar b_0-\bar b_1\mu},\qquad q\in\{0,\pi\},
$$

and, if $\mu_0=2\lambda_{\rm lo}$ with $\lambda_{\rm lo}\le\lambda_*$, the operator $\mathcal R$ that multiplies the coefficient of order $n\ge2$ by $\Delta_{\mathcal C}(n\lambda_*)^{-1}$ satisfies, in the max norm of class coordinates,

$$
B_k:=\sup_{n\ge2}n^k\lVert\Delta_{\mathcal C}(n\lambda_*)^{-1}\rVert\le\frac{2^{k+1}}{h_0},\qquad k=0,1,2 .
$$

*Proof.* For real $\mu$ the in-plane matrix $\mu I+\varepsilon J$ is normal with eigenvalues $\mu\pm i\varepsilon$, so the smallest singular value of $(\mu I+\varepsilon J)^2$ is $\mu^2+\varepsilon^2$. Each block differs from it by a matrix of norm at most $b_0(\mu)+b_1(\mu)\mu\le\bar b_0+\bar b_1\mu$, since $\lvert(\pm1)^me^{-\mu\tau_j}\rvert=e^{-\mu\tau_j}$ decreases in $\mu$. The Neumann series gives the first bound. The denominator $h(\mu)=\mu^2+\varepsilon^2-\bar b_0-\bar b_1\mu$ is increasing for $\mu\ge\bar b_1/2$ and positive at $\mu_0$; and $\mu/h(\mu)$ and $\mu^2/h(\mu)$ are decreasing there, because their derivatives have the signs of $\varepsilon^2-\bar b_0-\mu^2$ and of $2\mu(\varepsilon^2-\bar b_0)-\bar b_1\mu^2$, both negative when $\bar b_0>\varepsilon^2$; this is assumed (an upper bound $\bar b_0$ may always be enlarged to exceed $\varepsilon^2$, at the cost of $h_0$, and the float value is $2.80\,\varepsilon^2$). For $n\ge2$, $\mu=n\lambda_*\ge\mu_0$ and $n\le\mu/\lambda_{\rm lo}$. Finally, a class vector $\mathbf a=\Delta_{\mathcal C}^{-1}\mathbf f$ has $\mathbf a_0=\mathbf a_++\mathbf a_-$, $\mathbf a_1=\mathbf a_+-\mathbf a_-$ with $\lvert\mathbf a_\pm\rvert\le\lVert\Delta^{-1}_{0,\pi}\rVert\max_r\lvert\mathbf f_r\rvert$, which supplies the factor $2$. $\blacksquare$

The Neumann comparison works on the real axis and not on vertical lines: for complex $\mu$ the smallest singular value of $(\mu I+\varepsilon J)^2$ is only $\min\lvert\mu\pm i\varepsilon\rvert^2$, which on the line $\operatorname{Re}\mu=2\lambda_*$ falls to $(2\lambda_*-\varepsilon)^2\approx0.95\,\varepsilon^2$, below $b_0$. This is why the series route needs far less spectral information than a weighted-supremum route (Section 6).

## 3. The theorem

**Hypotheses.** H0 (certificate B0): an exact balanced ladder exists with $(\delta,\varepsilon)$ in a certified box $P$, $\delta\ge2$, $\varepsilon\le\frac12$, at $g=10^{-3}$. H1 (certificate B1): there are $0<\lambda_{\rm lo}<\lambda_{\rm hi}<\varepsilon$ such that $\det\Delta_\pi(\lambda_{\rm lo})$ and $\det\Delta_\pi(\lambda_{\rm hi})$ have opposite signs; then $\Delta_\pi$ has a real zero $\lambda_*\in(\lambda_{\rm lo},\lambda_{\rm hi})$ of its (real) determinant, and there is a real unit vector $\mathbf c$ with $\Delta_\pi(\lambda_*)\mathbf c=0$. H3 (certificate B3): upper bounds $\bar b_0,\bar b_1$ for $b_0(2\lambda_{\rm lo})$, $b_1(2\lambda_{\rm lo})$ with $h_0=4\lambda_{\rm lo}^2+\varepsilon^2-\bar b_0-2\lambda_{\rm lo}\bar b_1>0$. Analytic condition: $r_p\in(0,\tfrac14]$ and $\bar\vartheta$ satisfy R1 and R2 for all parameters in $P$. Put

$$
\eta_0=\min\Bigl(\frac{r_p}4,\ \frac{h_0\,r_p^2}{256\,M}\Bigr).
$$

**Theorem (conditional ancient departure).** Under these hypotheses, for every $\eta\in(0,\eta_0]$ there is a unique $\mathbf v\in\mathcal W_0^4$ with coefficients only of order $n\ge2$ and $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert\le\eta$ such that $\mathbf p=\eta w(\mathbf c,-\mathbf c)+\mathbf v$ solves (2.1) for all $n$. Its coefficients are real, $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert\le\eta/4$, $\sum_nn\lvert\mathbf v_n\rvert\le\eta/2$ and $\sum_nn^2\lvert\mathbf v_n\rvert\le\eta$. The class history with $\mathbf u_r(t)=\mathbf p_r(e^{\lambda_*t})$, $t\le0$, has the following properties.

1. **Exact solution.** It solves the unchanged Master Equation at every member of the infinite ladder and at every time $t\le0$, with absolutely convergent sums over all sources, exactly one causal root per source and no self root.
2. **Approach along the growing mode.** For $t\le0$, $\mathbf u_0(t)=\eta e^{\lambda_*t}\mathbf c+\mathbf y_0(t)$ and $\mathbf u_1(t)=-\eta e^{\lambda_*t}\mathbf c+\mathbf y_1(t)$ with $\lvert\mathbf y_r(t)\rvert\le\lVert\mathbf v\rVert e^{2\lambda_*t}\le\frac{64M}{h_0r_p^2}\eta^2e^{2\lambda_*t}$, and the same bound for $\lvert\mathbf y_r'(t)\rvert/\lambda_*$ with the factor $2$ and for $\lvert\mathbf y_r''(t)\rvert/\lambda_*^2$ with the factor $4$.
3. **Admissibility.** For all $t\le0$: $\lvert\mathbf u_r\rvert\le\frac32\eta$, $\lvert\mathbf u_r'\rvert\le\frac32\lambda_*\eta$, every member speed is at most $\varepsilon(1+2\eta)+2\lambda_*\eta<1$, every equal-time separation between distinct members is at least $\min(2,\delta)-3\eta>0$, every source keeps exactly one causal root with $D\ge1-\varepsilon(1+2\eta)-2\lambda_*\eta$, and no member has a positive-delay self root.
4. **Departure.** For $L>0$ the shifted history $\mathbf z_L(t)=\mathbf z(t-L)$, $t\le L$, is again an exact complete-history solution in the class, with $\mathsf N_0(\mathbf z_L)\le4\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}$ and, at time $L$, $\varepsilon^{-1}\lvert\mathbf u_0'(L)\rvert\ge\lambda_*\eta/(2\varepsilon)$. Consequently $\mathsf N_L(\mathbf z_L-\mathbf o)\ge\lambda_*\eta/(2\varepsilon)$ for every $\mathbf o\in\mathcal O$. Taking $\eta=\eta_0$: there are exact complete-history solutions starting at time $0$ within any prescribed $\mathsf N_0$-distance of the balance that, at a finite time, are at $\mathsf N$-distance at least $d_*=\lambda_*\eta_0/(2\varepsilon)$ from every phase-rotated copy of it.

*Proof.* *Fixed point.* Write $\mathbf p=\eta w\mathbf c_{\mathcal C}+\mathbf v$ with $\mathbf c_{\mathcal C}=(\mathbf c,-\mathbf c)$, so $\lVert\eta w\mathbf c_{\mathcal C}\rVert=\eta$ and $\Delta_{\mathcal C}(\lambda_*)\mathbf c_{\mathcal C}=0$ (it is $\mathbf c_-=\mathbf c$, $\mathbf c_+=0$). By Lemma A6 the order-one equation of (2.1) holds, and the remaining equations are $\mathbf v=\mathcal T(\mathbf v):=\mathcal R\,\mathcal N(\eta w\mathbf c_{\mathcal C}+\mathbf v)$. On the ball $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert\le\eta$ we have $\lVert\mathbf p\rVert\le\rho:=2\eta\le r_p/2$, so $(r_p-\rho)^2\ge r_p^2/4$ and Lemmas A6 and A8 give $\lVert\mathcal T(\mathbf v)\rVert\le B_0\cdot32M\eta^2/r_p^2\le64M\eta^2/(h_0r_p^2)\le\eta/4$ and $\operatorname{Lip}\mathcal T\le B_0\cdot32M\eta/r_p^2\le\frac14$, because $\eta\le h_0r_p^2/(256M)$. The contraction principle gives the unique fixed point, with $\lVert\mathbf v\rVert\le\eta/4$. The derivative sums follow from $B_1\le4/h_0$ and $B_2\le8/h_0$ in the same way: $\sum_nn\lvert\mathbf v_n\rvert\le128M\eta^2/(h_0r_p^2)\le\eta/2$ and $\sum_nn^2\lvert\mathbf v_n\rvert\le\eta$. All data are real ($\Delta_{\mathcal C}$ is real at real points, the row maps have real Taylor coefficients, $\mathbf c$ is real), so the conjugate of the fixed point is a fixed point in the same ball and equals it. *Solution.* By Lemma A3(e) the history is $C^2$ on $t\le0$ and the series identity (2.1), evaluated at $w=e^{\lambda_*t}\in(0,1]$, is (1.1) at time $t$, provided the analytic rows evaluated at $w$ are the canonical rows of the history. They are: at real $w$ the series $\tau_j+\vartheta_j(w)$ is real, positive (it differs from $\tau_j\ge\tau_{\min}$ by at most $\bar\vartheta<\tau_{\min}$), and satisfies $(\tau_j+\vartheta_j)^2=\lvert\mathbf r_j\rvert^2$ with the true delayed positions $\mathbf u_{k'}(t-\tau)=\mathbf p_{k'}(we^{-\lambda_*\tau})$; so it is a causal root, and by Lemma A2 (whose hypotheses are item 3) it is the only one. The analytic $\kappa_j$ evaluated at $w$ is $\tau D$ with $D>0$, which is $\tau\lvert D\rvert$. No self root exists (Lemma A2). The row sum converges absolutely (Lemma A5). Lemma A1 then gives the full law. *Asymptotics and admissibility.* For $t\le0$, $\lvert\mathbf y_r(t)\rvert\le\sum_{n\ge2}\lvert\mathbf v_n\rvert e^{n\lambda_*t}\le e^{2\lambda_*t}\lVert\mathbf v\rVert$, similarly for derivatives; $\lvert\mathbf u_r\rvert\le\eta+\lVert\mathbf v\rVert$ and $\lvert\mathbf u_r'\rvert\le\lambda_*(\eta+\sum n\lvert\mathbf v_n\rvert)$. A member's inertial velocity is $Q(\cdot)[\mathbf u'+\varepsilon J(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf u)]$, giving the speed bound. Distinct members of the same rung are separated by $\lvert2\mathbf e_1+2\mathbf u\rvert\ge2-3\eta$ in-plane, and members of different rungs by at least $\delta$ axially, since in-plane displacements do not change heights. *Departure.* The reduced equation is autonomous, so $\mathbf z_L$ solves it on $t\le L$; physically this is the original history shifted in time and rotated about the axis, which the law permits. For $s\le0$, $\lvert\mathbf u_r(s-L)\rvert\le e^{-\lambda_*L}\lVert\mathbf p\rVert\le2\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}$ and $\lvert\mathbf u_r'(s-L)\rvert\le\frac32\lambda_*\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}$, so $\mathsf N_0(\mathbf z_L)\le(2+\frac32\lambda_*/\varepsilon)\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}\le4\eta e^{-\lambda_*L}$ using $\lambda_*<\varepsilon$. At time $L$, $\lvert\mathbf u_0'(L)\rvert=\lvert\mathbf u_0'(0)\rvert\ge\lambda_*(\eta-\sum_nn\lvert\mathbf v_{0,n}\rvert)\ge\lambda_*\eta/2$. Every $\mathbf o\in\mathcal O$ has zero velocity in the rotating frame. $\blacksquare$

**What is closed and what is not.** Lemmas A1 to A8 and the theorem are proved as stated; the proofs are self-contained (Banach fixed point, Cauchy estimates, the Wiener algebra, Lemma A2) and import no finite-delay theorem. What remains open is only the numerical premises B0, B1, B3. The adjudicated first-variation formula is used through Lemma A7.

## 6. Choice of route, and what a weighted-supremum route would need

The suggested route was a contraction in a space such as $\sup_{t\le T}e^{-2\lambda_*t}\lvert\mathbf y(t)\rvert$ with derivatives. It can be carried out, but it needs strictly more. (a) Linear inverse: solving $\mathbf y''+\cdots=\mathbf f$ with $\lvert\mathbf f\rvert\lesssim e^{\beta t}$ in that space is convolution with the inverse transform of $\Delta_{\mathcal C}(\mu)^{-1}$ along $\operatorname{Re}\mu=\beta$, $\lambda_*<\beta\le2\lambda_*$. For the solution on $(-\infty,T]$ to need only the past of $\mathbf f$, the kernel must be causal, which requires no zero of $\det\Delta_{\mathcal C}$ in the closed half-plane $\operatorname{Re}\mu\ge\beta$ (a compact region by the adjudication's root bound, but complex). For a supremum bound the kernel times $e^{-\beta t}$ must be integrable; a sufficient certificate is an explicit bound on $\lVert\Delta_{\mathcal C}(\beta+iy)^{-1}\rVert$ and on its $y$-derivative for all real $y$, with the large-$\lvert y\rvert$ behaviour $\lVert\Delta^{-1}\rVert\le C/\lvert\mu\rvert^2$ from the dominance of $\mu^2$ over $b_0+b_1\lvert\mu\rvert$; the derivative involves $\sum_j\tau_je^{-\beta\tau_j}\lVert L_{w,j}\rVert$, which converges only because of the exponential weight (the unweighted sum $\sum_j\tau_j/\tau_j^2$ diverges logarithmically). (b) Nonlinearity: the map $\mathbf y\mapsto\mathbf y'(t-\tau(\mathbf y))$ is not Lipschitz from $C^1$ to $C^0$; one contracts in a weighted $C^1$ norm on a set bounded in weighted $C^{1,1}$, which is closed under $C^1$ limits, and recovers $C^2$ from the equation. Both are standard but each adds a certificate or a constant. The series route replaces (a) by real points only, where Lemma A8 needs no root search, and replaces (b) by analyticity strictly inside the disk (Lemma A3(c)). Its cost is that it constructs only the one-dimensional family of ancient histories tangent to $\lambda_*$, which is what a departure theorem needs.

## 7. What is not shown, and falsifiers

**Not shown.** No statement outside the class $\mathcal C$, in particular nothing about axial, $p=-1$ or general-$q$ perturbations, although the class is closed under the law and its histories are lawful histories of the full ladder. No unstable manifold, no parametrization of all departing histories, and uniqueness only inside the coefficient ball. No statement after the departure time, about the eventual fate, or about a finite segment. Nothing at other radii, although the same proof applies verbatim at any radius where B0, B1, B3 hold with $\delta\ge2$ (inferred that they do at all small speeds, since $b_0$, $\lambda_*$ and $\varepsilon$ all scale as their small-speed limits; not checked). No claim that the departure amplitude is optimal.

**Falsifiers.**
- A source in a subwake history with positive equal-time separation and two causal roots, or a member with a positive-delay self root, would refute Lemma A2.
- A real $\mu\ge2\lambda_{\rm lo}$ at which either certified block is singular, while B3 holds, would refute Lemma A8; the float evaluation in `class-spectrum-float-N20000.json` is where to compare.
- An in-plane to axial entry of $\Delta(\mu;q,+1)$ at $q\in\{0,\pi\}$ that does not vanish beyond rounding would refute the block reduction of Lemma A7 and the closure of the class; the receipt records below $1.5\times10^{-17}\varepsilon^2$.
- With certified inputs, failure of R1 or R2 over $P$ in `departure_constants.py` at every $r_p$ would block the theorem as stated (not refute it).
- Operator-checkable consistency test of the construction (not evidence for it): compute $\mathbf v_n$ for $n\le N$ by the recursion $\mathbf v_n=\Delta_{\mathcal C}(n\lambda_*)^{-1}[\mathcal N(\mathbf p)]_n$, build the truncated history, evaluate the exact law with every causal root solved at a time $t\le0$; a residual that does not fall like $\eta^{N+1}e^{(N+1)\lambda_*t}$ would show an error in the expansion or in Lemma A7.
- A certified enclosure in B1 with no sign change in any bracket near $0.98743\,\varepsilon$, or a certified $h_0\le0$ in B3, would remove the corresponding hypothesis.
