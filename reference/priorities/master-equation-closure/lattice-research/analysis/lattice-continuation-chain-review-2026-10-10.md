**L-3. The later curved-path theorem does not literally apply to this branch; its proof does, with the grouped stationary field as the remainder.** The [closure review](../../analysis/closure-architecture-review-2026-10-10.md) left open whether the theorem of 2026-10-05 could be applied to a lattice branch, since its hypothesis of bounded continuous rows from the others would have to be proved there. Item 9 above is that proof for this branch, and the hypotheses on the receiver are certified: its whole past is below wake speed, and it arrives with $v\cdot a>1.46$, which gives the positive linear rate.

**That is not enough, and my first version of this finding was wrong to conclude that the theorem applies.** Codex read the theorem's continuation class and found the condition I had skipped: the class requires that, at almost every time, "the sum of rows converges absolutely". On the infinite alternating lattice it does not. A stationary member at anchor $n$ contributes a row of magnitude $g/|X_i(t)-n|^2$, and the $R^3$ anchors with every coordinate between $R$ and $2R-1$ each contribute at least $g/(2\sqrt3R+|X_i(t)|)^2$, so the magnitudes over that cube alone sum to at least a constant times $R$. The lattice's field is defined by the eight-source grouped sum, in which alternating signs cancel; the individual magnitudes are not summable. So no continuation on this lattice is in Theorem U's class as written, and the theorem as stated says nothing here. The later note also normalizes the coupling to one, where this history has $g=16$.

**What does carry over is the proof.** I went back through it to see where absolute convergence is used. It is used only to say what the acceleration is. Every step after that uses two facts: that the acceleration is the sum of a remainder, bounded near the event and continuous at it with forward component at least $a$, and the own-path rows; and that each own-path row is a positive multiple of a chord direction within the forward cone. The lemmas on chord directions and on the existence of the newborn own-path root are geometry of the receiver's own path and involve no sum over other members. With the grouped stationary field plus the finitely many changed rows taken as the declared remainder, and the own-path rows, which are all of one sign and so converge absolutely among themselves, the chain runs unchanged: speed excess at least $at/2$, a newborn root of delay squared at most $12t\varepsilon/a$, and, with coupling $g$ restored, $(\varepsilon^2)'\ge5ga/(6t)$, which contradicts a bounded excess. **Derived, as an adaptation of the later proof to a declared grouped remainder; it is not the later theorem, and it has had no review beyond Codex's statement that the mechanism survives.** It is also not needed: the lattice investigation's own weaker-continuation theorem is stated for exactly this formulation, with the cross field as a continuous remainder and the original stationary summation retained, and it is the theorem that covers this event.

*Grade:* derived, for the non-applicability (Codex's argument, which I checked) and for the adaptation. *Consequence:* the event of Section 1.24 is covered by the lattice investigation's family. The later theorem covers finite arrangements such as the released balances, where the row sum is finite. The two families share a mechanism and their domains differ in exactly this respect. Whether the two proofs were constructed separately remains unknown, as the closure review says. *Falsifier:* for the non-applicability, a proof that the individual rows of the stationary lattice are absolutely summable, or a statement of Theorem U's class that admits a grouped field; for the adaptation, a step of the later proof that uses summability of the others' rows beyond defining the acceleration; for the bounded-rows hypothesis, a member whose row on the arriving member is discontinuous at the event. The place to look for the last is a member that is itself at speed one at the same instant, which the certificate does not exclude since the first member is unresolved among twelve.

That last clause is worth stating plainly, because it would be a real gap in the bounded-rows statement and not a formality: if two of the twelve candidates reach speed one at the same instant, each is a transmitter with a vanishing margin for the other only if its velocity points along their line of centres at speed one, since the transmitter factor is one minus the component of its velocity along that line. With ranges above $0.469$ and emission at least $0.469$ before the event, the sampled emission is strictly earlier than the event, where every speed is below one. So the transmitter factor stays positive even in that case, and the row stays continuous. **Derived; the gap closes.**

## What this review does and does not establish

It establishes that the chain's logic is sound where a hand reconstruction can reach, that its stages hand over consistently, that its population counts are right, and that the open reproducibility item is not on the path to the event.

It does not establish the event. The event's truth rests on numerical enclosures that I have not recomputed, and on the correctness of interval and rational arithmetic in scripts I have not read. A reader should take the event as derived by its authors with their stated checks, and as reviewed here for structure, joins and lemmas.
