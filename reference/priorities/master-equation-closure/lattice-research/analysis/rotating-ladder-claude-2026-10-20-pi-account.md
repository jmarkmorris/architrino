### 2. Exact solutions that leave the balance: a nonlinear departure theorem

**Result (derived by computer-assisted proof; the corrected theorem and its certified constants independently reviewed by Codex and accepted, conditional only on the correctness of the interval library and on the reviewed premises).** The full treatment is the [history-flow note](rotating-ladder-claude-2026-10-20-history-flow.md). Under three numerical premises at $R=1000$ (B0: the certified balance of Section 1; B1: a sign change of a real two-by-two determinant bracketing the growing root $\lambda_*\approx0.98743\,\omega$; B3: two explicit lattice-sum bounds), there are exact complete-history solutions of the unchanged Master Equation, at every member of the infinite ladder and at every past time, that tend to the balanced ladder in the far past along the growing mode and leave it. Time-shifted copies start arbitrarily close to the balance, in a norm that takes the supremum of position and velocity over the entire past, and reach a fixed positive distance from every rigidly rotated copy of the balance at a finite time. Earlier ladder work had only formal growing modes of the first variation; this theorem, once its premises are certified, connects one of them to actual nonlinear motion.

**The history class.** The construction works among histories with three symmetries: translation along the axis by two rung spacings, the half turn combined with exchange of polarities, and reflection of the axis. In that class every member stays in the plane of its rung, a member's displacement in its own rotating frame depends only on the parity of its rung, and the infinite population reduces to two independent in-plane displacement histories, each receiving infinite lattice-image sums. The class contains exactly the in-plane perturbations with wavenumber $0$ or $\pi$ in the sector in which the two members of a rung move alike, and it is closed under the nonlinear law because the law is equivariant under these isometries.

**The mechanism of the proof.** The solution is written as a power series in $w=e^{\lambda_*t}$, convergent with absolutely summable coefficients for $t\le0$, so it is an ancient history that decays to the balance as $t\to-\infty$. Substituting into the law, the coefficient of $w^n$ obeys a linear equation whose matrix is the delayed characteristic matrix evaluated at the real point $n\lambda_*$, forced by products of lower coefficients. The first coefficient is the growing mode itself. The remaining ones exist and stay small by a contraction argument, provided the characteristic matrix is invertible, with a bound, at the real points $2\lambda_*,3\lambda_*,\dots$. On the real axis the inertial part $(\mu+\omega J)^2$ has smallest singular value $\mu^2+\omega^2$, which grows with $\mu$ and dominates the delayed coupling for every $\mu\ge2\lambda_*$; this is certificate B3, and no search for roots elsewhere in the complex plane is needed. Two features particular to the infinite ladder are handled explicitly. First, the population and memory are infinite: rung $m$ is received with a delay of about $\lvert m\rvert d$, but every row and every one of its derivatives with respect to the history is bounded by a constant times the inverse square of the delay, which is summable over a one-dimensional lattice, uniformly in how far back the history is sampled. Second, the delays depend on the solution: a delayed evaluation of the history is the series evaluated at $we^{-\lambda_*\tau}$, strictly inside the disk of convergence, where its derivative is bounded by its size; so the moving causal root never costs a derivative. Each source keeps exactly one causal root, no member acquires a self root, every member stays strictly below wake speed and separations stay positive throughout.

**Limits.** The statement is nonlinear only within the declared class, norm and series class. It says nothing about perturbations outside the class (axial, other wavenumbers, the other sector), finite segments, other departing histories, or the eventual fate of the departing solutions. The departure distance from the illustrative constants is small (about $6\times10^{-6}$ in the norm's units); it is a fixed positive distance reached from arbitrarily small starts, which is what instability requires, not a statement that the departure is large. ### 4. Why delay makes neutral oscillations grow: the leading coefficient

**Result (derived, with an interval-enclosed constant and a remainder whose order is derived with one corrected bound and one derivative estimate in outline; measured agreement with exact roots; independently reviewed by Codex, which accepted $\Delta_1$, the shift formula and $c_1$, reproducing $c_1=5.37627120303651884164\ldots$ at 60 digits, with corrections recorded in the note).** The full treatment is the [delayed-growth note](rotating-ladder-claude-2026-10-20-delayed-small-speed-growth.md). In the zero-delay comparison the ladder has neutral oscillations; the adjudication measured that the delay makes them grow at a rate proportional to the member speed $\varepsilon=\omega R$, with ratio near $5.4$ for the in-step oscillation (wavenumber $0$, the two members of each rung moving alike). That ratio is now derived. Measuring time in units of $1/\omega$ and length in units of $R$, the characteristic matrix is the zero-delay matrix plus $\varepsilon$ times an explicit, absolutely convergent first-order matrix, plus a remainder of order $\varepsilon^2\log(1/\varepsilon)$, the logarithm arising where the delay phase of distant rungs reaches order one. First-order perturbation of a simple neutral root $i\Omega_0$ gives the growth rate. For the in-step oscillation the zero-delay frequency satisfies $\Omega_0^2=4-24T_5/S_0$ with $S_0=0.19153459$ the static inward sum and $T_5$ an explicit lattice sum, $\Omega_0=0.591281818$, and

$$
\frac{\operatorname{Re}\lambda}{\omega}=c_1\varepsilon+O\bigl(\varepsilon^2\log\tfrac1\varepsilon\bigr),\qquad c_1=\frac{\beta_*\delta_*\,(2-\Omega_0^2)}{2S_0\,\Omega_0^2}\in[5.3762712022,\ 5.3762712038],
$$

with $\beta_*$ and $\delta_*$ the slope and root of the first-order balance bracket. The tangential part of the first-order feedback for this mode is exactly the balance bracket, which vanishes at the balanced spacing; the growth comes entirely from the radial part.

*Mechanism.* Through the delay each source's velocity, as seen in the non-rotating frame one delay late, enters the receiver's acceleration; a smaller part enters through the dependence of the transmitter-side factor $D$ on the source velocity. For the in-step oscillation the radial part of this feedback acts as a drag on the radial velocity in the non-rotating frame, and the rung partner supplies 57 percent of it. Because the in-step oscillation has $\Omega_0^2<2$ and an elongated ellipse (tangential to radial amplitude ratio $2/\Omega_0=3.38$), its radial velocity in the non-rotating frame is opposite in phase to its radial velocity in the rotating frame, so the drag does positive work on a mode of positive rotating-frame energy. The growth rate is that work divided by twice the energy, and it is large because the mode's energy-like (Krein) quantity is small.

*Measured agreement.* Exact delayed roots from the worker's own evaluator (sums to $2\times10^5$ rungs, balance solved with its own rows) give $\operatorname{Re}\lambda/(\omega\varepsilon)=5.376246$, $5.375631$, $5.373712$, $5.148118$ and $4.976951$ at $\varepsilon=10^{-4}$, $5\times10^{-4}$, $10^{-3}$, $10^{-2}$ and $0.0138$, approaching $c_1$; the adjudication's measured rates are reproduced to every printed digit. The fit indicates no $\varepsilon^2$ or $\varepsilon^2\log$ term in the real part and a large cubic coefficient (about $-2564$), so the linear law is accurate only below $\varepsilon\approx0.01$ (inferred). The alternating-rung oscillation (sector with the two members of a rung moving oppositely, wavenumber $0$) has $c_1=0.3274585$ by a similar closed form.
