## Important changes to the proposed implications

The leading-plane calculation assumes $X_{i,x}(T)=UT+\chi_i$ throughout the relevant past. It cannot be applied to a carrier with internal axial motion merely because its center moves at $c_f$. Recurrent equal-offset contacts also persist as singularities at every reception time; they are not covered by a simple statement that ordinary roots are absent. The assessment therefore declines the unrestricted claim that every isolated self-bound carrier must have $c_\gamma<c_f$, and declines the claim that identifying observed light speed with $c_f$ leaves an environment as the only possible route. An environment can alter received accelerations, but alternative geometry and the unresolved singular domain also lie outside this proof.

The regular-hexagon anti-periodicity argument uses the complete all-past source histories and equal opposite angular-frequency magnitudes. The app's general inner/middle/outer layer paths need not meet those assumptions. A finite history truncation or incomplete root set can destroy the source-index bijection artificially; a numerical nonzero average from such a truncation would not overturn the theorem. A full ordinary ledger violating the exact bijection would.

For $U>0$ and plane gap $d>0$, the sign tests are

$$
US_L+dH_L=0,\qquad US_T-dH_T=0,
$$

with $S_L,S_T$ including both same-ring and cross-ring roots. Only after checking $S_L,S_T<0$ do the displayed equations imply $H_L>0$ and $H_T<0$. Otherwise use the equations directly. This distinction prevents treating the isolated-ring certificate as a numerical result about the full twelve-member pair.
