# Carrier and constituent speeds

A translating photon candidate carries internal motion as well as motion of its center. These two motions determine each constituent's absolute path and therefore which earlier emissions can reach it. The distinction is kinematic; a retained photon history still has to satisfy the Master Equation.

## Photon and field-speed separation

A photon-channel carrier has centerline propagation speed $c_\gamma$, while its constituent histories can include transverse internal motion. In an orthogonal idealization,

$$
\mathbf v_a
=
c_\gamma\hat{\mathbf e}+\mathbf v_\perp,
\qquad
\|\mathbf v_a\|^2
=
c_\gamma^2+\|\mathbf v_\perp\|^2.
$$

Plainly: an observer-facing carrier speed and a constituent's absolute path speed are different quantities. The causal-root ledger must expose any self-hit, partner-hit, inactive, or caustic rows created by the constituent histories.

Here $\hat{\mathbf e}$ is the fixed unit direction of translation, $c_\gamma$ is the carrier-center speed, $\mathbf v_a$ is one constituent's absolute velocity, and $\mathbf v_\perp$ is its internal velocity perpendicular to that direction. Taking the Euclidean squared norm of the velocity sum gives the displayed identity because the cross term vanishes. If the internal motion has a component along the translation direction, that cross term must be retained. Primitive wake speed $c_f$ remains a separate parameter; numerical instantiations use $c_f=1$.

**Claim grade: derived kinematic identity under the stated orthogonality condition.** The check is the squared norm of the supplied absolute velocity. A nonzero translation–internal cross term invalidates the reduced squared-speed expression for that preparation. The identity supplies no speed ceiling, retained branch, or equality between observed light speed and constituent speed.
