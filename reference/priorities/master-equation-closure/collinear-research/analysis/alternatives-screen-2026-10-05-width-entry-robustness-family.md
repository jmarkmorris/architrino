# A nontrivial compatible family near the original width preparation

The [relative-openness theorem](alternatives-screen-2026-10-05-width-entry-robustness.md) gives a neighborhood within a constrained history class. To ensure this does not merely name an isolated compatible point, vary the held half-separation $a$ near $1/2$ while retaining the same law and $\delta=1/2048$:

$$
x_{a,A}(s)=a\quad(s\le-\delta),\qquad
x_{a,A}(s)=a-\frac{A(s+\delta)^3}{6\delta}\quad(-\delta\le s\le0).
$$

The other particle is its negative. The exact release compatibility equation is $A=\mathcal F(a,A)$, where the complete width functional includes both self and partner channels. The original uniform contraction estimate on $A\in[0,2]$ is independent of the translation parameter $a$: varying $A$ changes the same small cubic patch by at most $\delta^2|\Delta A|/6$, and the global functional Lipschitz constant is geometry-independent.

For $49/100\le a\le51/100$, all release partner bands remain in the held tail and contribute $f_\rho(2a-d_0)$, where $d_0=A\delta^2/6$. The self window has $0\le R\le d_0$ and source gap derivative at least $1-\delta$, since $A\le2$. Therefore

$$
0<\mathcal F(a,A)\le
\frac1{(98/100-\delta^2/3)^2}
+\frac{\delta^2}{6(1-\delta)\rho^3}<2
$$

for both selected cores. The last inequality follows by direct rational comparison at the smaller core $\rho=1/64$; the other is smaller. The stationary partner band is separated from the short patch by more than $9/10$ in source age, whereas the patch lasts less than $1/1000$.

The contraction therefore supplies a unique compatible $A(a)\in(0,2)$ throughout this interval, continuous in $a$ by parameter-dependent contraction. These histories have distinct held positions and strictly positive release speeds. At $a=1/2$ they recover the exact original preparation, so a sufficiently small open interval of $a$ inherits any correctly certified strict contact-speed escape margin. This supplies a nonconstant compatible family for the relative-openness conclusion; no explicit width of the escaping $a$ interval is claimed.
