## Complete postfold clocks and acceleration

$$
Q(S_p)=L,\qquad P(S_s)=L,\qquad L=P(T)=T+x(T).
$$

The decreasing postbirth $P$ branch has $P(S)>P(T)$ for $t_c<S<T$, so it supplies no earlier self root. Increasing $Q$ supplies only the excluded current diagonal for the positive self family. The current postfold interval also supplies neither partner direction by the cross-map gaps established at contact. The complete census is therefore one partner and one self root.

Both distances are negative. Put $p_s=1+v(S_s)>0$, $q_p=1-v(S_p)>0$. The exact acceleration is

$$
A(T,L)=k\left[\frac{T-S_p(L)}{q_p(L)}-\frac{T-S_s(L)}{p_s(L)}\right].
$$

The partner row is positive and brakes negative velocity; the self row is negative. The sign of their sum is not fixed merely by those two channel names. A necessary and sufficient pointwise braking condition in this chart is

$$
\frac{T-S_p(L)}{q_p(L)}>\frac{T-S_s(L)}{p_s(L)}.
$$

For each fixed clock target $L$, acceleration is affine in reception time:

$$
A(T,L)=\alpha(L)T+\beta(L),\qquad \alpha=k(q_p^{-1}-p_s^{-1}),\qquad \beta=k(S_s/p_s-S_p/q_p).

These profiles use the entire already evolved precontact source history. They permit an event-aware sign certificate or independent falsifier without sweeping a new physical parameter.

The receiver coordinate $L$ decreases as long as $v<-1$. With $w=-(1+v)>0$, $Y=w^2$, its exact evolution is

The sign in $Y_L$ follows from $L_T=-w$ and $w_T=-A$. This system has no new response factor. It remains a regular chart while $Y>0$ and the two source inverses are regular. At an upward speed-one crossing $Y\to0$ a different event chart is required.

$$
T-S_p=-x-a>0,\qquad d=x+a<0;\qquad

## Existence of both upward-crossing branches and uniqueness obstruction

The multiplicity theorem requires a complete given pre-event history, a one-sided $C^3$ source minimum with positive left curvature, and no other new clock extremum at the same event. The surviving partner and old self roots must have positive delays bounded away from zero, nonzero source denominators, and locally Lipschitz acceleration as functions of receiver time and position. The receiver position is negative and its $Q$ clock is above the earlier global $P$ maximum by a positive gap. Before the event, $P$ decreases from that earlier maximum, while $Q$ increases throughout the whole history. These conditions establish the one-partner/one-self incoming census and the one-partner/two-self outgoing census. A present-state acceleration value alone does not establish them.

The continuation class is explicit: position and velocity are continuous across the event, velocity is locally absolutely continuous, and acceleration has finite one-sided traces. The complete root equation holds on the punctured incoming and outgoing intervals and in integrated acceleration form across the event. The right trace $b$ generally differs from incoming $H$, so a two-sided classical second derivative at the event is not asserted. This is the same finite-trace event class used at first self birth. Multiplicity here neither establishes uniqueness nor classifies all possibilities outside that class.

Let $B_L>0$ be the actual left curvature of the retained source minimum, and $H>0$ the smooth surviving older-root acceleration at the event. For an exact incoming solution $H=B_L=B$. To expose supplied-history interpolation discrepancies, keep them distinct initially. The new negative self row gives

$$
b=H-\frac{k}{B_L}-\frac{k}{\sqrt{B_Lb}},\qquad b>0.
$$

Put $u=\sqrt b$. The right trace exists if $H$ reaches the minimum of $u^2+k/B_L+k/(\sqrt{B_L}u)$. That minimum occurs at $u^3=k/(2\sqrt{B_L})$ and is

$$
H_{\rm crit}=\frac{k}{B_L}+3\left(\frac{k}{2\sqrt{B_L}}\right)^{2/3}.
$$

For $H>H_{\rm crit}$ there are exactly two positive roots, since the function diverges at both ends and has exactly one critical minimum. At equality there is a double root; below it there is none. When $H=B_L$, this agrees with the factorized exact-history condition $B^2\geq4k$.

Define the post-event source coordinate $q=-S>0$, the positive source slope magnitude $p(q)=-(1+v(-q))=B_Lq+O(q^2)$, future $w=1+v>0$, and local reception time $T>0$. The exact self-arrival identity is $P(T)=P(-q)$. It gives

$$
T_q=p/w,\qquad (w^2)_q=2pA_{\rm old}(T,x)-2k(T+q).
$$

Here $A_{\rm old}$ includes the surviving partner and earlier self root, both retained; its event value is $H$. The new self row is the displayed negative term. With $z=T/q$, $W=w/q$, $L=p/q$, the regular-singular system has limiting fixed points

$$
z_0=B_L/W_0,\qquad W_0^2=HB_L-k(z_0+1),\qquad W_0^2=B_Lb.
$$

The Jacobian is $J=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}-1&-B_L/W_0^2\\-k/W_0&-2\end{smallmatrix}\right)$, with trace $-3$ and determinant $2-kB_L/W_0^3$. The larger trace root has positive determinant and negative eigenvalue real parts. The smaller trace root has negative determinant and two real eigenvalues $\lambda_-<0<\lambda_+$. Both fixed points generate actual local solutions, not only formal trace candidates.

For the larger root, the earlier bounded integral contraction proves existence and uniqueness in its fixed-point branch. For the smaller root a different contraction supplies existence and a free parameter. Write $y=(z-z_0,W-W_0)$, use logarithmic time $\eta=\log(q/q_0)\leq0$, and diagonalize $J$. In its negative and positive eigencoordinates $a,c$, the nonlinear remainder $F(\eta,y)$ is $O(q_0e^\eta)+O(\|y\|^2)$, with an arbitrarily small Lipschitz constant after shrinking the tube. For each sufficiently small terminal positive-eigendirection value $c(0)=c_0$, solve

$$
a(\eta)=\int_{-\infty}^{\eta}e^{\lambda_-(\eta-s)}F_-(s,y(s))\,ds,
$$

$$
c(\eta)=e^{\lambda_+\eta}c_0-\int_{\eta}^{0}e^{\lambda_+(\eta-s)}F_+(s,y(s))\,ds.
$$

Choose $0<\sigma<\min(1,\lambda_+)$ and the norm $\sup_{\eta\leq0}e^{-\sigma\eta}\|y(\eta)\|$. The two integral operators have difference bounds proportional to $\delta/(\sigma-\lambda_-)$ and $\delta/(\lambda_+-\sigma)$, where $\delta$ is the remainder's local Lipschitz constant. The forcing is finite in this norm because $q_0e^\eta$ decays faster than $e^{\sigma\eta}$. Taking $q_0$, the tube and $c_0$ sufficiently small makes the operator a contraction into the tube. Thus each small $c_0$ yields a solution tending to the smaller fixed point as $q\to0$. Different $c_0$ give different solutions since they have different terminal positive-eigendirection values.

In either branch, $z,W$ stay positive. Recovering $T(q)$ from the exact arrival relation gives $T_q=p/w>0$ and permits inversion. Position and velocity match the given incoming history continuously, and $v'(0+)=b>0$ is finite. The current self root is genuinely earlier, with negative displacement and its actual absolute source weight. All older roots persist by the assumed positive-delay and denominator margins, and clock gaps exclude other immediate events. The complete ledger is one partner and two self roots. No velocity impulse or response alteration is used.

Consequently, for $H>H_{\rm crit}$ there are at least two locally admissible continuous-velocity continuations with different right acceleration traces, and the smaller-trace branch additionally carries a local family in this smooth source-coordinate class. For an exact history with $B>2\sqrt k$, this is a **derived obstruction to unique event evolution**. Promoting a later outer turn or return requires naming its branch; a numerical solver's choice is not an additional selector supplied by the law.
