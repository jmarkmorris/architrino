# Radial powers: a long-range threshold and compatible outward histories

Use the selected Section 15 radial law with $K=R_*=c_f=1$, every original self/partner root, two opposite-polarity collinear labels, and exponent $p\ge1$. The [initial screen](alternatives-screen-2026-10-05-collinear.md) establishes first-unit-before-contact for the explicit incoming preparations and the retained bounded-variation obstruction. The following results distinguish the $p=1$ control from the $p>1$ selected class.

## Complete strict-domain signs and finite boundary type

Start with a complete separated compatible uniformly subfield history, labeled $x_1>x_2$. On its maximal separated strict-subfield future interval there is exactly one partner root per receiver, no positive-delay self root, and

$$
v_1'=-Q_1<0,\qquad v_2'=Q_2>0,\qquad
Q_i=\frac1{R_i^pD_i}>0.
$$

At a finite proposed contact endpoint, the limiting root range must vanish. A positive limiting range would require a unit-average-speed chord in the partner history over an interval with strictly subfield interior, which is impossible. Thus $s_i\uparrow t_*$ and $R_i=t-s_i\le t_*-s_i$. The source-time change of variables gives

$$
Q_i\,dt=\frac{ds_i}{R_i^p[1-n_iv_i(t)]}
\ge\frac{ds_i}{2(t_*-s_i)^p}.
$$

This diverges for $p\ge1$, contradicting bounded subfield velocities and the one-signed velocity derivatives. The same argument covers contact simultaneous with first unit speed; the denominator bound uses only $|v_i|\le1$.

If finite separation stays positive up to a finite boundary, the ranges have a positive floor. Source times then remain in compact strictly earlier regular windows, because the complete old past has a uniform speed margin and the receiver positions remain bounded on finite intervals. All old source denominators retain a positive floor, so the usual method of steps continues the regular equation. Hence every finite maximal strict-domain endpoint is unit speed at positive present separation. This classification does not say that every such event is transverse or has the same continuation obstruction.

## The $p=1$ control must reach a finite unit event

Suppose instead the strict-domain solution persists for all future time. The monotone bounded individual velocities have limits $v_1^\infty,v_2^\infty$, and positive separation requires $v_1^\infty\ge v_2^\infty$. Since $v_1$ decreases and $v_2$ increases,

$$
-1<v_2(0)\le v_2^\infty\le v_1^\infty\le v_1(0)<1.
$$

Consequently the entire future retains a uniform subfield margin; combined with the supplied complete old-past margin, there is a common $\eta>0$ for every sampled history point. The root bound $R_i\le r/\eta$ and $D_i\le2-\eta$ gives

$$
Q_i\ge\frac{\eta^p}{(2-\eta)r^p}.
$$

But $r(t)\le r(0)+2t$. At $p=1$, the integral of this lower bound diverges logarithmically over the future. This contradicts the finite total variation of each bounded monotone velocity. Thus the $p=1$ law has a finite maximal strict-domain endpoint for every such compatible preparation; by the preceding classification it is unit speed at positive separation.

This is a class conclusion about the strict domain. It does not prove transverse crossing for every initial history, select a superfield continuation, or replace the more specific incoming-preparation bounded-variation result.

## An outward invariant for every $p>1$

In mirror geometry write the right member as $x>0$ with outward speed $v=x'$. Assume its complete past is nondecreasing and $x(s)\ge a>0$, $v(s)\ge0$. While the future remains outward, the partner root has $R=x(t)+x(s)\ge x(t)+a$ and transmitter factor $D=1+v(s)\ge1$. Hence

$$
v'=-\frac1{R^pD}\ge-\frac1{(x+a)^p}.
$$

The mathematical comparison function

$$
E=\frac{v^2}{2}-\frac{(x+a)^{1-p}}{p-1}
$$

satisfies $E'=v[v'+(x+a)^{-p}]\ge0$ while $v>0$. If $E(0)>0$ and the complete speed bound is below one, then $v$ cannot reach zero, remains at least $\sqrt{2E(0)}$, and decreases from its initial value. The global future therefore stays separated and uniformly subfield, with $x$ growing at least linearly. Its decreasing speed converges to a strictly positive terminal value. This gives a sufficient outward-scattering condition; the comparison function is not an imported physical energy or conservation premise.

## Explicit compatible histories for $p=3/2$ and $p=2$

Fix $a=100$ and $v_0=3/4$. Hold $x=a$ for $s\le-1$. On $[-1,0]$, with $q=s+1$, prescribe velocity

$$
v(s)=v_0(3q^2-2q^3)+Aq^2(1-q),
$$

and integrate from $x(-1)=a$. Then

$$
x(0)=a+v_0/2+A/12,\qquad v(0)=v_0,\qquad v'(0)=-A.
$$

The history joins the held tail with zero velocity and acceleration, is $C^{2,1}$, has nonnegative speed, and is uniformly subfield for $0<A<1/100$. Its release partner root lies in the held tail because its range is $R_0=2a+v_0/2+A/12>200$. Choose the unique root

$$
A=(2a+v_0/2+A/12)^{-p},\qquad 0<A<1/100.
$$

Existence and uniqueness follow because the left side is strictly increasing and the right side strictly decreasing, with opposite endpoint signs. This is exact release acceleration compatibility. The chosen law coefficients stay fixed.

For both $p=3/2$ and $p=2$, the invariant has positive initial value:

$$
E(0)\ge\frac9{32}-\frac{200^{1-p}}{p-1}>0.
$$

Indeed at $p=3/2$ the subtracted term is $2/\sqrt{200}<1/7$, and at $p=2$ it is $1/200$. The reflected pair therefore has a unique all-future separated uniformly subfield solution with strictly positive outward terminal speed. This exact compatible preparation differs from the incoming preparation that reaches unit speed; both belong to the same fixed radial law.
