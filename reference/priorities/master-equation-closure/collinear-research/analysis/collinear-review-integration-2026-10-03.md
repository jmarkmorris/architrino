## Local leading-order delay balance

Set $c_f=1$. Consider a complete strictly subfield mirror history, with exactly one partner root and no positive-delay self roots. At the reception under consideration let $x\ne0$, $v=x'(T)$, $\sigma=\operatorname{sgn}x$, $R=T-S=|x(T)+x(S)|$ and a selected distance response $kR^n$, where $k>0$ and $n\in\mathbb R$. Work on the partner branch with displacement sign $\sigma$. The acceleration and instantaneous comparison potential are

$$
x''=-\sigma\frac{kR^n}{1+\sigma v(S)},\qquad
U'(x)=\sigma k(2|x|)^n,\qquad
E=\tfrac12v^2+U(x).
$$

$E$ is a mathematical comparison quantity, not an imported primitive mechanical energy. Negative powers require a nonzero-range chart; a statement across contact needs separate integrability analysis.

**Derived local lemma.** Suppose velocity is absolutely continuous on $[S,T]$, $|v(t)|\le\varepsilon\le1/8$ there, and $M R\le\varepsilon^2$, where $M$ bounds $|x''|$ almost everywhere on that source-to-receiver interval. Then

$$
x''=-\sigma k(2|x|)^n+(n+1)k(2|x|)^n v+\mathcal R,
\qquad
|\mathcal R|\le C_n k(2|x|)^n\varepsilon^2,
$$

where a conservative explicit constant is

$$
B_n=|n(n-1)|\max_{1/2\le z\le2}z^{n-2},\qquad
C_n=10(1+|n|+B_n).
$$

Consequently

$$
E'=(n+1)k(2|x|)^n v^2+v\mathcal R.
$$

**Proof.** Integral Taylor expansion gives $x(S)=x(T)-v(T)R+r$, with $|r|\le MR^2/2$, and $|v(S)-v(T)|\le MR$. The root equation therefore gives

$$
\frac{R}{2|x|}=\frac1{1+\sigma v-\sigma r/R}
=1-\sigma v+e_R,\qquad |e_R|\le3\varepsilon^2.
$$

The source factor has the separate expansion

$$
\frac1{1+\sigma v(S)}=1-\sigma v+e_D,\qquad |e_D|\le3\varepsilon^2.
$$

The range ratio lies in $[1/2,2]$ and differs from one by at most $2\varepsilon$. Taylor's theorem for its $n$th power bounds the remainder by $(3|n|+2B_n)\varepsilon^2$. Multiplying by the source expansion gives the displayed $C_n$ bound. The range expansion contributes $n$ and the source weight contributes one. Differentiating $E$ completes the proof. No numerical run or instantaneous equilibrium assumption enters it.

For $n=1$ the two leading contributions to $E'$ are equal and total $4k|x|v^2$. For $n=-1$ the leading term cancels. For $n=-2$ it is negative on this restricted chart. These are leading-order local results, not a global sign theorem for complete trajectories. In particular the remainder may dominate near a velocity zero. The bound $MR\le\varepsilon^2$ is essential: a small receiver velocity does not alone make the delayed source velocity equal to it at leading order.

Using the unperturbed linear comparison $x=A\cos(\omega T)$, $\omega=\sqrt{2k}$, integration of the leading term gives

$$
\frac{\Delta E_{\mathrm{leading}}}{kA^2}=\frac{16}{3}\omega A
$$

per full cycle, or half that amount per half-cycle. This calculation is exact for the leading term evaluated on that comparison path. A theorem for the delayed solution additionally needs uniform small-amplitude history bounds and control of its difference from the comparison path over the cycle. Those estimates are not supplied here. The current release has $\omega A\approx0.3783$, outside this lemma's small-speed bound, so the formula does not establish its measured growth rate or sign by itself.

Falsifiers: an in-scope causal root violating either Taylor estimate or the stated acceleration remainder, or a direct algebraic counterexample to the expansion. A future small-amplitude prefix study can test the cycle prediction only after verifying its histories and uniform bounds; disagreement would challenge that extrapolation, not a global claim made here. The next rigorous object is a full-cycle perturbation estimate for the selected multiplier-free linear response.

Require that its complete monotone strictly subfield retained history extends continuously to that event, and that the earlier partner root remains inside a regular retained tube with positive range and transmitter factor. Alternatively require the full locally finite acceleration-measure equation with nonnegative inward self measure. There is no such extension.

The proof uses the [stationary analysis's exact local identities](stationary-binary-first-interval.md#the-inherited-partner-row-forces-increasing-inward-speed), rather than its release constants. Positive range gives $S_*<T_*$; the source is strictly subfield there, so the implicit-function theorem preserves the partner root. Compactness supplies $0<m\le P\le M$. Continuity keeps inward velocities positive near the event, so every admitted self chord points inward and every partner row is inward. Integrating yields $u(T_*+t)\ge1+mt$. With inward coordinate $z$, $h=z-T$ decreases before the event and increases afterward. Its incoming inverse supplies exactly one newborn self root and no outgoing-to-outgoing match. The exact delay measure is

$$
A_{\mathrm s}\,dT=\frac{K_{\mathrm s}}{\rho^2(w_-+w_+)}\,d\rho,
\qquad w_-=1-u(S),\quad w_+=u(T)-1.
$$

As $\rho\downarrow0$, both speed gaps tend to zero. Even a fixed upper bound on their sum forces a divergent positive integral of $\rho^{-2}$. No inward partner term cancels it, contradicting finite endpoint velocity or local finiteness. The argument is independent of stationary preparation and outgoing mirror symmetry, under the stated inherited-history assumptions.

This is conditional on reaching speed equality with that regular tube. First-exit ordering also permits a different earlier regularity boundary, so the combination does not close every history's fate. The broader jump and infinite-limit claims require checking their additional inherited Taylor, root-inventory and event-measure hypotheses at class level; they remain proposals. Initially superfield, nonmonotone, multi-root, incomplete-history, added-source and non-measure histories are not covered. A complete in-class counterexample with finite integrated self acceleration, or an omitted outward row consistent with these hypotheses, would falsify the extension.
