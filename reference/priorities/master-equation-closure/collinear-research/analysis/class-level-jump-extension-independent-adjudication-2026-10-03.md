## Pre-target reference

This section was written before the subject's proofs were read and is reproduced from the reviewer's working notes without amendment of its conclusions.

**Setting.** Let $x(t)$ be the receiver's coordinate, increasing toward the partner, with $x(0)=0$ at the event, and let $u=x'$ be the inward speed. The past has $0\le u(s)<1$ for $s<0$ and $u(0^-)=1$. Put $h=x-t$ and $k=x+t$, so $h'=u-1$ and $k'=u+1$. On the past, $h$ is strictly decreasing and positive, $k$ is strictly increasing and negative, and $h(0)=k(0)=0$. Write $K>0$ for the self coefficient, $\rho=t-s$ for the delay of a self root emitted at $s$ and received at $t$, and $\mathbf n$ for the unit chord from emission site to receiver, which on the line is $n=\pm1$ in the inward coordinate.

**(a) Root conditions.** A self root with inward chord, $n=+1$, has the emission site behind the receiver, $x(t)-x(s)=t-s$, which is $h(s)=h(t)$. A self root with outward chord, $n=-1$, has the emission site ahead, $x(s)-x(t)=t-s$, which is $k(s)=k(t)$. The transmitter factor is $\lvert1-u(s)\rvert=\lvert h'(s)\rvert$ on an inward chord and $\lvert1+u(s)\rvert=\lvert k'(s)\rvert$ on an outward chord. The polarity product of a particle with itself is $+1$, so the acceleration contribution has the sign of $n$: inward-chord roots accelerate the receiver inward and outward-chord roots accelerate it outward, whatever the transmitter factor. Strict monotonicity of $h$ and $k$ on the past means no two past times are joined, and no past time is joined to $t=0$.

**(b) Delay identity.** If $h(t)>0$ for small $t>0$, exactly one past time $s(t)<0$ has $h(s)=h(t)$, and $s\uparrow0$ as $t\downarrow0$. Differentiating gives $(u(t)-1)\,dt=(u(s)-1)\,ds$, hence $d\rho/dt=(u(t)-u(s))/(1-u(s))$. The row is $A_s=K/(\rho^2(1-u(s)))$, so

$$
A_s\,dt=\frac{K\,d\rho}{\rho^2\,(u(t)-u(s))},
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
(u(t)-u(s))\,A_s\,dt=-K\,d(1/\rho).
$$

If $0<u(t)-u(s)\le M$ then $\int_\varepsilon^{t}A_s\ge(K/M)\,[1/\rho(\varepsilon)-1/\rho(t)]\to\infty$ as $\varepsilon\downarrow0$. The mirror identity for a past outward-chord root, which exists when $k(t)<0$, is $(1+u(t))\,dt=(1+u(s))\,ds$, $d\rho/dt=(u(s)-u(t))/(1+u(s))$ and $A_s\,dt=-K\,d\rho/(\rho^2(u(s)-u(t)))$.

**(c) Inventory by right-hand trace $U$.**

- $U>1$: $h$ is strictly increasing after the event and $k$ is strictly increasing throughout, so there is exactly one self root, emitted in the past on an inward chord, with inward sign. By (b) its accumulated contribution is infinite. No bounded-variation continuation.
- $U=1$: $k'>0$ near the event, so every self root has an inward chord and inward sign, including any nonisolated root set where the transmitter factor vanishes. The partner contribution is inward and bounded below by some $c>0$. Hence $du\ge c\,dt$, $u>1$ immediately, and the case reduces to the previous one. No bounded-variation continuation.
- $-1<U<1$: $h(t)<0<h(s)$ and $k(s)<0<k(t)$ for $s<0<t$, with $h$ and $k$ strictly monotone on the outgoing side. There is no self root. Only the partner contributes, the equation is an ordinary differential equation with Lipschitz right side, and the local solution is unique. The jump $U-1<0$ at $t=0$ needs an outward impulse that the unpunctured equation cannot supply.
- $U=-1$: prediction that a punctured continuation exists, namely $u=-1+\int_0^tP$ with $P$ the partner row, for which $k>0$ is increasing, $h<0$, and there is no self root. Uniqueness is not automatic, because self contributions are now outward and compete with the inward partner contribution; it needs a separate argument that a self root cannot be born, either from the past when $k<0$ or at a fold of $k$, and both births are non-integrable by the mirror identity.
- $U<-1$: $h(t)<0$ so no inward-chord root; $k$ is strictly decreasing after the event, so there is exactly one self root, emitted in the past on an outward chord, with outward sign and regular transmitter factor $1+u(s)\in[1,2)$. Since $k(s)\approx2s$ and $k(t)\approx(1+U)t$, $s\approx(1+U)t/2$, $\rho\approx(1-U)t/2$ and $A_s\approx-2K/((1-U)^2t^2)$, not integrable. No bounded-variation continuation.

**(d) Atom at the event.** At $t=0$ there is no self root and the partner root is regular, so the acceleration at that instant is finite. A density times $dt$ gives no mass to a point wherever the density is locally integrable; where it is not locally integrable near $0^+$ there is no locally finite measure at all. The unpunctured measure equation therefore forces $U=u(0^-)=1$.

**Partner.** A source whose past is strictly below wake speed has a strictly monotone incidence residual, hence one root, whatever the receiver's speed; the canonical row contains the source-side transmitter factor only. For small $t$ the partner's root is emitted near the time $-d$, with $d$ the gap, so the partner's motion after the event is irrelevant and neither mirror symmetry nor simultaneity is needed.

**Control.** With $h(t)=mt^2/2$ and $h(s)=as^2/2$ the root is $s=-t\sqrt{m/a}$, $\rho=t(1+\sqrt{m/a})$, $1-u(s)=t\sqrt{am}$, and $A_s=K/(t^3(1+\sqrt{m/a})^2\sqrt{am})$.

## Itemised checks against the subject

1. **Root conditions and chord signs.** The subject's conditions $h(s)=h(t)$ for an inward chord and $k(s)=k(t)$ for an outward chord agree with (a). Its transmitter factors $w_-=1-u(s)$ on the inward root and $1+u(s)$ on the outward root are correct, and its signs, inward for an inward chord and outward for an outward chord, are correct. Its identity $A_s\,dt=K_s\,d\rho/(\rho^2(w_-+w_+))$ is (b), since $w_-+w_+=u(t)-u(s)$.

2. **Missed root families.** None found. Remote-past roots are excluded by H1 holding on the complete past: $h$ and $k$ are strictly monotone on all of $(-\infty,0]$, so each level is met at most once there. Roots between two outgoing times are excluded in each case by strict monotonicity of the relevant function on the outgoing side: $h$ for $U\ge1$ after $u>1$ is established, $k$ for $U<-1$, and both for $\lvert U\rvert<1$. The emission join $s=0$ is not a root in any of these cases because $h(t)\ne h(0)$ or $k(t)\ne k(0)$ respectively. The opposite chord family is excluded by the strict inequalities the subject states. The brute-force scan below found the same counts on a non-polynomial class path: one inward root for $U=1$ and $U=1.7$, one outward root for $U=-2.3$, and none for $U=0.4$ and $U=-0.6$.

3. **Case $U=1$: is $u>1$ forced immediately?** Yes. A right limit of $1$ keeps $u>0$ on some $(0,t_2)$, so $x(t)>x(s)$ for every $s<t$ and every self chord is inward; the self contribution is nonnegative even on a nonisolated root set, where it is $+\infty$ rather than of opposite sign. With the partner row $P\ge m>0$ this gives $u(t)\ge1+mt$. Only then is $h$ strictly increasing, which is the order the subject uses. The change of variable is legitimate for velocity of bounded variation without outgoing continuity: $h$ is Lipschitz, the past inverse of $h$ is continuously differentiable on positive levels because $w_->0$ for $s<0$, so $\rho(t)$ is locally absolutely continuous and increasing, and the bound $w_-+w_+\le 2B$ follows from boundedness of $u$.

5. **Case $U<-1$ and the asymptotic.** Correct. ## Corrections

**C1, definition of the measure form (wording; no result changes).** The canonical row is $\sigma K\,\mathbf n/(\tau^2\lvert1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_{\rm s}\rvert)$ per unit reception time, with $\mathbf V_{\rm s}$ the source velocity at emission. The incidence map has $dt/ds=(1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_{\rm s})/(1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_{\rm r})$, with $\mathbf V_{\rm r}$ the receiver velocity at reception, so the plain image of a receiver-independent emission-time measure $\sigma K\,\mathbf n\,ds/\tau^2$ would carry an extra factor $\lvert1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_{\rm r}\rvert$ and is a different law. The stationary analysis states the same point: receiver playback does not multiply the canonical acceleration. Replace the sentence beginning "Write the equation in its measure form" with: "Write the equation in its measure form: the distributional derivative of velocity equals the acceleration measure. On any set of reception times at which every positive-delay causal root is ordinary, meaning isolated with nonzero transmitter factor, this measure is the canonical root sum times $dt$, with the source-side transmitter factor only. It can assign mass to a single reception instant only if a set of emission times of positive length is received at that one instant with positive delay." The Lemma's proof then stands unchanged, because the set of emission times received at $t=0$ is empty for the receiver's own path and a single regular point for the partner.

**C2, hypotheses of Theorem 2.** Replace its opening sentence with: "Assume H1 to H3. **C3, regularity of the restart equation.** In part 3 replace "the motion solves the smooth ordinary equation $x''=P(t,x)$" with "the motion solves the ordinary equation $x''=P(t,x)$, whose right side is Lipschitz in $(t,x)$ by H2".

## What is not established

- **No right-hand limit.** Theorem 1 is complete for velocity of bounded variation, which always has a finite right limit. Theorem 2 covers a finite or infinite right limit only; a velocity that oscillates without limit as $t\downarrow0$ in the punctured class is not covered.
- **Nonordinary receptions of positive measure.** A waiting segment at exactly $u=\pm1$, a plateau of $h$ or $k$, or a source-join root persisting on a set of positive length is outside the punctured class. Theorem 1 handles the $u=1$ plateau through the sign of the self measure; Theorem 2 does not.
- **Uniqueness at $U=-1$ beyond the ordinary-root class,** and any statement about the $\lvert U\rvert\le1$ restarts after the short interval on which they are constructed.
- **Any event contribution defined by a limiting procedure.** The Lemma concerns the equation as written. It does not exclude a regularized or generalized equation that assigns an impulse at the event.
- **Different data.** A past that is not monotone inward, that has been at or above wake speed, or that already has a self root; wake speed reached at zero separation; a partner root that is not regular or not unique; a like-polarity or absent partner, for which the partner row is not inward and the case $U=1$ argument fails at its first step; additional sources; a zero self coefficient.
- **A different first exit:** a partner-root fold or birth, loss of monotonicity, or an edge of the retained history.
- **Non-collinear continuation,** discontinuous position, and the second label of a non-mirror pair before it reaches wake speed.
- **Existence of histories satisfying H1 to H3.** The theorems are conditional on such a history; only the stationary release has a separate certificate, and it was not re-examined here.
- **Physical outcome.** Nothing here says the receivers stop, pass, rebound or obey a ceiling.

A path with continuous position near the event on which a second partner root appears or the partner row is unbounded, with H2 as amended by C4, defeats Fact 2. A velocity of bounded variation with right trace $U\ge1$ and an admitted self contribution of outward sign, or with $U<-1$ and an admitted inward self contribution, or with finite $\int A_s\,dt$ despite the delay identity, defeats the corresponding case of Theorem 1.
