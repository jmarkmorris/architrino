# Relative openness of the finite-width escape preparations

Fix one selected $(h,\rho)$ law, with all self and partner channels retained and normalized coefficients equal to one. Suppose a compatible mirror-symmetric incoming preparation has complete held past, nonnegative inward past speed, strictly positive release speed, and an exact first contact speed satisfying

$$
\frac12u_c^2>\frac1\rho.
$$

Then the same monotone escape conclusion holds on a relative neighborhood of that preparation within the class of compatible mirror-symmetric, held-tail, nonnegative-inward-speed histories. This is a robustness statement for the fixed law, not a claim about arbitrary three-dimensional or nonsymmetric perturbations.

The topology controls complete-past position differences uniformly and release velocity differences; imposing a common finite patch interval and a stronger $C^{2,1}$ patch topology is sufficient. Compatibility is imposed as a constraint, and the neighborhood is relative to that constraint set and the nonnegative-speed cone. The theorem does not claim that these constraints define an open set in an unrestricted function space.

The previously proved global positive-core functional Lipschitz bound supplies continuous dependence on complete past and initial velocity on every finite future interval. To see the relevant estimate directly, for two histories let $\Delta(t)$ bound their position and velocity differences through time $t$, including the supplied old past. The integral form of the acceleration equation gives a finite-horizon inequality of the form

$$
\Delta(t)\le C_T\Delta(0)+C_T\int_0^t\Delta(s)\,ds,
$$

with finite $C_T$ from the fixed global spatial Lipschitz bound. Gronwall's inequality gives continuous dependence; no numerical evolution-order assumption is used.

Within the stated cone, while the two particles are separated and incoming, every self and partner source has nonnegative inward orientation. Since the release speed remains positive under a small enough perturbation, monotonic approach is invariant until contact. At the reference contact, $x'=-u_c<0$ is transverse. Uniform finite-time position and velocity continuity therefore gives a nearby unique first crossing, whose speed remains above the strict threshold. The complete braking-work theorem then rules out any later turn and proves escape with unbounded speed. If the separately derived held-tail asymptotic theorem is accepted, the same neighborhood shares its coefficient $C_{h,\rho}$ because the law is fixed.

In particular, a correctly adjudicated strict contact-speed certificate for an original preparation supplies more than a single isolated trajectory: it supplies a nonempty relative set of compatible preparations with the same escape fate. An explicit numerical radius is not claimed here; the global Lipschitz estimate proves existence but is far too coarse to advertise a useful radius without further bounds.
