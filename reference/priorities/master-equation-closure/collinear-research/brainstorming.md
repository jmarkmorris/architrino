### What classical point-charge theory already knows

The zero-distance and self-interaction difficulties have established classical counterparts. They must be distinguished from the first wake-speed obstruction in [the collinear comparison](manuscript.md#1-collinear-encounter-comparison). The following is an external classical comparison, not an introduction of mass, Newtonian mechanics or Maxwell equations as architrino premises.

**Two attracting points at zero separation.** The inverse-square Coulomb expression is undefined when their separation is zero. For an explicit classical example, let two massive point charges start at rest with separation $r_0$, retain only their instantaneous attractive Coulomb interaction, and omit radiation and self-interaction. Write $\alpha>0$ for the attraction coefficient and $\mu>0$ for the reduced mass. Newtonian relative motion then gives $\mu\ddot r=-\alpha/r^2$, hence $\mu\dot r^2/2=\alpha(1/r-1/r_0)$. The inward speed becomes unbounded as $r\to0$. The remaining travel time is finite because $dt/dr$ in magnitude is proportional to $\sqrt r$ near zero. This is a derived property of that approximation: it reaches a singular collision, not a regular finite-speed passage. Continuing beyond collision requires an additional mathematical prescription or a changed physical description. The underlying [Coulomb law](https://farside.ph.utexas.edu/teaching/em/lectures/node28.html) alone does not supply that prescription.

**A point charge interacting with its own field.** This is a separate classical difficulty, present even without a second charge. The field energy outside a spherical charge of fixed total charge grows without bound as its radius shrinks to zero; [Feynman, Volume II, Chapter 28](https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_28.html#Ch28-S1) derives that result. There are controlled ways to approximate self-interaction rather than evaluating an infinite field directly at a point. For example, [Gralla, Harte and Wald (2009)](https://arxiv.org/abs/0905.2391) derive corrections for a family of shrinking charged bodies with charge and mass also tending to zero. Their result has explicit small-body assumptions and does not supply a general two-charge collision rule.

**The architrino problem before coincidence.** Row 1 encounters a different event: each architrino begins to encounter its own previously emitted wake while the two architrinos still occupy distinct positions. The unchanged equation then gives an accumulated self acceleration incompatible with the tested finite continuous velocity continuations. Ordinary relativistic massive-charge motion remains below light speed; it does not include this crossing into motion faster than the propagating field. Moreover, the complete classical moving-charge field contains more than a delayed inverse-square term, as explained in the preceding subsection. The familiar Coulomb examples therefore cannot settle this event merely because both laws contain an inverse-square factor.

The comparison supports a limited conclusion: the collision and point-self-field problems are established classical difficulties, while identifying an exact classical counterpart of our wake-speed obstruction requires matching the full equations and their allowed trajectories. This bounded source check establishes neither novelty nor a ready-made remedy for the Architrino equation. The classical fall calculation above is derived; the literature descriptions are source-supported; the distinction between models follows from their stated assumptions. An asserted transfer of a classical solution would need to survive substitution into the unchanged Master Equation with the same complete history, including its self-wake terms. The next useful artifact is that explicit equation comparison, with each missing or different term identified.
