## 1. Law, geometry and conventions

The law is the preregistration's Section 1, which is Section 9 of the [equation-variants manuscript](../../equation-variants/manuscript.md#9-weber-inspired-relative-motion-response) at the frozen coefficients:

$$
\mathbf A_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K}{d_{ij}^{2}}\left[1-\frac{\dot d_{ij}^{2}}{2c_f^{2}}+\frac{d_{ij}\ddot d_{ij}}{c_f^{2}}\right]\mathbf e_{ij},\qquad \ddot d_{ij}=\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot(\mathbf A_i-\mathbf A_j)+\frac{\|\mathbf w_{ij,\perp}\|^2}{d_{ij}},
\tag{1.1}
$$

with $\mathbf w_{ij}=\mathbf V_i-\mathbf V_j$ and $\mathbf w_{ij,\perp}$ its part transverse to $\mathbf e_{ij}$. Every member receives the full acceleration (unit integration weights; architrinos have no mass). Support is instantaneous and pairwise over distinct partners; there is no self term, boundary response, softening or enforced ceiling. Because $\ddot d_{ij}$ contains every member's unknown acceleration, each evaluation assembles the $18\times18$ system $M\mathbf A=\mathbf b$ of [eq. (2.1) of the ring analysis](weber-overnight-ring.md#2-the-acceleration-matrix-of-the-four-member-system) pair by pair and solves it; the isolated-pair denominator $1-2\sigma K\mu_{\mathrm W}/(c_f^2d)$ is never applied to a six-body hit. A zero or non-finite pivot, $|\det M|<10^{-8}$ or a separation below $10^{-6}$ ends a regular case, and no continuation is selected past it. The orthogonal-plane exclusions recorded in the braid-program brainstorming were derived under the delayed canonical law and are not premises here.

A sphere candidate satisfies, for every member and time, $\|\mathbf X_i\|=R$, $\|\mathbf V_i\|=v$, $\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf V_i=0$, $\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf A_i=-v^2$ and $\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf A_i=0$, with the solved $\mathbf A_i$ equal to the path's actual second derivative in all three Cartesian components. The centre is fixed at the origin by $\sum_i\mathbf X_i=\sum_i\mathbf V_i=0$; the sum of velocities is an invariant of the law because pair contributions are antisymmetric.

## 2. Instruments

The instrument library is [weber-binding-sphere-instrument.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-instrument.mjs) (Node 26, no packages). It imports the validated [overnight pair instrument](../../binary-research/evidence/weber-overnight-pair-instrument.md) unmodified for the assembly and solve of $M\mathbf A=\mathbf b$ (`assemble`, `solveAccelerations`), its Gragg–Bulirsch–Stoer, Dormand–Prince and RK4 steppers (`makeStepper`), its event-locating driver (`runCase`), its finite-difference Jacobian and Francis QR eigenvalue routine (`jacobian`, `eigenvalues`) and its rotating-frame linearization helper (`linearize`, which refuses a spectrum about a state that is not an equilibrium of the chosen frame). Everything built on top is a new instrument and passed the known cases of Section 3 first.

### 2.1 Prescribed-path residual evaluator

`pathResidual` takes a parametrized family $\mathbf X_i(T)$, $\mathbf V_i(T)$ with the required acceleration $\mathbf A_i^{\mathrm{req}}(T)$ (for uniform circular motion about the origin $-\Omega^2\mathbf X_i$; for a rigid rotation about an axis $-\Omega^2\mathbf X_{i,\perp}$; for a general curve its actual second derivative), evaluates the solved $18\times18$ accelerations at $n_T\ge64$ equally spaced times over one period, and returns $\mathcal R$, the three Cartesian components of the worst residual separately, the raw and normalized radial quantity $\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf A_i+\|\mathbf V_i\|^2$ (normalized by $\Omega^2R^2$), the tangential quantity $\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf A_i$ (normalized by $\Omega^2Rv$), the binormal component, the minimum pair separation, $\min|\det M|$, the maximum exact 1-norm condition number, the speed labels, and along the path the spread of $\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$ and of $H$ together with $H+3v^2$ (Section 2.5).

### 2.2 Rigid balance function and least-squares solvers

`rigidBalance` evaluates the inverse-square relative-equilibrium residual $\mathbf r_i=\Omega^2\mathbf X_{i,\perp}+\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}K\mathbf e_{ij}/d_{ij}^2$ of Section 4 for a configuration, an axis and a rate, and returns its maximum norm normalized by $\Omega^2R$ together with the axial component separately. `levenbergMarquardt` minimizes a residual vector over family parameters with a central-difference Jacobian and multiplicative damping; `nelderMead` is the derivative-free fallback. Both are validated in Section 3 (K8).

### 2.3 Rotating-frame linearization and monodromy

`rotatingSpectrum` wraps the imported helper: the 36-state rotating-frame system is $\mathbf X'=\mathbf U$, $\mathbf U'=\mathbf A(\mathbf X,\mathbf U+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf X)-2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf U-\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf X)$, a rigid relative equilibrium has $\mathbf U=\mathbf 0$ in that frame, and the helper declines unless the balance residual is below $10^{-10}$. For planar configurations the wrapper also splits the Jacobian into its in-plane and axial blocks by the reflection $z\to-z$ and reports the largest cross-block entry. `monodromy` computes the $36\times36$ derivative of the flow map over one period by central differences with Richardson combination (steps $h$ and $h/2$), integrating each perturbed state with GBS at relative tolerance $10^{-13}$, and returns the Floquet multipliers; it is validated on the circle (K7b), where the multipliers are known from the rotating-frame spectrum.

### 2.4 Shooting objective and jet filter for F3

`shootingObjective` integrates a state for a window $T_w$ with fixed-step RK4 (so that the objective is a smooth function of the parameters) or with GBS for the final evaluation, and returns $J$ together with the discretized residual vector $\{(\|\mathbf X_i(T_k)\|-R)/R,\ (\|\mathbf V_i(T_k)\|-v)/v\}$ for least squares; an obstruction or contact inside the window returns a penalty above $10$ ordered by the time reached. `jetConditions` evaluates at $T=0$ the six values of $\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf A_i+\|\mathbf V_i\|^2$ and $\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf A_i$ and their first time derivatives by central differences along the flow (one RK4 step of $\pm10^{-4}$), as a cheap filter before any integration.

### 2.5 The PI identity and the two filters

With $I=\tfrac12\sum_i\|\mathbf X_i\|^2$, $G=I-\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$, $T_{\mathrm{kin}}=\tfrac12\sum_i\|\mathbf V_i\|^2$ and $H$ the energy-like invariant, the law satisfies

$$
\ddot G=T_{\mathrm{kin}}+H
\tag{2.1}
$$

identically (derivation by the PI; identity checked by the PI to $1.6\times10^{-14}$ on 200 random states, and here independently, Section 3 K10, by assembling $\ddot G=2T_{\mathrm{kin}}+\sum_i\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf A_i-\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}\ddot d_{ij}$ from the solved accelerations and the kinematic $\ddot d_{ij}$, and by a fourth-order finite difference of $G$ along the flow). The derivation is short: $\dot G=\sum_i\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf V_i-\sum\sigma_{ij}\dot d_{ij}$, so $\ddot G=2T_{\mathrm{kin}}+\sum_i\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf A_i-\sum\sigma_{ij}\ddot d_{ij}$; inserting (1.1) for $\mathbf A_i$ and using $\sum_i\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf A_{i\leftarrow j}$ summed over ordered pairs equals $\sum_{i<j}d_{ij}\,(\sigma_{ij}K/d_{ij}^2)[\dots]$, the bracket's $\mu_{\mathrm W}$ term cancels the $\ddot d$ sum and the remainder is $\sum_{i<j}(\sigma_{ij}K/d_{ij})(1-\dot d^2/2)=H-T_{\mathrm{kin}}$. Consequences for an all-time-bounded history with constant equal speeds $v$ on a fixed sphere: $I=3R^2$ is constant, $G$ is bounded, so $\ddot G$ has zero time average, and since $T_{\mathrm{kin}}=3v^2$ and $H$ are constant, $H=-3v^2$ exactly; then $\ddot G=0$ at every time, so $\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$ is constant in time, $\sum_{i<j}(\sigma_{ij}/d_{ij})(1-\dot d_{ij}^2/2)=-6v^2$ and $\sum\sigma_{ij}\ddot d_{ij}=0$. The two filters used below are the spread of $\sum\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$ along a candidate and the value of $H+3v^2$.

> Claim grade: derived (PI derivation, restated above). Falsifier: a six-member state at which the solved accelerations give $2T_{\mathrm{kin}}+\sum\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf A_i-\sum\sigma_{ij}\ddot d_{ij}$ differing from $T_{\mathrm{kin}}+H$ beyond round-off. Instrument: `identityCheck` in the library; worst relative difference $1.7\times10^{-13}$ on 50 random states, finite-difference side $\le10^{-3}$ at step $10^{-4}$ (Section 3).

## 4. Rigid reduction theorem

**Theorem (rigid reduction).** Let a configuration rotate rigidly about a unit axis $\hat{\mathbf n}$ through the origin at rate $\Omega$, so $\mathbf X_i(T)=Q(\Omega T)\mathbf X_i(0)$, $\mathbf V_i=\Omega\,\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf X_i$. Then every $d_{ij}$ is constant, $\dot d_{ij}=\ddot d_{ij}=0$, and the solved accelerations of (1.1) are the centripetal ones, $\mathbf A_i=-\Omega^2\mathbf X_{i,\perp}$, if and only if the inverse-square balance

$$
-\Omega^2\mathbf X_{i,\perp}=\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K}{d_{ij}^2}\mathbf e_{ij}\qquad\text{for every }i
\tag{4.1}
$$

holds, provided $M$ is invertible on the configuration. The velocity terms of the law are invisible to the exact balance of any rigidly rotating configuration, for every $\lambda_{\mathrm W}$ and $\mu_{\mathrm W}$.

*Proof.* Rigid rotation preserves every separation, so $\dot d_{ij}=0$ and $\ddot d_{ij}=0$ along the motion. The kinematic identity does not contradict this: with $\mathbf d_{ij}=\mathbf X_i-\mathbf X_j$ and $\mathbf w_{ij}=\Omega\hat{\mathbf n}\times\mathbf d_{ij}$, the relative velocity is perpendicular to $\mathbf d_{ij}$, so $\mathbf w_{ij,\perp}=\mathbf w_{ij}$ and $\|\mathbf w_{ij,\perp}\|^2=\Omega^2\|\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}\|^2$ where $\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}$ is the part of $\mathbf d_{ij}$ perpendicular to the axis; and if the accelerations are centripetal, $\mathbf A_i-\mathbf A_j=-\Omega^2\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}$, so $\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot(\mathbf A_i-\mathbf A_j)=-\Omega^2\,\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}=-\Omega^2\|\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}\|^2/d_{ij}$, because $\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}=(\mathbf d_{ij}\cdot\mathbf d_{ij,\perp})/d_{ij}=\|\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}\|^2/d_{ij}$ (the axial part of $\mathbf d_{ij}$ is orthogonal to $\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}$). The two terms of $\ddot d_{ij}$ cancel exactly, which is the three-dimensional form of the cancellation in [Section 4.1 of the ring analysis](weber-overnight-ring.md#41-the-velocity-terms-cancel-on-any-rigid-rotation). Now insert the candidate $\mathbf a^\ast_i=-\Omega^2\mathbf X_{i,\perp}$ into the system $M\mathbf a=\sum_{i<j}b_{ij}\mathbf u_{ij}$ with $M=I-\sum\alpha_{ij}\mathbf u_{ij}\mathbf u_{ij}^{\mathsf T}$, $b_{ij}=(\sigma_{ij}K/d_{ij}^2)[1+\lambda_{\mathrm W}\dot d_{ij}^2+\mu_{\mathrm W}\|\mathbf w_{ij,\perp}\|^2]$ (units $c_f=1$). Since $\mathbf u_{ij}\cdot\mathbf a^\ast=\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot(\mathbf a^\ast_i-\mathbf a^\ast_j)=-\Omega^2\|\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}\|^2/d_{ij}$,

$$
M\mathbf a^\ast=\mathbf a^\ast+\Omega^2\sum_{i<j}\alpha_{ij}\frac{\|\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}\|^2}{d_{ij}}\mathbf u_{ij}=\mathbf a^\ast+\sum_{i<j}\frac{\sigma_{ij}K\mu_{\mathrm W}\Omega^2\|\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}\|^2}{d_{ij}^2}\mathbf u_{ij},
$$

while the right side, with $\dot d_{ij}=0$ and $\|\mathbf w_{ij,\perp}\|^2=\Omega^2\|\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}\|^2$, is $\sum(\sigma_{ij}K/d_{ij}^2)\mathbf u_{ij}+\sum(\sigma_{ij}K\mu_{\mathrm W}\Omega^2\|\mathbf d_{ij,\perp}\|^2/d_{ij}^2)\mathbf u_{ij}$. The $\mu_{\mathrm W}$ terms are identical on the two sides and cancel; the $\lambda_{\mathrm W}$ term is absent because $\dot d_{ij}=0$. Hence $\mathbf a^\ast$ solves the system exactly when $\mathbf a^\ast=\sum_{i<j}(\sigma_{ij}K/d_{ij}^2)\mathbf u_{ij}$, which written member by member is (4.1); when $M$ is invertible the solution is unique, so (4.1) is necessary as well as sufficient. $\square$

**Corollary (equal speeds on a rigid rotation).** On a rigid rotation the speed of member $i$ is $\Omega\|\mathbf X_{i,\perp}\|$, so equal speeds force equal axial distances $\|\mathbf X_{i,\perp}\|=a$ for all six; with all members on a sphere of radius $R$ about the origin, $z_i^2=R^2-a^2$ is also common, so every member lies on one circle ($z_0=0$, $a=R$) or on the two circles $z=\pm z_0$ of equal radius $a=\sqrt{R^2-z_0^2}$. This reduces the rigid sphere candidates to F0 and F1 (with F1′ as named members of F1).

> Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: a rigidly rotating six-member state with invertible $M$ on which the solved accelerations are centripetal while (4.1) fails, or on which (4.1) holds while the solved accelerations are not centripetal. ## 5. F0: the planar alternating hexagon

### 5.1 Balance at every radius

Six members at angles $\theta_k=\Omega T+k\pi/3$ on a circle of radius $\rho$ with polarity $q_k=(-1)^k$. Member $0$ at $\rho(1,0,0)$ has two opposite-polarity neighbours at separation $\rho$ (chord of $60^\circ$) with directions $\mathbf e_{01}=(\tfrac12,-\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},0)$, $\mathbf e_{05}=(\tfrac12,\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},0)$; their contributions sum to $-K(1,0,0)/\rho^2$. The two like-polarity members at $\pm120^\circ$ are at separation $\sqrt3\rho$ with $\mathbf e_{02}=(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},-\tfrac12,0)$, $\mathbf e_{04}=(\tfrac{\sqrt3}{2},\tfrac12,0)$; their contributions sum to $+K(1,0,0)/(\sqrt3\rho^2)$. The opposite-polarity member at $180^\circ$ contributes $-K(1,0,0)/(4\rho^2)$. The tangential components cancel pairwise because the reflection through member $0$'s diameter exchanges members $1$ and $5$ and members $2$ and $4$, which carry equal polarities; this holds for any law depending on present separations only. The radial sum is

$$
\sum_{j\ne0}\frac{\sigma_{0j}K}{d_{0j}^2}\mathbf e_{0j}\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_0=-\left(1-\frac1{\sqrt3}+\frac14\right)\frac{K}{\rho^2}=-\left(\frac54-\frac1{\sqrt3}\right)\frac{K}{\rho^2}\approx-0.67265\,\frac{K}{\rho^2},
\tag{5.1}
$$

inward, so by the theorem the hexagon is exactly balanced at every radius with

$$
\boxed{\ \Omega^2=\left(\frac54-\frac1{\sqrt3}\right)\frac{K}{\rho^3},\qquad v=\Omega\rho=c_f\sqrt{\frac{5/4-1/\sqrt3}{x}},\qquad v=c_f\ \text{at}\ x_\ast=\frac54-\frac1{\sqrt3}\approx0.67265.\ }
\tag{5.2}
$$

This re-derives the PI's pre-computation. Measured by both instruments at $\rho\in\{0.1,0.3,0.5,x_\ast,1,1.5,2,3,10\}$: inverse-square balance residual and full-solve residual $\mathcal R\le5.1\times10^{-15}$, tangential and radial parts at round-off (receipt [weber-binding-sphere-f0.json](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f0.json), `balance`).

### 5.2 The determinant along the family and the singular radius

$M-I$ is homogeneous of degree $-1$ in $\rho$ and the hexagon geometry is scale-free, so every eigenvalue of $M$ on the family has the form $1+c_k/x$ with constants $c_k$ fixed by the geometry, and $\det M=\prod_k(1+c_k/x)$. The eighteen constants, computed as the eigenvalues of $x(M-I)$ at $x=1$ and identified where possible against $a+b\sqrt3$ with small rationals:

| $c_k$ | multiplicity | closed form | mode |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $-\sqrt3$ | 1 | $-\sqrt3$ | breathing (uniform radial): $-\tfrac16\sum\alpha_{ij}d_{ij}^2/\rho^2=-\tfrac16(-6+6\sqrt3+6)/x$ |
| $0$ | 9 | $0$ | the modes that change no separation to first order: three translations, rotation, two tilts, three axial warps |
| $0.0326513$ | 2 | roots of a quadratic over $\mathbb Q(\sqrt3)$ with sum $2+(3-\sqrt3)/2$ (measured) | in-plane $k=\pm1$ sector |
| $2-\sqrt3$ | 1 | $2-\sqrt3$ | in-plane |
| $(3-\sqrt3)/2$ | 2 | $(3-\sqrt3)/2$ | in-plane |
| $2.6013233$ | 2 | partner roots of the quadratic above (measured) | in-plane $k=\pm1$ sector |
| $3$ | 1 | $3$ | in-plane |

The breathing constant is derived by hand: a uniform radial displacement gives $\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot(\boldsymbol\xi_i-\boldsymbol\xi_j)=d_{ij}/\rho$ for every pair, and $\sum_{i<j}\alpha_{ij}d_{ij}^2/\rho^2=(6\cdot(-1)\cdot1+6\cdot(1/\sqrt3)\cdot3+3\cdot(1/2)\cdot4)/x=6\sqrt3/x$, so per member the quadratic form of $M$ on that mode is $1-\sqrt3/x$. Hence

$$
\det M=\left(1-\frac{\sqrt3}{x}\right)\left(1+\frac{2-\sqrt3}{x}\right)\left(1+\frac{3-\sqrt3}{2x}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{3}{x}\right)\left(1+\frac{c_5}{x}\right)^{2}\left(1+\frac{c_6}{x}\right)^{2},
\tag{5.3}
$$

with $c_5=0.0326513$, $c_6=2.6013233$. These two constants have sum $(7-\sqrt3)/2$ and product $(9-5\sqrt3)/4$ (identified by PSLQ in the shared venv at tolerance $10^{-11}$, mpmath `identify`; no integer quadratic of coefficient size up to 2000 fits either constant alone), so they are the roots of $t^2-\tfrac{7-\sqrt3}{2}t+\tfrac{9-5\sqrt3}{4}=0$, that is $c_{5,6}=\big(7-\sqrt3\mp\sqrt{16+6\sqrt3}\big)/4$, and the last two factors of (5.3) combine to $\big(1+\tfrac{7-\sqrt3}{2x}+\tfrac{9-5\sqrt3}{4x^2}\big)^2$. The closed form reproduces the directly assembled determinant to $5\times10^{-15}$ relative at $x\in\{0.3,0.5,1,1.7,3,10\}$. Every constant except $-\sqrt3$ is positive, so the only singular radius of the family is

$$
\boxed{\ x_{\mathrm s}=\sqrt3\approx1.7320508,\ }
$$

located independently by a sign scan of the direct determinant on 401 log-spaced radii in $[0.1,10]$ followed by bisection: one sign change, at $\rho=1.7320508076$ (width $10^{-15}$), $\det M<0$ below and $>0$ above, which is the PI's observation ($\det M<0$ at $\rho=0.3$ and $1$, $>0$ at $3$) made exact. The kernel at $x_{\mathrm s}$ is the breathing direction, in contrast with the square, where the kernel at $x=1$ was the elliptic direction. For $x<\sqrt3$ the matrix is indefinite with exactly one negative eigenvalue (the breathing one) but invertible, so the solve is regular. Relative to the equality-speed radius: $x_{\mathrm s}=\sqrt3>x_\ast=0.67265$, so the singular radius lies in the subfield part of the family (member speed $v=c_f\sqrt{0.67265/\sqrt3}\approx0.623\,c_f$ there), and the equality hexagon, like every hexagon with $x<\sqrt3$, has an indefinite but invertible $M$. As in the ring analysis at $x=1$, the hexagon at $x_{\mathrm s}$ is excluded from the well-posed family: the centripetal accelerations still satisfy the law there, but the right side is orthogonal to the kernel and the solution is not unique.

> Claim grade: derived for the form $1+c_k/x$, the breathing constant and the singular radius; the remaining constants are measured (eigenvalues of $x(M-I)$ at $x=1$) and their closed forms are inferred by integer-relation identification and verified against the direct determinant to $5\times10^{-15}$ at six radii, which is not a proof of the identification. ## 6. F1: rigid two-circle arrangements

By the corollary of Section 4, a rigid equal-speed sphere candidate that is not planar has three members on $z=+z_0$ and three on $z=-z_0$, on circles of common radius $a$, rotating about $\hat{\mathbf z}$; the balance is the inverse-square one (4.1), and the Weber terms play no part, so the result is scale-covariant and $a=1$ is fixed. The residual is reported in two normalizations: the preregistered $\max_i\|\mathbf r_i\|/(\Omega^2R)$, and the scale-free $\max_i\|\mathbf r_i\|/\max_i\|\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}K\mathbf e_{ij}/d_{ij}^2\|$, which is needed because at a geometry whose least-squares rate $\Omega^2_{\mathrm{LS}}=-\sum_i\mathbf X_{i,\perp}\cdot\mathbf F_i/\sum_i\|\mathbf X_{i,\perp}\|^2$ is negative (net outward inverse-square sum) the optimal $\Omega$ is zero and the preregistered normalization diverges; the two agree wherever $\Omega$ is of order the reference rate.

### 6.1 Segregated class: the one-signed axial obstruction

**Proposition.** In the segregated class no relative equilibrium exists for any $z_0>0$. *Proof.* Take a member on the upper circle (polarity $+$). Its two same-circle partners are like-polarity pairs lying at the same height, so their contributions $\sigma K\mathbf e_{ij}/d_{ij}^2$ have no axial component. Each of its three lower-circle partners has opposite polarity, $\sigma_{ij}=-1$, and the axial component of $\mathbf e_{ij}=(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf X_j)/d_{ij}$ is $+2z_0/d_{ij}>0$, so the contribution's axial component is $-2z_0K/d_{ij}^3<0$: every opposite-circle contribution points toward the other circle. The axial component of the sum is $-2z_0K\sum_j d_{ij}^{-3}<0$, while the required axial acceleration on a rigid rotation about $\hat{\mathbf z}$ is zero. Hence (4.1) fails in its axial component at every upper member, for every choice of the angles, $a$ and $\Omega$. $\square$ Numerically: on 2000 random segregated configurations in the box the axial component on every upper member was negative, the largest value being $-7.5\times10^{-3}$ (receipt, `segregatedAxialObstruction`). The 200-start Levenberg–Marquardt search over the five free angles, $\ln(z_0/a)\in[\ln0.01,\ln10]$ and $\Omega$ confirms it: best scale-free residual $6.31\times10^{-2}$, attained on the box boundary $z_0/a=0.01$ at the alternating angular arrangement (upper members at $0^\circ,120^\circ,240^\circ$, lower at $60^\circ,180^\circ,300^\circ$) with $\Omega^2_{\mathrm{LS}}=0.6720$, which is the hexagon's $\Omega^2=0.67265$ at $a=1$; the residual there is entirely axial ($6.31\times10^{-2}$) and the $z_0$ scan with the search's angles as seeds is monotone, $6.3\times10^{-2}$ at $z_0/a=0.01$, $8.4\times10^{-2}$ at $0.0133$ (linear in $z_0$), $0.55$ at $0.1$, and $1.0$ (no rate balances the radial sum, $\Omega^2_{\mathrm{LS}}<0$) for $z_0/a\ge0.56$. The class approaches the planar hexagon as $z_0\to0$ and is excluded at every $z_0>0$.

> Claim grade: measured (residuals), derived (identification of the body-diagonal octahedron with the segregated antiprism and its exclusion by Section 6.1). Falsifier: a rate $\Omega$ at which the full $18\times18$ solve on one of these solids returns centripetal accelerations to $10^{-8}$.

## 8. F2: three antipodal pairs on great circles

Pair $k$ (members $2k$ with $q=+1$ and $2k+1$ with $q=-1$) moves as $\pm R[\cos(s_k\Omega T+\phi_k)\mathbf u_k+\sin(s_k\Omega T+\phi_k)\mathbf u_k']$ with $(\mathbf u_k,\mathbf u_k')$ an orthonormal basis of the plane of normal $\mathbf n_k$, circulation $s_k=\pm1$ and phase $\phi_k$. The common $\Omega$ is forced: on a great circle of radius $R$ every member's path length is $2\pi R$, so equal speed $v=\Omega_kR$ forces equal $\Omega_k$. The instrument is the prescribed-path residual $\mathcal R$ with $\mathbf A^{\mathrm{req}}=-\Omega^2\mathbf X$ ([weber-binding-sphere-f2.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f2.mjs), receipt [weber-binding-sphere-f2.json](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f2.json); bounded-rate refinement [weber-binding-sphere-f2-refine.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f2-refine.mjs), receipt [weber-binding-sphere-f2-refine.json](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f2-refine.json); grids under `.local-data/master-equation-closure/weber-binding-sphere/f2/`). Unless all three normals coincide, F2 is not a rigid rotation, so the rigid reduction does not apply and the Weber velocity terms enter.

**Box.** Normals: the orthogonal triad and 50 seeded random orthonormal triads (seed 20261006). Phases: the $12^3$ grid for the orthogonal triad and an $8^3$ grid for each random triad. Senses: $(+++)$, $(++-)$, $(+-+)$, $(-++)$ (the other four are their time reversals). $R\in\{0.5,1,2\}$. $\Omega$ on the seven-point scan $\{0.5,0.7,0.85,1,1.2,1.5,2\}\times\Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}$ with $\Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}=\sqrt K/(2R^{3/2})$, the binary circle at $\rho=R$. Residual at 32 times per period on the grid, 64 times with the exact condition number at every reported point; 612 combinations, $3.3\times10^6$ full $18\times18$ solves, 26 minutes.

**Result.** No point of the box approaches balance. The grid minima of $\mathcal R$ are $3.18$ at $R=1$, $3.48$ at $R=0.5$ and $3.94$ at $R=2$ on the orthogonal triad, and between $5.1$ and $7.5$ on the random triads; the medians over the phase grids are $40$–$300$, and some phase combinations are singular because members of different pairs coincide (members of distinct great circles meet at the circles' intersections when the phases allow it). At the orthogonal-triad grid minima the residual decomposes into radial $1.2$–$2.9$ and tangential $1.8$–$3.0$ in units of $\Omega^2R$: the law's acceleration is neither centripetal nor of the right magnitude, and the two components are comparable. A first least-squares refinement with $\Omega$ free was found to be ill-posed: because $\mathcal R$ is normalized by $\Omega^2R$, the solver lowers it by sending $\Omega$ to $12\,\Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}$ (member speeds $4.3\,c_f$, $\mathcal R=2.07$) or to overflow, which is not a balance; the receipt records those values and they are not used. The bounded refinement with $\Omega\in[0.3,3]\,\Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and the three phases free, from the best three grid points of every orthogonal combination and of the ten best random ones, ends at the upper clamp $3\,\Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}$ in every $R=2$ case and at $2.63\,\Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}$ at $R=1$, with floor $\mathcal R=2.83$ (radial $1.47$, tangential $2.19$) and $2.87$ at $R=1$; at $R=0.5$ the refinement is worse than the grid ($7.25$ against $3.48$) because the 64-time evaluation catches a close approach the 32-time grid missed. The declared extension with the three normals free as well (ten parameters, from the ten best combinations) reaches $0.68$ at best (random triad 9, senses $(-++)$, $R=2$, $\Omega=1.8\,\Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}$, normals at mutual angles of $42^\circ$–$52^\circ$, minimum separation $1.64$, radial $0.25$, tangential $0.64$), still three orders of magnitude above the bounded-negative threshold $10^{-4}$ and with the PI filters violated there ($\sum\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$ spread $0.06$, $H+3v^2=0.46$). The histogram over all 612 combinations has no entry below $10^{-1}$.

**Phase landscape and phase locking.** On the orthogonal triad with senses $(+++)$ at $R=2$ and the refined rate, the residual over a $24^3$ phase grid has minimum $2.10$, median $38$ and unbounded maximum (coincidences), with $0.9\%$ of the grid within a factor $1.5$ of the minimum and 225 local minima, the lowest at $(225^\circ,345^\circ,15^\circ)$, $(345^\circ,105^\circ,135^\circ)$, $(120^\circ,240^\circ,270^\circ)$, $(60^\circ,180^\circ,210^\circ)$ and their images, all with equal value $2.0966$. The landscape is therefore sharply structured: it selects phase offsets of $120^\circ$ between pairs, related by the triad's symmetry. But the selected value is $2.1$, of the order of the centripetal acceleration itself, and the selection is the avoidance of the close approaches at the great-circle intersections, not a balance. The honest statement is that phase locking is not derivable from F2 because F2 contains no balanced history; the residual landscape has preferred phases at residual $O(1)$.

> Claim grade: measured, bounded negative for the stated box (minimum $\mathcal R\ge2.0$ on the grid and bounded refinement, $\ge0.68$ with free normals, against the thresholds $10^{-12}$ exact, $10^{-8}$ candidate and $10^{-4}$ bounded negative). Falsifier: an F2 state (normals, senses, phases, $R\in\{0.5,1,2\}$, $\Omega\in[0.3,3]\,\Omega_{\mathrm{ref}}$) whose independently evaluated full-solve residual on 64 times per period is below $10^{-8}$. What is not established: F2 at other radii, at rates outside the scan and clamp, or with unequal pair radii; and nothing about non-antipodal great-circle motions, which are F3.

## 9. F3: declared non-rigid spherical paths

F3 is the declared broader representation: periodic spherical histories that are not rigid rotations. Three strata were searched: the C3-symmetric shooting stratum and the unconstrained shooting stratum of the preregistration (Section 9.1, instrument [weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting.mjs)), and the single-curve choreography stratum added by the PI (Section 9.2, instrument [weber-binding-sphere-f3-choreography.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f3-choreography.mjs)). In all three, $R=1$ and $v$ is free within $[0.05,3]$ (shooting) or determined by the curve (choreography).

### 9.1 Shooting strata

The objective is $J=\max_{T\le T_w}\max_i[\,\lvert\|\mathbf X_i\|-R\rvert/R+\lvert\|\mathbf V_i\|-v\rvert/v\,]$, evaluated by fixed-step RK4 with 512 steps per window so that it is a smooth function of the parameters, with $T_w=\max(2\pi/\Omega_{\mathrm{bin}}(R),\,T_{\times})$ capped at $3\cdot2\pi/\Omega_{\mathrm{bin}}$, where $\Omega_{\mathrm{bin}}^2=K/(4R^3)$ is the binary circle rate at $\rho=R$ (period $4\pi$ at $R=1$) and $T_\times$ is the time of the first zero of some $\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf V_i$ on a coarse pre-integration; in every start recorded $T_w=4\pi=12.566$, which is $1.64$ hexagon periods at $\rho=1$.

**C3 stratum and its reduction.** The operation is the rotation by $120^\circ$ about $\hat{\mathbf z}$ combined with the member shift $k\to k+2$. Members $0,2,4$ ($q=+1$) are the three rotations of a generator at colatitude $\theta_+$, azimuth $0$ (the overall rotation is fixed), speed $v$ in the tangent direction $\psi_+$; members $1,3,5$ ($q=-1$) are the rotations of a generator at colatitude $\theta_-$, azimuth $\varphi_-$, direction $\psi_-$. The $x,y$ components of $\sum\mathbf X$ and $\sum\mathbf V$ vanish by the symmetry; the $z$ components vanish exactly when $\theta_-=\pi-\theta_+$ and $\cos\psi_-=-\cos\psi_+$, that is $\psi_-=\pi-\psi_+$ (branch 0) or $\psi_-=\pi+\psi_+$ (branch 1). The stratum therefore has four continuous parameters $(\theta_+,\psi_+,\varphi_-,v)$ and a branch bit; it is invariant under the law because the solve is unique and the law commutes with the operation. The planar hexagon is the point $\theta_+=\pi/2$, $\varphi_-=\pi/3$, $\psi_+=\pm\pi/2$, branch 0, $v=\Omega\rho$.

**Unconstrained stratum.** Members $0$–$4$ have free positions on the sphere $(\theta_i,\varphi_i)$ and tangent directions $\psi_i$; member $5$ is fixed by $\mathbf X_5=-\sum_{i<5}\mathbf X_i$, $\mathbf V_5=-\sum_{i<5}\mathbf V_i$, whose sphere and speed defects at $T=0$ are part of $J$; sixteen parameters.

**Procedure per start.** A seeded random start (rejected if any separation is below $0.05$); Nelder–Mead on the $T=0$ jet score (the normalized $\max_i|\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf A_i+\|\mathbf V_i\|^2|$ plus $\max_i|\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf A_i|$, no integration, 400 evaluations); then Nelder–Mead on $J$ over a window homotopy $T_w/8\to T_w/4\to T_w/2\to T_w$ (400–500 evaluations each), so that the optimizer is not dominated by the dispersal at late times; then Levenberg–Marquardt on the discretized residual vector over the full window. A start with $J\le10^{-6}$ enters the candidate pipeline (GBS re-evaluation over $2T_w$, period estimate by closest return, monodromy, perturbed evolutions).

Both strata ran 100 starts (seeds 20261007); the results are in Section 9.3, receipts [weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting-c3.json](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting-c3.json) and [weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting-free.json](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting-free.json).

**A control that the shooting instrument found by itself.** In the C3 stratum one random start (start 21) converged to $\theta_+=89.999^\circ$, $\varphi_-=60.000^\circ$, $\psi_+=270.03^\circ$, branch 0, $v=0.82015227$, which is the planar hexagon at $\rho=1$ ($v=\Omega\rho=0.82015226$), with $J=8.9\times10^{-7}$ over one window. The exact hexagon state itself, evaluated by the same objective, gives $J=3.6\times10^{-4}$ (RK4-512) and $9.2\times10^{-5}$ (GBS) over one window and $J\ge10$ over two windows (scratch `hexJ.mjs`, 00:24Z): the hexagon's own in-plane instability, seeded at round-off, reaches the $10^{-3}$ level within $1.6$ periods (Section 5.4), and the optimizer's lower value is obtained by tuning the initial state so that the round-off-seeded growth is partly cancelled inside the window. Two conclusions follow. The shooting instrument recovers the one known exact solution of the stratum from a random start, which is a validation of the search beyond K9. And $J$ over one window is not a reliable candidate signal near an unstable exact solution: the threshold $10^{-8}$ cannot be met by the hexagon over a full window with either integrator, and the arbiter for any low-$J$ start is the GBS re-evaluation over $2T_w$ in the candidate pipeline. The C3 sector contains the hexagon's fastest unstable pair (Section 5.3), so the stratum does not stabilize it.

### 9.2 Single-curve choreography (Fourier collocation)

Because $\sigma_{ij}=q_iq_j$, a global polarity flip is an exact symmetry of the law, and the ansatz $\mathbf X_k(T)=\mathbf X_0(T+kP/6)$, $q_k=(-1)^k$ is consistent with it: the equations of member $k$ at $T$ are those of member $0$ at $T-kP/6$. Equal constant speed is automatic across members and reduces to $\|\mathbf X_0'\|=v$ on one curve; the planar hexagon is the trivial member. The representation is $\mathbf X_0(T)=\sum_{m\le M}\mathbf a_m\cos(m\omega T)+\mathbf b_m\sin(m\omega T)$ with $\omega=2\pi/P$, the unknowns the coefficients and $\omega$, and the equations the three components of $\mathbf X_0''(T_c)=\mathbf A_0(T_c)$ at $N_c=6\lceil(4M+2)/6\rceil$ collocation points closed under the shift $P/6$, with $\mathbf A_0$ from the full $18\times18$ solve at the shifted configuration, in least squares. The gauge is fixed by $\mathbf a_0=0$ (which is exactly $\sum_k\mathbf X_k=6\mathbf a_0=0$ for $M\le5$, since the shifts of a harmonic $m\not\equiv0\pmod 6$ sum to zero), $b_{x,1}=0$ (time origin), $a_{y,1}=0$ (rotation about $z$) and $a_{z,1}=b_{z,1}=0$ (tilts, which are rotated hexagons). The known case was recorded first: the planar hexagon at $\rho\in\{x_\ast,1,3\}$ with $M=3$ has collocation residual $\le2.0\times10^{-15}$ and full-period residual (128 times, the curve's actual second derivative as the required acceleration) $\le6.3\times10^{-15}$, and a one-percent change of $\omega$ raises the residual to $1.4$–$1.8\times10^{-2}$.

Section 5.3 showed that the hexagon's axial frequencies in units of $\Omega$ are $1$ (tilts), $1.8978$ and $2.5136$ at every radius; a choreography perturbation $z_0(T)=\varepsilon\cos(m\omega T)$ gives member $k$ the phase $2\pi mk/6$, that is the sixfold character $m$, so a non-planar branch could bifurcate from the planar family only where an axial mode of character $m$ has frequency $m\Omega$; the character-1 tilt satisfies this trivially and the characters 2 and 3 miss it by $5\%$ and $16\%$ at every radius. The continuation therefore pins $a_{z,m}=\varepsilon\rho$ for $m\in\{2,3\}$, $\varepsilon\in\{0.01,0.03,0.1,0.3\}$, at $\rho\in\{x_\ast,1,3\}$ and $M\in\{3,4\}$, and solves the remaining unknowns from the hexagon seed. The residual floor is the same at every radius and both truncations and scales linearly with $\varepsilon$: for $m=2$, $3.97\times10^{-3}$, $1.19\times10^{-2}$, $3.95\times10^{-2}$, $0.118$ (local exponents $1.00$); for $m=3$, $2.68\times10^{-2}$, $8.04\times10^{-2}$, $0.268$ and $0.6$–$0.8$ (exponents $1.00$, $1.00$, then saturation), with the solver lowering $\omega/\Omega$ to $0.04$–$0.9$ in its attempt to reduce the residual. A branch would show as a floor dropping to the solver's precision ($10^{-15}$ on the hexagon); none does. Six unpinned solves from random non-planar perturbations of the hexagon (coefficients perturbed by $0.1\rho$, $\omega$ by $5\%$) at $\rho=1$ and $\rho=3$ all return to the planar family with residual $\le2.9\times10^{-15}$, $\max|z|\le10^{-19}\rho$ and speed deviation $\le7\times10^{-15}$: each is a hexagon at a different radius ($\omega/\Omega$ between $1.2$ and $8.4$ with the matching radius change). Twenty random pinned seeds at $\rho=1$, $M=4$ with coefficients perturbed by $0.3\rho$ end between $1.15\times10^{-2}$ and $2.6$. No candidate entered the pipeline.

> Claim grade: measured, bounded negative for the stated box (harmonics $M\le4$, pinned characters $2$ and $3$, $\varepsilon\le0.3$, three radii, seeds as recorded); derived for the resonance argument that excludes a bifurcation from the planar family. Falsifier: a set of Fourier coefficients with $M\le4$, a nonzero pinned out-of-plane harmonic and collocation residual below $10^{-8}$; or an axial mode of the hexagon of character $m\in\{2,3\}$ with frequency within $10^{-3}$ of $m\Omega$.

### 9.3 Shooting results

**C3 stratum** (100 starts, seed 20261007, 51 starts in branch 0 and 49 in branch 1, 26 minutes; receipt [weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting-c3.json](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting-c3.json)). Histogram of the final $J$: one start below $10^{-6}$, one below $10^{-3}$, two below $10^{-2}$, five below $10^{-1}$, median $31$; no window failed by contact or obstruction. The jet floor over the 100 refined starts is $1.4\times10^{-6}$. The five best starts are all the planar hexagon at $\rho=1$ or its slight deformations: start 21 (Section 9.1) at $\theta_+=90.00^\circ$, $\varphi_-=60.00^\circ$, $v=0.8202$, $J=8.9\times10^{-7}$; starts 63, 73, 56 and 19 at $\theta_+=90.4^\circ$–$93.6^\circ$, $\varphi_-\in\{60^\circ,180^\circ,300^\circ\}$ (the three azimuths at which the negative triangle interleaves the positive one, all the same hexagon), $v=0.815$–$0.822$, $J=9.6\times10^{-3}$–$3.9\times10^{-2}$. The monodromy was not computed because the return distance exceeded the pipeline's $10^{-3}$ gate; the hexagon's Floquet multipliers are $e^{\lambda P}$ with the Section 5.3 rates, that is $e^{2.46\cdot7.66}\approx1.5\times10^{8}$ for the largest. The stratum's outcome is therefore: the one exact member (the hexagon) is found from random starts and is confirmed unstable; no non-rigid C3 candidate reaches $10^{-8}$, and the floor of $J$ away from the hexagon's basin is $9.6\times10^{-3}$ (start 63, itself a deformed hexagon) and $0.1$–$31$ elsewhere.

**Unconstrained stratum** (100 starts, seed 20261007, sixteen parameters, 34 minutes; receipt [weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting-free.json](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-f3-shooting-free.json)). The outcome is a bounded negative at a weak level: the search establishes that no unconstrained start among 100, refined by the stated procedure over one binary period, approaches a spherical equal-speed history, with a floor four orders of magnitude above the preregistered bounded-negative level $10^{-3}$; it does not establish that none exists, because the optimizer's reach in sixteen dimensions over a dispersal-dominated objective is limited, and the honest box statement is the one in Section 14.

> Claim grade (both strata): measured, bounded negative for the stated boxes, representations, windows and start counts. The C3 result is strong (the stratum's only low basin is the known exact solution, which is unstable); the unconstrained result is weak (floor $31.6$ with a search that could not reach the one solution known to exist in the full space from random starts). ## 10. Integer frequencies, phasing and coexistence

The integer-frequency tabulation applies only to an exact family, and the only exact six-member family found in this investigation is F0, tabulated in Section 5.5: continuously adjustable in $\rho$ with $\Omega^2\propto\rho^{-3}$, so the integer rates $\Omega_n=n$ are sampled points of a continuum, not dynamically selected; all eight are regular (below the singular radius $\sqrt3$, with indefinite but invertible $M$), and only $n=1$ is subfield. ## 11. Stability and fate

### R2.3 The $k=3$ sector in closed form and the hexagon's instability at every regular radius

Instrument: [weber-binding-sphere-r2-k3-sector.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-r2-k3-sector.mjs), receipt [weber-binding-sphere-r2-k3-sector.json](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-r2-k3-sector.json). The linearized rotating-frame system about the balanced hexagon is $M\ddot{\boldsymbol\xi}+2\Omega J\dot{\boldsymbol\xi}+\mathcal K\boldsymbol\xi=0$ with $\mathcal K=\nabla^2U-\Omega^2\Pi$ (round 1, Section 5.3, after [ring analysis eq. 5.1](weber-overnight-ring.md#51-the-rotating-frame-second-variation)); the Weber terms enter only through $M$. $$

with the gyroscopic block $2\Omega\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\1&0\end{pmatrix}$ on $(a,b)$ and zero on $c$. $$

\tag{R2.1}
$$

### R4.2 Adversarial review of the reference lane's Section 18 (great-circle closest-approach lemma)

Scope: Section 18 of [weber-binding-sphere-independent-reference.md](weber-binding-sphere-independent-reference.md) (18.1–18.4) and its spot-check script `weber-binding-sphere-reference-F5-identities.mjs`, read after the PI's exposure at 03:22Z; nothing else of that document was read. Own instrument: [weber-binding-sphere-r4-review-18.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-r4-review-18.mjs), receipt [weber-binding-sphere-r4-review-18.json](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-r4-review-18.json), plus the scratch `fourier18.mjs` (03:23Z) for the refined Fourier checks; no code is shared with the reference script.

**(a) Pair identities (18.1)–(18.3).** Own construction: member $i$ on the great circle of oriented normal $\mathbf m_i$ with phase $\phi_i$, $\mathbf X_i=\cos(\Omega T+\phi_i)\mathbf u_i+\sin(\Omega T+\phi_i)\mathbf u_i'$ with $(\mathbf u_i,\mathbf u_i',\mathbf m_i)$ right-handed, so the phase origin is not the intersection line of the two planes and (18.1)'s $C$, $A$, $\alpha$ are extracted from the data rather than taken from the formula: the Fourier analysis of $\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf X_j$ over one cycle of $\tau$ gives a mean $C$, a $2\Omega$ amplitude $A$ and phase $\alpha$, and no $4\Omega$ component ($2.9\times10^{-16}$). On 200 random pairs ($\Omega\in[0.3,1.3]$) at five times each: $A=\tfrac12(1-\mathbf m_i\cdot\mathbf m_j)$ to $8.9\times10^{-16}$; $d^2=2(1-C)-2A\cos\tau$ to $4\times10^{-15}$; $\dot d=2A\Omega\sin\tau/d$ to $7.6\times10^{-15}$; $\|\mathbf w\|^2=\Omega^2(d^2+4A\cos\tau)$ to $1.2\times10^{-14}$; and the bracket with the centripetal accelerations inserted, computed from the law's own form $1-\dot d^2/2+d\ddot d$ with $\ddot d=\mathbf e\cdot(\mathbf A_i-\mathbf A_j)+\|\mathbf w_\perp\|^2/d$ and $\mathbf A=-\Omega^2\mathbf X$, equals $1+4A\Omega^2\cos\tau-6A^2\Omega^2\sin^2\tau/d^2$ to $2.7\times10^{-14}$. Confirmed. (The reference's closed form $1-\tfrac32\dot d^2+\|\mathbf w\|^2-\Omega^2d^2$ follows from $d\ddot d=-\Omega^2d^2+\|\mathbf w\|^2-\dot d^2$ on the centripetal ansatz; also confirmed by the same numbers.)

**(b) Sign and decay of the Fourier coefficients of $\sqrt{1-k\cos\tau}$.** The algebraic sign argument is sound as written: $\binom{1/2}{n}$ has the sign $(-1)^{n+1}$ for $n\ge1$, $(-k)^n$ the sign $(-1)^n$, so every term of the binomial series with $n\ge1$ is negative, and $\cos^n\tau$ expands in $\cos m\tau$ with the non-negative coefficients $2^{1-n}\binom{n}{(n-m)/2}$; hence $c_m<0$ for every $m\ge1$ and every $0<k<1$ (the series converges absolutely for $k<1$). Numerically (FFT with $2^{14}$–$2^{16}$ points): at $k\in\{0.9,0.99\}$ all $c_1,\dots,c_{60}$ are negative; at $k=0.1$ and $0.5$ the first non-negative value appears at $m=17$ and $m=31$ with $|c_m|=1.4\times10^{-16}$ and $9\times10^{-18}$, which is double-precision round-off (the preceding coefficients are $2.3\times10^{-16}$ and $3.0\times10^{-16}$), so the numerical check is limited to coefficients above $10^{-15}$ and the sign claim rests on the proof, which is correct. Decay: with $\rho=1/k-\sqrt{1/k^2-1}$, the ratio $c_m/c_{m-1}$ against the Darboux form $\rho\,((m-1)/m)^{3/2}$ agrees to $8.4\times10^{-3}$ at $k=0.9$ and $1.8\times10^{-2}$ at $k=0.99$ over $m\in[10,40]$ (the deviation is the expected $O(1/m)$ subleading correction, decreasing with $m$: at $m=20$ the ratios are $0.57915$ versus $0.58037$ and $0.79911$ versus $0.80336$); at $k=0.1$ and $0.5$ the coefficients are below round-off beyond $m\approx12$ and $30$ and the ratio cannot be tested there. The per-member dominance claim, that the coefficients of $F_3=\sin^2\tau\,(1-k\cos\tau)^{-3/2}$ grow by a factor $m$ relative to those of $F_1=(1-k\cos\tau)^{-1/2}$ at the same $\rho$, is confirmed at $k=0.9$: $(F_{3,m}/F_{1,m})/m=-0.945,-1.013,-1.034,-1.045$ at $m=10,20,30,40$ (tending to a constant, of negative sign), and at $k=0.99$ the same ratio is still drifting ($-0.17$ to $-0.26$), consistent with slower convergence as $\rho\to1$. Confirmed, with the remark that the reference's "three digits" for the Darboux ratio at $m=12$ is optimistic; two digits is what the subleading terms allow at that order, and nothing in the theorem depends on the constant.

**(c) The logical step from "$\sum\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$ constant" to the phase-group cancellation.** The chain is: (i) on a solution of the closed system the PI identity $\ddot G=T_{\mathrm{kin}}+H$ with $I=3R^2$ constant forces $\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$ to be constant in $T$ (Section 2.5 of this document; the reference's 15.1); (ii) each non-rigid $d_{ij}=\sqrt{a_{ij}}\sqrt{1-k_{ij}\cos\tau_{ij}}$ with $\tau_{ij}=2\Omega T+\alpha_{ij}$ has the Fourier series $\sqrt{a_{ij}}\sum_mc_m(k_{ij})\cos(m\tau_{ij})$, so the coefficient of $e^{2im\Omega T}$ in the constant sum is $\tfrac12\sum\sigma_{ij}\sqrt{a_{ij}}c_m(k_{ij})e^{im\alpha_{ij}}$ and must vanish for every $m\ge1$; (iii) dividing by $c_m(k^\ast)\ne0$ of the pairs of minimal $\kappa$ (largest $k$, largest $\rho$) and letting $m\to\infty$, the other pairs' contributions vanish geometrically, leaving $P_m=\sum_S\sigma\sqrt a\,e^{im\alpha}\to0$; (iv) the Cesàro mean $N^{-1}\sum_{m\le N}P_m\bar z_l^m$ tends to the weight $W_l$ of the phase group $l$ because the cross terms are bounded geometric sums over $N$, so every $W_l=0$. Each step is sound. Two remarks. First, step (ii) uses that every pair shares the same base frequency $2\Omega$, which holds exactly because all members share one rate; with distinct rates the coefficient sum would separate by frequency instead and the conclusion would be stronger, not weaker (functions at incommensurate frequencies cannot cancel), so the common rate is used but is not a loophole; it is the reason the sum is analysed at one frequency. Second, the remainder estimate is printed as $O((\rho'/\rho^\ast)^mm^{3/2})$; since both numerator and denominator carry $m^{-3/2}$, the correct form is $O((\rho'/\rho^\ast)^m)$, which is what the argument needs; the printed power of $m$ is a slip without consequence. The division by $c_m(k^\ast)$ requires $c_m(k^\ast)\ne0$ for all large $m$, which is the sign claim. The per-member version applies the same chain to the per-member identity with the three functions $F_1,F_2,F_3$; the dominance of $F_3$ by a factor $m$ at common $\rho$ (checked in (b)) means that in the limit only the $F_3$ terms of the minimal-$\kappa$ partners survive, their common leading constant divides out, and the weights reduce to $\sigma_{ij}k^{\ast2}\sqrt{a_{ij}}\propto\sigma_{ij}\sqrt{a_{ij}}$. Sound. One hypothesis should be stated explicitly: the theorem needs the minimal-$\kappa$ set to be non-empty, that is at least one non-rigid pair, and the rigid pairs ($A=0$) contribute constants only; both are stated. Confirmed.

**(d) The corollary for the fully symmetric orthogonal F2 triad.** Own computation of the twelve cross pairs of the orthogonal triad (normals $\hat{\mathbf z},\hat{\mathbf x},\hat{\mathbf y}$, three antipodal opposite-polarity pairs, $\Omega=1/2$, three phase choices in my basis convention): with equal phases, the minimal $\kappa=1.000$ is attained by four pairs in two phase groups, $\{0^\circ:-2,\ 180^\circ:+2\}$ in the weights $\sum\sigma\sqrt a$, neither zero; with phases $0,120^\circ,240^\circ$, $\kappa_{\min}=1.134$ is attained by two pairs in one group of weight $-2.13$; with generic phases $17^\circ,63^\circ,115^\circ$, $\kappa_{\min}=1.283$ by two pairs in one group of weight $-2.27$. In every case a phase group fails to cancel, so the lemma excludes the configuration, in agreement with the corollary and with the measured F2 residuals of Section 8 (never below $2$). The structural reason is as the reference states: within one circle pair the four cross pairs split into two same-polarity pairs with $(C,\alpha)$ and two mixed pairs with $(-C,\alpha+\pi)$, each group single-signed, so cancellation needs another circle pair at the same $\kappa$ with the opposite sign at the same phase, and with three congruent circle pairs the counts cannot balance (two groups of equal $|\,W|$ and opposite sign would need an even number of circle pairs at the minimal $\kappa$ sharing phases, which three cannot supply in the symmetric case). The additional odd-harmonic argument "$p_1=p_3=0$ is impossible for three unit phases" was not re-derived here (it belongs to the reference's Section 17 apparatus, not read); the exclusion stands on the lemma alone. Confirmed.

**Verdict.** Theorem 18.3 and its corollaries (a)–(c): confirmed, no defect found. The identities (a) are verified to $3\times10^{-14}$ by independent code, the sign claim (b) is proved algebraically and numerically consistent down to round-off, the decay and the $F_3$ dominance are numerically confirmed where the coefficients are resolvable, the logical chain (c) is sound with one typographical slip in the remainder's power of $m$ that does not affect the argument, and the orthogonal-triad corollary (d) is reproduced with explicit $\kappa$ and phase-group weights. ### S3. Necessary conditions for any bounded equal-speed sphere (derived)

With $I=\tfrac12\sum_i\|\mathbf X_i\|^2$, $G=I-\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$, $T_{\mathrm{kin}}=\tfrac12\sum_i\|\mathbf V_i\|^2$ and the law's energy-like invariant $H=T_{\mathrm{kin}}+\sum_{i<j}(\sigma_{ij}/d_{ij})(1-\dot d_{ij}^2/2)$, the law satisfies $\ddot G=T_{\mathrm{kin}}+H$ identically (PI derivation, 23:44Z; checked by three separately authored codes to $1.6\times10^{-14}$, $1.7\times10^{-13}$ and $3\times10^{-7}$ along a flow). Any history bounded for all time therefore has $H=-\langle T_{\mathrm{kin}}\rangle\le0$, and a bounded equal-speed sphere has exactly $H=-3v^2$, $\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$ constant in time, $\sum_{i<j}(\sigma_{ij}/d_{ij})(1-\dot d_{ij}^2/2)=-6v^2$ and $\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}\ddot d_{ij}=0$. The reference lane sharpened these to per-member conditions using $\mathbf X_i\cdot\mathbf e_{ij}=d_{ij}/2$ on a sphere: $\sum_{j\ne i}(\sigma_{ij}/d_{ij})[1-\dot d_{ij}^2/2+d_{ij}\ddot d_{ij}]=-2v^2$ and $\sum_{j\ne i}(c_{ij}/d_{ij})\mathbf V_i\cdot\mathbf X_j=0$ for every member and time, and showed that on any sphere history an antipodal partner contributes exactly its static inverse-square acceleration, and that every great-circle history must be centripetal at one common rate so that the implicit solve is eliminated for that sub-class. None of these is an obstruction; no class-level obstruction for non-rigid equal-speed spherical histories was found, and that question is open.

### S4. Rigid reduction (derived, both lanes)

On any rigid rotation about an axis through the origin every pair separation is constant, so $\dot d_{ij}=\ddot d_{ij}=0$ and the Weber velocity terms cancel exactly in the solve; the solved accelerations are centripetal if and only if the instantaneous inverse-square balance $-\Omega^2\mathbf X_{i,\perp}=\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}K\mathbf e_{ij}/d_{ij}^2$ holds. Equal speed on a rigid rotation forces equal axial distance, so every rigid sphere candidate lies on one circle or on two circles $z=\pm z_0$ of equal radius. Rigid spherical arrangements under this law are therefore the relative equilibria of the inverse-square comparison, and the Weber terms enter only through the determinant of the solve and through the stability problem.
