## Independent derivation

Let $S$ be the complete candidate source range, lying strictly on one side of zero, and let $u_0\in S$. The exact source velocity is absolutely continuous on $S$. On positive time this follows from the finite, ordered polynomial mesh, exact shared velocity nodes and continuous first derivative; an ordinary acceleration jump is allowed. On negative time it follows from the declared smooth joined rigid branch. With $|Q_j''|\le M$ almost everywhere,

$$
Q_j(u)-Q_j(u_0)-Q_j'(u_0)(u-u_0)
=\int_{u_0}^{u}(u-r)Q_j''(r)\,dr,
\qquad
\left|Q_j(u)-Q_j(u_0)-Q_j'(u_0)(u-u_0)\right|
\le \frac{M}{2}|u-u_0|^2.
$$

For $u<u_0$, reversing both the integral orientation and the sign of $u-r$ gives the same absolute bound. Both knot acceleration traces are safely included in the source box; the bound does not require a classical acceleration at every knot.

For each reception parameter, the actual candidate source time lies in $S$, and $r\ge|s_c-u_0|$. The point position and velocity intervals contain the exact center values. Consequently their interval-polynomial linear expression plus an independent uniform error $Mr^2/2$ contains the exact composed source in each component. Treating point intervals independently may enlarge the result but cannot exclude the exact correlated values. A vector norm remainder bounded by $Mr^2/2$ is contained in the component box with that same radius in each coordinate. The polynomial's existing coefficient and uniform-error terms remain present. No derivative of the arbitrary uniform remainder is used.

For a candidate positive delay $\tau_c$, let $R_c=Q_i(t)-Q_j(t-\tau_c)$. The identity

$$
|\tau_c-|R_c||=\frac{|\tau_c^2-|R_c|^2|}{\tau_c+|R_c|}
\le \frac{|\tau_c^2-|R_c|^2|}{\tau_{\min}}
$$

requires $\tau_{\min}>0$ but no sign assumption on the squared gap. The complete fixed-source causal function has lower two-point slope $1-L$ for a complete $L$-Lipschitz source. Given the already stated root-existence and separation premises, the root displacement is therefore bounded by the recorded absolute squared-gap bound divided by $\tau_{\min}(1-L)$.

This root argument uses the complete source path, not merely the Taylor interval. Thus an enlarged true-root interval may cross birth even though $S$ cannot. The returned vector remains a candidate-vector enclosure; the downstream consumer must still add $P+L\delta$, with $\delta=\delta_0+P/(1-L)$, construct the whole true-source interval, include every velocity/acceleration trace and retain the source-zero jump allowance. The Taylor approximation grants no permission to omit those terms.
