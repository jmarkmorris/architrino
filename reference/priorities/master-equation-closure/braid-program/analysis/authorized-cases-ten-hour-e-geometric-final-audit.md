## Independent reconstruction of the RMS lower bound

For arbitrary four labeled positions, put

$$
\bar X=\frac14\sum_jX_j,\qquad
A=\frac{X_0-X_2}{2},\quad
B=\frac{X_1-X_3}{2},\quad
C=\frac{X_0+X_2-X_1-X_3}{4}.
$$

After optimal RMS translation, the centered positions are $A+C,B-C,-A+C,-B-C$. Their mean squared discrepancy from an oriented comparison square is

$$
\frac{|A-r_*u|^2+|B-r_*v|^2}{2}+|C|^2.
\tag{4}
$$

All mixed terms with $C$ cancel. Maximizing $A\cdot u+B\cdot v$ over orthonormal two-frames gives the sum $\sigma_1+\sigma_2$ of the singular values of the matrix $[A\ B]$. To see this without an orientation assumption, diagonalize its two-column Gram matrix by an orthogonal two-dimensional change of basis. The transformed columns are perpendicular, and each projection is bounded by its length; their normalized directions attain both bounds. A missing direction in the rank-deficient case can be completed orthogonally. Every resulting two-frame still extends to a proper spatial rotation.

Since $\sigma_1\sigma_2=|A\times B|$, the exact least-RMS distance is

$$
L(X;r_*)^2
=|C|^2+\frac{|A|^2+|B|^2}{2}+r_*^2
-r_*\sqrt{|A|^2+|B|^2+2|A\times B|}.
\tag{5}
$$

This agrees with the independently frozen cross-product reference and the subject's equivalent singular-value formula. Since the RMS discrepancy for any comparison is no greater than its maximum-member discrepancy,

$$
d_\infty(X;r_*)\ge L(X;r_*).
\tag{6}
$$

For configurations $X,\widetilde X$ with $\max_j|X_j-\widetilde X_j|\le E$, their configuration RMS separation is at most $E$. Distance to the same set is one-Lipschitz, giving

$$
|L(X;r_*)-L(\widetilde X;r_*)|\le E.
\tag{7}
$$

There is no extra factor from subtracting the center: optimal translation is already inside this set-distance argument. Likewise changing the comparison radius by $\Delta r$ moves each comparison point by $|\Delta r|$, so $L$ is one-Lipschitz in radius.

These facts retain all three spatial components and fixed labels. They are geometry statements, not evidence that an arbitrary square solves the E+M equation.
