## 1. Law, question and scope

The law is the instantaneous Weber comparison law with $K=c_f=1$:

$$
\mathbf A_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{d_{ij}^{2}}\left[1-\frac{\dot d_{ij}^{2}}{2}+d_{ij}\ddot d_{ij}\right]\mathbf e_{ij},\qquad \ddot d_{ij}=\mathbf e_{ij}\cdot(\mathbf A_i-\mathbf A_j)+\frac{\lVert\mathbf w_{ij,\perp}\rVert^{2}}{d_{ij}},
\tag{1.1}
$$

where $\mathbf w_{ij,\perp}$ is the part of $\mathbf V_i-\mathbf V_j$ perpendicular to $\mathbf e_{ij}$. Members carry unit weights and no mass; the law gives accelerations directly. Because $\ddot d_{ij}$ contains every member's unknown acceleration, each evaluation assembles and solves the full $18\times18$ linear system $M_{\mathrm{acc}}\mathbf A=\mathbf b$ (for the two-member known case, the $6\times6$ system). The assembly and the solve are imported unmodified from the validated pair instrument ([weber-overnight-pair-instrument.mjs](../../binary-research/evidence/weber-overnight-pair-instrument.mjs), functions `makeParams`, `assemble`, `luFactor`, `luSolve`); no isolated-pair denominator is applied anywhere. A separation below $10^{-6}$, a zero pivot or $\lvert\det M_{\mathrm{acc}}\rvert<10^{-8}$ at any evaluated phase ends a regular case: a trial step that produces one is rejected, and a fine-grid evaluation that meets one is marked singular and cannot be a periodic orbit.

The question is whether six members, three of each polarity, can move for all time on one sphere of radius $R=1$ at one common constant speed in a periodic history other than the planar alternating hexagon. The first run searched by integrating over a lap and could not cover the unconstrained stratum at $R=1$, because generic states there collide before a quarter lap. Collocation represents the whole closed history at once and integrates nothing, so that obstacle does not arise. What collocation can establish is bounded in a different way: it finds zeros of a finite system from finitely many starts, so a negative is a floor for the stated strata, harmonics, starts and tolerances, never an exclusion of every spherical history.

## 2. The instrument as implemented

The library is [weber-binding-sphere-mc-lib.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-mc-lib.mjs); the search driver is [weber-binding-sphere-mc-search.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-mc-search.mjs).

### 2.1 Representation

Each member's history is a closed curve with one fundamental rate,

$$
\mathbf X_i(T)=\mathbf a_{i,0}+\sum_{m=1}^{M}\left[\mathbf a_{i,m}\cos(m\omega T)+\mathbf b_{i,m}\sin(m\omega T)\right],
\tag{2.1}
$$

so six members carry $18(2M+1)$ real coefficients ($126$ at $M=3$, $198$ at $M=5$). Positions and the phase derivatives $\mathbf X_i'$, $\mathbf X_i''$ are evaluated analytically from the coefficients; $\mathbf V_i=\omega\mathbf X_i'$ and the curve's own acceleration is $\omega^2\mathbf X_i''$. The same code runs with any member count; the known case KC3 uses two members.

### 2.2 Residuals

At the $N_c$ equispaced phases $\tau_k=2\pi k/N_c$, with $N_c=4M+4$ in every six-member run (the specification requires $N_c\ge4M+2$; KC3 uses exactly $4M+2$), the law's accelerations $\mathbf A^{\mathrm{law}}(\mathbf X,\mathbf V)$ are obtained from the full solve and three residuals are formed:

$$
\mathbf E_i=\frac{\omega^2\mathbf X_i''-\mathbf A_i^{\mathrm{law}}}{\omega^{2}R},\qquad S_i=\frac{\lVert\mathbf X_i\rVert^{2}-R^{2}}{R^{2}},\qquad V_i=\frac{\omega^2\lVert\mathbf X_i'\rVert^{2}-v^{2}}{v^{2}}.
\tag{2.2}
$$

Stage 1 solves $\mathbf E=0$ alone, which asks for a periodic orbit of the law of any shape. Stage 2 solves $\mathbf E$, $S$ and $V$ together with $v$ unknown, which asks for an equal-speed history on the sphere. Stage 2 is run in two ways from every start: directly, and from the stage-1 result of the same start. At $M=3$ in the unconstrained stratum stage 2 has $107$ unknowns ($105$ coefficients, $\omega$ and $v$) and $480$ residual rows besides the penalty rows, so a collocation zero is strongly over-determined; whether it is a zero of the continuous problem is decided on the fine grid (Section 2.6).

Two penalty blocks are appended as further residual rows. The separation penalty is the specified one: for every pair and collocation phase, $10\,\max(0,\,0.2R-d_{ij})/R$; the number of active rows at the final iterate is reported. The speed box is an addition of this lane, used only in the runs labelled as such: for every member and collocation phase, $10\,\max(0,\lVert\mathbf V_i\rVert-v_{\mathrm{box}})/v_{\mathrm{box}}$ with $v_{\mathrm{box}}=3$. It declares a search box and is zero inside it; it changes no acceleration.

### 2.3 Gauges, exactly as imposed

- **Centre.** $\sum_i\mathbf X_i\equiv0$ is imposed on every harmonic, including the constant one, by construction: the unknowns are coordinates in an orthonormal basis of the subspace of coefficient vectors whose six-member sum vanishes for each harmonic and axis ($105$ dimensions at $M=3$, $165$ at $M=5$). For the harmonics $m\ge1$ this loses nothing, because (1.1) gives $\sum_i\mathbf A_i=0$ (the pair contributions are antisymmetric) and a periodic history has zero mean velocity. For the constant harmonic it is a restriction in stage 2: an equal-speed sphere whose centroid is a fixed point other than the sphere's centre is outside the box. This follows the specification and is listed in Section 10.
- **Time phase and the three rotations.** These are not fixed by extra residual rows. At every Levenberg–Marquardt iteration the four tangent vectors of the current curve set under a time shift and under rotations about the three axes are computed analytically, projected to the reduced coordinates, normalized, and each adds $\mu\,\hat{\mathbf g}\hat{\mathbf g}^{\mathsf T}$ to the normal matrix with $\mu$ the largest diagonal entry of $J^{\mathsf T}J$; the gradient is unchanged. Since the gauge tangents lie in the kernel of $J$ at a solution, the step has no component along them and the normal matrix is non-singular along them. The reason for a step condition instead of four fixed rows is the hexagon itself: for a rigid rotation the time-shift tangent coincides with the rotation tangent about the rotation axis, so the gauge orbit is three-dimensional and four independent linear rows anchored at a perturbed start cannot all be met on it. The start's own phase and orientation are therefore kept, not normalized.
- **Stage 1 normalization.** The fundamental rate $\omega$ is an unknown and one residual row fixes the time-mean of $\tfrac16\sum_i\lVert\mathbf X_i\rVert^2$, computed exactly from the coefficients by Parseval's identity, to $R^2$. (A fixed-period variant with $\omega$ removed from the unknowns exists in the library and was not used.) Stage 2 needs no normalization: the sphere rows fix the scale.
- **Symmetry strata** restrict the same basis further (Section 2.4); the gauge tangents are projected into the stratum, which leaves exactly the gauge directions that commute with the symmetry.

### 2.4 Strata

A symmetry acts on a curve set by $(g\mathbf X)_{\pi(i)}(\tau)=\mathsf R\,\mathbf X_i(\epsilon\tau)$ with a rotation $\mathsf R$, a member permutation $\pi$ and $\epsilon=\pm1$ (time reversal, a symmetry of (1.1) because velocities enter only through $\dot d^2$ and $\lVert\mathbf w_\perp\rVert^2$). A permutation that exchanges the two polarity classes is allowed because (1.1) depends on polarities only through $\sigma_{ij}$; this is the polarity flip. The stratum is the fixed subspace of the group generated, obtained by averaging over the group and orthonormalizing; the hexagon $\mathbf X_k=R(\cos(\tau+k\pi/3),\sin(\tau+k\pi/3),0)$ lies in each (checked to $6\times10^{-16}$ at every launch).

Equal speed on paths of different length means different lap counts per common period; in this representation that is a member whose dominant harmonic is $2$ or $3$. The class is not enforced after the start, and the dominant harmonic of every result is recorded.

### 2.5 Solver

Levenberg–Marquardt on the stacked residual vector $\mathbf r$. The Jacobian is assembled by the chain rule: the dependence of $\mathbf X$, $\mathbf X'$, $\mathbf X''$ on the coefficients and of the residuals on $\omega$ and $v$ is analytic, and the only numerically differentiated object is the $18\times36$ matrix $\partial\mathbf A^{\mathrm{law}}/\partial(\mathbf X,\mathbf V)$ at each collocation phase, by central differences with steps $10^{-6}R$ in position and $10^{-6}$ times the rms speed in velocity (72 full solves per phase). Against a central difference of the whole residual the assembled Jacobian agrees to $7\times10^{-10}$ relative (Section 3). The step solves $(J^{\mathsf T}J+\lambda\,\mathrm{diag}(J^{\mathsf T}J)+10^{-15}\mu I+\text{gauge borders})\,\boldsymbol\delta=-J^{\mathsf T}\mathbf r$ by Cholesky factorization; $\lambda$ starts at $10^{-3}$, is divided by $5$ after an accepted step and multiplied by $4$ after a rejected one, with the damping floor $\lambda\ge10^{-12}$. A trial point is rejected if its cost is not lower, if $\omega\le0$ or $v\le0$, or if any collocation phase is singular in the sense of Section 1. Termination: $\lVert\mathbf r\rVert^2\le10^{-24}$; or no descent up to $\lambda=10^{14}$; or 25 consecutive iterations each reducing the cost by less than $10^{-4}$ relative ("stalled"); or the iteration cap (250 at $M=3$).

### 2.6 Fine-grid evaluation and classification

Every result is re-evaluated on $N_f=256$ equispaced phases ($1024$ for KC3) by a routine that shares only the curve evaluation and the full solve with the solver. It reports the maximum over phases and members of $\lVert\mathbf E_i\rVert$, $\lvert S_i\rvert$, $\lvert V_i\rvert$ (in stage 1, $v$ is replaced by the rms speed), the minimum separation, the range of $\det M_{\mathrm{acc}}$, the radius and speed ranges with the speed label, the mean and variation of $H$, the first-run filter $H+3v^2$, the variation of $\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}d_{ij}$ over the period, the violation of $\ddot G=T_{\mathrm{kin}}+H$, the largest variation of any pair distance over the period (zero for a rigid configuration) and each member's dominant harmonic. Classification:

- **periodic orbit (measured):** not singular and fine-grid $\max\lVert\mathbf E\rVert\le10^{-8}$, and in addition the same residual divided by $v_{\mathrm{rms}}^2/R$ instead of $\omega^2R$ is $\le10^{-8}$. The second condition is a guard added after the degenerate limits of Section 5 were seen; it can only remove objects, never add one.
- **equal-speed sphere candidate:** a periodic orbit with fine-grid $\max\lvert S\rvert\le10^{-8}$ and $\max\lvert V\rvert\le10^{-8}$.
- **hexagon family member:** a periodic orbit with pair-distance variation and radius spread below $10^{-6}$ of the mean radius $r$ and $(v_{\mathrm{rms}}/r)^2r^3$ within $10^{-6}$ of $5/4-1/\sqrt3$; reported with $r$ and its lap period against $2\pi/\sqrt{(5/4-1/\sqrt3)/r^3}$.
- **hexagon neighbourhood:** as above with both tolerances $10^{-3}$; this labels stage-1 iterates that approach the hexagon slowly for the reason given in Section 4.
- **rigid periodic configuration, not the hexagon; other periodic orbit; non-convergence** otherwise. The score of a non-converged result is the largest of its fine-grid residuals; the smallest score over a stratum's starts is that stratum's floor.

### 2.7 Starts

Every start is generated from the seed $20261006+7919\,k+104729\,s$ for start index $k$ and stratum index $s$, by a 32-bit Mulberry generator, so any start can be regenerated alone. The first three starts of every stratum that contains the hexagon are reach starts: the hexagon's coefficients perturbed by $20\,\%$ of their Euclidean norm with independent Gaussian components over all harmonics and all three axes, projected into the stratum, with $\omega$ and $v$ each multiplied by a uniform factor in $[0.8,1.2]$. The remaining starts cycle through four constructions in the unconstrained and symmetric strata: (i) smooth spherical curves, each member a random great circle with random phase and sense, deformed by random first and second harmonics with Gaussian coefficients of scale $0.05$ to $0.30$ divided by the harmonic number, projected pointwise to the sphere on 64 phases and re-fitted to $M$ harmonics; (ii) random coefficients with Gaussian amplitudes decaying as $1/(1+m^2)$, scaled to mean-square radius $R^2$; (iii) the stacked-latitude-circle geometries of the first run's Section R2.1 (three antipodal opposite-polarity pairs on coaxial circles with radii $(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac13)$, $(1,\tfrac12,\tfrac12)$, $(\tfrac12,1,\tfrac12)$, a random triple in $[0.5,1]$, or $(\tfrac{\sqrt3}2,\tfrac{\sqrt3}2,1)$, or the same radii as same-circle pairs, with random phases and senses, each circle traversed the integer number of times that keeps the speeds equal up to $M$); (iv) the great-circle geometry of the first run's Section 8 (three antipodal opposite-polarity pairs on great circles, with the orthogonal triad of normals or three independent random normals). In the winding strata each member starts on a circle of radius proportional to $1/w_i$ traversed $w_i$ times, in antipodal opposite-polarity pairs on coaxial circles, in antipodal pairs with independent random axes, or as six independent circles, with a small random deformation. Every start is projected into its stratum; the start speed is uniform in $[0.5,1.2]$ and fixes $\omega$ through the start's rms phase speed.

## 4. Structure of the collocation system at the hexagon

In the unconstrained stratum the hexagon is a degenerate zero. The eigenvalues of $J^{\mathsf T}J$ at the hexagon, without gauge borders, have five values at round-off ($\lvert\cdot\rvert\le10^{-13}$) in both stages, followed by $3.4\times10^{-2}$ in stage 1 and $1.27$ in stage 2 (script [weber-binding-sphere-mc-hexagon-kernel.mjs](../evidence/weber-binding-sphere-mc-hexagon-kernel.mjs), arguments `3 1` and `3 2`, run at 22:17Z). One null vector is the in-plane first harmonic, the coincident time-shift and axis-rotation tangent. The other four are out-of-plane first-harmonic vectors, and only two dimensions of that four-dimensional space are the tilt gauges.

The two extra null directions are explained as follows. For a planar rigid ring an out-of-plane displacement $z_k$ changes no separation to first order, so $\dot d_{ij}$, $\ddot d_{ij}$ and the in-plane solve are unchanged to first order and the bracket of (1.1) stays $1$; the linearized out-of-plane motion is $\ddot z_i=\sum_{j\ne i}(\sigma_{ij}/d_{ij}^3)(z_i-z_j)$, a constant symmetric circulant system. Its eigenvectors are $\cos(jk\pi/3+\phi)$ with a frequency that depends only on $\lvert j\rvert$. A rigid tilt of the ring is the solution $z_k\propto\cos(\Omega T+k\pi/3+\phi)$, so the $j=1$ frequency is exactly $\Omega$, and then $z_k\propto\cos(\Omega T-k\pi/3+\phi)$, a tilt wave running against the rotation, solves the same linear system with the same period. It is not a symmetry, so the residual grows at second order along it. Consequences: Newton-type convergence to the hexagon is linear instead of quadratic in the unconstrained stratum (about 120 iterations in stage 2 against 6 in C3, where the counter-running wave is excluded by the symmetry), and stage 1 from a perturbed hexagon was still descending at the 250-iteration cap with motion residual $3\times10^{-10}$ and pair-distance variation $3\times10^{-6}$; these iterates are labelled "hexagon neighbourhood", and the stage 2 run started from each of them returned the exact hexagon.

> Claim grade: the kernel dimension is measured (the script above, $M=3$, both stages); its identification with the counter-running tilt wave is derived from the linearized out-of-plane equation, and was not separately tested by projecting the computed null vectors on $\cos(\tau-k\pi/3)$. Falsifier: a null vector of $J^{\mathsf T}J$ at the hexagon with content outside the first harmonic, or a C2, C3 or D3 kernel larger than the gauge directions that survive the symmetry.

## 5. Two degenerate limits of the specified motion residual

The specified motion residual divides by $\omega^2R$. With $\omega$ an unknown and no ceiling on it, that normalization gives the stage-1 and stage-2 systems two families of states that are not solutions of the law and whose residuals are nevertheless small or arbitrarily small. Both were found by the specified search from the first batch of starts, and they dominate its floors.

### 5.1 Micro-loops (the residual tends to zero)

Let each member trace a small closed loop of size $\epsilon$ about a fixed point of the sphere at speed $v$, so $\omega\sim v/\epsilon$. The curve's acceleration has magnitude of order $\omega v$, while the law's acceleration stays bounded as $\epsilon\to0$ for a non-singular static configuration with bounded speeds. Hence

$$
\lVert\mathbf E_i\rVert\le\frac{\omega v+\lVert\mathbf A_i^{\mathrm{law}}\rVert}{\omega^{2}R}=\frac{v}{\omega R}+O(\omega^{-2})\longrightarrow0\quad(\omega\to\infty),
\tag{5.1}
$$

while $S_i=O(\epsilon)$ and $V_i$ can vanish identically. The stage-2 residual therefore has infimum zero along states in which nothing satisfies the law: the members sit almost still while the law asks for accelerations of order one. This is a property of the normalization, not of the law.

### 5.2 Fast rigid rotation of a tight configuration (the residual tends to a small constant)

For a rigid rotation at rate $\omega$ about a fixed axis, with $\mathbf X_{i,\perp}$ the part of $\mathbf X_i$ perpendicular to the axis, $\dot d_{ij}=0$ and the centripetal accelerations $\mathbf A^{c}_i=-\omega^2\mathbf X_{i,\perp}$ give $\ddot d_{ij}=0$. Inserting $\mathbf A^{c}$ into $M_{\mathrm{acc}}\mathbf A=\mathbf b$ leaves the defect $\mathbf b-M_{\mathrm{acc}}\mathbf A^{c}=\mathbf g$ with $\mathbf g_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}\mathbf e_{ij}/d_{ij}^2+\omega^2\mathbf X_{i,\perp}$ (the velocity terms cancel between the two sides, which is the rigid reduction of the first run). So $\mathbf A^{\mathrm{law}}=\mathbf A^{c}+M_{\mathrm{acc}}^{-1}\mathbf g$ and

$$
\mathbf E=-\frac{M_{\mathrm{acc}}^{-1}\mathbf g}{\omega^{2}R}\longrightarrow-\frac{M_{\mathrm{acc}}^{-1}\mathbf X_{\perp}}{R}\quad(\omega\to\infty).
\tag{5.2}
$$

The limit does not depend on $\omega$, and it is small when $M_{\mathrm{acc}}$ has an eigenvalue of large magnitude along $\mathbf X_\perp$. Since $M_{\mathrm{acc}}-I=-\sum_{i<j}(\sigma_{ij}/d_{ij})\mathbf u_{ij}\mathbf u_{ij}^{\mathsf T}$ scales as the inverse separations, the optimizer drives pairs to the $0.2R$ penalty boundary and $\omega$ to thousands, with member speeds of hundreds of $c_f$. A hand check on the C3 floor state of Section 6.1: its members form two like-polarity triangles of side $0.2$ at distance $v/\omega=0.1155$ from the axis near the two poles; the breathing form of one triangle is $1-\tfrac13\cdot3\cdot(1/0.2)(0.2)^2/0.1155^2=-14$, so (5.2) predicts $\lVert\mathbf E\rVert\approx0.1155/14=8.2\times10^{-3}$ against the measured $8.3\times10^{-3}$; and the C3 run ended on this same state from at least nine solves with $\omega$ between $2568$ and $46093$ and the same motion residual $8.26\times10^{-3}$ to three digits, which is the $\omega$-independence that (5.2) states. Likewise (5.1) predicts $v/(\omega R)=4.0\times10^{-6}$ for the unconstrained floor state ($\omega=5756$, $v=0.0228$) against the measured $8.4\times10^{-6}$.

> Claim grade: (5.1) and (5.2) are derived (elementary bounds from the definition (2.2) and the matrix form of (1.1)); that the specified search is drawn to these limits is measured (Section 6, columns "final speed above 3" and the floors). Neither limit is a solution of the law or a sphere candidate, and no claim about the law follows from them. Falsifier of the measured part: the dumps named in Section 11, in which a floor state with $\omega>100$ must show either member speeds far above $c_f$ or loop sizes $v/\omega$ far below $R$.

### 5.3 The speed-normalized companion mode

To obtain floors that mean something, the targets were also run with the motion residual divided by the centripetal scale of the common speed instead, which is the normalization the first run used for its prescribed-path residuals:

$$
\mathbf E^{(v)}_i=\frac{(\omega^2\mathbf X_i''-\mathbf A_i^{\mathrm{law}})\,R}{v^{2}},
\tag{5.3}
$$

with $v$ the stage-2 unknown, or in stage 1 the rms speed $\omega\langle\tfrac16\sum_i\lVert\mathbf X_i'\rVert^2\rangle^{1/2}$ computed from the coefficients, together with the speed box $v_{\mathrm{box}}=3$ of Section 2.2. $\mathbf E^{(v)}=(\omega R/v)^2\mathbf E$, so the two residuals have exactly the same zeros; on a micro-loop $\lVert\mathbf E^{(v)}\rVert\approx\omega R/v\to\infty$, and the fast rigid limit lies outside the box. This mode is an addition to the frozen specification, declared here as such; it passed the hexagon zero and the Jacobian check in both stages before use (Section 3), and its reach is tested by its own reach starts (Section 6). The search box it declares is: member speeds at most $3\,c_f$ at the collocation phases, pair separations at least $0.2R$ at the collocation phases, with both penalties reported when active at a final iterate.

## 8. Floors and their boxes

A floor is the smallest fine-grid score reached by a non-hexagon stage-2 result in a run. It is a bounded negative for exactly its box: the stratum, $M=3$ with $N_c=16$, the starts counted in Section 6 generated as in Section 2.7 from seed 20261006, the three solves per start, the iteration cap of 250, the separation penalty at $0.2R$, and for the boxed runs member speeds at most $3$. Three things limit what these floors say.

First, under the specified residual the floors are set by the degenerate limits of Section 5 and not by the law. The unconstrained floor, $8.4\times10^{-6}$, is a micro-loop state with $\omega=5756$, common speed $0.0228$ and loop size $v/\omega=4\times10^{-6}$, still descending at the iteration cap; its motion residual in the speed normalization is of order $(\omega R/v)^2\times8\times10^{-6}\approx5\times10^{5}$. The C3 floor, $8.3\times10^{-3}$, is a fast rigid state with speeds near $1100$ at the separation-penalty boundary. With more iterations the micro-loop floor would keep falling by (5.1), so under the frozen acceptance rule alone (fine-grid $E$, $S$, $V$ at most $10^{-8}$) such a state could eventually be mis-read as a sphere candidate. No result of this run reached that threshold, and the periodic-orbit test of Section 2.6 carries the guard that excludes it.

Second, the informative number for the specified-residual runs is therefore the best score among finals that stayed at ordinary rates, defined here as $\omega\le10$ with all member speeds between $0.1$ and $3.1$ (the soft speed box lets speeds settle a few hundredths above $3$); it is given in the table below with the number of such finals, which is small because most runs left that region.

Third, in the speed-normalized runs, whose floors are free of both limits, the floors are between $0.3$ and several hundred and sit on the speed-box boundary. They are box-limited, and what they show is that from these starts the solver did not come near any zero other than the hexagon; they do not measure how far the nearest spherical history is.

### R2.3 Caution for any later use of this representation: the two large-rate limits

This restates Section 5 in the form asked for by the Principal Investigator. With the motion residual divided by $\omega^2R$ and $\omega$ free, two families of non-solutions have small residuals.

*Micro-loops.* Members that trace loops of size $v/\omega$ about fixed points have curve accelerations of magnitude at most $M\omega v$ (Bernstein's inequality for a trigonometric polynomial of degree $M$ applied to $\mathbf X'$; the factor $M$ is missing from (5.1) of round 1, as the adjudication of round 1 pointed out, and the conclusion is unchanged) while the law's accelerations stay bounded, so $\lVert\mathbf E\rVert\le Mv/(\omega R)+\lVert\mathbf A^{\mathrm{law}}\rVert/(\omega^2R)\to0$ as $\omega\to\infty$, with $S\to0$ and $V=0$: the specified stage-2 system has infimum zero on states where nothing obeys the law. Measured instance: $\omega=5756$, $v=0.0228$, score $8.4\times10^{-6}$ against $v/(\omega R)=4.0\times10^{-6}$, inside the bound with $M=3$.

*Fast rigid rotation.* For a rigid rotation at rate $\omega$ the centripetal accelerations leave the defect $\mathbf g_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}\mathbf e_{ij}/d_{ij}^2+\omega^2\mathbf X_{i,\perp}$ in $M_{\mathrm{acc}}\mathbf A=\mathbf b$, so $\mathbf E=-M_{\mathrm{acc}}^{-1}\mathbf g/(\omega^2R)\to-M_{\mathrm{acc}}^{-1}\mathbf X_\perp/R$, independent of $\omega$ and small when $M_{\mathrm{acc}}$ has a large eigenvalue along $\mathbf X_\perp$, which tight configurations supply because $M_{\mathrm{acc}}-I$ scales as the inverse separations. Measured instance: two like-polarity triangles of side $0.2$ at axial distance $0.1155$; the triangle's breathing form is $1-\tfrac13\cdot3\cdot(1/0.2)(0.2)^2/0.1155^2=-14$, giving the estimate $0.1155/14=8.2\times10^{-3}$ against the measured $8.26\times10^{-3}$, the same to three digits for $\omega$ from $2568$ to $46093$.

The speed normalization removes the first limit outright ($\lVert\mathbf E^{(v)}\rVert\approx\omega R/v$ on a micro-loop) and the bounds remove the second. A third tendency survives the amendment and governs the round-2 results (R2.5): since $\mathbf E^{(v)}=(\omega^2\mathbf X''-\mathbf A^{\mathrm{law}})R/v^2$ and the inverse-square part of $\mathbf A^{\mathrm{law}}$ does not grow with speed, the mismatch of that part is suppressed like $1/v^2$, and a least-squares descent with $v$ free moves toward larger $v$ until a bound stops it. Any later use should therefore pin or scan $v$, or continue in $v$, and not leave it free inside a wide box.

> Claim grade: the two limits and the $1/v^2$ suppression are derived from the definitions; the two measured instances are from the round-1 dumps (`search-free-nobox-0`, `search-c3-nobox-0`). Falsifier: a micro-loop state whose $\omega^2R$-normalized residual exceeds $Mv/(\omega R)+\lVert\mathbf A^{\mathrm{law}}\rVert/(\omega^2R)$, or a rigid state whose residual differs from $\lVert M_{\mathrm{acc}}^{-1}\mathbf g\rVert/(\omega^2R)$.
