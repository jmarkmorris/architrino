# A first-half-turn tangential exclusion and an ordinary superfield sector

## Scope and useful conclusion

Claim grade: derived, pending independent review. Three neutral antipodal pairs on complete common-center circular paths cannot balance when all their admitted delayed hits remain within the first positive half-turn relative to their positive-endpoint phase differences. In that geometry, every logarithmic tangential contribution is positive. This sign argument covers every possible delay, rather than relying on a sampled root list.

A concrete continuous superfield sector below has exactly thirty partner roots and six positive-delay self roots, all ordinary. It enlarges the earlier narrow aligned-phase example in radius, angular-rate and phase extent while preserving the unchanged coefficient-one logarithmic equation. Neither the sign theorem nor this sector excludes general relative phases or arbitrary superfield speeds.

## Law, histories and all-root sign condition

Use $K_{\log}=c_f=1$, unit fixed polarities, unchanged transmitter weighting and every ordinary positive-delay partner or self root. There is no ceiling, altered coefficient or singular continuation. The complete histories are $X_{a,s}(t)=s r_a e^{i(\omega t+\phi_a)}$ for every real $t$, where $a=1,2,3$, $s=\pm1$, $q_{a,s}=s$, $r_1=1<r_2<r_3$ and $\omega>0$. The simultaneous half-turn supplies the negative receiver equations, so consider a positive receiver of radius $a$ and a source of radius $b$.

Let $\epsilon=\phi_b-\phi_a$ denote a chosen real representative of the positive-endpoint phase difference. For a same-polarity source, the delayed relative angle is $\theta=\epsilon-\omega\tau$; for an opposite-polarity source, it is $\theta=\pi+\epsilon-\omega\tau$. Put $\eta=\omega\tau-\epsilon$. Every positive causal root satisfies

$$
|a-b|\le\tau\le a+b.
$$

At an ordinary root, the selected logarithmic law gives the same tangential expression for either polarity product:

$$
A_t=\frac{b\sin\eta}{\tau^2|D|},\qquad D=1-\widehat r\cdot V_b.
$$

Suppose every distinct pair of radii satisfies

$$
|\phi_b-\phi_a|<\omega|r_b-r_a|,
\qquad
\omega(r_a+r_b)+|\phi_b-\phi_a|<\pi,
$$

and every pair radius satisfies $2\omega r_a<\pi$. For interpair hits, these inequalities give $0<\eta<\pi$ at every possible root. Intrapair and self channels have $\epsilon=0$, so any positive root also has $0<\eta\le2\omega r_a<\pi$. Hence every ordinary row has $A_t>0$ independently of the sign of $D$ or the number of roots in its channel. At least one partner root exists in each partner channel: its distance-minus-delay is positive at zero and nonpositive at $a+b$. Within an everywhere-ordinary configuration, the sum therefore cannot have the zero tangential component required by circular motion.

This is an all-root sign exclusion on the ordinary subset of the displayed phase region. A singular root makes that configuration outside this theorem's equation domain; it is not silently omitted or assigned a value. The sign theorem alone does not assert that every parameter point in the larger region is ordinary. The next section establishes that stronger property for one closed sector.

## Declared ordinary superfield box

Select

$$
r_2\in[23/20,6/5],\qquad r_3\in[13/10,27/20],\qquad
\omega\in[21/20,27/25],
$$

$$
\phi_1=0,\qquad \phi_2,\phi_3\in[-1/100,1/100].
$$

Every speed exceeds one and is at most $729/500=1.458$. Distinct radii differ by at least $1/10$. Every positive-endpoint phase difference has magnitude at most $1/50$. Thus $|\epsilon|<\omega|a-b|$ holds for every interpair channel, and the full-delay upper angle is at most

$$
\omega(a+b)+|\epsilon|\le\frac{27}{25}\frac{27}{10}+\frac1{50}
=\frac{367}{125}=2.936<3<\pi.
$$

These estimates already establish the all-root tangential sign whenever the chart is ordinary. We now count the entire past and prove simplicity throughout the closed box.

## Gap functions and absence of early roots

For either source polarity let $L=a+b$ and $\eta=\omega\tau-\epsilon$. The squared causal gaps are

$$
G_+(\tau)=\tau^2-(a-b)^2-4ab\sin^2(\eta/2),
$$

$$
G_-(\tau)=\tau^2-a^2-b^2-2ab\cos\eta.
$$

The plus subscript means same polarity, including self; the minus subscript means opposite polarity. Positive zeros are exactly the causal roots, and $D=G_\pm'(\tau)/(2\tau)$ on a root. No root can occur beyond $L$.

Set $t_0=1/2$. For $0\le\tau\le t_0$, $|\eta|\le14/25$ and $|\eta/2|\le7/25$. The elementary inequality $\sin x\ge x-x^3/6$ on this range and oddness imply

$$
\sin^2(\eta/2)\ge\frac{97}{100}(\eta/2)^2,
$$

because $(1-(7/25)^2/6)^2>97/100$. In a same-polarity interpair channel, $ab\ge23/20$, so $(97/100)ab>1$. It follows that

$$
G_+(\tau)\le\tau^2-(a-b)^2-(\omega\tau-\epsilon)^2
\le\frac{\epsilon^2}{\omega^2-1}-(a-b)^2
\le\frac4{1025}-\frac1{100}<0.
$$

The middle expression is bounded by the exact maximum of its concave quadratic, so the entire initial interval is excluded. For self, $a=b$, $\epsilon=0$ and

$$
G_+(\tau)\le\tau^2\left[1-\frac{97}{100}a^2\omega^2\right]<0
\quad(0<\tau\le t_0),
$$

using $(97/100)(21/20)^2>1$. The same-time self zero is excluded by the selected equation, and no other root is hidden near that endpoint.

For opposite polarity, $\cos\eta\ge1-(14/25)^2/2>0$ on the initial interval. Hence $G_-(\tau)<1/4-2<0$. This covers intrapair opposite partners as well as interpair sources.

## Exactly one ordinary later root per channel

On $t_0\le\tau\le L$,

$$
\frac{101}{200}\le\eta\le\frac{367}{125}<\pi.
$$

Thus $\sin\eta>0$ throughout. For same polarity,

$$
G_+'=2\tau-2\omega ab\sin\eta,\quad
G_+''=2-2\omega^2ab\cos\eta,\quad
G_+'''=2\omega^3ab\sin\eta>0.
$$

At $t_0$, the allowed angle interval is $[101/200,14/25]\subset(0,\pi/2)$, and therefore

$$
G_+'(t_0)\le1-\frac{21}{10}\left[\frac{101}{200}-\frac{(101/200)^3}{6}\right]<0.
$$

At $L$,

$$
G_+'(L)\ge2(a+b-\omega ab)
\ge2\left[2-\frac{27}{25}\left(\frac{27}{20}\right)^2\right]
=\frac{317}{5000}>0.
$$

Since $G_+''$ is strictly increasing, $G_+'$ either increases throughout or first decreases and then increases. Starting negative and ending positive, it crosses zero exactly once. Thus $G_+$ decreases and then increases on this interval. It starts negative, while $G_+(L)=2ab(1+\cos(\omega L-\epsilon))>0$ because the angle lies strictly below $\pi$. It has exactly one zero, on its increasing part, where $G_+'>0$ and $D>0$. This includes one strictly positive self root for each of the six members.

For opposite polarity, $G_-'=2\tau+2\omega ab\sin\eta>0$ throughout this later interval, and $G_-(L)=2ab(1-\cos(\omega L-\epsilon))>0$. Since it starts negative, it too has exactly one positive root, with positive factor. Together with the initial-strip and full-delay bounds, this proves a complete census of thirty partner and six self roots at every parameter point. Every delay exceeds $1/2$. Compactness and simplicity provide a uniform positive factor margin, but no numerical value is asserted.

All thirty-six tangential rows are strictly positive. Their complete sum cannot be the circular acceleration, so the box contains no exact circular reference. Rotation covariance extends the root census and sign contradiction to every reception time on the complete histories. No stability analysis is admissible or needed for these unbalanced paths.

The general sign domain and the everywhere-ordinary concrete box are distinct claims. No law is assigned at a fold or coincidence, and the entire superfield parameter class remains open.
