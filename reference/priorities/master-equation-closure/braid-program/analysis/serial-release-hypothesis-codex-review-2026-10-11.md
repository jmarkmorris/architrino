## Independent mathematical check of the continuity question

For a source whose continuous history has a uniform speed bound $u_j<1$, the delay residual $g(\tau)=\tau-|\mathbf x_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(T-\tau)|$ satisfies $g'(\tau)=1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_j\ge1-u_j>0$ wherever differentiable. A finite initial velocity jump leaves the position and residual continuous, so this monotonicity persists across the jump. For distinct separated members $g(0)<0$; the specified rigid tail has $g(\tau)\to+\infty$, because its axial speed has magnitude below one and its rotational displacement stays bounded. Hence there is exactly one partner root. A strictly sub-wake receiver path has chord length smaller than elapsed time and therefore no positive-delay own-path root. These are **derived conditional statements about continuous paths**, not deductions from sampled speeds.

At a kick reception the source position, root delay and direction agree on the two sides but the source velocity jumps. Writing $D_+=D_- -\mathbf n\cdot\Delta\mathbf V_j$ and assuming both factors positive, the acceleration row satisfies $\mathbf A_+/\mathbf A_-=D_-/D_+$ in scalar magnitude, and its relative jump is $|\Delta D|/D_+$. Away from that reception, an ordinary separated root gives a locally continuous partner row by the implicit-function theorem. A nonzero kick residual at the arrival supplies local separation from that reception for the specified continuous histories; it does not locate a future crossing of an uncomputed EOM continuation.

In Theorem U, continuity at arrival supplies a sufficiently small interval where $\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf B\ge a/2$. Step 2 may shrink that interval to satisfy the direction modulus and remote-root exclusion; no lower bound on its size is required. The later logarithmic contradiction holds in any such positive interval. This independently resolves the report's proposed fixed-neighbourhood question, conditional on U1/U2; it does not establish those hypotheses for exact numerical-release solutions.
