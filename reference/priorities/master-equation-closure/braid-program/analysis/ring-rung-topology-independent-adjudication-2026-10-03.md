## Compact count invariant reconstructed

Let the parameter interval be compact and the number of labels finite. Compactness of reception positions and their stipulated noncoincidence give one positive minimum cross-channel clearance $\eta$. Joint continuity of source positions on a small compact past-time strip makes $|X_j^a(-d)-X_j^a(0)|<\eta/4$ uniformly for small enough positive $d$. Reducing that interval until $c_fd<\eta/4$ gives a strictly positive causal gap for every cross channel. Thus no cross root can escape through delay zero.

The common all-past bound $B$ gives $|X_i^a(0)-X_j^a(-d)|\le2B$. Choose a strict upper cutoff $d_+>2B/c_f$; all roots lie below it, and the gap is uniformly negative there and beyond. Every root is therefore in the interior of one fixed compact delay interval. The cutoff is a proven geometric bound, not a modification of the equation or exclusion of an admitted hit.

At a root, separation is $c_fd>0$, so the norm has no differentiation singularity there. Direct differentiation of the source position at emission gives

$$
\partial_d\bigl(|X_i^a(0)-X_j^a(-d)|-c_fd\bigr)
=n\cdot V_j^a(-d)-c_f=-c_fD.
$$

The source velocity is its own time derivative. No reception-path velocity or delay-derivative multiplier is inserted. Joint position/velocity continuity makes this delay derivative continuous in both variables near each root; parameter differentiability is unnecessary.

A nonzero derivative gives a strictly monotone root bracket whose endpoints have opposite signs. Both properties persist for nearby parameters, giving exactly one continuing root in that bracket. At a fixed parameter an infinite root set in the compact interval would have an accumulation root, contradicting that monotonicity. The root set is consequently finite. Choose disjoint brackets around it; the compact remaining delay set has a positive absolute gap minimum, which continuity preserves locally in the parameter. No additional root appears in that complement. The count is locally constant, and connectedness of the parameter interval makes it constant globally.

This construction is separate from the subject's numerical ring instruments. It establishes the count in each labelled cross channel and hence their total. A permutation of endpoint labels cannot avoid the total-count difference. No self-root condition enters the proof: recent self-root creation may change the self census while leaving the cross invariant intact.

## Finite-time application and its limits

For reception time $T$ on a finite compact interval, use the same gap with $X_i(T)$ and $X_j(T-d)$. A common bound on receivers and all past sources makes the complete delay range uniformly compact; continuous paths and velocities give the same joint continuity, and noncoincidence gives the same minimum reception clearance. Alternatively a separately proved complete uniform delay bound supplies the compact upper boundary, which must be chosen strictly beyond every possible root. The count proof then applies with $T$ as its parameter.

To identify a departure or arrival neighborhood with a circular rung's census, require sufficiently small $C^1$ deviations throughout the entire window $[T-d_+,T]$ for that complete bound. Uniform closeness preserves the circular root brackets and complement margins. A comparison limited to a recent cycle or a shorter stored history proves neither the absence of older roots nor the endpoint census. This is why the finite-time clarification is necessary.

If those endpoint windows have different cross counts, some intermediate time must fail an ordinary-root, noncoincidence, regularity or bounded/complete-delay hypothesis. The theorem supplies no location or type of that boundary event. In particular it proves neither that a fold can be continued lawfully nor that it cannot; the unchanged equation has received no continuation rule here. It gives no required impulse magnitude, neighboring-rung attraction, dispersion outcome or first departure direction. A local same-count branch, an unbounded ancient history and a history that reaches a singular event remain outside the exclusion as appropriate.
