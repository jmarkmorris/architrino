## 1. Setting and route

Members $k=0,1,2,3$ with polarities $q_k=(-1)^k$ sit at $\mathbf X_k=R\,\mathbf n_k$, $\mathbf n_k=(\cos\varphi_k,\sin\varphi_k,0)$, $\varphi_k=\varphi_0+k\pi/2+\Omega T$, and move with $\mathbf V_k=v\,\mathbf t_k$, $\mathbf t_k=(-\sin\varphi_k,\cos\varphi_k,0)$, $v=R\Omega$. Adjacent pairs $(k,k\pm1)$ have opposite polarity, $\sigma=-1$, separation $R\sqrt2$; the two diagonal pairs $(k,k+2)$ have the same polarity, $\sigma=+1$, separation $2R$. The two contributions are kept apart in every formula below as $\alpha=1/(4\sqrt2R)$ (adjacent) and $\beta=1/(4R)$ (diagonal), $\alpha=\beta/\sqrt2$. The frozen functional is $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}=\tfrac12\mathbf V^{T}H(\mathbf X)\mathbf V-\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}/r_{ij}$ with $H_{ii}=I$, $H_{ij}=M_{ij}=\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{2r_{ij}}(I+\mathbf e_{ij}\mathbf e_{ij}^{T})$ for $i\ne j$, so the Euler–Lagrange line is $H\mathbf A=\mathbf G$ with $\mathbf G_i=\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}/\partial\mathbf X_i-\sum_j\dot M_{ij}\mathbf V_j$; the second term is the mixed term from the time derivative of the velocity-coupling matrix, $\dot M_{ij}=\frac{\sigma}{2}\bigl[-\frac{\dot r}{r^2}(I+\mathbf e\mathbf e^{T})+\frac1r(\dot{\mathbf e}\mathbf e^{T}+\mathbf e\dot{\mathbf e}^{T})\bigr]$ with $\dot r=\mathbf e\cdot(\mathbf V_i-\mathbf V_j)$, $\dot{\mathbf e}=(I-\mathbf e\mathbf e^{T})(\mathbf V_i-\mathbf V_j)/r$.

The route is symmetry-adapted coordinates. The map $\mathcal T$: shift $k\to k+1$ composed with the rotation $Q$ by $\pi/2$ about $z$ preserves the configuration, the polarity pattern and every term of $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$, so $H$ and the linearized equations commute with it. In the local frames $(\mathbf n_k,\mathbf t_k,\hat{\mathbf z})$ the blocks of $H$ depend only on $l=j-k \bmod 4$: $B_0=I$, $B_2=\operatorname{diag}(-2\beta,-\beta,\beta)$, $B_1=\alpha\begin{psmallmatrix}1&3&0\\-3&-1&0\\0&0&-2\end{psmallmatrix}$, $B_3=B_1^{T}$, using $\mathbf n_{k+1}=\mathbf t_k$, $\mathbf t_{k+1}=-\mathbf n_k$, $\mathbf e_{k,k+1}=(\mathbf n_k-\mathbf t_k)/\sqrt2$, $\mathbf e_{k,k+2}=\mathbf n_k$. A vector whose local components carry the phase $i^{mk}$ lies in sector $m\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ and $H$ acts on it by the symbol $S_m=B_0+i^mB_1+i^{-m}B_1^{T}+(-1)^mB_2$. Axial and in-plane components never mix (every $\mathbf e_{ij}$ lies in the plane), so each sector splits into a scalar axial block and a $2\times2$ in-plane block. The two-body scalar structure is not used anywhere: the pair separations $R\sqrt2$ and $2R$ enter only through $\alpha$ and $\beta$.

## 2. The 12×12 velocity Hessian at the ring

### 2.1 Spectrum in closed form

$S_0=\operatorname{diag}(1+2\alpha-2\beta,\;1-2\alpha-\beta,\;1-4\alpha+\beta)$ and $S_2=\operatorname{diag}(1-2\alpha-2\beta,\;1+2\alpha-\beta,\;1+4\alpha+\beta)$ are diagonal; $S_1=\overline{S_3}$ has the Hermitian in-plane block $\begin{psmallmatrix}1+2\beta&6i\alpha\\-6i\alpha&1+\beta\end{psmallmatrix}$ and axial entry $1-\beta$. With $\alpha=\beta/\sqrt2$, $\beta=1/(4R)$, the twelve eigenvalues are

| sector | in-plane | axial |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $m=0$ | radial (breathing) $1-\dfrac{2-\sqrt2}{4R}$; tangential (rigid rotation) $1-\dfrac{1+\sqrt2}{4R}$ | common $z$: $1-\dfrac{2\sqrt2-1}{4R}$ |
| $m=2$ | radial (rectangle) $1-\dfrac{2+\sqrt2}{4R}$; tangential (rhombus) $1+\dfrac{\sqrt2-1}{4R}$ | alternating $z$: $1+\dfrac{2\sqrt2+1}{4R}$ |
| $m=\pm1$ (each eigenvalue twice) | $1+\dfrac{3\mp\sqrt{73}}{8R}$ | tilt: $1-\dfrac1{4R}$ |

The $m=\pm1$ in-plane values come from trace $2+3\beta$ and determinant $(1+2\beta)(1+\beta)-36\alpha^2=(1+2\beta)(1+\beta)-18\beta^2$, whose discriminant is $73\beta^2$. The rigid-rotation direction $\mathbf V_k=v\mathbf t_k$ is an exact eigenvector with eigenvalue $\lambda_t(R)=1-(1+\sqrt2)/(4R)$; the adjacent (opposite-polarity) pairs contribute $-\sqrt2/(4R)$ to it and the diagonal (same-polarity) pairs $-1/(4R)$, both negative, because adjacent velocities are perpendicular ($\mathbf V_k\cdot\mathbf V_{k+1}=0$) and the projected product $(\mathbf V_k\cdot\mathbf e)(\mathbf V_{k+1}\cdot\mathbf e)=v^2/2$ is positive under $\sigma=-1$. In the rotating pair the antiparallel velocities made the corresponding contribution positive; this sign reversal is the first place where transferring the pair structure fails. Grade: derived; measured against a Jacobi eigensolve of the Cartesian assembly at $R\in\{0.2,0.5,0.75,1,2,10,50,100\}$ with maximal error $8.9\times10^{-15}$, sector leakage below $10^{-15}$ and per-sector label error below $2\times10^{-14}$ (receipt `targets.T1_spectrum`). Falsifier: any eigenvalue of the Cartesian $H$ at a ring configuration differing from this table by more than $10^{-12}$.

## 3. Exact balance of the rigid rotation

### 3.1 Components that vanish by symmetry

The state $(\mathbf X,\mathbf V)$ is invariant under $\mathcal T$, so $\mathbf G$ and $H\mathbf A$ lie in sector $m=0$: $\mathbf G_k=g_n\mathbf n_k+g_t\mathbf t_k+g_z\hat{\mathbf z}$ with the same three numbers for every $k$. $g_z=0$ because the configuration and velocities lie in the plane $z=0$ and the law is invariant under $z\to-z$. $g_t=0$ because the reflection $\rho_k$ across the line through $\mathbf n_k$ maps the square to itself preserving polarities (it swaps $k\pm1$), sends $\mathbf t_k\to-\mathbf t_k$, and composed with time reversal $\mathbf V\to-\mathbf V$ fixes the state; $\mathbf G$ is even in $\mathbf V$ (both $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathbf X$ and $\dot M\mathbf V$ are quadratic in velocities plus a velocity-free pair term) and transforms as a vector under $\rho_k$, so its $\mathbf t_k$ component is odd under a symmetry of the state and vanishes. The acceleration $\mathbf A_k=-R\Omega^2\mathbf n_k$ is $m=0$ radial, on which $S_0$ is diagonal, so $H\mathbf A=\lambda_n\mathbf A$ with $\lambda_n=1-(2-\sqrt2)/(4R)$, not $\lambda_t\mathbf A$: the Hessian eigenvalue that multiplies the acceleration is the breathing eigenvalue, while the one that multiplies the velocity in $\mathbf p=H\mathbf V=\lambda_t\mathbf V$ is the rigid-rotation eigenvalue. Grade: derived; measured $\max_k|g_t|,|g_z|$ below $10^{-15}\|\mathbf G\|_\infty$ at every tabulated radius.

### 3.2 The radial component and the balance relation

Direct evaluation of the three contributions to $\mathbf G_k$, with $\dot r=0$ on every pair (rigid motion) and $\dot{\mathbf e}_{k,k\pm1}=v(\mathbf n_k\pm\mathbf t_k)/(R\sqrt2)$, $\dot{\mathbf e}_{k,k+2}=v\mathbf t_k/R$: each adjacent pair gives pair term $-(\mathbf n_k\mp\mathbf t_k)/(2\sqrt2R^2)$, velocity-gradient term $+(\mathbf n_k\mp\mathbf t_k)v^2/(8\sqrt2R^2)$ and mixed term $-v^2\mathbf n_k/(2\sqrt2R^2)$; the diagonal pair gives $+\mathbf n_k/(4R^2)$, $+v^2\mathbf n_k/(8R^2)$ and $+v^2\mathbf n_k/(4R^2)$. The tangential parts cancel between $k+1$ and $k-1$ as Section 3.1 requires, and

$$
g_n=-\frac{2\sqrt2-1}{4R^2}-\frac{3(\sqrt2-1)}{8R^2}\,v^2 ,
\qquad
\lambda_n\Bigl(-\frac{v^2}{R}\Bigr)=g_n
\;\Longleftrightarrow\;
v^2\bigl(8R-1-\sqrt2\bigr)=2\,(2\sqrt2-1).
$$

So the exact balance of the full equations $H\mathbf A=\mathbf G$ is

$$
v^2=\frac{2\,(2\sqrt2-1)}{8R-1-\sqrt2},\qquad
\Omega^2=\frac{2\,(2\sqrt2-1)}{R^2\,(8R-1-\sqrt2)} .
$$

A second, independent route gives the same relation: on the $\mathcal T$-fixed subspace $\mathbf X_k=Q^k(R\cos\theta,R\sin\theta,z)$ the functional restricts to $\mathcal L_{\mathrm{red}}=2\lambda_n\dot R^2+2\lambda_tR^2\dot\theta^2+2\lambda_z\dot z^2+(2\sqrt2-1)/R$, whose radial Euler–Lagrange equation at $\dot R=0$ reads $2(\lambda_tR^2)'\dot\theta^2=(2\sqrt2-1)/R^2$ with $(\lambda_tR^2)'=2R-(1+\sqrt2)/4$; the fixed subspace is invariant because the Euler–Lagrange vector field is $\mathcal T$-equivariant. The two routes share no algebra beyond $\lambda_t$ and $\lambda_n$. Consistency of the inertial form with the rotating-frame fixed-point condition $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathbf X=\Omega\,\hat{\mathbf z}\times H\mathbf V$ requires $\sum_j\dot M_{kj}\mathbf V_j=(\lambda_t-\lambda_n)\mathbf A_k=\frac{(2\sqrt2-1)v^2}{4R^2}\mathbf n_k$, which the three mixed terms above give exactly; a sign or indexing error in the mixed term would break this identity and the script checks both residuals separately.

Properties. A real $\Omega$ exists exactly for $R>(1+\sqrt2)/8=0.301776695297$, where $v\to\infty$; $v=c_f$ at $R=(2(2\sqrt2-1)+1+\sqrt2)/8=0.758883476483$. The pole sits at half the rigid-rotation singular radius $(1+\sqrt2)/4$, because it is the zero of $(\lambda_tR^2)'$, not of $\lambda_t$. For $(1+\sqrt2)/8<R<(1+\sqrt2)/4$ a balanced ring exists with $\lambda_t<0$, so its generalized momenta point against its velocities; every such ring sits where $H$ is indefinite. Zero-coupling counterpart (velocity coupling deleted, inverse-distance term kept): the elementary inverse-square sum gives $v_0^2=(2\sqrt2-1)/(4R)$, $\Omega_0^2=(2\sqrt2-1)/(4R^3)$, and

$$
\frac{v^2}{v_0^2}=\frac{8R}{8R-1-\sqrt2}>1 :
$$

the coupled ring turns faster than its zero-coupling counterpart, by $0.30\%$ at $R=50$ and $0.15\%$ at $R=100$, whereas the coupled pair turned slower ($v^2/v_0^2=4r/(4r+1)$). The reversal has the same origin as the sign of $\lambda_t$ in Section 2.1. Grade: derived; measured residuals $\|H\mathbf A-\mathbf G\|_\infty/\|\mathbf G\|_\infty<10^{-15}$ at $R\in\{0.5,1,5,20,50,100,200,1000\}$ and generic phase $\varphi_0=0.37$ (receipt `targets.T3_balance`). Falsifier: a nonzero relative residual of the Cartesian $H\mathbf A-\mathbf G$ at this $v(R)$, or a different $v$ at which it vanishes.

## 4. Invariants of the adapted law on the balanced ring

The functional has no explicit time dependence and is invariant under common translation and rotation, so $E=\tfrac12\mathbf V^{T}H\mathbf V+\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}/r_{ij}$ (the Jacobi function; the velocity part is homogeneous of degree two), $\mathbf P=\sum_k\mathbf p_k$ and $\mathbf J=\sum_k\mathbf X_k\times\mathbf p_k$ with $\mathbf p=H\mathbf V$ are exact invariants. They are invariants of an adapted law and not physical energy, momentum or angular-momentum accounts; the unit weight is a normalization, not a mass. On the balanced ring $\mathbf p_k=\lambda_tv\mathbf t_k$ and $\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}/r_{ij}=-(2\sqrt2-1)/R$, so

$$
E=2\lambda_tv^2-\frac{2\sqrt2-1}{R}=-2v^2=-\frac{4\,(2\sqrt2-1)}{8R-1-\sqrt2},\qquad
\mathbf P=\mathbf 0,\qquad
J_z=4R\lambda_tv=v\,(4R-1-\sqrt2).
$$

$E=-2v^2$ is the ring's version of the pair identity $E=-v^2$. Values: $R=50$: $E=-0.01839529668431$, $J_z=18.94934053104$; $R=100$: $E=-0.009169807967179$, $J_z=26.92129659302$; $R=200$: $E=-0.004577975443368$, $J_z=38.15918912599$. Grade: derived; measured against the Cartesian evaluation to $10^{-15}$ relative. Falsifier: drift of any of the three along a Cartesian history of the full equations beyond integration error.

## 5. Linearization about the balanced rotation (item 5)

In the frame rotating at $\Omega$, $\mathbf X=\mathcal R(\Omega T)\mathbf Y$, the functional becomes autonomous, $\mathcal L'(\mathbf Y,\dot{\mathbf Y})=\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}(\mathbf Y,\dot{\mathbf Y}+\Omega\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf Y)$, the balanced ring is a fixed point, and the second-order equations read $H(\mathbf Y)\ddot{\mathbf Y}=\mathbf R(\mathbf Y,\dot{\mathbf Y})$ with $\mathbf R=\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathbf X-\Omega\hat{\mathbf z}\times H\mathbf V-H(\Omega\hat{\mathbf z}\times\dot{\mathbf Y})-\sum_j\dot M_{ij}|_{\dot{\mathbf Y}}\mathbf V_j$, $\mathbf V=\dot{\mathbf Y}+\Omega\hat{\mathbf z}\times\mathbf Y$. Because $\mathbf R=\mathbf 0$ at the fixed point (residual below $2\times10^{-19}$, recomputed in the same run), the linearization is the quadratic pencil $\lambda^2H_0+\lambda C+K$ with $C=-\partial\mathbf R/\partial\dot{\mathbf Y}$, $K=-\partial\mathbf R/\partial\mathbf Y$ (fourth-order central differences; the term from $\partial H^{-1}$ multiplies the residual and is absent). The pencil commutes with $\mathcal T$ and with $z\to-z$, so it splits into four sectors, each a scalar axial quadratic and a $2\times2$ in-plane quartic; roots by Durand–Kerner with Newton polish; geometric multiplicity of a root in the first-order system equals the nullity of the pencil there. ## 6. Adversarial notes for a Cartesian subject derivation

Sector mixing. A Cartesian eigensolve returns the twelve numbers without labels; the two double eigenvalues ($m=\pm1$ in-plane lower and upper, axial tilt) are easily mislabelled as $m=0$ or $m=2$ modes, and a subject that identifies "the rotation mode" by the eigenvector closest to $\mathbf t_k$ at a generic phase may pick an $m=\pm1$ vector when the two are close in value. Check: apply $H$ to the explicit vectors $(\mathbf t_k)_k$ and $(\mathbf n_k)_k$ and verify eigenvalues $1-(1+\sqrt2)/(4R)$ and $1-(2-\sqrt2)/(4R)$ to $10^{-12}$; block-diagonalize in the sector basis of Section 1 and report the off-sector leakage.

Mixed term. The sign of $\sum_j\dot M_{ij}\mathbf V_j$ (it is subtracted from $\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathbf X$), its indexing ($\dot M_{ij}$ uses $\mathbf V_i-\mathbf V_j$ with $\mathbf e_{ij}$, and multiplies $\mathbf V_j$, not $\mathbf V_i$), and dropping its $\dot{\mathbf e}$ part because $\dot r=0$ on a rigid motion are the three likely errors; the last removes the whole term here. Check: the identity $\sum_j\dot M_{kj}\mathbf V_j=(2\sqrt2-1)v^2\mathbf n_k/(4R^2)$ on the ring, and agreement of the inertial residual $\|H\mathbf A-\mathbf G\|$ with the rotating-frame residual $\|\partial\mathcal L/\partial\mathbf X-\Omega\hat{\mathbf z}\times H\mathbf V\|$, which does not contain the mixed term. A wrong mixed term shifts the balance to $v^2(8R-1-\sqrt2\mp\ldots)$ with a different constant; the zero-coupling limit does not expose it, because the mixed term vanishes there.

Pair singular radii as ring radii. Reporting $R=1/\sqrt2$ or $R=\tfrac12$ (where an adjacent or diagonal pair reaches separation one) as a ring obstruction is wrong; the ring Hessian is regular at both and singular at the six radii of Section 2.2. Check: $\det H$ at $R=1/\sqrt2$ equals $-3.53\times10^{-5}$ (negative, nonzero; this radius lies in the interval $(R_5,R_6)$ with one negative eigenvalue), at $R=1/(2\sqrt2)$ equals $-1.679$, and at $R=\tfrac12$ equals $+0.017578125$ exactly ($=9/512$); all three from the closed-form product and the Cartesian determinant.

Tangential component of $\mathbf G$. A subject that finds $g_t\ne0$ has a phase-dependent error (typically $\mathbf e_{ij}$ used with the wrong sign for $j<i$), because Section 3.1 forces $g_t=0$ at every phase. Check: evaluate $\mathbf G$ at two generic phases and project on $\mathbf t_k$.

### Independent reading of the mirror-subspace orbital-stability theorem (13.5(i))

The theorem of 13.5(i) states nonlinear orbital stability of the opposite-polarity circle family inside the mirror subspace $\mathbf X_2=-\mathbf X_1$, $\mathbf V_2=-\mathbf V_1$, for every $r_0>0$ off the two full-Hessian singular separations $\{\tfrac12,1\}$, with global existence of the perturbed mirror solution. It was read against Sections 1–3 and 7.2 of [the pair adjudication](../../binary-research/analysis/darwin-overnight-independent-adjudication.md), written blind in round 1, and spot-checked by `round3-compare.mjs` (Part F).
