## 1. Configuration and the acceleration equations

Members $k=0,1,2,3$ carry polarities $q_k=(+,-,+,-)$ and occupy $\mathbf X_k=R\,\mathbf r_k$ with $\mathbf r_k=(\cos\phi_k,\sin\phi_k,0)$, $\phi_k=\phi_0+k\pi/2$. Define the local tangential unit vector $\mathbf t_k=(-\sin\phi_k,\cos\phi_k,0)$ and the axial unit vector $\hat{\mathbf z}$; the identities $\mathbf t_k=\mathbf r_{k+1}$ and $\mathbf t_{k+1}=-\mathbf r_k$ (indices modulo 4) are used throughout. The rigid rotation is $\phi_0=\Omega T$, so $\mathbf V_k=v\,\mathbf t_k$ with member speed $v=R\Omega$ and $\mathbf A_k=-R\Omega^2\mathbf r_k=-(v^2/R)\mathbf r_k$. The four adjacent pairs $(k,k\pm1)$ have opposite polarity, $\sigma=-1$, separation $R\sqrt2$ and unit vector $\mathbf e_{k,k+1}=(\mathbf r_k-\mathbf t_k)/\sqrt2$; the two diagonal pairs $(k,k+2)$ have the same polarity, $\sigma=+1$, separation $2R$ and $\mathbf e_{k,k+2}=\mathbf r_k$. The dimensionless interaction parameters are $\epsilon_{\mathrm{adj}}=1/(R\sqrt2)$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}=1/(2R)$.

From Section 1 of the investigation file the Euler–Lagrange line is the implicit acceleration system $H(\mathbf X)\mathbf A=\mathbf G(\mathbf X,\mathbf V)$ with $H_{kk}=I$, $H_{kl}=M_{kl}=\dfrac{\sigma_{kl}}{2r_{kl}}\left(I+\mathbf e_{kl}\mathbf e_{kl}^{\mathsf T}\right)$ for $k\ne l$, and $\mathbf G_k=\partial\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}/\partial\mathbf X_k-\sum_{l\ne k}\dot M_{kl}\mathbf V_l$, the second term being the mixed derivative $\partial^2\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}/\partial\mathbf V_k\partial\mathbf X_l$ contracted with $\mathbf V_l$. For the ring the two adjacent blocks of each member carry the factor $a=-1/(2\sqrt2R)$ and the diagonal block the factor $b=+1/(4R)$; the opposite-polarity and same-polarity contributions are kept separate as $a$ and $b$ until the final formulas. This assembly was verified for $N=4$ with alternating polarity by central finite differences of a direct evaluation of $\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$ at three random three-dimensional states and at one ring state (Section 8: maximum discrepancy $3.6\times10^{-10}$ for $H$ and $5.9\times10^{-9}$ for $\mathbf G$, consistent with step-size noise at steps $10^{-3}$ and $10^{-4}$) before the ring computations were run (derived-and-computed).

## 2. The $12\times12$ velocity Hessian and its exact spectrum

The out-of-plane components decouple exactly: every $\mathbf e_{kl}$ lies in the plane, so $(I+\mathbf e\mathbf e^{\mathsf T})$ has axial–planar entries zero and axial–axial entry $1$. The axial block is the $4\times4$ circulant with diagonal $1$, nearest-neighbour entry $a$ and opposite entry $b$. With the discrete Fourier index $m\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ of the cyclic shift $k\to k+1$ its eigenvalues are $1+2a\cos(m\pi/2)+b\cos(m\pi)$:

$$
\lambda^{z}_{0}=1-\frac{2\sqrt2-1}{4R},\qquad
\lambda^{z}_{1}=\lambda^{z}_{3}=1-\frac{1}{4R},\qquad
\lambda^{z}_{2}=1+\frac{2\sqrt2+1}{4R}.
$$

For the in-plane $8\times8$ block, express the component of member $k$ in its local basis $(\mathbf r_k,\mathbf t_k)$. The rotation by $\pi/2$ that sends member $k$ to member $k+1$ preserves the pair structure (adjacent pairs stay adjacent and opposite, diagonal pairs stay diagonal and same), so $H$ commutes with it and in the local bases becomes block-circulant with $2\times2$ blocks $C_0=I$, $C_1$ (member $k$ against $k+1$), $C_2$ (against $k+2$), $C_3=C_1^{\mathsf T}$ (against $k-1$). Direct evaluation with $\mathbf e_{k,k+1}=(\mathbf r_k-\mathbf t_k)/\sqrt2$ and $\mathbf t_{k+1}=-\mathbf r_k$ gives

$$
C_1=a\begin{pmatrix}-\tfrac12&-\tfrac32\\[2pt] \tfrac32&\tfrac12\end{pmatrix},\qquad
C_2=-b\begin{pmatrix}2&0\\0&1\end{pmatrix},
$$

and the Fourier symbols $\hat C(m)=I+i^mC_1+i^{3m}C_1^{\mathsf T}+(-1)^mC_2$ are

$$
\hat C(0)=\operatorname{diag}\!\big(1-a-2b,\;1+a-b\big),\qquad
\hat C(2)=\operatorname{diag}\!\big(1+a-2b,\;1-a-b\big),\qquad
\hat C(1)=\overline{\hat C(3)}=\begin{pmatrix}1+2b&-3ia\\ 3ia&1+b\end{pmatrix}.
$$

Substituting $a=-\sqrt2/(4R)$ and $b=1/(4R)$, the eight in-plane eigenvalues are, with their mode content,

$$
\begin{aligned}
&\lambda_1=1-\frac{2-\sqrt2}{4R}\ \ (m=0,\ \text{radial: breathing}),\qquad
\lambda_2=1-\frac{1+\sqrt2}{4R}\ \ (m=0,\ \text{tangential: rigid rotation}),\\
&\lambda_3=1-\frac{2+\sqrt2}{4R}\ \ (m=2,\ \text{radial: rhombic shear}),\qquad
\lambda_4=1+\frac{\sqrt2-1}{4R}\ \ (m=2,\ \text{tangential: alternating twist}),\\
&\lambda_{5,6}=1-\frac{\sqrt{73}-3}{8R},\qquad
\lambda_{7,8}=1+\frac{\sqrt{73}+3}{8R}\ \ (m=1,3,\ \text{each double: centre-drift type}).
\end{aligned}
$$

The $m=1,3$ pair follows from $\operatorname{tr}\hat C(1)=2+3b$ and $\det\hat C(1)=(1+2b)(1+b)-9a^2$, that is $\lambda=1+\tfrac32b\pm\sqrt{b^2/4+9a^2}$ with $b^2/4+9a^2=73/(64R^2)$. All twelve eigenvalues are of the form $1\mp c/R$ with positive $c$, so each decreasing family crosses zero exactly once. The six singular radii, in increasing order, are (derived)

| Singular radius $R$ | Value | Mode | Multiplicity |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $(2-\sqrt2)/4$ | $0.1464466094$ | in-plane breathing ($m=0$ radial) | 1 |
| $1/4$ | $0.25$ | axial tilt ($m=1,3$) | 2 |
| $(2\sqrt2-1)/4$ | $0.4571067812$ | uniform axial displacement ($m=0$) | 1 |
| $(1+\sqrt2)/4$ | $0.6035533906$ | rigid rotation ($m=0$ tangential) | 1 |
| $(\sqrt{73}-3)/8$ | $0.6930004682$ | in-plane $m=1,3$ lower | 2 |
| $(2+\sqrt2)/4$ | $0.8535533906$ | rhombic shear ($m=2$ radial) | 1 |

Consequently $\det H=\prod_{\nu}\lambda_\nu$ is positive for $R>(2+\sqrt2)/4$ (no negative eigenvalue), negative on $\big((\sqrt{73}-3)/8,(2+\sqrt2)/4\big)$ (one), negative on $\big((1+\sqrt2)/4,(\sqrt{73}-3)/8\big)$ (three), positive on $\big((2\sqrt2-1)/4,(1+\sqrt2)/4\big)$ (four), negative on $\big(1/4,(2\sqrt2-1)/4\big)$ (five), negative on $\big((2-\sqrt2)/4,1/4\big)$ (seven), and positive on $\big(0,(2-\sqrt2)/4\big)$ (eight). $H$ is positive definite exactly on $R>(2+\sqrt2)/4\approx0.8536$, equivalently adjacent separation $R\sqrt2>(1+\sqrt2)/2\approx1.207$ or $\epsilon_{\mathrm{adj}}<2(\sqrt2-1)\approx0.828$ (derived). This is not the pair domain: the pair Hessian is positive definite for $r>1$, whereas here the last mode to fail is a shear of the square driven jointly by the two opposite-polarity neighbours and the same-polarity diagonal member, and it fails at an adjacent separation larger than $1$. Every singular radius lies far inside the declared approximation domain ($R\ge10\sqrt2$ already from the weak bound), so the obstruction is a property of the adapted law outside the domain of comparison. The closed forms were verified against Jacobi eigenvalues of the assembled matrix at $R\in\{0.2,0.5,0.75,1,2,10,50\}$ (maximum discrepancy $1.1\times10^{-14}$) and $\det H$ by LU pivoting agreed with the product of closed forms at every radius, with negative-eigenvalue counts $7,4,1,0,0,0,0$ matching the interval table (derived-and-computed, Section 8). Falsifier: any $R>(2+\sqrt2)/4$ at which an LU factorization of the assembled $H$ reports a non-positive pivot, or any listed singular radius at which the Jacobi spectrum has no eigenvalue below $10^{-12}$ in magnitude; both are printed by the script.

## 3. Symmetry reduction of the balance and the exact circular relation

The rigid rotation is invariant under the cyclic shift, so $\mathbf A=-(v^2/R)(\mathbf r_0,\mathbf r_1,\mathbf r_2,\mathbf r_3)$ lies in the $m=0$ sector with purely radial local coefficient, and $H\mathbf A$ is obtained from $\hat C(0)$: $(H\mathbf A)_k=-(v^2/R)\,\lambda_1\,\mathbf r_k$, with vanishing tangential and axial components. Because $\mathbf G$ is built from the same cyclically symmetric state it also lies in the $m=0$ sector, $\mathbf G_k=g_r\mathbf r_k+g_t\mathbf t_k$ with coefficients common to all members. The tangential coefficient vanishes identically: $\mathbf G$ is even in $\mathbf V$ (the position gradient is a constant plus a quadratic form in velocities, and $\dot M\mathbf V$ is bilinear), and the reflection across the line through $\mathbf X_0$ and $\mathbf X_2$ maps the configuration to itself (it exchanges members 1 and 3, which share a polarity) while reversing the sense of rotation; combining the two, $\mathbf G_0$ is invariant under that reflection and hence has no component along $\mathbf t_0$ (derived). The balance $H\mathbf A=\mathbf G$ therefore reduces to the single scalar equation $-(v^2/R)\lambda_1=g_r$.

The radial coefficient is computed at $\phi_0=0$ for member 0 with the closed forms of the investigation file. From each adjacent pair the position gradient contributes $-(1-v^2/4)/(2\sqrt2R^2)$ radially (the velocity-dependent bracket cancels exactly because $S_{01}=v^2/2$ and $(\mathbf V_1\cdot\mathbf e)\mathbf V_0+(\mathbf V_0\cdot\mathbf e)\mathbf V_1=v^2\mathbf e$), and the mixed term $-\dot M_{01}\mathbf V_1$ contributes $-v^2/(2\sqrt2R^2)$ (here $\dot r_{01}=0$ and the transverse relative velocity is $\mathbf w_{01}=v(\mathbf r_0+\mathbf t_0)$). From the diagonal pair the position gradient contributes $+(1+v^2/2)/(4R^2)$ (with $S_{02}=-v^2$, both longitudinal velocity components zero) and the mixed term contributes $+v^2/(4R^2)$. Summing the two adjacent and the one diagonal contributions,

$$
g_r=-\frac{2\sqrt2-1}{4R^2}-\frac{3(\sqrt2-1)}{8}\,\frac{v^2}{R^2},
$$

in which the velocity-independent part is the inverse-square gradient of the alternating square and the velocity-dependent part collects $-3\sqrt2/8$ from the opposite-polarity neighbours and $+3/8$ from the same-polarity diagonal member. The balance equation becomes

$$
\frac{v^2}{R}\left(1-\frac{2-\sqrt2}{4R}\right)=\frac{2\sqrt2-1}{4R^2}+\frac{3(\sqrt2-1)}{8}\frac{v^2}{R^2}
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
v^2\left(R-\frac{1+\sqrt2}{8}\right)=\frac{2\sqrt2-1}{4},
$$

so that (derived)

$$
v^2=\frac{2(2\sqrt2-1)}{8R-1-\sqrt2},\qquad \Omega=\frac{v}{R},\qquad
v_0^2=\frac{2\sqrt2-1}{4R}\ \ \text{(zero coupling)},\qquad
\frac{v^2}{v_0^2}=\frac{R}{R-(1+\sqrt2)/8}.
$$

A real $\Omega$ exists exactly for $R>(1+\sqrt2)/8\approx0.3018$; for $R\le(1+\sqrt2)/8$ no rigid rotation of the ring satisfies the equations. The zero-coupling control (velocity term deleted, $H=I$, $\mathbf G$ the inverse-square gradient) gives $v_0^2=(2\sqrt2-1)/(4R)$ for every $R>0$ by the same reduction; the velocity coupling raises the required speed by the factor $\big(1-(1+\sqrt2)/(8R)\big)^{-1/2}$, first order in $\epsilon$ and tending to one in the weak limit, and introduces the threshold radius that the control lacks. Both the Hessian correction ($\lambda_1<1$) and the velocity-dependent part of $g_r$ act in the same direction, each demanding more speed at a given $R$; a solution exists on radii where $H$ is indefinite or singular, because the rigid rotation lies in the $m=0$ radial sector, whose eigenvalue $\lambda_1$ is positive for all $R>(2-\sqrt2)/4$, and the balance equation never divides by a vanishing eigenvalue of another sector. At the singular radii of Section 2 the rigid rotation still satisfies $H\mathbf A=\mathbf G$, but the implicit solve is not unique there, and a history through such a radius is an obstruction event under the frozen rules; an exact-solution statement at such $R$ is therefore made for the configuration, not for any history.

Declared domain. The member speed $v\le0.1$ requires $8R-1-\sqrt2\ge200(2\sqrt2-1)$, that is $R\ge R_{0.1}=\big(200(2\sqrt2-1)+1+\sqrt2\big)/8=46.0124548140$; the weak bound $\epsilon_{\mathrm{adj}}=1/(R\sqrt2)\le0.05$ requires $R\ge10\sqrt2=14.1421356237$. The speed bound is the binding one: the circular family lies in the declared domain exactly for $R\ge46.0124548140$, where $\epsilon_{\mathrm{adj}}\le0.01537$ and $\epsilon_{\mathrm{diag}}\le0.01087$ (derived). Falsifier for the relation: a residual $\|H\mathbf A-\mathbf G\|_\infty$ above $10^{-12}\|\mathbf G\|_\infty$ at any $R>(1+\sqrt2)/8$ when $\Omega$ is taken from the formula and $H$, $\mathbf G$ are assembled from the finite-difference-verified closed forms at a generic phase $\phi_0$; or a nonzero tangential component of $\mathbf G_k$ above rounding.

## 5. Invariants restricted to the balanced ring

The three invariants of Section 3 of the investigation file are invariants of the adapted law, not physical energy, momentum or angular-momentum accounts. Because $\mathbf p=H\mathbf V$ and the rigid-rotation velocity is the uniform tangential $m=0$ vector, $\mathbf p_k=\lambda_2\mathbf V_k$ with $\lambda_2=1-(1+\sqrt2)/(4R)$ (derived). Hence, at any $(R,v)$ on the circular family,

$$
E=2v^2+\frac{1-2\sqrt2}{R}-\frac{(1+\sqrt2)\,v^2}{2R},\qquad
\mathbf P=\sum_k\mathbf p_k=\mathbf 0,\qquad
J_z=4Rv\,\lambda_2=4Rv-(1+\sqrt2)\,v,
$$

where the three terms of $E$ are the quadratic velocity term, the pair term ($-4/(R\sqrt2)$ from the opposite-polarity neighbours and $+1/R$ from the same-polarity diagonals) and the velocity coupling ($-\sqrt2v^2/(2R)$ from the adjacent pairs with $S=v^2/2$, $-v^2/(2R)$ from the diagonal pairs with $S=-v^2$). $\mathbf P=0$ follows from the $C_4$ symmetry alone: an in-plane vector invariant under rotation by $\pi/2$ vanishes. The generalized momentum per member changes sign at $R=(1+\sqrt2)/4$, so $J_z$ has the sign of $v$ for $R>(1+\sqrt2)/4$ and the opposite sign on $(1+\sqrt2)/8<R<(1+\sqrt2)/4$. In the domain, with $v$ from Section 3, $E<0$ (for example $E=-0.0183952966843$ at $R=50$, $J_z=18.9493405310$). These closed forms were checked against the direct evaluation $E=\mathbf p\cdot\mathbf V-\mathcal L_{\mathrm D}$, $\mathbf P=\sum\mathbf p_k$, $\mathbf J=\sum\mathbf X_k\times\mathbf p_k$ from the assembled $H$ at every radius of Section 4 (discrepancies at most $2.7\times10^{-15}$ for $E$, $1.4\times10^{-14}$ for $J_z$, $5.6\times10^{-16}$ for $\|\mathbf P\|_\infty$; derived-and-computed). Falsifier: a direct evaluation at a balanced state that departs from these formulas beyond rounding.

## 6. Where the two-body scalar structure does not transfer

Same-polarity (diagonal, $b$) and opposite-polarity (adjacent, $a$) contributions remain separated in $\hat C(m)$, $g_r$ and $E$ above.

## 7. What the perturbation analysis requires (not performed in this round)

The next round linearizes the full twelve-dimensional system $H(\mathbf X)\mathbf A=\mathbf G(\mathbf X,\mathbf V)$ about the balanced rotation, which is an exact solution only for $R>(1+\sqrt2)/8$ with $\Omega$ from Section 3; linearizing anywhere else is barred. It needs the state written in the frame rotating at $\Omega$, where the balanced ring is a fixed point, the $24\times24$ first-order Jacobian of $(\dot{\mathbf X},\dot{\mathbf V})=(\mathbf V,H^{-1}\mathbf G)$ transformed to that frame (requiring $H^{-1}$, hence $R$ away from the six singular radii, in practice $R>(2+\sqrt2)/4$), and the derivatives $\partial(H^{-1}\mathbf G)/\partial\mathbf X$ and $\partial(H^{-1}\mathbf G)/\partial\mathbf V$, which include $\partial H/\partial\mathbf X$ contracted with $\mathbf A$ and are best obtained in closed form from the pair blocks and checked by finite differences of the assembly. The cyclic-shift symmetry and the two reflections block-diagonalize the Jacobian into the sectors $m=0,1,2,3$ of Section 2, each with axial and in-plane parts, giving blocks of size at most $4\times4$ in the complex representation. The non-symmetric perturbations that must be included, by sector: breathing ($m=0$ radial, coupled to the rotation-rate change, $m=0$ tangential); in-plane rhombic shear ($m=2$ radial) and alternating twist ($m=2$ tangential), which are the polarity-asymmetric in-plane modes because the shift by one member exchanges polarities while the shift by two does not; out-of-plane tilt ($m=1,3$ axial) and uniform axial displacement ($m=0$ axial, a neutral direction) and the alternating axial saddle ($m=2$ axial); centre drift ($m=1,3$ in-plane), which does not separate from the relative motion because a boost is not a symmetry of this functional, so the $m=1,3$ blocks must be kept and their Jordan structure examined as in the pair case. Each block's spectrum carries the falsifier that a finite-difference Jacobian of the assembled system in the rotating frame, at the same $(R,\Omega)$, has eigenvalues differing by more than the finite-difference error estimate from the closed-form block spectrum, with the Jordan-block caution ($\sqrt\delta$ splitting) already recorded in the investigation file.

## 9. Linearization about the balanced ring (round 2)

The balanced ring of Section 3 is an exact solution only for $R>(1+\sqrt2)/8$ with $\Omega^2=v^2/R^2$ and $v^2=2(2\sqrt2-1)/(8R-1-\sqrt2)$; every linearization below is formed at such a state, and at each radius the script recomputes $\Omega$ from that relation, assembles $H$ and $\mathbf G$ at the state, and records $\|H\mathbf A-\mathbf G\|_\infty$ against $10^{-12}\|\mathbf G\|_\infty$ before any derivative is taken (the recertification table of Section 11). In the frame rotating at $\Omega$ about $\hat{\mathbf z}$ through the ring centre, with rotating positions $\mathbf Y_k$ and rotating velocities $\mathbf W_k=\dot{\mathbf Y}_k$, the non-rotating-frame velocity is $\mathbf V_k=\mathbf W_k+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf Y_k$ and, by the rotational covariance of $H$ and $\mathbf G$, the law becomes the autonomous first-order system

$$
\dot{\mathbf Y}=\mathbf W,\qquad
\dot{\mathbf W}=\mathbf A(\mathbf Y,\mathbf W+\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf Y)-2\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf W-\boldsymbol\Omega\times(\boldsymbol\Omega\times\mathbf Y),\qquad
\mathbf A=H(\mathbf Y)^{-1}\mathbf G(\mathbf Y,\mathbf V),
$$

whose fixed points with $\mathbf W=0$ are exactly the states with $\mathbf A=-\Omega^2\mathbf Y_\parallel$, that is, the balanced ring (the fixed-point residual equals the balance residual; both are printed). Its $24\times24$ Jacobian is $\begin{pmatrix}0&I\\ J_{YY}&J_{YW}\end{pmatrix}$ with $J_{YY}=\partial\mathbf A/\partial\mathbf X+(\partial\mathbf A/\partial\mathbf V)\,C-C^2$ and $J_{YW}=\partial\mathbf A/\partial\mathbf V-2C$, where $C$ is the block-diagonal matrix of $\boldsymbol\Omega\times$ and $-C^2=\Omega^2\operatorname{diag}(1,1,0)$ per member. The derivatives of the solve are obtained by differentiating $H\mathbf A=\mathbf G$: $\partial\mathbf A=H^{-1}\big(\partial\mathbf G-(\partial H)\mathbf A\big)$, so the term from $\partial H/\partial\mathbf X$ contracted with $\mathbf A$ is included. From the pair blocks, with $\mathbf e=\mathbf e_{ij}$, $r=r_{ij}$ and $\mathbf a=\mathbf A_j$,

$$
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf X_i}\big(M_{ij}\mathbf A_j\big)=\frac{\sigma_{ij}}{2r^2}\Big[-\big(\mathbf a+(\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf a)\mathbf e\big)\mathbf e^{\mathsf T}+\mathbf e\,\mathbf a^{\mathsf T}(I-\mathbf e\mathbf e^{\mathsf T})+(\mathbf e\cdot\mathbf a)(I-\mathbf e\mathbf e^{\mathsf T})\Big],
\qquad
\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf X_j}\big(M_{ij}\mathbf A_j\big)=-\frac{\partial}{\partial\mathbf X_i}\big(M_{ij}\mathbf A_j\big),
$$

(derived; it uses $\partial(1/r)=-r^{-2}\mathbf e^{\mathsf T}$ and $\partial\mathbf e=r^{-1}(I-\mathbf e\mathbf e^{\mathsf T})$). The derivatives of $\mathbf G$ with respect to $\mathbf X$ and $\mathbf V$ were taken by forward-mode (dual-number) differentiation of the closed-form pair expressions of Section 1 of the investigation file, which applies the chain rule to those formulas exactly and is therefore exact to rounding; the closed form displayed above was checked against the same route at a random state (discrepancy $2.1\times10^{-17}$ on entries of size $0.076$, receipt field `K2`). Everything is then scaled to units of $\Omega$ (time $\Omega T$, velocities $\mathbf W/\Omega$), so that the reported exponents are $\lambda/\Omega$.

The exact Jacobian was verified against central finite differences of the assembled rotating-frame field at relative steps $h\in\{10^{-3},10^{-4},10^{-5},10^{-6}\}$ (position step $hR$, velocity step $hR\Omega$). The maximum entry discrepancy $\delta_h$ in scaled units is $1.8\times10^{-6}$, $1.8\times10^{-8}$, $2.0\times10^{-10}$ and $4.9\times10^{-10}$ at $R=50$ (the first three scale as $h^2$, the last is the rounding floor), and within a factor two of these at every other radius; the step-to-step differences of the finite-difference Jacobians themselves follow the same sequence independently of the exact route (derived-and-computed). Three structural checks were applied to the exact Jacobian at every radius. The cyclic shift $\mathcal S$ (rotation by $\pi/2$ composed with $k\to k+1$) commutes with it to $7\times10^{-16}$ relative. The reversing involution $\mathcal R:(\mathbf Y_k,\mathbf W_k)\mapsto(\varsigma\mathbf Y_{-k},-\varsigma\mathbf W_{-k})$, with $\varsigma$ the reflection across the line through members $0$ and $2$, satisfies $\mathcal R J\mathcal R=-J$ to $1.1\times10^{-15}$ relative: the law in the non-rotating frame is even in velocities, time reversal sends a rotating-frame solution at $\Omega$ to one at $-\Omega$, and the reflection restores $\Omega$ while mapping the ring to itself (derived). ## 10. Sector blocks and spectra

Block structure. In the local bases $(\mathbf r_k,\mathbf t_k,\hat{\mathbf z})$ of Section 1 the Jacobian commutes with the cyclic shift, so the discrete Fourier transform over $k$ (sector $m\in\{0,1,2,3\}$, basis $i^{mk}/2$) block-diagonalizes it into four $6\times6$ complex blocks, each of which splits further into an in-plane $4\times4$ block (local radial and tangential position and velocity) and an axial $2\times2$ block, because every $\mathbf e_{kl}$ lies in the plane and $C$ acts in the plane. The off-block leakage after the transform is at most $9\times10^{-16}$ relative between sectors and exactly $0$ between axial and in-plane parts at every radius (derived-and-computed). Sectors $m=1$ and $m=3$ are complex conjugates of each other. The spectra are independent of the phase $\phi_0$ (maximum difference $1.7\times10^{-8}$ between $\phi_0=0.37$ and $1.1$ at $R=100$, which is the Jordan-pair rounding split).

Exact exponents from symmetries (derived; all confirmed numerically to $\le1.6\times10^{-8}$ in real part and $\le5\times10^{-10}$ in modulus). The $m=0$ tangential direction carries a double zero with geometric multiplicity one: the phase along the rigid rotation and the shift along the circular family $R\mapsto\Omega(R)$, a Jordan block as for the pair. The $m=0$ axial direction carries a double zero with geometric multiplicity one: uniform axial displacement and its conserved generalized momentum $P_z$ (free axial drift). The $m=1$ and $m=3$ axial blocks each carry one exponent of modulus exactly $\Omega$ ($+i\Omega$ in $m=1$, $-i\Omega$ in $m=3$): a tilt of the ring plane about a fixed non-rotating axis is a rotation of the solution, and in the rotating frame its axis turns at $-\Omega$. The $m=1$ and $m=3$ in-plane blocks each carry $\pm i\Omega$ with algebraic multiplicity two and geometric multiplicity one (rank of $B-\lambda I$ equal to three on the $4\times4$ block, pivots printed in the receipt): a translation of the ring is a symmetry, and its partner is the conserved generalized momentum $\mathbf P$, which, exactly as in the pair case, produces linear drift rather than a second independent oscillation because a boost is not a symmetry of the functional. This is the centre-drift Jordan structure that Section 7 asked to be examined; the finite-difference Jacobians split these pairs by $\sqrt{\delta_h}$ (for example $4\times10^{-3}$ at $h=10^{-3}$) and the exact Jacobian by $\le1.6\times10^{-8}$, confirming that no genuine splitting exists.
