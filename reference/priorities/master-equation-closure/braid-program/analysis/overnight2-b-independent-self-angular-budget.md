## Geometry, normalization and a uniform self-row bound

Use the subject's six-member alternating-polarity histories

$$
X_j(t)=R\bigl(\rho(\tau)\cos(\theta(\tau)+j\pi/3),\rho(\tau)\sin(\theta(\tau)+j\pi/3),(-1)^jz(\tau)\bigr),\qquad \tau=t/R,
$$

with fixed $R>0$, $K=c_f=1$ and complete $C^2$ profiles. Assume

$$
0<r_-\le\rho\le r_+,\quad 0<\omega_-\le\omega=\dot\theta\le\omega_+,\quad
|\dot\rho|\le v_r,\quad |z|\le h,\quad |\dot z|\le v_z.
$$

Normalized first derivatives are physical velocities because $d(Rx(t/R))/dt=\dot x$. Every complete normalized position lies in the fixed ball of radius $\sqrt{r_+^2+h^2}$, while radial, tangential and axial velocities are orthogonal. Thus

$$
D_*=2\sqrt{r_+^2+h^2},\qquad
V_*=\sqrt{v_r^2+r_+^2\omega_+^2+v_z^2}
$$

bound every positive causal delay and every source speed, respectively. In particular the complete remote gap is negative for every $d>D_*$. Boundedness of an inspected lookback alone would not suffice; here the complete-history bounds supply both the cutoff and its negative remote sign.

Fix a self root at reception $\tau$, with delay $d>0$. In the receiver's present radial/tangential frame, set

$$
s=\rho(\tau-d),\qquad
\delta=\theta(\tau)-\theta(\tau-d)=\int_{\tau-d}^{\tau}\omega(v)\,dv.
$$

The separation has planar coordinates $(\rho(\tau)-s\cos\delta,s\sin\delta)$. Its tangential component is therefore $s\sin\delta$, independent of the axial separation. With $a=\omega_+D_*<\pi$,

$$
0<\omega_-d\le\delta\le\omega_+d\le a<\pi.
$$

For $f(x)=\sin x/x$, the derivative numerator is $x\cos x-\sin x$. That numerator vanishes at zero and has derivative $-x\sin x<0$ on $(0,\pi)$. Hence $f$ decreases there and stays positive, giving

$$
\sin\delta=\delta f(\delta)\ge\omega_-d f(a).
$$

The [canonical source weight](../../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/master-equation.md) uses $D_s=1-n\cdot V_s$, where $n=Q/d$ at the root and $V_s$ is the delayed source velocity. Ordinariness and the global speed bound give

$$
0<|D_s|\le1+|V_s|\le1+V_*.
$$

Self polarity is positive. Consequently its normalized tangential contribution satisfies

$$
a_{t,s}=\frac{s\sin\delta}{d^3|D_s|}
\ge\frac{r_-\omega_- f(a)}{d^2(1+V_*)}
\ge\frac{r_-\omega_- f(a)}{D_*^2(1+V_*)}=c_*>0.
$$

No lower bound on $d$ or $|D_s|$ enters this estimate. The useful stronger intermediate estimate is $a_{t,s}\ge C_0/d^2$, where $C_0=r_-\omega_-f(a)/(1+V_*)>0$. Small delays or divisors increase the lower bound. A negative signed divisor does not reverse the row, because the law uses its absolute value. The result applies separately to every receiver, whose current tangential frame differs only by rotation and axial reflection.

For a finite root set, summation gives $A_{t,s}\ge c_*N_s$. For an infinite root set, this statement has an extended nonnegative scalar-sum meaning: the tangential self sum diverges because each term is at least $c_*>0$. It is not a definition of a convergent vector canonical sum or a license to subtract divergent partner contributions.

## Root counts and the integrability obligation

The present hypotheses justify the integral statements without an additional uniform second-derivative assumption. The reasoning can be reconstructed directly in this special class, as follows.

At every reception the simultaneous planar separation from any partner is at least $r_-$. For every sufficiently small positive $d$, uniformly in reception,

$$
|Q_{ij}(\tau,d)|-d\ge r_--(1+V_*)d>0.
$$

Choose a strict cutoff smaller than $r_-/(1+V_*)$. All partner roots are then confined between that cutoff and an exterior endpoint larger than $D_*$. At fixed reception, ordinary roots are isolated because for the squared gap $G=|Q|^2-d^2$,

$$
G_d=-2dD_s\ne0.
$$

The root set in the closed delay interval is compact; an infinite set would accumulate at another root, violating isolation. Thus the complete partner root set is finite. The implicit function theorem continues these roots locally. The remaining compact delay complement is bounded away from zero gap at the original reception and remains root-free nearby. This proves local completeness, not merely continuation of a possibly incomplete list. Partner rows and their complete sum are locally continuous and bounded on the ordinary reception interval.

At a fixed reception, infinitely many self roots could accumulate only at zero, because the same compact isolation argument excludes positive-delay accumulation. Their tangential terms are all positive and at least $c_*$, so a finite vector canonical acceleration plus the finite partner sum excludes infinitely many self roots. Exactness then supplies local uniformity: on a small reception neighborhood, $RL_t$ is bounded by $C^2$ regularity and the complete partner tangential sum is bounded by the preceding chart argument. Hence

$$
0\le A_{t,s}=RL_t-A_{t,p}\le B
$$

for some finite $B>0$. Each possible self root obeys $C_0/d^2\le B$. A strict smaller positive delay than $\sqrt{C_0/B}$ is therefore uniformly root-free. The self roots now lie in a common compact delay interval away from zero; ordinary continuation and compact-complement exclusion give a finite complete local self chart as well.

All complete counts, including $N_s$, are locally constant and hence constant on a connected exact reception interval. Every row, the complete partner/self sums and $N_s$ are continuous or locally constant there, and all are bounded and integrable on compact subintervals. The tangential frame is continuous because $\rho>0$ and $\theta$ is smooth. Relative one-sided neighborhoods handle included endpoints. This supplies the needed regularity even at unit-speed receptions; no global recent-root floor on an unbounded future is inferred.

This reasoning agrees with the frozen [independent exact-root-chart review](overnight2-b-independent-exact-root-chart-structure.md) and [wake-speed review](overnight2-b-independent-wake-speed-crossing.md), while the positive tangential bound makes the local self estimate especially direct. The stronger complete bounded-position premise needed for negative remote sign is present here. A chart whose roots become nonordinary, or an interval where exactness fails, is outside the integration argument.

## Weighted partner identity and finite intervals

Kinematics alone gives

$$
L_t=2\dot\rho\,\omega+\rho\dot\omega,\qquad
J=\rho^2\omega,\qquad \dot J=\rho L_t.
$$

Since physical prescribed acceleration is $L/R$ and canonical acceleration is $A/R^2$, exact balance is $RL=A$. Its tangential component gives

$$
\rho A_{t,p}=R\dot J-\rho A_{t,s}.
$$

Integrating on a compact exact interval $[b,b+T]$, $T>0$, is justified by the previous section and yields

$$
\int_b^{b+T}\rho A_{t,p}\,d\tau
=R[J(b+T)-J(b)]-\int_b^{b+T}\rho A_{t,s}\,d\tau.
$$

The positive rate bounds imply

$$
r_-^2\omega_-\le J\le r_+^2\omega_+,\qquad
\Delta J=r_+^2\omega_+-r_-^2\omega_-\ge0.
$$

Using $\rho A_{t,s}\ge r_-c_*N_s$ gives exactly

$$
\frac1T\int_b^{b+T}\rho A_{t,p}\,d\tau
\le\frac{R\Delta J}{T}
-r_-c_*\frac1T\int_b^{b+T}N_s\,d\tau.
$$

The weight $\rho$ is essential: replacing the left side by an unweighted mean requires an additional argument and is not part of this theorem. There is no assumed angular-momentum conservation or mass factor. This is an identity derived from the canonical acceleration equation and planar kinematics.

If $\rho$ and $\omega$ have a common period $P$ and exactness holds throughout $[b,b+P]$, then $J(b+P)=J(b)$ and

$$
\frac1P\int_b^{b+P}\rho A_{t,p}\,d\tau
\le-r_-c_*\frac1P\int_b^{b+P}N_s\,d\tau.
$$

Neither the absolute angle nor height nor the causal root chart needs to be periodic for this single-interval identity. It only uses the endpoint equality for $J$. This must not be reinterpreted as a proof that partner accelerations or ordered roots repeat after $P$.

## Strict speed, time fractions and infinite futures

For the self distance-minus-delay gap $g(\tau,d)=|x(\tau)-x(\tau-d)|-d$,

$$
\lim_{d\downarrow0}\frac{g(\tau,d)}d=|\dot x(\tau)|-1.
$$

Strict speed above one therefore makes $g$ positive for all sufficiently small delays at that reception. For every $d>D_*$ it is negative. Continuity supplies a positive root between these regimes. In the everywhere-ordinary domain it is ordinary and retained, so $N_s\ge1$. This is a bounded-complete-history argument; a current above-wake speed by itself would not force a root for an unbounded straight complete path. The stated complete position bound rules out that counterexample.

If speed is strictly above one throughout the exact interval, then

$$
\frac1T\int_b^{b+T}\rho A_{t,p}\,d\tau
\le\frac{R\Delta J}{T}-r_-c_*.
$$

For a common radius/rate period this reduces to a strictly negative required partner mean at most $-r_-c_*$. For an exact unbounded future with the same root-presence condition, fixed $R$ and fixed global constants,

$$
\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac1T\int_b^{b+T}\rho A_{t,p}\,d\tau
\le-r_-c_*.
$$

Each finite integral exists by local chart regularity; the limit of the averages need not exist. Taking the limsup does not require bounded row magnitudes uniformly over the infinite future. The finite boundary term tends to zero because $J$ stays in its fixed bounded interval.

More generally the strict above-wake set $E_T\subset[b,b+T]$ is measurable: the speed is continuous, so the corresponding set is relatively open. Pointwise $N_s\ge\mathbf1_{E_T}$ yields

$$
\frac1T\int_b^{b+T}\rho A_{t,p}\,d\tau
\le\frac{R\Delta J}{T}-r_-c_*\frac{|E_T|}{T}.
$$

In particular, if $\liminf_{T\to\infty}|E_T|/T\ge q$, the limsup partner mean is at most $-r_-c_*q$. With no positive lower count or time-fraction premise, the general conclusion furnished by this calculation is only a nonpositive limsup. The subject's stronger negative limsup is valid with its preceding above-wake premise retained. Unit speed alone does not decide self-root presence; on an exact connected interval the complete chart can carry roots from a strict above-wake reception through a unit-speed touch, but that is a chart consequence rather than an inference from the instantaneous unit speed.
