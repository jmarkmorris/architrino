# A dissociating member in the spiral's plane meets degenerate roots (ceiling-contact open research, problem 5)

**The equation in this record is the field-speed ceiling variant, not the Master Equation.** It is an authorized research variation with the canonical equation retained as baseline. Nothing here adopts it, and nothing here selects a contact rule, event map or continuation.

This record closes obligation 5 of the [contact study](ceiling-contact-two-hour-2026-10-06.md#10-validation-files-remaining-obligations-and-review-status) under the [launch brief](ceiling-contact-open-research-launch-brief.md): whether a dissociating member that lies in the plane of the spiral meets a degenerate root from the spiralling pair, given that C1's margin of $67.5^\circ$ is particular to C1 and that the rows concerned arrive after the inferred coincidence. Units $c_f=1$.

## 1. Result

**A receiver exactly in the plane of the spiral meets a degenerate root on every turn, and the turns are infinitely many; a receiver at elevation $\beta$ above the plane keeps a transmitter factor of at least $1-\cos\beta$ on every row from the pair (derived).** For a source riding the ceiling its velocity is a unit vector; the transmitter factor of its row on a receiver is $1-\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf V_j(S)$, which vanishes exactly when the source's velocity at emission points at the reception point. A spiralling source sweeps every direction in its plane once per turn, so for a reception point in that plane there is one emission instant per turn at which the velocity points at it, and the root is degenerate there. For a reception point out of the plane, $\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf V_j\le\cos\beta$ where $\beta$ is the angle between the line of sight and the plane, so the factor is at least $1-\cos\beta$. C1's $67.5^\circ$ gives $0.617$; C3's two dissociating members, at $76.5^\circ$ and $36.4^\circ$, give $0.77$ and $0.19$ (measured in the [C3 record](ceiling-contact-open-research-c3-and-one-rider-contacts-2026-10-11.md)).

**These rows fall inside what the frozen specification already determines (derived).** Let $d$ be the distance from the dissociating member to the meeting point at about the coincidence instant $t^\ast$. The pair's wakes emitted before $t^\ast$ reach the dissociating member until about $t^\ast+d$, and nothing emitted by the pair at or after $t^\ast$ can reach it earlier than that, because wakes travel at speed one. So on the interval $(t^\ast,\ t^\ast+d-\delta)$, with $\delta$ the small correction for the receiver's own motion, the dissociating member's rows are read entirely from the pair's history before $t^\ast$, which the specification determines, and from its own zero self-row; its motion there does not depend on what is decided at coincidence. The emission instants $S_n$ of the spiral's successive aimed emissions accumulate at $t^\ast$ (the turns are geometric, each $34.2$ times shorter than the last), and their reception instants $T_n=S_n+\lvert\mathbf X_1(T_n)-\mathbf X_j(S_n)\rvert$ accumulate at the end of that interval. For an in-plane receiver the degenerate roots therefore occur inside the causally determined window, infinitely many of them, accumulating at its end; the specification stops at the first. For a receiver out of the plane no root on those rows is degenerate, and the specification determines the dissociating member's motion through the whole window without any continuation rule. That is the statement the brief asked for: what can be said without a continuation rule.

**Exactly planar histories put every member in the spiral's plane (derived); the census's planar balances are kicked slightly out of their plane, and their smallest transmitter factors are at the size that kick implies (measured, with an inferred reading).** A history whose positions and velocities all lie in one plane, for all past time, stays in that plane under the specification, because every row lies in the plane and the response is a projection along the velocity. For such a history the spiral's plane is that plane, every other member is in it, and whichever member dissociates meets a degenerate root from the spiralling pair inside the determined window, unless the contact ends the specification first. The census does not run exactly planar histories: its kicks are three-dimensional, of total size $10^{-4}$. A member at a small elevation $\beta$ keeps the floor $1-\cos\beta\approx\beta^2/2$, positive and tiny. Of the eight census contacts on planar balances, five have a smallest transmitter factor between $0.9\times10^{-8}$ and $2.3\times10^{-8}$, which is $\beta^2/2$ for $\beta$ of $1.3$ to $2.1\times10^{-4}$ radians, the size of the kick; the other three have $0.12$, $0.40$ and $0.46$. Of the fifty-two contacts on non-planar balances one has a factor below $10^{-7}$ ($1.8\times10^{-8}$, a ten-member mirror balance with six members in its mid-plane) and the median is $0.044$. The census records the smallest factor of a run without naming its row, so these values do not by themselves show that a dissociating member's row carried the minimum; in the planar one-rider runs of the C3 record, where the row is identified, the minimum of $3\times10^{-8}$ to $7\times10^{-8}$ was on the free member's own row from the rider, both in the balance's plane, while the rider was aimed within $0.2^\circ$ of it. The reading that near-planarity produces near-degenerate rows at the level $\beta^2/2$ is therefore inferred from the derivation and consistent with every measured value, not separately established for a dissociating member.

**What the degenerate rows would do if they were admitted (derived; outside the specification, stated for orientation only).** In emission time the row of a ceiling-riding source is $\mathbf n/(\tau^2(1-b))$ with $dT/dS=(1-b)/(1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_i)$, so its contribution to the receiver's velocity per unit emission time is $\mathbf n/(\tau^2(1-\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf V_i))$, bounded, and the velocity change delivered across an isolated zero of $1-b$ is finite; the rows of the two members of the pair, of opposite polarity and nearly coincident, cancel to the order $r/\tau^3$, and the turns' emission durations form a geometric series. A receiver that passed through these roots would receive a finite, small, oscillating velocity change. None of this is in the frozen specification, whose ordinary-root clause stops at the first degenerate root; it is recorded so the operator can see the size of what the clause excludes.

## 2. Derivations

**Degeneracy condition.** For receiver $i$ and source $j$ at emission $S$, $D_t=1-\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf V_j(S)$ with $\hat{\mathbf n}$ the unit vector from the emission point to the reception point. If $\lVert\mathbf V_j(S)\rVert=1$ then $D_t=0$ if and only if $\mathbf V_j(S)=\hat{\mathbf n}$: the source moves exactly toward the reception point at wake speed. With $\lVert\mathbf V\rVert\le1$ the causal function $S\mapsto\lvert\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(S)\rvert-(T-S)$ is nondecreasing and $D_t\ge0$, so a zero of $D_t$ is a tangency of the root, generically of second order in $S$.

**Sweep.** Along the limiting spiral the source's velocity direction turns through $2\pi$ per turn in the spiral's plane, monotonically; so for any fixed direction in the plane there is exactly one emission instant per turn at which the velocity has that direction. The reception point of a given row is the receiver's position at the reception time, which varies slowly compared with the turn; the condition "velocity at $S$ points at the reception point of that emission" is a continuous equation in $S$ whose left side sweeps through the required direction once per turn, so it has a solution on each turn. For the actual C1 motion the velocity direction's turn per unit of log separation is measured at the spiral's value, and the same argument applies turn by turn.

**Elevation bound.** If the line of sight from emission point to reception point makes the angle $\beta$ with the plane that contains $\mathbf V_j(S)$, then $\hat{\mathbf n}\cdot\mathbf V_j(S)\le\cos\beta$, so $D_t\ge1-\cos\beta$. On C1 the direction to member 1 stands $67.5^\circ$ above the pair's plane from $0.3$ sizes inward (study, Section 8; the plane's normal varies by less than $10^{-4}$ degrees inside $10^{-3}$ sizes), and the pair's rows actually received by member 1 during the approach had factors $0.831$ and $1.408$ (study) and, on C3, $0.634$ to $1.453$ (C3 record). The bound concerns the rows not yet received, emitted during the final turns, which arrive after $t^\ast$.

**Causal window.** With speeds at most one, a wake emitted at $S\ge t^\ast$ from within distance $\epsilon$ of the meeting point reaches a receiver at distance $d$ from it no earlier than $S+d-\epsilon-(T-S)v$, where $v\le1$ is the receiver's speed toward it; so for $T<t^\ast+d/2$ at least, nothing emitted at or after $t^\ast$ has arrived, and the exact bound is $T<t^\ast+d(1-\epsilon/d)/(1+v)$. On C1, $d=0.4276$ at the last stored node and member 1 moves at $0.86$ with its velocity mostly outward, so the determined window after $t^\ast$ is at least $0.23$ and at most $0.43$ time units, that is $0.18$ to $0.33\,P$. The aimed emissions' reception instants accumulate at the end of the window because the emission instants accumulate at $t^\ast$ and the distance from the meeting point to member 1 at the corresponding reception times tends to $d$.

**Planar invariance.** If every position and velocity lies in a plane $\Pi$ and every past position does, then every row $\hat{\mathbf n}/(\tau^2D_t)$ lies in $\Pi$ and the response (a projection along the velocity) keeps it there, so the motion stays in $\Pi$. The spiral formed by two members of a planar balance is in $\Pi$, and so is every other member.

## 3. Measurements

From the corrected census (`census-cap-v2.json`, frozen; planarity read from each balance file as all heights zero and no axial velocity): eight contacts are on planar balances, with smallest transmitter factors $8.9\times10^{-9}$, $9.7\times10^{-9}$, $1.3\times10^{-8}$, $1.9\times10^{-8}$, $2.3\times10^{-8}$, $0.12$, $0.40$ and $0.46$ (four two-rider and four one-rider contacts); fifty-two are on non-planar balances, with median $0.044$ and five values below $10^{-4}$: $1.8\times10^{-8}$, $1.6\times10^{-6}$, $2.3\times10^{-5}$, $3.1\times10^{-5}$ and $4.8\times10^{-5}$, all on mirror balances with two-fold symmetry that have several members in the mid-plane. The census does not record which row carried each minimum. From the one-rider continuations of the C3 record, where the row is recorded: in the three planar runs the minimum of $3.0\times10^{-8}$ to $7.1\times10^{-8}$ is on the free member's row from the rider. From the study's and the C3 record's diagnostics, the elevations of the dissociating members above the pair's plane and the resulting floors: C1 member 1, $67.5^\circ$, floor $0.617$; C3 member 1, $76.5^\circ$, floor $0.77$; C3 member 2, $36.4^\circ$, floor $0.19$.
