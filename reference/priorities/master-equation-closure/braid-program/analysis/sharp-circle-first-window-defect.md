# Interval equation-defect bounds on the first circular-departure window

**Date:** 2026-09-26. **Scope:** an explicit-use evaluator of the full sharp capped-equation defect on $0\le t\le1$, with $c_f=1$ and the analytic supplied circular past. **Grade:** outward-rounded interval computation for a precisely defined comparison curve, conditional on the arithmetic backend and the derivation below. This is not an enclosure of the exact released solution, a propagation-of-error theorem, or an escape certificate. No smoothing, extra reception rule or self action is used.

The [independent escape review](sharp-circle-escape-independent-review.md) identifies two separate steps: bound how accurately a comparison curve satisfies the equation, then bound how that defect affects the actual motion. This implementation completes the first step on a window where every partner emission still lies in the known analytic past. It does not infer that a small defect is the same as a small trajectory error.

The retained instrument is [sharp-circle-first-window-defect.py](../../../../../scripts/field-speed-ceiling/sharp-circle-first-window-defect.py). It is an equation-error evaluator, not a replacement production EOM solver. The [compact receipt](../evidence/sharp-circle-first-window-defect-receipt.json) retains final-source identity, controls, exact comparison coefficients and result summaries. Full per-box binary interval endpoints remain in the literal local directory `.local-data/braid-analysis/sharp-circle-defect/`.

## 1. A comparison curve that satisfies the speed constraint exactly

The initial data are $X(0)=(r_0,0)$ and $V(0)=(0,1)$, with $r_0=1001/1000$. The source history for $s\le0$ is

$$
P(s)=r_0(\cos(s/r_0),\sin(s/r_0)),\qquad
U(s)=(-\sin(s/r_0),\cos(s/r_0)).
$$

The actual partner is at $-P(s)$. Its acceleration contribution at a receiver position $X$ has hit vector $X+P(s)$, as in the [braking analysis](sharp-circle-braking-continuation.md). The comparison curve starts at the exact same released position and velocity. It is built as continuous unit-speed arcs, with a constant angular rate $\omega_j>0$ on each interval of length $h=1/N$. If the interval starts at position $x_j$ and angle $\phi_j$, define, for $0\le q\le h$,

$$
\phi(q)=\phi_j+\omega_jq,\quad
\widetilde V(q)=(-\sin\phi(q),\cos\phi(q)),\quad
N(q)=(-\cos\phi(q),-\sin\phi(q)),
$$

$$
\widetilde X(q)=x_j+
\frac{1}{\omega_j}
\begin{pmatrix}
\cos\phi(q)-\cos\phi_j\\
\sin\phi(q)-\sin\phi_j
\end{pmatrix}.
$$

The next arc starts at the exact preceding endpoint. Consequently $|\widetilde V|=1$, $\dot{\widetilde X}=\widetilde V$, and $\dot{\widetilde V}=\omega_jN$ exactly, not merely at knots. Velocity is continuous and piecewise smooth; jumps in angular rate cause no velocity impulse. Coefficients are generated by high-precision midpoint iterations using the sharp partner row, then stored as fixed decimal rationals. Their generation is not certified integration. Once fixed, these rationals define the exact comparison curve whose equation defect is enclosed. This curve is not asserted to be the physical solution or a replacement dynamics.

For the controls, the coefficient generator is bypassed in favor of independently known circular curves. The evaluator itself does not substitute a closed-form causal root or zero defect.

## 2. Complete roots and interval evaluation

All interval arithmetic uses `mpmath.iv` 1.3.0 at 180 bits in the repository's shared virtual environment. The coupling $k=4D(1+\sin D)$ is enclosed after sign-certified bisection of $D-\cos D$ on $[1/2,1]$. All later transcendental evaluations are interval evaluations. Decimal input coefficients are enclosed as exact decimal values. Roundoff in concatenated arc endpoints is carried forward in their intervals.

At a fixed receiver endpoint the evaluator brackets the root of

$$
g(t,s)=|\widetilde X(t)+P(s)|-(t-s)
$$

between $s=-4$ and $s=0$, requiring certain opposite signs. Point bisection advances an endpoint only when the interval gap has a certain sign; otherwise it retains the current bracket. The circular past is unit-speed, so its gap is nondecreasing in $s$ over the whole past. Thus the negative sign at $-4$ excludes all earlier sources. The comparison future is also exactly unit-speed, so the positive gap at zero excludes later emission times up to the receiver time. A positive transmitter factor on the enclosed root interval makes the retained root unique and ordinary.

For a whole receiver-time box $[a,b]$, solve the endpoint roots with intervals. Unit receiver speed makes $g(t,s)$ nonincreasing in $t$, so the source-time root is nondecreasing. The hull of the two endpoint brackets therefore contains every source time throughout the box. The evaluator requires its upper endpoint to remain negative and verifies positive range, transmitter factor and raw forward acceleration over the whole box. These are conditions on the comparison curve and its exact analytic source, not the unknown true receiver history.

## 3. The full defect and its centered Taylor enclosure

Let $A$ be the sharp partner acceleration on the comparison curve and put $f=N\cdot A$. Positive forward acceleration $P_f=\widetilde V\cdot A>0$ is enclosed on every box in these runs. Hence the admissible normal-cone reaction is $\widetilde\nu=P_f\widetilde V$. The full vector defect is exactly

$$
\mathcal R=\dot{\widetilde V}+\widetilde\nu-A
=(\omega_j-f)N,\qquad |\mathcal R|=|\omega_j-f|.
$$

This reduction uses the exact orthonormal pair $(\widetilde V,N)$, so it does not discard a radial residual. If a box failed the positive-forward test, the evaluator would stop; it does not suppress braking. The implemented window does not exercise a ceiling release.

A direct interval range for $f$ loses dependencies between receiver time and emission time. A first-degree centered Taylor enclosure reduces that loss. The derivative must include the moving causal root. Define $r=\widetilde X+P(s)$, $\ell=|r|$, $n=r/\ell$, $J=1+n\cdot U(s)$ and source acceleration $B(s)=(-\cos(s/r_0),-\sin(s/r_0))/r_0$. Differentiating the causal equality gives

$$
s'=\frac{1-n\cdot\widetilde V}{J},\quad
r'=\widetilde V+U(s)s',\quad
\ell'=n\cdot r',\quad
n'=\frac{r'-n\ell'}{\ell},\quad
J'=n'\cdot U+n\cdot B\,s'.
$$

Differentiating the sharp acceleration and then its transverse component yields

$$
A'=-\frac{k}{\ell^2J}
\left[n'-n\left(\frac{2\ell'}{\ell}+\frac{J'}J\right)\right],\qquad
f'=-\omega_j\widetilde V\cdot A+N\cdot A'.
$$

For midpoint $c=(a+b)/2$, enclose $f(c)$ with a separate point-root bracket and $f'([a,b])$ using the whole source-time interval. The mean-value theorem then supplies

$$
\omega_j-f(t)\in
\omega_j-f(c)-f'([a,b])\,[a-c,b-c],\qquad a\le t\le b.
$$

The largest absolute endpoint bounds the defect throughout that box. Summing each bound times its box width bounds the integrated defect over $[0,1]$. The derivative jumps between arcs are handled by separate boxes; the comparison velocity has no jump, so there is no omitted atomic contribution. Raw endpoints are retained in the binary tuple representation used by the backend. Reported decimal upper bounds are rounded upward by exact integer arithmetic, not formatted from nearest-rounded decimal summaries.

## 4. Known cases before the target

The final instrument first passed elementary interval checks and two analytic controls, with the zero-defect case checked at two box resolutions:

1. The compatible circle $r_0=1$, $\omega=1$ has zero full capped defect. Every box enclosure contains zero. Halving the receiver-box width reduces the worst enclosure from about 0.06512 to 0.01466, consistent with reduced interval overestimation. The evaluator does not claim those positive upper bounds are the actual nonzero error of the exact circle.
2. The same circle with the finer box partition verifies that the moving-root derivative is included without losing zero from the enclosure.
3. A unit-speed circle with $r_0=2$, $\omega=1/2$ has retained normal acceleration $1/4$, so its full normal defect is exactly $1/2-1/4=1/4$. Every box encloses $1/4$ and excludes zero. This detects a known nonzero residual; it is not proposed as a dynamical solution.

The target command requires a passed known-case receipt bound to the same script hash. Earlier developmental runs remain separately named; the compact receipt refers only to final-source controls followed by final-source targets. No independent-review instrument or historical receipt was modified.

## 5. Bounds for the expanding input's comparison curves

Each arc is split into four receiver-time boxes. Both target constructions cover exactly $[0,1]$ and have the exact released initial data. They differ in the number of arcs, so refinement compares two separately defined approximation curves; it is not itself the reason either bound is valid.

| Arcs | Time boxes | Upper bound on $\sup|\mathcal R|$ | Upper bound on $\int_0^1|\mathcal R|\,dt$ |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 64 | 256 | 0.000212732921265453 | 0.000175498108787106 |
| 128 | 512 | 0.000053736821978284 | 0.000044155325273996 |

For the finer curve the enclosed transmitter factor stays above 1.6706, hit range above 1.4780, and raw forward acceleration above 0.8995. Every source time is below $-0.4798$. These conservative decimal margins follow from the retained box enclosures. Thus the entire evaluated interval remains in the analytic-past ordinary chart with positive forward acceleration. The direct, uncentered interval calculation has a worst defect upper bound about 0.009264 on the same finer partition; the centered Taylor enclosure is substantially tighter for this case. That comparison measures enclosure width, not a general performance result.

The interval result is rigorous with respect to the specified comparison curve assuming correctness of the outward-rounded backend and implementation. Known cases check selected formulas and signs; they are not formal verification of the software. The bound is far larger than the tiny defects suggested by the review's pessimistic long-interval amplification estimates. No claim that these first-degree bounds suffice for time 300 follows.

## 6. What this evaluator establishes and the subsequent trajectory bound

In the reviewed comparison lemma,

$$
\frac{d}{dt}|V-\widetilde V|
\le |A[X]-A[\widetilde X]|+|\mathcal R|,
$$

this work encloses only the second term. It has not enclosed the first term over an error tube, proved existence of the exact released motion through the window by that tube, or propagated a position–velocity error bound. It therefore certifies no positive-time trajectory prefix and does not establish the finite hypotheses of the escape theorem.

The separate [first-window trajectory analysis](sharp-circle-first-window-tube.md) now supplies the missing sensitivity and continuation argument without changing this evaluator. It closes position error below 0.0000450 and velocity error below 0.0001342 throughout $[0,1]$, with all sources still negative-time and the ceiling active. That conclusion depends jointly on the present defect bounds and the new proof and arithmetic receipt; it does not follow from defect size alone. Extension beyond this window must carry the nonzero endpoint errors and eventually control errors in positive-time source history. More significant arithmetic digits alone do not remove interval dependency or first-degree enclosure width.

**Falsifiers:** an analytically known defect outside a reported box, a root outside the endpoint hull, a missed dependence in the moving-root derivative, a failure of exact cap or position–velocity consistency, or invalid outward rounding would invalidate the affected bound. Failure to close a trajectory tube would limit the next certification step; it would not invalidate a correctly bounded comparison-curve defect or refute escape.
