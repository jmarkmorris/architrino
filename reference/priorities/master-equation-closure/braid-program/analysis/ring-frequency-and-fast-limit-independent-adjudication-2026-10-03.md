## 1. Frequency inversion with its actual remainder

$$

$$

$$
w=\frac{\beta^2}{a}\left(1-\frac{b}{a\beta}+o(\beta^{-1})\right)
=\frac{\beta^2}{a}-\frac b{a^2}\beta+o(\beta).
$$

Put $y=\sqrt{aw}$. The last relation implies $y/\beta\to1$ and $\beta^2-y^2=(b/a)\beta+o(\beta)$. Dividing by $\beta+y$ gives $\beta-y=b/(2a)+o(1)$. Therefore

$$
\beta=\sqrt{\frac32w}+\frac{\log2}{\pi}+o(1),
\qquad
r=\frac\beta w=\sqrt{\frac{3}{2w}}+\frac{\log2}{\pi w}+o(w^{-1}).
$$

## 4. Receiver tensor reconstructed from vector geometry

$$
\delta r=u+v(n\cdot u)/D,\qquad
\delta\ell=(n\cdot u)/D,

$$
\delta n=\frac{kk^{\mathsf T}u}{\ell}
+\frac{(v\cdot k)kn^{\mathsf T}u}{\ell D},
\qquad
\delta D=-v\cdot\delta n+(n\cdot a_s)(n\cdot u)/D.

$$

## 6. $C^1$ spectral limit and largest-real-root confinement

Scale the real positive spectral parameter as $z=\eta\beta^4$, with $\eta$ in any compact subset of $(0,\infty)$. The uniform delay floor makes every delayed exponential at most $e^{-c\eta\beta^2}$. $$
\beta^{-8}A(\eta\beta^4)\longrightarrow\eta^2I-2e_1e_1^{\mathsf T},
\qquad
\beta^{-16}\det A(\eta\beta^4)\longrightarrow f(\eta)=\eta^2(\eta^2-2).
$$

The convergence is $C^1$ in $\eta$. Differentiating a delayed exponential introduces $\beta^4\ell$; since the exact circular delay is also bounded above by $2R=O(\beta^{-1})$, this adds only an $O(\beta^3)$ polynomial factor. Differentiating $zH$ likewise adds a polynomial factor, and the same exponential suppression wins uniformly on the compact positive interval. Matrix entry convergence and its first derivative imply determinant convergence and its first derivative by the finite determinant product formula.

Since $f'(\sqrt2)=4\sqrt2>0$, sufficiently small fixed intervals about $\sqrt2$ eventually have opposite determinant signs at their endpoints and strictly positive derivative throughout. There is exactly one simple real root inside each such interval. Shrinking the intervals proves its ratio to $\beta^4$ tends to $\sqrt2$.

To prove that this is the largest positive real root, one also needs a global scale bound. If $A(z)$ is singular with $\operatorname{Re}z\ge0$, choose a kernel vector of infinity norm one. Because $|e^{-z\ell}|\le1$, its equation gives

$$
|z|^2\le\left(2|\Omega|+\sum\|H\|\right)|z|
+\Omega^2+\sum(\|C\|+\|F\|).
$$

The two coefficients are $O(\beta^4)$ and $O(\beta^8)$ respectively. Solving this scalar quadratic inequality confines every such root to $|z|\le C_0\beta^4$ with one sufficiently large constant. Uniform determinant convergence on $[\sqrt2+\epsilon,C_0]$, where $f>0$, excludes a larger positive real root outside the small crossing interval. Within that interval uniqueness has already been proved. This closes the largest-positive-real assertion, rather than only constructing some fast root.

Confinement also bounds the scale of complex right-half-plane roots; it does not locate them or prove that the largest real root dominates their real parts. Restoring symbolic units gives $\lambda_{\max}\sim(\sqrt2c_f^3/K)\beta^4$. No stability claim follows merely from the finite frequency projection; the fast-root theorem itself establishes growing formal common-sector modes on sufficiently high exact rungs.
