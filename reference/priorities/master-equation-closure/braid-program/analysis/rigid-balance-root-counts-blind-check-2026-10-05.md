# Rigid balance root counts: blind independent check (2026-10-05)

This record is an independent, blind count of growing characteristic roots for eight rigid arrangements S1 to S8, plus a second reading of a hand proof about like-polarity pairs. The checker built the evaluator, the first variation, the characteristic matrix and the counter from the problem statement alone, read only the input file `blind-sample.json` of the 2026-10-05 geometry session, and was not told the expected counts. Units are wake speed $c_f=1$ and coupling $K=1$. Grades used below: derived means a proof written in this record; measured means a double-precision computation by the instrument named in the same sentence.

**Task B verdict: accept, with two corrections to the wording of the proof.** The argument is sound (derived, re-checked step by step below), and the claim holds for every like-polarity pair for which the acceleration of the outer member is defined, apart from one vacuous case that the hypothesis must exclude.

Step check. Put the receiver, the member on the circle of larger-or-equal radius $R_o$, at $x=R_o e$ with $e$ a unit vector in the plane, and an emission point at $X=R_e e'$ with $e\cdot e'=\cos\psi$, where $R_e=R_i$ for a partner row and $R_e=R_o$ for an own-path row. A row is $a=s_is_j\,(x-X)/(\tau^3\lvert D\rvert)$, and for like polarity and for own-path rows $s_is_j=+1$, so the row is a positive multiple of $x-X$, pointing from the emission point to the receiver, which is the stated convention that like polarity repels. Its radial part is $e\cdot a=(R_o-R_e\cos\psi)/(\tau^3\lvert D\rvert)$. For a partner row $R_o-R_i\cos\psi\ge R_o-R_i\ge0$, with equality only when $R_i=R_o$ and $\cos\psi=1$, which is $X=x$, hence $\tau=\lvert x-X\rvert=0$, not a positive-delay root. For an own-path row $R_o(1-\cos\psi)\ge0$ with equality only when $X=x$, again $\tau=0$; in particular an own-path emission exactly one or more whole turns earlier is at zero distance and so is not a root. The factor $1/(\tau^3\lvert D\rvert)$ is positive whenever $D\ne0$; because the equation carries $\lvert D\rvert$ and not $D$, rows with $D<0$ (which do occur above wake speed) keep the outward sign. The required acceleration of uniform circular motion at angular rate $w$ is $-w^2x$, with radial part $-w^2R_o\le0$. A sum of strictly positive radial parts cannot equal a non-positive number, so the outer member is not in balance. All of this is derived, and the sign conventions in the offered proof are correct.

Correction 1 ($D=0$). The offered proof asserts $\lvert D\rvert>0$ as if it were a property of every root. It is not: for a source at or above wake speed a root can be a tangent root, where the source velocity component along the line to the receiver equals the wake speed and $D=0$. At such a root the row is unbounded and the acceleration of the receiver is not defined, so the equation is not satisfied there either; and as $D\to0$ the row keeps its outward sign because of $\lvert D\rvert$. The proof should therefore split cases: if some row on the outer member has $D=0$ the equation has no finite value to satisfy; otherwise every row has $\lvert D\rvert>0$ and the sign argument applies. The conclusion is unchanged.

Correction 2 (the empty and the degenerate cases). The proof should say what happens when the outer member receives no row at all, and should exclude one degenerate arrangement. If no row exists the sum is zero, and balance would need $w^2R_o=0$. If $w=0$ and the two positions differ, exactly one partner row exists and it is strictly outward, so there is no balance. The only arrangement that slips through is two architrinos at rest at the same point ($w^2R_o=0$ and coincident positions, including both sitting at the centre): no positive-delay root exists, both sides are zero, and the equation is satisfied vacuously. The claim should be stated for two architrinos at distinct positions, or equivalently with $w^2R_o>0$. For $w^2R_o>0$ the proof also covers coincident co-moving members, because their partner rows are then own-path-type rows with strictly positive radial part, or absent.

A numerical spot check agrees with the verdict (measured; see Controls).

## Method

**Paths and roots.** Member $j$ follows $X_j(s)=(R_j\cos(\phi_j+ws),R_j\sin(\phi_j+ws),z_j+us)$ with velocity $V_j(s)$ and acceleration $A_j(s)=-w^2(X_{j,x},X_{j,y},0)$. Because the arrangement is rigid in the co-moving frame, it is enough to evaluate each receiver $i$ at time $T=0$. A causal root is a delay $\tau>0$ with $g(\tau)=\tau^2-R_i^2-R_j^2+2R_iR_j\cos(\phi_i-\phi_j+w\tau)-(z_i-z_j+u\tau)^2=0$. All roots lie below a bound $\tau_{\max}$ obtained from $\tau^2(1-u^2)-2u(z_i-z_j)\tau-(z_i-z_j)^2\le(R_i+R_j)^2$ (derived); the evaluator scans $(0,\tau_{\max}]$ on 40001 points and refines each sign change with Brent's method (measured). For $j=i$ the scan starts just above zero so that the zero-distance double zero at $\tau=0$ is excluded. The row is evaluated as $a=\sigma\,r/(\tau^2\lvert E\rvert)$ with $\sigma=s_is_j$, $r=x-X_j(-\tau)$, $n=r/\tau$, $D=1-n\cdot V_j$ and $E=\tau D=\tau-r\cdot V_j$.

**First variation of a row (derived).** Let the paths be displaced by small $\xi_j(s)$. Write $\delta\rho=\xi_i(T)-\xi_j(s)$ for the receiver displacement minus the source displacement at the unperturbed emission time, and $\dot\xi_j(s)$ for the source velocity change. The emission time shifts by $-\delta\tau$, so $\delta r=\delta\rho+V_j\,\delta\tau$, and the root condition $\tau=\lvert r\rvert$ gives $\delta\tau=n\cdot\delta r$, hence

$$
\delta\tau=\frac{n\cdot\delta\rho}{D}.
$$

The source velocity at the shifted emission time changes by $\delta V=\dot\xi_j(s)-A_j(s)\,\delta\tau$, which is where the source acceleration at emission enters. Then $\delta E=\delta\tau-V_j\cdot\delta r-r\cdot\delta V=(1-\lvert V_j\rvert^2+r\cdot A_j)\,\delta\tau-V_j\cdot\delta\rho-r\cdot\dot\xi_j$, and

$$
\delta a=\sigma\left[\frac{\delta r}{\tau^2\lvert E\rvert}-\frac{2r\,\delta\tau}{\tau^3\lvert E\rvert}-\frac{r\,\delta E}{\tau^2E\lvert E\rvert}\right]=M\,\delta\rho+S\,\dot\xi_j(s),
$$

with the $3\times3$ matrices

$$
M=\frac{\sigma}{\tau^2\lvert E\rvert}\left[I+\frac{Vn^{\mathsf T}}{D}-\frac{2nn^{\mathsf T}}{D}-\frac{r}{E}\left(\frac{(1-\lvert V\rvert^2+r\cdot A)\,n^{\mathsf T}}{D}-V^{\mathsf T}\right)\right],\qquad S=\frac{\sigma\,rr^{\mathsf T}}{\tau^2E\lvert E\rvert}.
$$

For a source at rest ($V=0$, $A=0$, $D=1$, $E=\tau$) this reduces to $M=\sigma(I-3nn^{\mathsf T})/\tau^3$, the gradient of $\sigma r/\tau^3$, and $S=\sigma\,nn^{\mathsf T}/\tau^2$. An own-path row uses the same formula with $j=i$, so that $\delta\rho=\xi_i(T)-\xi_i(s)$.

**Characteristic matrix (derived).** Let $\mathcal R(\alpha)$ be the rotation by angle $\alpha$ about $z$ and $J$ its generator, and take $\xi_j(s)=\mathcal R(ws+\phi_j)\,q_j\,e^{\lambda s}$ with constant complex 3-vectors $q_j$. Then $\dot\xi_j=\mathcal R(ws+\phi_j)(\lambda+wJ)q_je^{\lambda s}$ and $\ddot\xi_j=\mathcal R(ws+\phi_j)(\lambda+wJ)^2q_je^{\lambda s}$. Setting $\ddot\xi_i(0)$ equal to the sum of the row variations of member $i$ and multiplying by $\mathcal R(-\phi_i)$ gives $C(\lambda)q=0$ with the $3N\times3N$ matrix whose blocks are

$$
C_{ij}(\lambda)=\delta_{ij}\Big[(\lambda+wJ)^2-\sum_{k\in\text{rows}(i)}\mathcal R(-\phi_i)M_k\mathcal R(\phi_i)\Big]+\sum_{k\in\text{rows}(i\leftarrow j)}e^{-\lambda\tau_k}\,\mathcal R(-\phi_i)\big[M_k-S_k(\lambda+wJ)\big]\mathcal R(\phi_j-w\tau_k),
$$

where rows$(i)$ are all rows received by $i$ and rows$(i\leftarrow j)$ those emitted by $j$ (including $j=i$ for own-path rows). The entries are entire functions of $\lambda$, so the determinant has zeros and no poles.

**Counting rule (measured).** The counter works with $F(\lambda)=\det\big[\Lambda(\lambda)^{-1}C(\lambda)\big]$, where $\Lambda$ is block diagonal with blocks $(\lambda+wJ)^2$. $F$ tends to 1 at infinity in the closed right half plane, has the same zeros as $\det C$ away from $0$ and $\pm iw$, and its only possible poles are at $0$ and $\pm iw$, which are left of the contour. The contour is the rectangle with left edge at real part $d=0.0005\,w$, right edge at $d+1.05\,B$ and horizontal edges at imaginary part $\pm1.05\,B$. The sampling rule is: 400 uniform points per edge; on each edge, geometric clusters of points with ratio $\sqrt2$ and innermost offset $d/8$ about the projections of $0$ and $\pm iw$; then every segment across which the phase of $F$ steps by more than $0.2$ rad or the logarithm of its modulus by more than $0.5$ is bisected, repeatedly, until no such segment remains. The count is the total phase change divided by $2\pi$. In every run the bisection terminated with no unresolved segment and the count was an integer to $10^{-14}$.

**Bound beyond which no root with non-negative real part lies (derived inequality, measured constants).** Suppose $C(\lambda)q=0$ with real part of $\lambda$ non-negative, and let $i$ be a member with the largest Euclidean norm $\lvert q_i\rvert$. The matrix $(\lambda+wJ)^2$ is normal with eigenvalues $\lambda^2$ and $(\lambda\pm iw)^2$, so $\lvert(\lambda+wJ)^2q_i\rvert\ge(\lvert\lambda\rvert-w)^2\lvert q_i\rvert$ for $\lvert\lambda\rvert>w$. On the other side $\lvert e^{-\lambda\tau}\rvert\le1$, rotations preserve norms and $\lVert\lambda+wJ\rVert\le\lvert\lambda\rvert+w$, so the remaining terms are bounded by $\big[m_i+s_i(\lvert\lambda\rvert+w)\big]\lvert q_i\rvert$ with $m_i=\sum_{k\in\text{rows}(i)}2\lVert M_k\rVert$ and $s_i=\sum_{k\in\text{rows}(i)}\lVert S_k\rVert$ (spectral norms). A root therefore needs $(\lvert\lambda\rvert-w)^2\le m+s(\lvert\lambda\rvert+w)$ with $m=\max_im_i$, $s=\max_is_i$, and $B$ is the larger solution of $(B-w)^2=m+s(B+w)$. Every root with non-negative real part has $\lvert\lambda\rvert\le B$, and the rectangle used contains that half disc.

**Direct location of the roots (measured).** Independently of the contour count, the roots were located by Newton iteration on $\det C$ in the form $\lambda\leftarrow\lambda-1/\operatorname{tr}\big(C^{-1}\,dC/d\lambda\big)$ with the analytic derivative, from 2257 seeds on a geometric grid covering real parts from $d$ to $d+1.05B$ and imaginary parts from $0$ to $1.05B$ (step capped at a quarter of $\max(\lvert\lambda\rvert,w)$, 80 iterations, converged points kept when the last step is below $10^{-9}$ relative). Converged points were folded to non-negative imaginary part, merged at tolerance $10^{-7}w$, and counted once if real and twice if not. The largest real part in the table is the largest real part in that list; no bisection on a contour is involved.

## Controls

1. Counter on a known case first (measured). Before any target run the counter was applied to a rational-times-exponential test function with 8 planted zeros inside the rectangle (two of them at real part $0.00051$ next to $\pm i$, one at $0.0006$) and 10 planted outside (among them $0.00049\pm i$, $0.0004$, a double zero at $0$ and zeros at $\pm i$); it returned 8.0.
2. Balance and census (measured). The all-root evaluator reproduces $-w^2(x,y,0)$ on every member of every configuration to between $4\times10^{-16}$ and $2.4\times10^{-14}$ relative; a missing or spurious root would break this at order one. The row census is unchanged when the delay scan is refined from 40001 to 2000001 points, and the smallest $\lvert D\rvert$ over all rows is 0.39 (S2), so no root is near tangency.
3. First variation by finite differences on perturbed paths (measured). For each configuration, two random draws of complex $q$ and complex $\lambda$ define real displaced paths $X_j+\epsilon\,\mathrm{Re}[\mathcal R(ws+\phi_j)q_je^{\lambda s}]$ with $\epsilon=\pm10^{-6}$; the delays are re-solved on the displaced paths by Newton iteration and the rows re-summed. The centred difference of the total acceleration of every member agrees with the prediction of the assembled matrix, $\mathrm{Re}\big[\mathcal R(\phi_i)\big((\Lambda-C)q\big)_i\big]$, to relative error between $2\times10^{-11}$ and $3\times10^{-9}$. This tests the receiver, source-displacement, source-velocity and delay-change (source acceleration) terms together, including own-path rows in S1, S6 and S7.
4. Neutral roots (measured). The exact symmetry directions are null vectors of $C$ to rounding: rotation about $z$ and translation along $z$ at $\lambda=0$, and translation in the plane at $\lambda=\pm iw$ (residuals below $3\times10^{-16}$ relative). $C(0)$ has two singular values at rounding level in every configuration; $C(iw)$ has two when $u=0$ and one in S2, where $u\ne0$.
5. Agreement of two counting methods (measured). The Newton list reproduces the contour count in all eight configurations: 6, 13, 11, 16, 32, 24, 26, 35. A contour whose left edge sits just to the right of the largest real part encloses zero roots in all eight.
6. Refinement and enlargement (measured). The count is unchanged with phase threshold $0.1$ rad and 800 points per edge; with the rectangle enlarged to $2.1B$; with the rectangle enlarged to $8B$ at threshold $0.05$ rad and 1600 points per edge; and on a half-disc contour of radius $1.5B$ (4000 chords). It is also unchanged when the left edge is moved to $0.25d$, $0.5d$, $2d$ and $10d$, so no root sits near the threshold line. The $2.1B$ and refined variants were run for all eight. The $8B$ variant and the left-edge shifts to $0.5d$, $2d$ and $10d$ were run for all eight (for S2 by `s2_diag.py`); the half-disc variant was run for all but S2. The left-edge shift to $0.25d$ failed to resolve for S2 with the normalisation $F$ and was redone with a second normalisation, as described in the next control.
7. Second normalisation of the determinant (measured). Forming $\Lambda^{-1}C$ is ill-conditioned within about $d$ of the neutral points, and in S2 (the one configuration with $u\ne0$, where $C(iw)$ has a single null vector) the bisection at left edge $0.25d$ ran into rounding noise and did not terminate; that run was stopped and is not used. The count was therefore repeated for all eight configurations with $F_2=\det C/\big[\lambda^2(\lambda^2+w^2)^2\big]^N$, computed from the logarithm of $\det C$ without forming $\Lambda^{-1}C$, at left edges $d$, $0.5d$, $0.25d$ and $0.1d$ (threshold $0.1$ rad, 800 points per edge). Every one of these 32 runs terminated with no unresolved segment and returned the tabulated count (`f2_control.py`, `out_f2_control.txt`).
8. Nearest roots to the threshold line (measured). A Newton search seeded on both sides of the imaginary axis (run for all but S2) finds, as the non-neutral roots closest to the line, real parts $-0.0015\,w$ (S7, imaginary part $1.3202\,w$) and $-0.0023\,w$ (S4, imaginary part $1.0110\,w$) on the decaying side and $+0.0110\,w$ (S1) on the growing side. For S2 a Newton search from a tight cloud of seeds within $0.003\,w$ of $0$ and $\pm iw$ converged only to the neutral roots themselves.
9. Task B spot check (measured). For 500 random like-polarity pairs on coplanar concentric circles at one angular rate (radius ratio between 0 and 1, one case in five with equal radii, speeds from well below to several times the wake speed), the all-root evaluator found 1247 rows on the outer member, 523 of them own-path rows and 226 with $D<0$; every one has a strictly positive radial part (smallest $6.7\times10^{-3}$). This illustrates the proof and is not a substitute for it.

## Limits

- The counts are measured in double precision; they are integers because the argument principle returns integers, but the placement of roots relative to the threshold rests on the float evaluation of $C$. The margin is large: the nearest growing root is at $0.011\,w$ and the nearest decaying non-neutral root at $-0.0015\,w$, against a threshold of $0.0005\,w$ and root-location errors near $10^{-9}w$.
- The bound $B$ is a proof for roots with non-negative real part given the row matrices, which are themselves float values; it says nothing about the left half plane, where infinitely many roots lie.
- The Newton list is complete only in the sense that its count equals the contour count; multiplicities are inferred from that agreement, not measured root by root.
- The count is a statement about the linearisation in the co-moving frame for perturbations of the stated exponential form. It assumes that small displacements neither create nor destroy causal roots, which holds here because no row is near $D=0$ (smallest $\lvert D\rvert$ is 0.39).
- Both counting methods share one implementation of $C(\lambda)$, so their agreement checks the counting, not the matrix; the matrix is checked separately by controls 3 and 4, which use an independent delay solve on displaced paths and exact symmetries.
- Two exploratory runs of `extra_controls.py` exceeded the five-minute run limit (a slow delay-scan loop in the first, the unresolved S2 bisection in the second) and were stopped; `out_extra.txt` is the output of the second up to the point where it was stopped (it holds the completed extra controls for S1, S6, S7 and S3), and the remaining outputs are `out_extra_S8_S5_S4.txt`, `out_extra_S2.txt` (delay-scan check only), `out_s2_diag.txt`, `out_f2_control.txt` and `out_extra_taskB.txt`. For S2 the half-disc contour and the two-sided nearest-root search were not run.
- The checker did not read the generated startup router or any other repository analysis, by the terms of the blind check and its 40-minute time box.
