# Where a disturbed balance goes, and a first search for motions that are not rigid

## Result

Exact rigid balances of the unchanged Master Equation were released from their complete rigid pasts with a small velocity kick and followed with the delayed equation. No released history returned toward its balance.

**Balances entirely below wake speed.** All 65 of the first search were released, in 154 runs. Every run ended the same way: within $1.4$ periods a member's speed rose to wake speed, at positive distance from every other member and with no change in the number of causal roots. A repeat of the 130 census runs on 2026-10-06 recorded the rate and the transmitter factors in every run: the arriving member's speed was rising at the event in all 130, at between $0.0023$ and $211$ per unit time, and no transmitter factor fell below $0.21$ in any run. The 61 balances below wake speed added by the [second campaign](rigid-balance-search-2026-10-04.md#a-second-campaign-2026-10-06) were released the same way, in 122 runs: 120 end with a member arriving at wake speed, within $2.3$ periods, at a positive rate and with no transmitter factor below $0.18$; **two end with the arrangement dispersing and every member still below wake speed.** On a line that event is the proved first-exit obstruction. A new theorem, recorded in the [curved-path obstruction](../../analysis/curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions.md) and accepted by a delegated reviewer's [adjudication](../../analysis/curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions-independent-adjudication-2026-10-05.md) in the same session, extends it to curved paths: when a member's whole past is below wake speed, its speed reaches wake speed at a positive rate, and the rows from the others are bounded and continuous there, the equation, imposed across the event, has no continuation with continuous, absolutely continuous velocity and ordinary roots. So in 274 of the 276 runs the disturbed history runs to a point where the unchanged equation has no continuation of that kind, on the measured premises of a positive rate and regular rows; the member's whole earlier past below wake speed holds by construction. In the other two the history stays regular for as long as it was followed.

**Balances with a member above wake speed.** All 28 were released again on 2026-10-06 with an integrator that lands on each birth and merger of causal roots, two kicks each at two settings; 55 of the 56 runs completed. Thirty-eight runs converged between the two settings. Of those, 31 end with a member arriving at wake speed from below, 5 with a fast member slowing to wake speed from above, and 2 with the members dispersing and no member reaching wake speed. The other 17 runs passed through many root births and mergers and did not converge; they are indications only. In the converged arrivals from below that follow a root birth, the interval between the birth and the arrival obeys a closed formula, the [root-birth law](../../analysis/root-birth-impulse-law.md).

**The six-member ring reference T02**, which has every member above wake speed, was released with three kicks: in each a member slows to wake speed from above after $0.83$ to $0.85$ of a period, and a symmetric disturbance grows at the ring research's certified rate to within half a percent.

Further results of the same work:

1. **The fastest growing mode is a real motion.** On the four balances where it was measured, the early departure grows at the rate of the largest characteristic root found by the linear analysis, to within half a percent. Nothing was measured about the slower roots.
2. **A mirror opposite pair below wake speed that turns in one sense gains angular momentum at every instant.** This is an exact identity, and it rules out every periodic history of that kind, rigid or breathing.
3. **A search for periodic motions that are not rigid found none**, in about 1,830 starts over breathing arrangements of one to three pairs and of two or three members with no imposed symmetry, all below wake speed. The search is thin for more than two members.
4. **Twelve members can travel.** A search of rigid travelling arrangements of twelve members found two, one of them with no net polarity; both are enclosed and both are unstable.

**Claim grade:** measured for the releases, by a float integrator with step refinement and controls; derived, with an adjudication by a delegated reviewer in the same session, for the two curved-path obstructions, and measured on 250 runs for the statement that released histories below wake speed meet the hypotheses of the first; derived at leading order, with a blind re-derivation and test by a delegated adjudicator, for the root-birth law; derived, with a second reading, for the angular-momentum identity; measured for the searches, which exclude nothing beyond their starts; computer-assisted derived for the two twelve-member balances, by one interval instrument. The equation is unchanged: every positive-delay causal root, own-path roots included, with no speed cap and no event rule. Values use $c_f=1$ and $K=1$.

## The release instrument and its controls

The instrument is `release.py` in `.local-data/master-equation-closure/geometry-session-20261005/`. Each member's history is its exact rigid motion for all past time. At time zero the members receive a small velocity kick of random direction, so paths stay continuous. The path is then advanced by a fixed-step fourth-order method, with the computed history interpolated by cubic Hermite segments.

Every causal root is carried, own-path roots included, so members above wake speed are allowed. The roots are found once by a full scan of the delay and then followed by Newton continuation; the full scan is repeated every 25 steps, and a change in the number of roots stops the run. The size of an arrangement means its largest initial radius, or its largest height offset if that is greater. A run also stops when a member that was below wake speed reaches it, when a transmitter factor falls below $0.02$, when two members come within two or three percent of the arrangement's size, or when a pair is more than six sizes apart.

Two controls ran first, on the closed-form pair.

- **No kick.** The balance is held to the integrator's accuracy. The departure starts at $5\times10^{-13}$ of the size with 3,000 steps per period and at $7\times10^{-12}$ with 1,500, a ratio of sixteen as a fourth-order method should give, and it then grows by a factor $3.8$ per quarter period, which is $e^{0.853\pi/2}$: the rounding error itself grows at the linear rate.
- **A small kick.** With a kick of $10^{-8}$ the departure, fitted while it lies between $10^{-6}$ and $3\times10^{-4}$ of the size, grows at $0.8498\,\omega$ and $0.8530\,\omega$ for two kick directions. The largest located root is $0.8531\,\omega$.

The same fit on three other balances gives the following.

The integrator shares no code with the linear analysis beyond the statement of the equation. Its agreement with the located roots is therefore a check of both.

**Step refinement.** Thirteen runs were repeated with half the step. The event and the member involved were the same in every case, and the event times agreed to within $0.001$ of a period. Kicks of $10^{-6}$ and $10^{-3}$ gave the same kinds of outcome, later and earlier by the amount the linear growth rate predicts: for the travelling three-member balance the delay between a $10^{-4}$ and a $10^{-6}$ kick is $0.222$ of a period, and $\ln100$ divided by the largest rate is $0.222$.

**Blind reproduction.** A separately written integrator, built from a statement of the problem by a delegated checker who was given three arrangements and their kicks but not the outcomes, [reproduced the releases](released-balances-blind-reproduction-2026-10-06.md). For the travelling three-member, planar six-member and three-dimensional seven-member balances it found the same event and the same arriving member, at $0.49664$, $0.84761$ and $0.32103$ periods, against this instrument's $0.4968$, $0.8477$ and $0.3213$; the differences are within this instrument's step. Its two steppers and six resolutions agree with one another to seven digits or better. It measured the growth rate of the first at $3.297\,\omega$ to $3.299\,\omega$, against the located root $3.2987\,\omega$, and confirmed one causal root per pair throughout.

**Adaptive steps and changes of the root census.** Two extensions were added after the first runs. `release2.py` shrinks the step during close passages, to a hundredth of the smallest distance divided by the largest speed, and stops when a member that was above wake speed falls to it. `release3.py` also follows changes in the number of causal roots: when a rescan finds new roots, which are born in pairs where a transmitter factor passes through zero, the tracked set is replaced and the run continues. A row is unbounded but integrable at such a birth, and the integrator steps over it; times after a census change are therefore approximate. The two later instruments stop a run only when two members come within a millionth of the size or a pair is more than forty sizes apart, which is why closer passages and wider separations are reported below than the first instrument's stop rules allow. On the outward branch of the closed-form pair, which has no census change, the fixed-step and adaptive instruments give the same event time, $2.985$ periods.

**Resolved births and mergers.** A fourth instrument, `release4.py`, was written on 2026-10-06 to remove that approximation. For every receiver and source it tracks, besides the causal roots, the delays at which the transmitter factor vanishes, which are the extrema of the root function in the delay. Two roots are born or merge exactly when the value at such an extremum passes through zero. The step is limited to a fraction of the time to that zero, so the mesh is graded toward each event; at the event the two new roots are constructed directly from the extremum, and every tracked root is re-solved in a bracket between its neighbours. A full rescan every 25 steps checks the tracked set, and a mismatch is counted as a missed change. A run is called converged when two settings, 1,500 and 3,000 base steps per period with step and grading tolerances halved, give the same event and member, no missed change, and event times within $0.002$ of a period. Its controls are the [blind reproduction](../../binary-research/analysis/released-pair-blind-reproduction-2026-10-06.md) of the closed-form pair, which agrees on the arrival time to six digits and on the birth time to seven, the growth rate of a symmetric disturbance of the six-member ring against that ring's certified root, and the [root-birth law](../../analysis/root-birth-impulse-law.md), whose predicted interval it reproduces to between one and two percent. It is a float instrument (measured).

**The birth impulse, applied analytically.** `release4.py` integrates the step that contains a birth without the two new rows, and so loses the velocity change they deliver between the birth and the end of that step, about a hundredth of wake speed in the runs examined. `release5.py`, written on 2026-10-06, adds that change in closed form from the first integral of the [root-birth law](../../analysis/root-birth-impulse-law.md) and rescans every 10 steps. With it the interval from a birth to the following arrival at wake speed agrees with the law to $0.1$ to $0.2$ percent at the census settings, against one to eleven percent before, and for the closed-form pair it is $2.905\times10^{-6}$ against the blind reproduction's $2.90438\times10^{-6}$. It does not change arrival times at the precision quoted in this note: those are set by the time of the birth, whose error is first order in the step. Eight of the 17 runs that had not converged with `release4.py` were repeated with it. Two now converge: a four-member balance, with a member slowing to wake speed at $1.1152$ periods at both settings, and a seven-member ring with a central member, at $0.6903$ and $0.6902$. Two agree between settings on the event, the member and the time to $0.0003$ of a period, but each still has one missed root change: the six-member ring T02 with the census kick, at $0.8092$ and $0.8089$ periods, and a four-member balance at $1.0612$ and $1.0614$. The equal-radius two-member circle with its second kick again shows no event in eight periods at both settings, now with no missed change and the same four births and six mergers. One four-member run still gives different times at the two settings, $1.633$ and $1.443$ periods; one reached its time limit; and one stopped because the rescan lost roots at large separation, which is a defect of the rescan and not of the equation. So the analytic impulse and the more frequent rescan remove some of the failures, and the runs with many root changes remain the weak part of the instrument.

## The closed-form pair in detail

The pair's fastest root is real, so the departure has two directions.

**Outward.** The fast member spirals outward and slows. After about three periods its orbital radius has grown from $0.755$ to $2.13$, the separation from $0.68$ to $2.59$, and its speed has fallen from $\pi/2$ to wake speed at the rate $0.021$ per unit time. No root is born or lost on the way, and the smallest transmitter factor is $0.66$. At the crossing a new own-path root appears at vanishing delay.

**Inward.** The fast member spirals inward and speeds up. It passes the slow member at a distance of $0.0033$, which is $0.4$ percent of its original orbit radius, at $28.7$ times wake speed, and swings past; two step tolerances agree on both numbers. During the approach the slow member barely responds, because it receives the fast member's wake from where that member was most of a unit of time earlier. Two new causal roots from the fast member's passage then reach the slow member, $0.0014$ of a period after the closest approach, and the slow member is driven from $0.51$ to wake speed within a further $10^{-6}$ of a period. On the two newborn rows the transmitter factor starts at zero and grows as the square root of the time since the birth, so the rows are unbounded at the birth and their sum over time is finite; the newborn delay is $0.0041$, and a row scales as the inverse square of its delay, which is why so short an interval is enough.

**The inward run was resolved and reproduced blind.** The integrator `release4.py`, which lands on each root birth and constructs the newborn roots, gives the arrival at $1.844237$, $1.844232$ and $1.844229$ periods at 2,000, 4,000 and 8,000 base steps per period, with the birth $10^{-6}$ of a period earlier each time. A [delegated blind reproduction](../../binary-research/analysis/released-pair-blind-reproduction-2026-10-06.md), which wrote its own integrator from the statement of the equation and was not told the outcome, gives the arrival at $1.8442290677$ periods, the birth at $1.8442281057$, the smallest separation $0.0033058$ and the largest speed $28.73275$; this session's values for the last two are $0.0033058$ and $28.7327$. For the outward kick it gives the descent to wake speed at $2.98474$ periods with no root change, against $2.9849$ here, where the last digit is limited by the step. Both instruments are float integrators written by the same model family, so the agreement tests the arithmetic and the handling of root births and not the equation (measured).

**The downward crossing, in numbers.** At a downward crossing with deceleration $\alpha$ the [curved-path analysis](../../analysis/curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions.md#asymptotics-under-extra-smoothness) gives a newborn own-path root with delay $2(1-v)/\alpha$ at leading order, transmitter factor of magnitude $1-v$, and a forward row of magnitude $\alpha^2/(4(1-v)^3)$. This comparison is an indication only (inferred): the formula is leading order on a prescribed path with equal rates before and after, and the sampled state lies just past the crossing. In the first fixed-step run, with $1-v=2\times10^{-4}$ and $\alpha=0.036$ estimated from that run's last samples, the predicted delay is $0.011$ and the rescan found a root at $0.016$, a difference of 45 percent. The adaptive run of the same branch gives the rate $0.021$ quoted above, measured over its last five steps.

## Balances entirely below wake speed

All 65 balances with every member below wake speed were released: the five in the table above with several kicks each, and all 65 in a census of two kicks each with the adaptive instrument. They have three to twenty members and include planar, three-dimensional and travelling arrangements, with and without net polarity.

All 154 runs ended with a member reaching wake speed from below, between $0.04$ and $1.37$ periods after the kick. In none did the number of causal roots change before the event, and the closest any two members came in any run was $0.9$ percent of the arrangement's size, in the eighteen-member balance. The member that arrives is positive in about half the runs and negative in the rest. The charged trimer is listed in the table below for comparison although it belongs to the next section, having two members above wake speed; the member that arrives is the central one, which is at rest in the balance.

**Every run measured, 2026-10-06.** The census of two kicks per balance was repeated with the rate of rise of the arriving member's speed and the smallest transmitter factor on any row recorded for every run. All 130 runs end with an arrival at wake speed from below, between $0.04$ and $1.27$ periods after the kick, with no change in the number of causal roots. The rate of rise at the event is positive in all 130: its smallest value is $0.0023$ per unit time, on a fifteen-member planar balance, its median is $0.49$ and its largest is $211$. The smallest transmitter factor reached on any row in any run is $0.21$, on a ten-member three-dimensional balance; the median over runs is $0.48$. The rate over the last step and over the last five steps agree to within a factor of $1.3$ in every run. This replaces the six-run sample of the first version as the measured basis for applying the curved-path theorem to these releases.

**The balances of the second campaign.** The 61 new balances below wake speed, of three to twenty members, were released with two kicks each. In 120 of the 122 runs a member arrives at wake speed from below, between $0.07$ and $2.24$ periods after the kick, at a positive rate, the smallest being $0.0016$ per unit time, and with no transmitter factor below $0.18$. The new three-member balance at rest ends this way in under half a period, and the four four-member balances with no net polarity in $0.4$ to $1.9$ periods.

**Two runs disperse below wake speed.** The seven-member planar balance with no symmetry, with its second kick, and one of the twelve-member three-dimensional balances with no net polarity, with its second kick, come apart without any member reaching wake speed. Both were repeated with the resolving integrator at two settings, because the adaptive instrument loses roots when delays outgrow its scan range. The results agree: the members are 40 sizes apart at $2.6724$ and $2.6719$ periods for the first, and at $3.0408$ and $3.0406$ periods for the second; no root is born or lost; the largest speed any member reaches is $0.70$ and $0.85$ of wake speed; and at the end the members move at between $0.05$ and $0.45$, and between $0.25$ and $0.84$, of wake speed. With their first kicks the same two balances end with an arrival at wake speed, at $0.48$ and $0.68$ periods. So the ending depends on the direction of the disturbance, and a disturbed balance below wake speed can have a regular history that is not bound.

**Reproduced blind.** A delegated [blind reproduction](dispersal-blind-reproduction-2026-10-06.md) was given the seven-member balance, the two kicks and the stopping rules, and was not told the outcomes. With its own integrator, at six resolutions for each case, it finds for the first kick that member 6 reaches wake speed at $0.476761$ periods with its speed rising at $0.4029$ per unit time and no transmitter factor below $0.5075$; this session's values are $0.4768$, $0.405$ and $0.508$. For the second kick it finds that the members are 40 sizes apart at $2.67163$ periods, that no member ever exceeds $0.70222$ of wake speed, that the closest approach is $0.0748$ sizes, that no transmitter factor falls below $0.3771$, and that all 21 pair distances are increasing at the end; this session's values are $2.6719$, $0.702$, $0.0748$ and $0.377$. The final speeds agree member by member to three digits. Both instruments are float integrators written by instances of one model family (measured).

**How often.** Sixteen random kicks of the same size were applied to each of the two balances with the adaptive instrument. The seven-member balance ends with an arrival at wake speed in 13, disperses in 2, and in 1 the instrument stopped on a lost root. The twelve-member balance arrives in 13 and disperses in 3. In the dispersing runs the largest speed any member reaches is $0.96$ and $0.95$ of wake speed. For comparison, the new three-member balance at rest and two of the four-member balances with no net polarity arrive at wake speed in all 16 kicks each. The three largest characteristic roots of the seven-member balance are real, at $3.42$, $3.10$ and $2.98$ in units of its angular rate, so the direction of departure is set by the signs and sizes of several real modes and not by one. Only the two census runs were confirmed with the resolving integrator; the other dispersals are indications from the adaptive instrument.

**A wider scan for a history that stays bound.** To look for a disturbed balance that neither reaches wake speed nor disperses, every balance below wake speed with at most eight members, 18 in all, was given 24 further random kicks of size $10^{-4}$ and 8 of size $10^{-2}$ and followed to 12 periods, with a single-root integrator that is exact for the unchanged equation while every member is below wake speed. Of the 576 runs, 572 end with a member arriving at wake speed, the latest at $2.75$ periods, and 4 disperse: three more runs of the seven-member balance and one of a five-member three-dimensional balance, the last with the larger kick. **None stays bound.** In the dispersing runs the largest speed reached lies between $0.69$ and $0.83$ of wake speed, so the line between the two endings appears to be whether a close encounter carries a member to wake speed, and no long-lived state was seen between them (measured, float, one instrument and one setting).

**The seven-member dispersal, followed further.** The first of these runs was continued to 30 of the balance's periods, about 32,600 units of time. By then the seven members are single and far apart: the closest two are 77 sizes from each other, all 21 pair distances are increasing, the slowest at $0.09$ of wake speed, and the members move at between $0.09$ and $0.45$ of wake speed, changed by less than $0.004$ over the second half of the run. For every pair of opposite polarity the square of the relative speed times the distance exceeds the slow-motion escape value by a factor of at least seven. No root was born or lost and no root change was missed. On this evidence the history is regular for all time: an exact balance for all earlier time, a small kick, and then seven free architrinos, every one below wake speed throughout (measured, float, one instrument and one setting for the continuation; the escape comparison is a slow-motion estimate and not a bound). **Runs that did not converge (17).** Eleven end with a descent to wake speed, three with members far apart, two with an arrival from below, and one, the equal-radius circle with its second kick, with no event in eight periods: the pair opens steadily, from $0.17$ to $1.3$ in separation by six periods, with both members between $1.17$ and $1.23$ times wake speed. All 17 pass through between 4 and 56 births and mergers, and most have a missed root change at one setting or the other. The event kind agrees between the two settings in 15 of them, and the event times in several, but by this note's rule they are indications. Of the 28 runs with any birth or merger, 11 converged; all 27 runs without one converged.

**What separates the two groups.** Where members sit on both sides of wake speed, 33 of 42 runs end with a slower member reaching wake speed from below. Where every member is above wake speed, 11 of 13 completed runs end with a member slowing to wake speed, and none ends with an arrival from below, since no member is below. In both groups the first event is at wake speed in 49 of 55 runs.

## Arrival at wake speed on a curved path is obstructed

The [curved-path analysis](../../analysis/curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions.md) proves two statements for a member of any arrangement, in two or three dimensions.

- **Arrival from below.** If a member's whole past is below wake speed, its speed reaches wake speed at a positive rate, and the rows from the other members are bounded near the event and continuous at it, then the equation, imposed across the event, has no continuation with continuous, absolutely continuous velocity and ordinary roots. The partners' rows carry the speed above wake speed; the member then overtakes the wake it emitted just before the event; that own-path row points forward, and it makes the square of the speed excess grow at least logarithmically toward the event, which no finite speed allows.
- **Slowing from above.** If a member's speed comes down to wake speed at a positive rate and the opposing rows, including those from its own remote past, are bounded, then no continuation of that class has speed below wake speed afterwards. Continuing at or above wake speed is not excluded, and the boundedness was not measured on any release. Just below wake speed the member meets the wake it emitted just above it, and the forward row is not integrable.

A released history has a velocity kick at time zero, so its past is not smooth there; the proofs use only that the speed was below wake speed on the whole past and the stated regularity near the event, which the kick does not affect.

Both rest on one geometric fact with no counterpart on a line: every own-path chord of short delay near the event lies within a small angle of the direction of motion, so every such row pushes forward, however many there are. An [adjudication](../../analysis/curved-path-wake-speed-obstructions-independent-adjudication-2026-10-05.md) by a delegated reviewer in the same session accepts both with corrections to wording and to two subleading coefficients. A velocity that jumps is covered separately. In the measure form of the equation a velocity of bounded variation cannot jump at such an arrival, and no continuation of bounded variation exists at all when the singular part of the acceleration measure is built from row directions with nonnegative weights, as its construction gives ([jump exclusion](../../analysis/curved-path-wake-speed-jump-exclusion.md), derived conditional on that formulation, independently adjudicated). Not covered: an arrival at zero rate, a member that touches wake speed from above and stays at or above it, partner rows that are unbounded at the event, and a velocity that is not of bounded variation.

## A mirror opposite pair below wake speed gains angular momentum at every instant

Let two architrinos of opposite polarity move as mirror images through a centre, at $\pm\mathbf x(t)$, with $\mathbf x=\rho(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)$ and every speed below wake speed. Each receives exactly one row, from its partner at the emission time $s=t-\tau$:

$$
\mathbf a(t)=-\frac{\mathbf x(t)+\mathbf x(s)}{\tau^3\lvert D\rvert}.
$$

The angular momentum per member is $\ell=\mathbf x\times\dot{\mathbf x}=\rho^2\dot\theta$, and its rate of change is $\mathbf x\times\mathbf a$:

$$
\frac{d\ell}{dt}=\frac{\rho(t)\,\rho(s)\,\sin\bigl(\theta(t)-\theta(s)\bigr)}{\tau^3\lvert D\rvert}.
$$

The angle swept during the delay is less than a quarter turn in magnitude. Two steps show it. First, the straight distance from $\mathbf x(s)$ to $\mathbf x(t)$ is no more than the path length, which is less than the delay $\tau=\lvert\mathbf x(t)+\mathbf x(s)\rvert$ because the speed is below wake speed; $\lvert\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(s)\rvert<\lvert\mathbf x(t)+\mathbf x(s)\rvert$ means $\mathbf x(t)\cdot\mathbf x(s)>0$, so the two positions are less than a quarter turn apart in direction. Second, the path cannot have wound further: if the swept angle reached half a turn, the path would first meet the ray opposite $\mathbf x(s)$ at some point of radius $\rho_*$, and its two pieces would have lengths at least $\rho(s)+\rho_*$ and $\lvert\rho(t)-\rho_*\rvert$, a total of at least $\rho(s)+\rho(t)\ge\tau$, again more than a member below wake speed can travel. Hence the swept angle lies strictly between minus and plus a quarter turn, and $d\ell/dt$ has its sign.

The single root exists for any history that satisfies the equation on its whole past, and for any history with bounded radius; for a prescribed path that recedes fast enough it can fail to exist at early times, and the statement is then empty there. The transmitter factor lies between $0$ and $2$ and cannot vanish.

If the pair turns in one sense, $\dot\theta>0$, the swept angle is positive and $\ell$ increases strictly, at every instant and at every speed below wake speed. A periodic history, in the fixed frame or in a turning one, would need $\ell$ to return to its value. So no mirror opposite pair below wake speed has a periodic history that turns in one sense, whether rigid or breathing. For like polarity the sign reverses, and $\ell$ falls for as long as the pair keeps turning in the same sense.

This extends the first-order statement of the [encounter map](../../binary-research/analysis/slow-mirror-encounter-first-order-map.md), that angular momentum per member grows by $K/(4c_f)$ per radian at small speed, to an exact inequality at every speed below wake speed. It does not exclude a history whose sense of rotation reverses, in which $\ell$ changes sign; the search below found none. A check on 400 random one-sense mirror histories below wake speed found the rate positive at every sampled time, with the largest swept angle $1.43$ radians, inside a quarter turn (measured). A [second reading](../../binary-research/analysis/mirror-pair-angular-momentum-second-reading-2026-10-05.md) accepted the identity and its consequence; the quarter-turn bound, the first-crossing form of the path-length argument and the remark on existence of the root are its corrections.

## A search for periodic motions that are not rigid

Two instruments were used. `breathing.py` represents each of $n$ antipodal pairs by a radius and an angle that are periodic, with $H$ harmonics, in a frame turning at a rate $\Omega$, with breathing frequency $\nu$. `breathing_free.py` does the same for single members with no imposed symmetry. A rigid balance is the special case with no harmonics. The delayed equation is imposed at $2H+1$ times per period for one member of each pair, or for every free member, which gives as many conditions as unknowns. A candidate is accepted only if its residual between those times, on a grid four times finer, is also small and its breathing is not negligible. Controls: the planar six-member balance entered with no breathing gives a residual of $6\times10^{-15}$ on both grids in the first instrument and $2\times10^{-14}$ as six free members in the second, and the two instruments agree to $3\times10^{-16}$ on a mirror pair.

## What it means

- **Below wake speed, instability almost always ends at the equation's own boundary, and that boundary is now a theorem on curved paths.** In 274 of 276 runs of the 126 balances below wake speed a member arrived at wake speed within about two periods with every root regular and its speed rising, and from such an arrival no continuation with continuous velocity exists. The assembly question and the continuation question are one question for these histories. The two exceptions disperse.
- **The fastest formal mode is realized.** Four balances grow, under the full delayed equation, at their largest computed rate. The counts of the other roots remain formal.
- **Close approaches are passages.** A member above wake speed can pass another at a few thousandths of the arrangement's size without a collision, because the member it passes does not feel it until the wake of the passage arrives.
- **The simplest motion that is not rigid is excluded by proof**, and wider searches found none.
- **A close passage of a fast member is terminal for a slow one, by a closed law.** The two causal roots born from the passage drive the slower member to wake speed in a time given by the [root-birth law](../../analysis/root-birth-impulse-law.md), provided the passing distance times the passing speed is small in natural units.
- **Not every disturbed balance ends at wake speed.** One three-member rotor with a member above wake speed disperses with both kicks, and a seven-member planar balance below wake speed disperses with one kick of two. In those runs every member stays on its own side of wake speed and the history remains regular for as long as it was followed.
- **Above wake speed the picture is less settled.** With births and mergers resolved, 38 of 55 runs converge; the 17 that do not are the ones with many root changes, mostly among balances with every member above wake speed.

## Limits and falsifiers

The releases are float trajectories from kicks of a few sizes and directions; all 93 balances of the first search and the 61 new balances below wake speed of the second campaign were released, most with only two kicks, and one of the 56 resolved runs above wake speed did not finish. The first three instruments do not resolve root births; the fourth does, and its statements are limited to the 38 runs that converged between two settings. The dispersals were followed only until the members were 25 or 40 sizes apart, and nothing is claimed about what the dispersed members do later. The statement that the released histories meet the theorem's hypotheses is measured. The theorems exclude continuations with continuous velocity, and jumps only within the measure form of the equation. The angular-momentum identity is a hand derivation with a numerical check and one second reading. The searches for motions that are not rigid, and for travelling twelve-member arrangements, are thin.

A released history that returns toward its balance, a continuation with continuous velocity from a curved arrival at wake speed with regular partner rows, a mirror opposite pair below wake speed turning in one sense whose angular momentum decreases at some instant, or a periodic breathing solution in the families searched would overturn the corresponding statement.
