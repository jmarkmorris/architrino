# From cell bounds to the actual compatible history

**Derived conditional continuation argument.** This note supplies the analytical interpretation of the finite certificate for the single unchanged E+M preparation in [the case freeze](authorized-cases-ten-hour-e-method.md). It is not a numerical result, an instability theorem for a family, or a change to the prescribed history. The required inequalities are supplied only by independently assessed, contiguous residual and error receipts.

Let the exact prescribed past be the complete circle-plus-offset history with the exact compatibility patch. It is $C^{2,1}$: its acceleration is continuous and Lipschitz, with permitted jumps in jerk. Suppose a receiving cell $[t_k,t_{k+1}]$ has a trial position/velocity tube in which every partner source window lies strictly below $t_k$, all source and receiving speeds are below a fixed $b<1$, and simultaneous member separation is positive. The actual past through $t_k$ is already fixed. Each partner clock gap is strictly monotone in emission time, with derivative bounded away from zero. Its ancient limit and its positive value at zero delay give exactly one positive-delay root. The same strict-speed history excludes every positive-delay self root by the strict path-length inequality. Thus the prescribed response on the receiving cell has exactly twelve partner contributions and no self contributions.

The source windows are completed history, so the delayed source acceleration is known as a function of emission time during this local continuation. On the first cell it is Lipschitz by the explicit past construction. Inductively it remains Lipschitz on every completed finite interval: the clock roots depend Lipschitz-continuously on reception time and current position, the completed source acceleration is Lipschitz, and the response is smooth in its finite-dimensional arguments wherever range and the clock denominator stay positive. The ordinary first-order system for current position and velocity therefore has a locally Lipschitz right-hand side. A local unique solution exists, and its acceleration is Lipschitz. This regularity persists across receiving cell faces because the response and source acceleration are continuous; the exact release patch gives the required compatibility at zero.

This existence argument uses qualitative finite actual-past Lipschitz constants. The error calculation does not require their numerical values. It splits actual source acceleration from the trial at the same emission time, then transports only the trial acceleration between the two roots. Consequently the quantitative bound uses completed acceleration error and a trial jerk bound, including both sides of every source seam. It does not substitute an assumed bound on unknown actual jerk.

The residual and comparison inequalities hold throughout the whole proposed current tube, not merely at its center. If the resulting uniform position and velocity errors are strictly smaller than the tube caps, a first-exit argument closes that tube: a first boundary time would violate its strict comparison estimate. Positive range, positive clock denominator and strict speed then prevent a local singularity inside the compact finite cell. The solution extends to the full right face. Its uniform position, velocity and acceleration errors become completed-history data for later cells. Induction applies only to contiguous cells with exact shared faces and correctly clipped final reception time; a missing cell cannot be bridged by endpoint data.

No small-gain bound around an exact circle is required in this finite method-of-steps argument. The delayed highest derivative is sampled only at previously completed times with a certified positive delay. Its full error coefficient is retained at every later source hit. A bound such as the exact-circle $q<0.80$ neither replaces this coefficient nor establishes a nonlinear tube.

At an independently admitted observation time, let $L_{\rm trial}$ be a lower bound on the trial RMS distance to the fixed-radius translated/rotated exact square, and let $e_x$ bound all four actual position errors. Distance to a fixed set is 1-Lipschitz, and maximum-member distance dominates RMS distance. Hence the actual maximum-member spatial distance is at least $L_{\rm trial}-e_x$. If this exceeds twice the admitted initial maximum-distance upper bound, the single actual compatible preparation has a finite twofold spatial departure certificate. This conclusion concerns instantaneous configurations only. It does not establish complete-history norm instability, an asymptotic fate, capture, binding or unit-speed arrival.

Falsifiers are a noncompleted source window, failed speed/separation/root domain, missing source acceleration error, failure of the regularity induction, a nonstrict tube comparison, an omitted receiving cell, or an inconsistent observable radius and label orbit. Until the finite directed inequalities and independent output assessment are present, this note remains a conditional interpretation of the method rather than a certificate for the target.
