# What the frozen specification determines at and after the coincidence, and whether the association is more than transient (ceiling-contact open research, problem 6)

**The equation in this record is the field-speed ceiling variant, not the Master Equation.** It is an authorized research variation with the canonical equation retained as baseline. Nothing here adopts it. **This record selects, executes or simulates no contact rule, event map, smoothing, softening or continuation.** Section 4 lists candidate rules as proposals in plain language with their exact specification, for the operator to select or reject; a proposal is not approval, and no proposal was run.

This record addresses obligation 8 of the [contact study](ceiling-contact-two-hour-2026-10-06.md#10-validation-files-remaining-obligations-and-review-status) (the operator's problem 6) under the [launch brief](ceiling-contact-open-research-launch-brief.md) and within its approval boundary. Units $c_f=1$; sizes $0.196735$; $P=1.282228$.

## 1. Result

**At the coincidence instant the frozen specification determines the positions and nothing else; after it, it determines nothing about the pair (derived, conditional on the coincidence, which remains inferred for C1).** On the inferred approach the two members reach one point at one instant $t^\ast$ with their velocities turning without limit (Proposition C of the study, and exactly so on the limiting spiral). The specification's regular solution law requires an absolutely continuous velocity on closed intervals and a radial normal-cone reaction almost everywhere; an absolutely continuous function on a closed interval has a limit at its endpoint, so the velocity of at least one member, and on the spiral of both, admits no extension to $t^\ast$ within the law. The solution exists on $[0,t^\ast)$ and ends there. The ordinary-root ledger is also silent beyond $t^\ast$: every row of the pair on itself has delayed range tending to zero, the rows are unbounded in size, and the positive-delay root geometry after coincidence depends on paths that the law does not supply. So the specification says: positions converge to the meeting point $\mathbf x^\ast$; the velocities have no limits; there is no solution at $t^\ast$ and none after it. "Whether the association of members 0 and 2 is more than transient" is therefore not a question the frozen specification can answer, in either direction: the association it describes lasts exactly until $t^\ast$, and what follows requires a rule it does not contain.

**Decision and correction, 2026-10-20.** After a walkthrough of Section 4 the operator selected proposal P1, terminal coincidence, as the continuation rule, together with recording the finding that the association of two members that reach coincidence is transient under the frozen specification. Proposals P2 to P6, any bond, any softening and any other contact rule are not approved, and selecting P1 launches no run. The approval, its exact scope and its exclusions are recorded in the [variant index](../../equation-variants/README.md) in its paragraph dated 2026-10-20; this note only points there. In the same discussion the operator corrected proposal P3: see the dated correction under the table in Section 4.

**Before the coincidence, nothing but descent is available to the pair near the spiral (derived in the planar symmetric pursuit limit; inferred for the variant).** The brief asked whether histories near the spiral could instead settle onto the variant's exact circular pair at wake speed. They cannot. That circular pair has a fixed absolute size, radius $R=0.2021$, set by the equation with $K=1$, so its separation is $0.404$, about two sizes of C1 and $10^{3}$ to $10^{5}$ times the separations at which C1 is observed on the spiral; and it is unstable in the census's own control (a kick of $10^{-4}$ disperses it). Within the planar exchange-symmetric pursuit limit the [delay-ratio theorem](ceiling-contact-open-research-delay-ratio-bound-2026-10-11.md) proves that every history within $0.15$ of the spiral in the scalar variable converges to it and reaches coincidence in finite time, so no other ending is available there. For the variant itself the study's Theorems A and B give the same conclusion conditionally on the delay-ratio bound, and the measured approach of C1 and C3 holds that bound with margin as far as any integrator goes. There is no second attractor at the pair's scale in the specification: the only bound pair of the variant with both members at the ceiling that is known is the circular one, at a scale the spiral has long passed.

**The rows that leave the coincidence are determined up to it, and they fix part of what any continuation inherits (derived).** The wakes emitted by the pair before $t^\ast$ propagate outward independently of what is decided at $t^\ast$; the [in-plane record](ceiling-contact-open-research-in-plane-degenerate-roots-2026-10-11.md) shows that the dissociating member's motion is determined by them for a time of order its distance from the meeting point ($0.18$ to $0.33\,P$ for C1). The pair's rows on member 1 are of opposite sign and nearly cancel as the pair closes, as $r/\tau^3$; by the time the spiral's last turns reach member 1 their net contribution is below $10^{-6}$ of its earlier rows. Member 1's motion after $t^\ast$ therefore depends on the continuation only through the pair's emissions after $t^\ast$, and those arrive after the determined window.

## 2. The coincidence instant

**What converges.** With both speeds one, $\lVert\mathbf X_i(t)-\mathbf X_i(t')\rVert\le\lvert t-t'\rvert$, so $\mathbf X_i(t)$ is Cauchy as $t\to t^\ast$ and converges; on the inferred approach both limits are the meeting point, which for C1 is at radius $0.1018$ and height $0.1843$ ($0.937$ sizes), inside member 0's original circle (study, Section 8). The emission clocks also converge: $S_i(t)\to t^\ast$.

**What does not.** Proposition C of the study: under the hypotheses of Theorem B up to $t^\ast$ the velocities do not both converge. On the limiting spiral the velocity direction turns by $k\ln(r_1/r)$ between separations $r_1$ and $r$, without bound; neither converges. The turning rate times the separation, $2\sin\alpha=1.743$, is measured on C1 at $1.74334$ from $10^{-5}$ down to $5\times10^{-7}$ sizes by the [implicit integrator](ceiling-contact-open-research-implicit-lock-integrator-2026-10-11.md), which resolves the lock, and on C3 at $1.7434$ at $10^{-3}$ sizes. The acceleration $\dot{\mathbf V}_i$ has size $1.743/r$ and is not integrable in time near $t^\ast$ in the sense required for a limit of $\mathbf V$ (its integral oscillates without converging), while the path length is finite.

**What the regular law requires and does not get.** The [definition's Section 1.3](../../equation-variants/field-speed-ceiling/definition.md#13-the-velocity-constraint-and-response-order) frames the response as an inclusion $\dot{\mathbf V}_i+\mathbf n_i=\mathbf A_i^{\mathrm{ord}}$ with $\mathbf V_i$ absolutely continuous and $\mathbf n_i$ in the normal cone almost everywhere, and its Section 1.4 proves existence of the response for a supplied integrable input on a fixed interval. At $t^\ast$ the input $\mathbf A^{\mathrm{ord}}$ is not integrable (size $1/(\tau^2(1-b))$ with $\tau\to0$ and $1-b\to0.883$), and the output cannot be absolutely continuous on any closed interval containing $t^\ast$. Both hypotheses of the law fail at once. This is a statement about the specification as written; it is not a defect found by numerics and no numerics are needed for it.

**What the ordinary-root ledger says after $t^\ast$, if paths were supplied.** Suppose, hypothetically, that both members were continued from $\mathbf x^\ast$ along some paths. Each member's rows from the other are evaluated at the positive-delay root of $\lvert\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(S)\rvert=T-S$. For $S<t^\ast$ the source is on its spiral at distance $\lVert\mathbf X_j(S)-\mathbf x^\ast\rVert\le t^\ast-S$ from the meeting point; for a receiver that stays within $\epsilon$ of $\mathbf x^\ast$ after $t^\ast$, the left side is at most $t^\ast-S+\epsilon$ against $T-S>t^\ast-S$, so pre-coincidence emissions can still be roots only for $T-t^\ast\le\epsilon$: the spiral's wakes pass the meeting point within the time it takes to cross the receiver's displacement from it. After that, the rows come only from post-coincidence emissions, which the continuation would have to supply. For two members that were exactly coincident with equal velocities on a straight line at speed below one, no positive-delay root exists at all (the chord grows slower than the delay), so such a pair would be causally silent to itself; at speed one on a straight line, every $S$ is a root, the nonordinary inactive family of the definition's Section 2. Rows from member 1 on such a pair would be equal and opposite on its two members (same point, opposite polarity), separating them at once. These facts bound what any continuation could do; they select none.

## 3. Before the coincidence: is anything else available to the associated pair?

Three candidates were examined within the frozen specification. (i) *The variant's exact circular pair at wake speed.* Its radius $R=1/(4\cos D(1+\sin D))$ with $D=\cos D$ is fixed by $K=1$; the separation $2R=0.4042$ exceeds the whole of C1's configuration, and the census control found the circle unstable (dispersal under a $10^{-4}$ kick at $9.1\,P$). Nothing at the spiral's scale can "settle onto" an object of fixed absolute size a thousand times larger; a family of circular pairs at smaller radii does not exist because the balance of the inward row $1/(4R^2\cos D\,(1+\sin D))$ against $1/R$ fixes $R$. (ii) *A wider spiral, or a spiral that stops closing.* In the planar symmetric pursuit limit the scalar variable $c$ is confined to within $0.15$ of $c^\ast$ and converges to it (theorem), so the closing rate $-\dot r\to0.980$ and coincidence follows in finite time: there is no solution of the reduced system near the spiral that stops closing. In the variant, Lemmas 2 to 4 of the study give $\dot r\le-1/\Lambda+12\Lambda r<0$ whenever the delay ratio is bounded, and the measured ratio stays near $1.29$. (iii) *Turning or re-separation.* Not found in any run to $10^{-5}$ sizes in C1, C3 or the nine converted one-rider contacts; in the reduced class it is excluded by the theorem; in the variant it is excluded by Theorem B conditionally on the ratio bound.

So the honest statement is: near the spiral, descent to the common point is the only ending the specification offers the pair, proved in the reduced class, conditional in the variant, and measured in every run.

**Correction to P3, 2026-10-20 (the operator's observation; derived from the ledger clauses).** Two coincident members of opposite polarity receive from every arriving wake contributions that are exactly equal and opposite. The source, the direction, the delayed range and the transmitter factor are the same for both, and only the polarity sign differs. The P3 row reports only the average of the two, which cancels exactly. The difference does not cancel: it accelerates the two members apart at once, with relative acceleration twice either contribution. P3 keeps them together only by discarding that difference, which is a bond the frozen specification does not supply. It therefore has the same defect this table already identifies in P2. A size estimate, inferred and not computed for any run: in C1 the third member's row is about $7$ on each member, so the separating part is about $14$. Once the two separate, their mutual attraction returns, of order $1/r^2$, and exceeds the separating part below a separation of roughly a quarter of a unit. They would therefore fall back together, and the frozen specification would end again at the next meeting. A lasting association therefore needs an explicitly chosen rule at the meeting itself; none is selected.

Of these, P1 is the only one that adds no path and no velocity; it is the statement that the specification's domain ends where the specification ends. P2 and P3 are the two readings of "the association is lasting", and the frozen rows already show that P2 is inconsistent and P3 makes the composite neutral to leading order and silent to itself. P4 to P6 prescribe histories that the law does not produce.

**Correction to the preceding paragraph, 2026-10-20 (derived from the ledger clauses).** Its description of P3 as making the composite "neutral to leading order and silent to itself" omits the separating part. The common part of the other members' rows cancels, but the difference between the two members' rows separates them at once, so P3 is inconsistent for the same reason as P2. The operator has since selected P1; see the dated note in Section 1.
