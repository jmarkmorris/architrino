# Noether Sea Research: Populations, Embedded Assemblies, and Shared Response

## 1. The population being investigated

The Noether sea investigation concerns a populated environment of braids and the response of that environment to embedded assemblies. A braid's internal history, the spatial distribution of many braids, and the resulting collective response are different objects. Establishing one constituent history does not establish a population made from copies of it: each copy receives the histories of the others, and those received contributions can change the internal motion as well as the motion of the population.

The candidate preparation considered here is a dense population of highly energetic scaled braids surrounding regions containing assemblies proposed to represent standard-model particles. This is a research hypothesis, not a derived equilibrium or an accepted retained population. “High energy” must ultimately refer to an accepted account evaluated on those histories. It does not supply an intrinsic mass or license an independent acceleration law. Changing a constituent's geometric scale is also a candidate construction; it does not prove that the scaled history continues to satisfy the same equation in its new surroundings.

**Tiny does not necessarily mean tightly packed.** What matters is assembly size compared with the distance between assemblies. Shrinking both together leaves the relative gaps unchanged. If an orbital envelope has radius $a$ and neighboring assembly centers are separated by $d$, the geometric comparison is $a/d$. Replacing both lengths by the same positive fraction leaves that ratio unchanged. This statement concerns relative geometry; it does not establish that the rescaled histories satisfy the unchanged dynamical equation.

These assemblies have dynamic wake envelopes rather than solid surfaces. Their architrinos emit expanding records of earlier motion, and those wakes can cross gaps and interact strongly even when the orbital envelopes do not touch. The useful picture is therefore interacting regions of organized motion before little balls pressed together. The possibility of interaction across a gap follows from wake propagation; its strength, sign and collective coherence require the actual received histories. Many overlapping wakes do not by themselves establish a coordinated resonance, a pressure law or a retained dense population.

The [scale calculation](analysis/preparation-controls-and-population-scales.md#a-scaled-history-needs-an-equation-check) makes the dynamical restriction explicit: a generic length/time dilation at fixed wake speed changes the coupling. At unchanged constants, the exact alternating-ring family instead has ledger-selected size and speed. Its high-rung radii and periods shrink at different rates; they are branch properties, not a transition law or universal physical scale spectrum.

A matter-containing pocket is a region identified by its assembly population and local environment. It need not be an empty cavity or have a rigid boundary. Ambient braids can occupy the region, cross its boundary, or change their histories in response to the embedded assemblies. A calculation must specify which of those possibilities its preparation admits. No exclusion surface, confining wall, pressure law, or equilibrium is supplied by the word “pocket.”

The present mathematical target is the [shared pressure-dependent constitutive response](analysis/pressure-dependent-noether-sea-constitutive-response.md). A constitutive response describes how measured properties of a populated state change under a specified disturbance. Here it must be obtained from the delayed interaction of complete histories and from accounts defined on that same evolution. It is not an additional primitive medium law.

## 2. A complete preparation has several independent parts

A useful population description must identify both the contents and their histories. The following distinctions organize candidate preparations; they do not assert that a member of any proposed class exists dynamically.

| Part of the preparation | What must be specified | What the specification does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Constituent histories | Persistent architrino identities, polarity, internal braid paths, scale and cadence, and the evidence supporting each constituent | Persistence after immersion in a different environment |
| Population arrangement | Number density, spatial correlations, orientation and phase relationships, and distributions of constituent scales | An equilibrium, a temperature, isotropic transport, or a physical pressure law |
| Complete past | Earlier constituent and ambient histories sufficient to determine every contributing causal reception | Compatibility with the equation throughout that past unless separately proved |
| Embedded assemblies | Their internal histories and the ambient members interacting with them | Standard-model identification merely from geometric resemblance |
| Exterior and boundaries | The surrounding population, supplied incoming histories, and the treatment of any finite computational boundary | Screening, confinement, or disappearance of omitted contributions |
| Equation and summation | The unchanged Master Equation or an explicitly identified alternative, all causal roots, and any infinite-population summation prescription | Unique continuation across a singular event or ordering independence without proof |
| Readout and accounts | The physical observable, averaging scale and window, and accepted wake and assembly accounts on the same record | A conservation law defined only by a residual or a fitted response coefficient |

A homogeneous candidate controls the simplest environmental comparison. An inhomogeneous candidate allows density, orientation, scale or cadence distributions to vary. An embedded-assembly candidate introduces reciprocal interaction with a specified material assembly. These descriptions select different questions; moving among them requires a new preparation and its corresponding causal-history and boundary justification.

An ordered population can be studied as a lattice and as a candidate sea. Lattice research owns questions about the repeated arrangement and its disturbances. Sea research owns the collective ambient response and its reciprocal interaction with embedded assemblies. Both use the same underlying evidence when their preparations coincide. The already studied lattice of individual architrinos is not thereby a population of retained Noether braids.

An infinite preparation needs actual received-root and derivative tail bounds. The [lattice summation results](../lattice-research/manuscript.md#18-the-topology-and-summation-rule-are-part-of-the-result) show why equal-time neutrality or rigid-cell geometry alone cannot provide them. A decaying distant-past envelope is one sufficient proposed history class, not a requirement that every sea realization adopt that class.

The [weak-coupling checkerboard lemma](../lattice-research/analysis/checkerboard-weak-coupling-rate.md) gives a derived coherent linear growth timescale for that bare-architrino control. It does not establish a disordered-sea growth rate, a braid-density stability criterion or a transport speed. Constituent stability and collective response remain distinct.

## 3. Acceleration strength and history playback

The shared response must retain the distinction between how strongly a received source contributes to acceleration and how its emission time changes as reception time advances. For a source emission at time $T_t$ and reception at time $T_r$, let $\hat{\mathbf r}_t$ point from the emission position to the reception position. Let $\mathbf V_t(T_t)$ be source velocity and $\mathbf V_r(T_r)$ receiver velocity. The source and receiver factors are

$$
D_t=c_f-\hat{\mathbf r}_t\cdot\mathbf V_t(T_t),
\qquad
D_r=c_f-\hat{\mathbf r}_t\cdot\mathbf V_r(T_r).
$$

On an ordinary simple causal root, the acceleration weight and signed playback rate are

$$
W^{\mathrm{acc}}=\frac{c_f}{|D_t|},
\qquad
\frac{dT_t}{dT_r}=\frac{D_r}{D_t}.
$$

The first expression enters the acceleration contribution. The second describes which part of the source history is sampled as the receiver advances. A reversed or stationary playback rate does not by itself replace the acceleration weight. The distinction must survive the comparison between an original population and a disturbed one. Numerical instantiations use $c_f=1$.

Every response record consequently needs persistent transmitter and receiver identities, the contributing roots, their sign and separation margins, and the complete retained histories. A finite root census and finite acceleration on one interval support only that interval and that admitted domain. If a branch changes or a singular event occurs, the record must identify the transition instead of treating it as a smooth response of the original state.

## 4. One shared pressure response

Pressure here is a proposed collective descriptor at a declared averaging scale. It is not an architrino-level premise. A pressure-labeled comparison requires a same-record stress or boundary-flux account, dimensional calibration and an explicit change of prepared histories or boundary data. The scientific target is to compare accepted populated records under that operation while retaining the same causal update and account definitions.

The response includes changes in braid density, signal delay, cadence, stress and inertia-related readouts. Those changes must come from one retained population record. Clock, signal, effective-metric, material and cosmological consumers then project that common record into their respective observables. Supplying a separately fitted medium response for each consumer would leave the common constitutive problem unresolved.

The existing response packet specifies one original pressure state, one signed subthreshold perturbation, a wrong-sign or record-mismatch negative control, a smaller pressure step and a tighter numerical refinement. The comparison is meaningful only when the histories, branch identity, causal wake accounts and physical readouts remain tied to the same states. Its finite-difference response is a target for an accepted retained branch; no numerical coefficient is supplied here.

The [kinematic-control proposal](analysis/preparation-controls-and-population-scales.md#proposed-dimensionless-preparation-coordinate) prepares a log-density comparison without applying an undefined primitive pressure. It remains an unselected amendment or companion to the existing v0 target. Effective speed also needs a named disturbance and readout; the [two-station group-delay proposal](analysis/preparation-controls-and-population-scales.md#effective-speed-is-a-channel-readout) preserves the distinction between collective channel delay and primitive wake speed.

A vanishing first response in one disturbance direction would not exclude a higher-order response or a response averaged over periodic driving. Either conclusion would require its own declared disturbance and observable. Conversely, a nonzero isolated-constituent acceleration does not determine the response of a dense population. The environment can change both the source history and the receiving assembly, so an isolated calculation is a control rather than the constitutive result.

The same requirement applies to polarization. A directional photon response must use the orientation or strain record that also supplies the other medium readouts. An isotropic homogeneous limit requires coincident polarization eigenvalues within the stated limiting construction. A directional split, if established, must arise from that same retained state; a separate fitted refractive tensor does not close the shared response target.

Projecting a three-dimensional trace-free strain into the transverse plane can shift both the mean susceptibility and its eigenvalue gap. The [packet decomposition](analysis/pressure-dependent-noether-sea-constitutive-response.md#polarization-resolved-strong-loading-bridge) separates those effects and retains the unresolved anisotropic remainder in the isotropic-limit requirement.

## 5. Present evidence boundary

The constitutive response remains a candidate with status `defer-with-blocker`. The present blocker is an accepted EOM-evolved retained branch that binds the population disturbance, transmitter-side acceleration, signed playback, causal wake accounts and downstream projections to one record. A directory, a prepared geometry, a finite diagnostic, or an adopted averaging notation does not supply that missing branch.

The [Braid Program](../braid-program/manuscript.md) owns qualification of constituent and finite-assembly histories. [Lattice research](../lattice-research/manuscript.md) supplies controlled ordered-population examples at their established grades. The [shared equation account](../manuscript.md) retains the population-domain, continuation and account questions that those applications must satisfy. Observable identification and sector-specific recovery remain with their mapping owners.

The proposed response would fail if a disturbance changed branch identity without a declared transition, if required roots or accounts were missing or mismatched, if playback were used as acceleration strength, or if the response failed the declared refinement comparison. Incompatible coefficients for different consumers of the same state would also refute the claimed shared constitutive description. Agreement with observer-level clock, signal, birefringence, dispersion, preferred-frame and weak-field constraints remains a recovery requirement; those effective laws are not premises for the constituent dynamics.

These boundaries leave a concrete scientific question: whether a specified population of interacting braid histories supports a common, reproducible response that also governs embedded assemblies. The dense energetic preparation and matter-containing pockets state the candidate environment to investigate. They do not yet determine its stability, transport, relaxation or long-time fate.

### 5.1 What the fixed-sphere six-architrino studies establish

The fixed-sphere studies examine one neutral group of three electrinos and three positrinos, the two architrino polarities. Each point is required to remain at a selected distance from a common center. The sphere is a geometric constraint, not a solid shell or an identified physical constituent. The canonical delayed interaction determines motion along the surface; an additional normal acceleration, directed inward or outward along the radius, supplies whatever correction is needed to keep that radius fixed. Recording that correction makes the artificial support visible instead of attributing it to the assembly itself.

Two investigated symmetric arrangements expose different motions. The equatorial arrangement places the six alternating polarities at equal sixty-degree separations around one great circle. It is two interleaved equilateral triangles. In the admitted motion the regular hexagon rotates as a whole, so its architrinos keep equal simultaneous speeds while their common speed changes. The meridional arrangement puts three members of one polarity at a common latitude and one-hundred-twenty-degree longitude separations, with their three opposite-polarity partners at antipodal positions. Their admitted paths follow meridians: the common latitude changes while the longitudes remain fixed. These are selected, symmetric surface motions; they do not establish irregular motion covering the sphere or a completed recurring orbit.

The added [six-pole preparation](analysis/spherical-three-three-six-pole-plan.md) places a positrino at each positive coordinate pole, $(R,0,0)$, $(0,R,0)$ and $(0,0,R)$, and an electrino at each opposite pole. The six positions form a regular octahedron. Each architrino begins with tangent velocity magnitude $1/2$ in normalized wake-speed units, retaining $R=10,K=1,c_f=1$. The selected initial directions compare rotation about the positive diagonal, motion toward opposite diagonal caps, and a pattern in which opposite partners initially move in the same direction. Every pattern has the same initial speed norms but a different complete velocity/history arrangement. A specified smooth great-circle preparation supplies its past; the future remains determined by the delayed equation and normal support. A fourth comparison changes only the positive-x positrino's direction from rotation to cap motion, leaving the other five initial directions unchanged.

For the first three preparations, the [independently adjudicated analytical continuation](analysis/spherical-three-three-six-pole-review-adjudication.md) establishes actual constrained motion through physical time $T=1$. All thirty directed partner roots still sample the supplied stationary poles, and no positive-delay self reception occurs. On this interval the required normal acceleration is strictly inward, with $-7/200<\lambda<-3/200$. Equal speeds persist within each preparation because its complete history has a symmetry that exchanges all six members. Even the same-direction opposite partners retain such a symmetry, although they immediately lose antipodal pairing. The initial octahedron therefore does not alone determine which equalities survive.

The speed changes distinguish these histories. In the cap preparation the common speed starts decreasing at rate $-1/100$. Both rotation preparations have zero first speed derivative and positive second derivative $21/80000$, so their speeds initially rise at quadratic order. These are derived local changes, not a claim of monotonic speed over the whole interval. The rotation preparations have identical scalar speed and support histories while they receive only the stationary past; their later moving histories differ and must be treated separately when received.

The fourth comparison shows why equal initial speed norms are insufficient. Its [independently verified motion](analysis/spherical-three-three-six-pole-dynamics-asymmetric-adjudication.md) exists through physical time $1/10$, with complete stationary-source roots and $-3/125<\lambda_i<-21/1000<0$. The positive-x positrino keeps slowing and stays strictly slower than the other five throughout $0<T\le1/10$. Those five retain equal speed histories on this interval because their individual receiver equations still read the same old stationary pole histories. This equality does not come from symmetry of the entire asymmetric moving group. At release the changed member's speed derivative is $-1/100$, while the others have zero first derivative and positive second derivative. An independently bounded remainder proves the finite-interval ordering, rather than extending an initial Taylor sign without control. Once the recent, different source paths arrive, these equalities need new proof. The initial positions and six speed magnitudes therefore leave important dynamical information unspecified: the tangent directions and complete delayed histories determine which members change speed together.

The distinction between equal speed and constant speed is decisive. Full-history symmetry gives the six members equivalent delayed environments, making their changes of speed equal. It does not make those changes vanish. A normal acceleration is perpendicular to motion along the sphere and therefore cannot cancel the component of the canonical acceleration along a path. If $s_i$ is speed, $\mathbf v_i$ velocity and $\mathbf A_i$ canonical acceleration, then $d(s_i^2/2)/dT=\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf A_i$. This kinematic identity explains why normal support preserves the radius without prescribing speed. The [independently reviewed studies](analysis/spherical-three-three-review.md) construct both increasing-speed equatorial motion and meridional motion that slows, stops and reverses. Small changes to direction, speed or positional phase have separately checked finite-interval effects; no asymptotic stability or chaotic motion follows from those comparisons.

The extended equatorial case uses radius ten, initial speed one quarter and normalized wake speed $c_f=1$, with coupling $K=1$. Its supplied past is stationary before a short smooth preparation, and its future after release is determined by the delayed equation with normal support only. A receiver samples different portions of different partners' pasts. First it receives supplied preparation; later it begins receiving motion generated after release. The [actual continuation proof](analysis/spherical-three-three-hexagon-extension-review-twentyone-reference.md) and [independent adjudication](analysis/spherical-three-three-hexagon-extension-symmetry-twentyone-adjudication.md) establish motion through $T_F+21/20$, where $T_F$ is the first reception of a partner's release-time emission and satisfies $15/2<T_F<9$. Thus the reached interval includes a nonzero duration in which generated motion changes later motion through delayed interaction.

Throughout that admitted feedback interval the common speed increases and the required normal support is strictly inward. With $\lambda$ the signed normal acceleration and positive sign outward, the analytical bounds are $-19/600<\lambda<-187/16000000<0$. All thirty directed partner interactions are included; absence of a positive-delay self reception is proved on the admitted speed domain. Six leading receptions read generated history, while twenty-four nonleading receptions still read stationary history. No additional preparation boundary or support zero is reached there. An earlier outward-to-inward support crossing is separately established, but possible further crossings between the earlier preparation arrival and first generated feedback remain unresolved.

An [independently verified bound on the rate of change of support](analysis/spherical-three-three-preparation-reception-review-sign-adjudication.md) carries that inward certificate an additional physical duration $187/36800$ beyond $T_F+21/20$, with $\lambda<-187/32000000$ on the added interval. The proof combines the already negative endpoint value with the maximum allowed increase of support, including delayed source acceleration and the changing emission times. It bounds support along actual motion already known to exist; it does not prescribe a continuation or infer a sign from unrelated extreme values of speed and radial acceleration. This smaller extension still precedes trailing preparation reception and therefore leaves support at that later reception unresolved.

The [separately reconstructed continuation proof](analysis/spherical-three-three-preparation-reception-review-adjudication.md) extends the same actual hexagon to its first trailing partner's reception of the start of moving preparation, at $T_F+21/20<T_E<11$, and for a further physical time $1/10$ beyond it. All thirty directed partner roots remain ordinary and complete. After this new reception six roots read generated leading motion, six read the exact trailing preparation, and eighteen still read stationary history. The entering preparation matches its stationary past through second derivative, so the received acceleration and signed support have continuous first derivatives; no impulse or additional crossing rule is introduced. The [independently reconstructed support refinement](analysis/spherical-three-three-preparation-reception-symmetry-support-adjudication.md) proves that the canonical radial acceleration remains inward on this enlarged interval. It bounds the additional normal support by $-421/19200<\lambda<359633441/231000000000$. Because this enclosure still spans zero, later support sign and possible additional zeros remain unresolved. An inward canonical acceleration and inward additional support are distinct statements: the latter depends on how the canonical radial component compares with the radial acceleration required by the current speed. The old strict inward-support certificate retains its original domain.

These are independently checked analytical results on finite constrained intervals. They establish how a selected six-member geometry changes under its complete delayed interaction and how much normal support that motion requires. They do not establish a freely confined assembly, a physical source of the support, a relation assigning radius and speed to an energy level, a recurring frequency, a sphere-to-ellipsoid transition or the collective response of a Noether sea. Those questions need their own histories and derivations. A failed root census, unavailable source history, invalid continuation bound or incorrect normal projection would overturn the corresponding finite-interval result.

### 5.2 Curvature, preparation and delayed feedback on a sphere

The broader [curvature investigation](analysis/spherical-curvature-coordination.md) compares six-pole arrangements, alternating and twisted equatorial hexagons, two three-member triangles on opposite latitudes, lifted hexagons, directional perturbations and seeded placements. An alternating hexagon has successive opposite polarities at sixty-degree separations. Twisting changes the relative angular placement of its two polarity triangles. In the latitude arrangements the triangles approach the equator along meridians. Lifting moves the two triangles above and below the equator. These configurations share six members and a spherical surface, but their paths, polarity neighborhoods and complete preparations differ. The [coverage atlas](analysis/spherical-curvature-claude-atlas.md) distinguishes release diagnostics, prescribed histories, actual finite motion and unresolved cells; it is not a collection of qualified persistent assemblies.

Here curvature means the surface's normal curvature, $\mathcal C=1/R$: a smaller sphere has higher curvature, with $\mathcal C=1$ at radius one. This is a geometric research constraint in Euclidean void, not a recovered gravitational curvature. The selected motion keeps only the tangent component of the canonical all-root acceleration. With $\mathbf n_i=\mathbf X_i/R$, $P_i=I-\mathbf n_i\mathbf n_i^{\mathsf T}$ and canonical acceleration $\mathbf A_i$, its Cartesian description is

$$
\mathbf X_i''=P_i\mathbf A_i-\frac{|\mathbf v_i|^2}{R}\mathbf n_i.
$$

The normal term bends a tangent path to follow the sphere. It is geometric bookkeeping required by the fixed radius; it does not restore the discarded radial interaction or provide physical confinement. This equation is algebraically identical to adding exactly the normal correction required in the earlier fixed-sphere studies. No tangent correction holds the speed fixed. Equal speed changes therefore still require symmetry of the complete interacting histories.

**Derived scaling:** measuring length in units of $R$ and time in units of $R/c_f$ leaves the interaction parameter $g=K/(Rc_f^2)$. At fixed $K=c_f=1$, increasing curvature increases $g$. The interaction acceleration scales as $K/R^2$, while the geometric bending at a specified speed scales as $v^2/R$. Shrinking the geometry at fixed coupling consequently changes their relative importance and does not reproduce the same motion at a faster clock. If a repeating branch were qualified, its frequency would have the form $f=(c_f/R)\widehat f(g,\text{configuration,complete history})$. Holding $g$ and the entire dimensionless preparation fixed would give frequency proportional to curvature; keeping $K$ fixed changes $g$, so that proportionality alone is not a dynamical prediction. The familiar great-circle value $v/(2\pi R)$ is a prescribed constant-speed control.

**Derived local distinctions:** the resting six poles are not an equilibrium under tangent interaction, whereas the resting alternating equatorial hexagon is an exact equilibrium. Independent linearizations and a real-axis argument establish that this hexagon is linearly unstable for every positive coupling. A growing oscillatory perturbation has a frequency, but that frequency describes an expanding departure from equilibrium rather than a repeating six-member history. A separate whole-range argument excludes uniform alternating-hexagon rotation at strictly sub-wake speed. It does not exclude every nonlinear recurring pattern. No actual repeating motion was established by this investigation.

Curvature changes whether generated emissions return before a selected motion reaches wake speed. The [rest-release enclosures](analysis/spherical-curvature-claude-review-adjudication-19.md), under their declared numerical arithmetic premise and derived source-domain bounds, establish five configurations at $g=1$ that reach an enclosed wake-speed boundary while their partner roots still read the stationary preparation. These include the six poles and three latitude arrangements. Lifted-hexagon cases instead reach the end of their stationary-source windows below wake speed; their later reversals are not reached results. At weaker coupling the six poles receive their own generated motion before reaching that boundary. Sampled comparisons suggest a switch between these orderings as radius changes; uniqueness of a switch remains a measured observation rather than a theorem.

Wake speed does not supply a primitive speed cap. The [ordinary-chart obstruction](analysis/spherical-curvature-claude-review-adjudication-1.md), with its corrected hypotheses retained in the coordination account, excludes upward crossing for spherical continuations with bounded acceleration, continuous velocity, separated partners and the complete canonical root sum. It does not classify unbounded-acceleration continuations or prescribe an event law. In particular, the selected common-rotation six-pole release at $R=1,v=c_f=1$ has no continuation in that ordinary bounded-acceleration class. A release acceleration evaluation is therefore not evidence of an attained above-wake trajectory.

The strongest accepted weak-coupling feedback comparison uses the six poles released from rest at $g=1/10$. The [independently adjudicated interval construction](analysis/spherical-curvature-claude-review-adjudication-21.md), supplemented by the coordinator's initial delay-domain proof, reaches dimensionless time $1.866684593623$, with speed enclosed by $[0.1887244343,0.1887427709]$. With $K=1,R=10,c_f=1$, this corresponds to physical time $18.66684593623$. These are actual analytical interval bounds under the stated numerical premise, not a trajectory produced by the EOM solver. Both approaching electrinos and receding positrinos are now received from generated history. The net speed excess over the fixed-image comparison remains positive, enclosed by approximately $[1.97,2.16]\times10^{-4}$ at the reached endpoint. Class-selective diagnostics give opposing effects: the approaching unlike partners increase speed, while the receding like partners reduce that increase. Those altered-source comparisons explain contributions; they are not alternative canonical evolutions or a proof that nonlinear feedback has no cross terms. The later antipodal reception remains unreached.

The twisted-hexagon feedback comparison also supports a finite positive speed excess, but its tighter endpoint and upper wake-crossing estimate retain unresolved initial-domain and quadrature-enclosure obligations in the coordinator's account. They are not used here as an enclosed crossing bracket. Some prepared latitude histories turn back in measured comparisons, while their proposed ordering of quarter-turn times remains unproved. These limits matter: surface geometry, short motion, feedback and an unstable oscillatory mode do not establish recurrence, long-term relaxation, an energy ladder or a populated Noether sea. A missing causal root, failed initial delay containment, invalid arithmetic enclosure or inconsistent preparation would overturn the corresponding finite claim.
