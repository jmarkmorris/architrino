Justification (derived): (i) the pair $s=|\mathbf X|^2-1$, $p=\mathbf X\cdot\dot{\mathbf X}$ obeys $s'=2p$, $p'=-s\,(\mathbf X\cdot\mathbf F+|\dot{\mathbf X}|^2)$ with $s(0)=p(0)=0$, so the solution stays on the sphere and the system coincides there with $P\mathbf A-|\mathbf v|^2\mathbf n$; (ii) every partner root lies in the stationary past because, with the speed enclosure below ($|\mathbf v|\le0.50384$) the path length is at most $0.031$ on $[0,0.0611]$, neighbour chords stay above $\sqrt2-0.031>T+\tfrac14$ and separations above $\sqrt2-2(0.031)-0.026>T+\tfrac14$ exclude any root with emission in $(-\tfrac14,T)$; (iii) no self root, by the sphere theorem (speed at most $0.50384<c_f$ on the whole emission window). **Method (explicit, not a stepping loop).** Four Taylor steps, each: (1) Taylor coefficients of the solution at the step's start to order $N$ by exact power-series recursion of the right-hand side ($\mathbf F$ via the series of $(\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf d)^{-3/2}$), in 40-digit arithmetic (`mpmath`); (2) a proven remainder bound from the Cauchy majorant: if $|G|\le M$ on the complex ball of radius $\rho$ about the step's initial state (Hermitian norm on $\mathbb C^6$), the solution is analytic for $|t|<\rho/M$ with values in that ball, so its coefficients satisfy $|c_k|\le\rho(M/\rho)^k$ and the tail after order $N$ on $0\le t\le h=\theta\rho/M$ is at most $\rho\,\theta^{N+1}/(1-\theta)$; $M$ is bounded explicitly by $|\mathbf F|\le K\sum_j(r_j+\rho)(r_j^2-2r_j\rho-\rho^2)^{-3/2}$ (which also keeps $\mathbf d\cdot\mathbf d$ in the right half-plane so the principal branch is analytic), $|\mathbf X|\le1+\rho$, $|\dot{\mathbf X}|\le\tfrac12+\rho$; $\rho$ is chosen on a grid to maximize $\rho/M$, $\theta=0.85$, and $N$ so that the tail is at most $10^{-9}$; (3) between steps the true state lies within $\epsilon_k$ of the computed center, and Gronwall with an explicit Lipschitz bound $L$ for $G$ on the real ball of radius $\rho$ (from $|\nabla\mathbf F|\le2K\sum_j(r_j-\rho)^{-3}$ and block norms) propagates it as $\epsilon_{k+1}=\epsilon_k e^{Lh}+\text{tail}$, with the consistency condition $\theta\rho+\epsilon_ke^{Lh}\le\rho$ checked.
