## (iv) Theorem: no uniformly bounded-acceleration continuation of the reference cell exists from release

### Setting and definitions

Reference cell: six poles G1, common rotation V1, history H1 with $\beta=1$: for $s\le-1/4$ every member is at rest at its pole; for $-1/4\le s\le0$ the receiver of interest (positrino at $\mathbf e_x$, the others by the $\mathbb Z_6$ symmetry) moves on its great circle with angle $\theta(s)=s(1+4s)^3$, so $\theta'(s)=(1+4s)^2(1+16s)=1+24s+144s^2+256s^3$ and $\theta''(s)=24(1+4s)(1+8s)$. On the preparation $|\theta'|\le1$ (equality only at $s=0$), $|\theta''|\le24$, and the preparation acceleration satisfies $|\ddot{\mathbf x}|\le\theta'^2+|\theta''|\le25$. Let $\mathbf x(t)$ be the receiver's position for $t\ge0$ and $\beta(t)=|\mathbf x'(t)|$ its speed, $\beta(0)=1$.

**Ordinary chart on $(0,\epsilon)$ (definition).** A continuation of all six members on $(0,\epsilon)$ such that (O1) each $\mathbf x_i$ is twice differentiable with $\sup_{(0,\epsilon)}|\mathbf x_i''|\le M<\infty$ for one constant $M$ (a uniform bound; a finite acceleration at each positive time that is unbounded as $t\to0^+$ does not satisfy O1); (O2) every admitted root has positive separation, the zero-delay self contact being excluded by the canonical Heaviside convention; (O3) $\mathbf x_i''$ equals the selected law's right-hand side evaluated on the complete history. O1 implies that $\beta$ is Lipschitz with constant $M$ on $[0,\epsilon)$, since $|d\beta/dt|\le|\mathbf x''|$; write $\delta(t)=\max_{[0,t]}(\beta-1)\le Mt$. Put $M'=\max(M,25)$, a uniform acceleration bound across the release seam, where $\mathbf x'$ is continuous.

**Theorem.** There is no ordinary chart on any $(0,\epsilon)$ for the reference cell. Equivalently, every continuation from release either violates O1 or is not generated by the law.

### Proof, branch 1: the speed stays at or below $c_f$ on some $(0,\epsilon)$

Suppose $\beta(t)\le1$ on $(0,\epsilon)$. Self roots are absent: for $u>0$ the member's path over $[t-u,t]$ has length at most $u$ (speed at most one after release, $|\theta'|\le1$ before), and the chord of a curve lying on the sphere is strictly shorter than its length because the straight chord leaves the sphere, so chord $<u$ and the self residual never vanishes. Partner roots are the stationary ones: a partner root with emission $s\in(-1/4,t)$ needs chord $=t-s<t+1/4$, while the chord between two distinct members is at least $\sqrt2-2(27/1024)-2t$ (each member is within $27/1024$ of its site inside the preparation and within $t$ after release), and $\sqrt2-0.0528-2t>t+1/4$ holds for $t<0.370$. So for $t<\min(\epsilon,0.37)$ every root is a stationary-site root with $D_t=1$, and the receiver obeys the reduced equation $\mathbf x''=P\mathbf F(\mathbf x)-\beta^2\mathbf x$ with $\mathbf F$ the static field of the five fixed sites, which is smooth near the pole. Its solution is smooth on the window, and the release derivatives computed in wave-1 Deliverable 4 give $\beta'(0^+)=0$ and $\beta''(0^+)=g^2/\beta+g\beta/8=9/8$. By continuity of $\beta''$, $\beta(t)=1+\tfrac9{16}t^2+o(t^2)$, so $\beta(t)>1$ for all sufficiently small $t>0$, contradicting the branch hypothesis. The case of identically constant speed is included (it would need $\beta''\equiv0$). Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: a chord of a sphere path equal to its length, or a release value $\beta''(0^+)\ne9/8$ (explicit from $|P\mathbf B|=1$, $B_n=-1/4$, $\mathbf x'\cdot D\mathbf B\,\mathbf x'=-\beta^2/8$).

### Proof, branch 2: the speed exceeds $c_f$ at times accumulating at release

Otherwise there are $t_k\to0^+$ with $\beta(t_k)>1$. Fix such a $t$ small enough that $t\le\min\big(0.1,\ 0.2/M,\ \sqrt{0.036/M},\ 1/(2M'(1+\sqrt{M/9}))\big)$; all four thresholds are explicit and are met by every $t_k$ beyond some index. By O1 the speed on $[0,t]$ is at most $1+Mt'$ at time $t'$, so the post-release path length is $L(t)\le t+\tfrac12Mt^2\le1.1\,t\le0.11$ under these thresholds; this, not $t$ itself, is the displacement bound used below.

*Step 1, chord-versus-path remainders on the candidate continuation.* For $0<u\le t+1/4$ consider the window $[t-u,t]$, which may straddle release. Since $\mathbf x'(s)=\mathbf x'(t)-\int_s^t\mathbf x''$ and $|\mathbf x''|\le M'$ throughout,

$$
\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(t-u)=u\,\mathbf x'(t)-\mathbf E(u),\qquad |\mathbf E(u)|\le\tfrac12M'u^2 ,
$$

so the chord obeys $u\beta(t)-\tfrac12M'u^2\le\mathrm{chord}(u)\le u\beta(t)+\tfrac12M'u^2$, and also $\mathrm{chord}(u)\le$ path length. The path length over $[t-u,t]$ is at most $L(t)\le t+\tfrac12Mt^2\le t(1+\delta(t))$ for the post-release part plus the preparation path over $[-w,0]$ with $w=u-t$. For $0\le w\le1/16$ the preparation angle is monotone and the path equals $w(1-4w)^3\le w-12w^2+48w^3$; for $1/16\le w\le1/4$ it is at most $0.0264+(w-1/16)/4\le w-0.036$ (the speed is at most $1/4$ where $\theta'<0$); for $w>1/4$ the emission point is the rest site and the chord is at most the total displacement $t(1+\delta)+27/1024<u$. Hence the self residual $F(u)=\mathrm{chord}(u)-u$ satisfies

$$
F(u)\ \ge\ u\,(\beta(t)-1)-\tfrac12M'u^2>0\qquad\text{for }0<u<2(\beta(t)-1)/M',
$$

$$
F(t+w)\le t\delta(t)-12w^2+48w^3\le t\delta(t)-9w^2\ \ (0\le w\le\tfrac1{16}),\qquad
F(t+w)\le t\delta(t)-0.036<0\ \ (\tfrac1{16}\le w\le\tfrac14),\qquad F(u)<0\ \ (u>t+\tfrac14).
$$

With $\delta\le Mt$ and $t\le\sqrt{0.036/M}$ the middle inequality is strict, and $F(t+w)\le0$ for all $w\ge w^*:=\sqrt{t\delta/9}\le t\sqrt{M/9}\le1/16$. By continuity of $F$ in $u$ there is at least one self root, and every self root lies in $u\in\big(0,\ t(1+\sqrt{M/9})\big]$. This proves, on the actual candidate continuation and with the displayed remainders, that the small-delay self root exists and that all self-root delays tend to zero along $t_k$ (PI refinement 4).

*Step 2, the tangent projection of every self hit is forward.* For a self root $u\le t(1+\sqrt{M/9})\le1/(2M')$, the unit line of action $\hat{\mathbf r}=(\mathbf x(t)-\mathbf x(t-u))/\mathrm{chord}$ satisfies, by the decomposition of Step 1 and $|\mathbf x'(t)|=\beta$,

$$
\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\hat{\mathbf v}(t)\ \ge\ \frac{u\beta^2-\tfrac12M'u^2\beta}{(u\beta+\tfrac12M'u^2)\,\beta}=\frac{\beta-\tfrac12M'u}{\beta+\tfrac12M'u}\ \ge\ \frac{1-1/4}{1+1/4}=\frac35 ,
$$

using $\beta\ge1$ and $M'u\le1/2$. Since $\hat{\mathbf v}$ is tangent, $P\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\hat{\mathbf v}=\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\hat{\mathbf v}\ge3/5$. Every self hit is repulsive ($\sigma_{ii}=+1$) and points from the earlier own position to the present one, so each contributes at least $\tfrac35W/u^2$ to the tangent acceleration along the present velocity; there is no cancellation among self hits, however many there are (PI refinements 1 and 2).

*Step 3, transmitter weight.* The emitting velocity $\mathbf v(t-u)$ has speed at most $1+\delta$ (post-release) or at most $1$ (preparation), so $|D_t|=|1-\mathbf v(t-u)\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}|\le2+\delta$ and $W=1/|D_t|\ge1/(2+\delta)\ge1/3$ for $\delta\le1$.

*Step 4, the partner sum on the same domain.* With every member displaced by at most $L(t)\le0.11$ after release and by at most $27/1024$ inside the preparation, the chord between two distinct members at any time in $[-1/4,t]$ is at least $\sqrt2-0.0528-0.22=1.139$ for neighbours and $2-0.0528-0.22=1.727$ for antipodes. A partner root with emission $s\in(-1/4,t)$ would need chord $=t-s<t+1/4\le0.35<1.139$, impossible; so every partner root is a stationary-site root with $D_t=1$, the receiver-to-site chords are at least $\sqrt2-0.11>1.30$ and $2-0.11>1.89$, and the partner sum has magnitude at most $4/1.30^2+1/1.89^2<2.65$ in every direction (the cruder bound $4/1.139^2+1/1.727^2<3.5$ also suffices and is the one carried below).

*Step 5, contradiction with O1.* Let $u_1$ be a self root supplied by the intermediate value theorem in Step 1 (no attainment of a smallest root is needed); $u_1\le t(1+\sqrt{M/9})$. Every self hit contributes a nonnegative amount along $\hat{\mathbf v}(t)$ by Step 2, so the tangent acceleration along $\hat{\mathbf v}(t)$ is at least the one hit's $\tfrac35\cdot\tfrac13\,u_1^{-2}$ minus the partner bound $3.5$, hence at least

$$
\frac{1}{5\,t^2\,(1+\sqrt{M/9})^2}-3.5 ,
$$

which exceeds $M$ once $t<\big(5(M+3.5)\big)^{-1/2}(1+\sqrt{M/9})^{-1}$; along $t_k\to0$ this violates $|\mathbf x''|\le M$. Both branches are contradictory, so no ordinary chart exists on any $(0,\epsilon)$. $\square$

Claim grade: derived for every step shown (the chord decomposition, the three preparation path bounds, the $3/5$ projection, the weight bound, the partner bound and the final inequality all carry explicit constants). Falsifiers: a self root of the candidate continuation with delay above $t(1+\sqrt{M/9})$ (it would require a preparation path longer than $w-9w^2$ on $w\le1/16$ or longer than $w-0.036$ on $[1/16,1/4]$, both contradicted by the explicit profile); a self hit with $\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\hat{\mathbf v}<3/5$ at delay $u\le1/(2M')$; a partner root with emission inside the preparation while $L(t)\le0.11$.

### What the canonical law does and does not supply here

The per-hit law is stated for admitted roots at positive separation and excludes the zero-delay self contact; it supplies no finite acceleration for a self hit whose separation tends to zero, no regularization, no event rule and no continuation through the onset. The surface-only law adds only the tangent projection and the geometric turning term, neither of which bounds a diverging self hit. Consequently the reference cell's law-generated motion is undefined on every ordinary chart from the release instant onward; this is reported as an exact obstruction and no remedy is added. A continuation whose acceleration is finite at every positive time but unbounded as $t\to0^+$ is not an ordinary chart and is neither constructed nor excluded here; the canonical law provides no rule by which to construct it. Claim grade: derived (a reading of the displayed canonical conventions). Falsifier: a canonical clause supplying a zero-separation self law, which the canonical section explicitly withholds.

## (i) The uniformly rotating alternating hexagon: the azimuthal condition on $0<\beta\le1$

Setting. Six sites at azimuths $k\pi/3$ on the equator, polarity $(-1)^k$, in uniform rotation for all time at dimensionless rate $\omega=\beta$ (unit sphere, $c_f=1$): $\mathbf x_k(t)=(\cos(k\pi/3+\omega t),\sin(k\pi/3+\omega t),0)$. This is a prescribed complete history, an input. For it to be an exact solution of the surface-only law the tangent acceleration must vanish, because uniform great-circle motion has $P\ddot{\mathbf x}=0$: the condition is $P\mathbf B=0$ on the complete root chart, and by the equatorial reflection only the azimuthal component $B_\phi(\omega)$ (along the direction of motion $\mathbf e_\phi$ at the receiver) can be nonzero. The condition is independent of $g$, so a zero $\omega_*$ would give a relative equilibrium at every radius and coupling, the scale-free class recorded in the wave-1 correction companion.

Root census (derived for $\beta\le1$). For partner $k$ the delay solves $u^2=2-2\cos(k\pi/3-\omega u)$; the residual $u-\mathrm{chord}$ has derivative $1-\mathbf v_k\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}\ge1-\beta\ge0$ with equality only at isolated points for $\beta=1$, so each partner has exactly one root on $(0,2]$ (the reviewer's sphere theorem covers the equality case); the self residual $u-2\sin(\omega u/2)$ is positive for $u>0$ when $\omega\le1$, so there is no self root. Transmitter factor $D_t=1-\mathbf v_k(-u)\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}>0$.

## (ii) Second-order speed laws for the hexagon and the antiprism release directions

The six-pole law of wave 1 generalizes to any release site where the stationary field $\mathbf B$, its radial part $B_n$ and the Jacobian $J=D\mathbf B$ are known: with release velocity $\mathbf x'=\beta\hat{\mathbf v}$ tangent, on the stationary-source window

$$
\beta'(0^+)=g\,\hat{\mathbf v}\cdot\mathbf B,\qquad
\beta''(0^+)=\frac{g^2|P\mathbf B|^2-g^2(\hat{\mathbf v}\cdot\mathbf B)^2+g\beta^2\,\hat{\mathbf v}\cdot J\hat{\mathbf v}-g\beta^2B_n}{\beta},
$$

from $\beta\beta'=g\mathbf x'\cdot\mathbf B$ and $\beta\beta''+\beta'^2=g(\mathbf x''\cdot\mathbf B+\mathbf x'\cdot J\mathbf x')$ with $\mathbf x''=gP\mathbf B-\beta^2\mathbf x$. Closed forms (sympy, exact; the numpy instrument reproduces each to $10^{-15}$):

**Hexagon vertex** (receiver at azimuth $0$; tangent basis $\mathbf e_\phi$ along the rotation direction and $\mathbf e_z$): $P\mathbf B=0$, $B_n=-5/4+\sqrt3/3$, $J_{\phi\phi}=19/8+\sqrt3/18$, $J_{zz}=-17/8+2\sqrt3/9$, $J_{\phi z}=0$. For $\hat{\mathbf v}=\cos\chi\,\mathbf e_\phi+\sin\chi\,\mathbf e_z$,

$$
\beta'(0^+)=0,\qquad
\beta''(0^+)=g\beta\Big[\Big(\tfrac{29}8-\tfrac{5\sqrt3}{18}\Big)\cos^2\chi-\Big(\tfrac78+\tfrac{\sqrt3}9\Big)\sin^2\chi\Big]
=g\beta\,[\,3.1439\cos^2\chi-1.0675\sin^2\chi\,].
$$

At $\chi=0$ this is the rotating-hexagon coefficient $gc\beta$ of wave 1 ($J_{\phi\phi}-B_n=c$, checked to $10^{-15}$). At $\chi=\pi/2$ (release along $\pm\mathbf e_z$, every member toward the same pole) the speed is stationary at release and decreases at second order, $\beta''(0^+)=-1.0675\,g\beta$; the pattern "all six toward $+z$" is preserved by the $\pi/3$ rotation with swap and by the vertical reflections (order 12, transitive), so all six speeds stay equal. At $\beta=1$ this is a wake-speed release with zero first-order work and negative second-order work: the speed drops below $c_f$ immediately, so no self root and unique partner roots follow on the stationary-source window and the ordinary chart continues; it is the "zero first derivative, negative second derivative" alternative named in the corrections above, realized. The direction $\chi$ with $\beta''=0$ is $\tan^2\chi=3.1439/1.0675$, $\chi=59.8^\circ$; there the second order vanishes and the third order is not computed.

**Antiprism vertex** ($\alpha=\pi/6$, receiver positrino at azimuth $0$; tangent basis $\mathbf e_\alpha$ pole-ward and $\mathbf e_\phi$): $B_\alpha=-6\sqrt{21}/49-2\sqrt3/9=-0.9460$, $B_\phi=0$, $B_n=5/12-2\sqrt7/7=-0.3393$, $J_{\alpha\alpha}=29/216-31\sqrt7/343=-0.1049$, $J_{\phi\phi}=5/216-4\sqrt7/343=-0.0077$, $J_{\alpha\phi}=0$. For $\hat{\mathbf v}=\cos\chi\,\mathbf e_\alpha+\sin\chi\,\mathbf e_\phi$,

$$
\beta'(0^+)=gB_\alpha\cos\chi=-0.9460\,g\cos\chi,\qquad
\beta''(0^+)=\frac{g^2B_\alpha^2\sin^2\chi}{\beta}+g\beta\big[(J_{\alpha\alpha}-B_n)\cos^2\chi+(J_{\phi\phi}-B_n)\sin^2\chi\big]
=\frac{0.8950\,g^2\sin^2\chi}{\beta}+g\beta\,[\,0.2344\cos^2\chi+0.3316\sin^2\chi\,].
$$

For the meridional pattern V2 ($\chi=0$): $\beta'=-0.9460g$, $\beta''=+0.2344\,g\beta$. For azimuthal release ($\chi=\pi/2$): $\beta'=0$, $\beta''=0.8950g^2/\beta+0.3316g\beta>0$, so an azimuthal (rotating-antiprism) release at $\beta=1$ falls under the transferred obstruction theorem once its own margins are checked (chords $1.323$, $1.5$, $2$; not done here).

Claim grade: derived (closed forms) and measured (instrument agreement). Falsifier: any listed Jacobian entry failing the five-source sum $\sum\sigma_k(I/d_k^3-3\mathbf r_k\mathbf r_k^{\mathsf T}/d_k^5)$ at the stated site.

## (iii) Lower bounds on the first upward wake-speed crossing for the lawful $v=c_f$ cells

For G1 V2 and G3 V2 at $\beta=1$ the speed decreases at first order, so the motion is strictly sub-wake for small $t>0$ and the stationary-source reduction holds on its window (G1: $t<0.37$; G3: chords $\ge1.323$, so $t<(1.323-0.053-0.25)/3=0.34$). On that window write $\varphi(t)=\hat{\mathbf v}\cdot\mathbf B(\mathbf x)$, so $\beta'=g\varphi$. Then $|\varphi'|\le|\hat{\mathbf v}'||\mathbf B|+\|J\|\,\beta$ with $\hat{\mathbf v}'=(\mathbf x''-(\mathbf x''\cdot\hat{\mathbf v})\hat{\mathbf v})/\beta$, $|\mathbf x''|\le g|P\mathbf B|+\beta^2$; before any upward crossing $\beta\le1$, and $\beta\ge\beta_{\min}(T)=1-gM_B(T)\,T$ from $|\beta'|\le gM_B$. With chords to the fixed sites at least $d_k-T$ (path length at most $T$ while $\beta\le1$), $M_B(T)=\sum_k(d_k-T)^{-2}$ bounds $|\mathbf B|$ and $M_J(T)=\sum_k2(d_k-T)^{-3}$ bounds $\|J\|$ (the operator norm of $I/d^3-3\mathbf r\mathbf r^{\mathsf T}/d^5$ is $2/d^3$). Hence $|\varphi'|\le C_1(T):=(gM_B+1)M_B/\beta_{\min}+M_J$ on $[0,T]$, and $\beta'(t)\le g(\varphi(0)+C_1(T)\,t)<0$ for $t<|\varphi(0)|/C_1(T)$. The largest $T$ with $|\varphi(0)|/C_1(T)\ge T$ is therefore a lower bound on the first upward crossing time (the speed is strictly decreasing up to it). Instrument values ($g=1$, scan of $T$ in steps of $2\times10^{-5}$): G1 V2, $\varphi(0)=-1$: $T\ge0.0709$; G3 V2, $\varphi(0)=-0.9460$: $T\ge0.0666$. In both cells the speed is still falling at these times, so these are crude lower bounds, not estimates of the crossing. Claim grade: derived (the inequality chain is explicit; the instrument only evaluates it). The second-order series ($\beta\approx1-gt+\tfrac1{16}gt^2$ for G1 V2) would place a return to $\beta=1$ near $t=16/g$, far outside the stationary-source window; that number is a guessed extrapolation and not a crossing time. Falsifier: a member of either cell whose speed rises back to $c_f$ before the stated bound, which would require $|\varphi'|>C_1$ somewhere on the window.

## (v) Necessary conditions for symmetry-preserving path families, and an audit of the wave-1 classification

Each family below is a set of complete histories closed under a symmetry group; the group forces the motion to stay in the family while the ordinary-chart continuation is unique, and the law then reduces to fewer degrees of freedom. Necessary conditions are stated at exact scopes; nothing about existence or stability is claimed.

**Family A: three antipodal pairs at a common colatitude (polarity-segregated antiprism), azimuths fixed, latitude $\lambda(t)$ free (the meridional antiprism family).** Positrinos at $(\cos\lambda\cos\phi_k,\cos\lambda\sin\phi_k,\sin\lambda)$, $\phi_k=2\pi k/3$, electrinos at the antipodes; velocities meridional. The group (rotations by $2\pi/3$ about $z$, the three vertical reflections through the positrino meridians, inversion with polarity swap; order 12, transitive) is preserved by any history in the family, and the vertical reflection through a member's meridian forces the azimuthal component of its delayed acceleration to vanish identically, so the family is invariant: the law reduces to one scalar delay equation $\lambda''=g\,B_\alpha[\lambda(\cdot)]$ for the common latitude, with all six speeds equal. Necessary conditions: (1) any lawful motion of the family is a solution of that scalar equation on its complete root chart; (2) the family contains exactly one stationary solution, the alternating equatorial hexagon at $\lambda=0$ (at every other latitude the stationary field has a nonzero pole-ward component, see (3)); (3) with stationary sources the pole-ward acceleration $f(\lambda)=\mathbf e_\alpha\cdot\mathbf B$ is negative, that is equator-ward, at every latitude sampled on $(0,\pi/2)$: $-0.0036$ ($\lambda=0.001$), $-0.343$ ($0.1$), $-0.946$ ($\pi/6$), $-1.48$ ($0.9$), $-7.9$ ($1.3$), diverging as the three positrinos approach the pole (minimum chord $\to0$; collision excludes the endpoint), with slope $f'(0)=-3.577\approx-(3+1/\sqrt3)$ at the hexagon (measured by finite difference; the closed form is a guess); so a release from rest anywhere in the family accelerates toward the hexagon, and by inversion symmetry the same holds for $\lambda<0$; (4) a strictly periodic motion of the family would be a scalar oscillation through the hexagon with $\int\lambda'B_\alpha\,dt=0$ over a period and a periodic root chart; the frozen first-turn and first-incoming-preparation results (release pole-ward at speed $1/4$ from $\lambda=\pi/6$, turn below $1/28$ radians, return, then the first incoming preparation at $T_B\in(3/4,1.07)$) are the known lawful motion in this family and, by the law identity, transfer to the surface-only law as recorded there; whether the delayed equation sustains an oscillation is open and is the one recurrence candidate this role would nominate (a scalar delay equation is the cheapest possible object for the review role to continue). Claim grade: derived for (1), (2), the invariance and the equal speeds; measured for the table in (3) (static five-source sum at the listed latitudes, run at 00:34 UTC, values in this section); the oscillation in (4) is not asserted. Falsifier: a latitude in $(0,\pi/2)$ with $f(\lambda)\ge0$, or a nonzero azimuthal component of the delayed sum for a history in the family.

**Family B: rigid rotation about $z$ of Family A at fixed colatitude (including six poles about the diagonal, which is the case $\sin\lambda=1/\sqrt3$).** Prescribed uniform rotation at any rate $\Omega$, including $\Omega=0$, at any $0<\lambda<\pi/2$: excluded as an exact solution on every ordinary complete root chart by the sign lemma re-derived in wave 1 (each admitted hit contributes a nonpositive pole-ward component, strictly negative for a same-ring partner hit, while rigid rotation needs $+\Omega^2\sin\lambda\cos\lambda\ge0$); the lemma needs one admitted same-ring partner root, which the intermediate-value argument on the ring supplies for any rate, so the exclusion covers super-wake rates on ordinary charts as well. At $\lambda=0$ (the hexagon) the lemma is empty and item (i) excludes $0<\beta\le1$; above wake speed the hexagon rotation is unexamined. Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: an admitted hit with positive pole-ward projection under the two-ring geometry.

**Family C: the hexagon with common polar velocities (all six toward $+z$, or all toward $-z$).** Preserved by the $\pi/3$ rotation with swap and the vertical reflections (order 12, transitive); the motion stays a regular hexagon of six equal speeds on a latitude circle that is the same for all members, $z(t)$ common, so it is a one-degree-of-freedom family distinct from Family A (here the positrinos and electrinos move to the same side). Release from the equator: $\beta'=0$, $\beta''=-(7/8+\sqrt3/9)g\beta<0$ by item (ii). Necessary condition for its lawful continuation at $\beta=1$: none beyond the reduced equation, since the speed drops below $c_f$ at second order; this is the realized "zero first derivative, negative second derivative" wake-speed release. Claim grade: derived.

**Audit of the wave-1 classification against these families.** (a) Wave 1 listed G3 V2 as order-12 transitive with meridional confinement: confirmed, and sharpened to the invariant one-dimensional Family A with its scalar delay equation. (b) Wave 1 excluded rigid rotation of G1 about $\mathbf w$ and of G3 about $z$ by the sign lemma: confirmed at the exact scope $0<\lambda<\pi/2$, any rate, ordinary chart with one admitted same-ring partner root; the lemma does not apply to Family A with varying latitude, where the emission latitude differs from the reception latitude, and wave 1 did not claim that. (c) Wave 1 called the rotating hexagon the one open rigid-rotation case: corrected above; it is excluded on $0<\beta\le1$ by the frozen proof plus item (i). (d) Wave 1 stated equal speeds for transitive groups "for as long as a unique continuation exists": unchanged; the theorem of item (iv) shows that for the $\beta=1$ cells V1, V3, V4 and the rotating hexagon that interval is empty on ordinary charts, so the equal-speed statement there is vacuous and the atlas should mark those cells as obstructed at release rather than as symmetric cells. (e) The wave-1 statement that stationary six poles accelerate toward the alternating-hexagon great circle is consistent with Family A only in direction: the six-pole configuration rotated about $\mathbf w$ is a member of Family A with $\sin\lambda=1/\sqrt3$ and azimuths fixed, and its static release acceleration is the Family A value $f(\lambda)$ at that latitude, equator-ward; the hexagon it points toward is the $\lambda=0$ member of the same family. (f) No wave-1 bound is contradicted; the two scope corrections (sufficiency versus necessity for periodicity certification, and the wake-speed sufficiency direction) are recorded at the top of this file.
