## Exact zero curves

For the meridional partner classes the numerator is

$$
N(q,p)=\frac{\cos(q+p)}{\sqrt2}-\frac{\cos q\sin p}{2}=\frac{\cos q\cos p}{\sqrt2}\left[1-\tan p\left(\tan q+\frac1{\sqrt2}\right)\right].
$$

On the controlled chart $0.81\le q\le1.10$, $0<p\le0.85$, the prefactor is positive. Thus the exact sign threshold is $\tan q=\cot p-1/\sqrt2$. Where the right side is positive, the receiver coordinate at reversal is $q_*(p)=\arctan(\cot p-1/\sqrt2)$. Larger receiver or sampled source coordinate moves the numerator toward its negative side. The two meridional classes use the same function but sample different delayed coordinates $p$, so they need not reverse together. At $p=0$, $N=\cos q/\sqrt2>0$ on this chart; the limiting zero lies at $q=\pi/2$ outside it.

The transmitter numerator satisfies $M(q,p)=N(p,q)$. Its zero instead obeys $\tan p=\cot q-1/\sqrt2$. Consequently a tangent reversal and a transmitter factor passing through1 are different geometric events. Positive separation and transmitter denominator leave the per-hit tangent sign equal to the sign of $N$; no causal root is removed by either event.

## Monotonicity on this chart

Direct differentiation gives

$$
\partial_qN=-\frac{\sin q}{\sqrt2}\left(\cos p-\frac{\sin p}{\sqrt2}\right)-\frac{\cos q\sin p}{\sqrt2},\qquad
\partial_pN=-\frac{\cos q\sin p+\sin q\cos p}{\sqrt2}-\frac{\cos q\cos p}{2}.
$$

Both are strictly negative on this chart. In the first expression the remaining coefficient is positive: elementary bounds give $\cos p\ge1-0.85^2/2=0.63875$ and $\sin p/\sqrt2\le0.85/1.4<0.608$, using $\sqrt2>1.4$. Therefore, wherever the actual receiver velocity and sampled source velocity are positive and $ds/dt>0$, $dN(q(t),q(s(t)))/dt<0$. This conclusion concerns the numerator; the contribution's magnitude also depends on its chord and transmitter denominator, and its derivative is not inferred solely from $dN/dt$.

Hand-known checks of the algebra are $N(q,0)=\cos q/\sqrt2$, $N(0,p)=(\cos p-\sin p/\sqrt2)/\sqrt2$, and exact zero at $q=\pi/4$, $p=\arctan[1/(1+1/\sqrt2)]$. These are derived identities, not claims that a detector was run. A wrong sign in the source sine term fails the exact-zero identity.
