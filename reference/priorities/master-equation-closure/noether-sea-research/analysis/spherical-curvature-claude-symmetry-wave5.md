## (a) Census of stationary equilibria: analytic ansätze

A stationary equilibrium of the surface-only law with identically stationary history is a configuration of three positrinos and three electrinos on the sphere at which the tangent part of the static field $\mathbf A_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}(\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_j)/d_{ij}^3$ vanishes for every member. Since $\mathbf A_i=-\nabla_iU$ with the scalar generating function $U=\sum_{i<j}\sigma_{ij}/d_{ij}$ (a mathematical identity for the inverse-square kernel, not an imported energy), an equilibrium is exactly a critical point of $U$ restricted to the product of six unit spheres. $U$ is unbounded below (an unlike pair can approach) and coincident unlike pairs are singular, not equilibria; unboundedness below says nothing about the local character of an equilibrium, which is decided only by the constrained second variation at that equilibrium (see the companion at the end of this section). Each equilibrium found is a lawful base for linearization; nothing about stability follows from existence.

**Two-ring polarity-segregated families (D$_{3d}$ antiprism, D$_{3h}$ prism, every D$_3$ twist between them).** Positrinos on the circle at latitude $\lambda\in(0,\pi/2)$ at azimuths $0,2\pi/3,4\pi/3$, electrinos on the circle at $-\lambda$ at azimuths $\phi,\phi+2\pi/3,\phi+4\pi/3$ for any twist $\phi$. The wave-1 sign lemma applies to any source on either ring: a same-ring (like) source contributes $h\rho(\cos\delta-1)/r^3\le0$ and an opposite-ring (unlike) source $h\rho(-\cos\delta-1)/r^3\le0$ to the pole-ward component at a positrino, with strict negativity from the same-ring partners; so the pole-ward tangent component is strictly negative for every $\lambda\in(0,\pi/2)$ and every twist, and no two-ring polarity-segregated configuration off the equator is an equilibrium. At $\lambda=0$ the two rings merge into a planar D$_3$ hexagon with twist $\phi$; by the vertical-reflection argument its azimuthal component vanishes identically only for $\phi=\pi/3$ (alternating) and $\phi=0$ (coincident, singular). For other twists the azimuthal component at a positrino is $S(\phi)=\sum_{j=0}^{2}G(\phi+2\pi j/3)$ with $G(\delta)=\cos(\delta/2)/(4\sin^2(\delta/2))$ (the two like partners cancel by symmetry); $S$ is odd about $\pi/3$ and $2\pi/3$-periodic, with $S(\pi/3)=0$. Uniqueness of that zero on $(0,2\pi/3)$ is derived from monotonicity: $G'(\delta)=-(1+\cos^2(\delta/2))/(8\sin^3(\delta/2))<0$ on $(0,2\pi)$, and for $\phi\in(0,2\pi/3)$ the three arguments $\phi,\phi+2\pi/3,\phi+4\pi/3$ all lie in $(0,2\pi)$ and increase with $\phi$, so $S$ is a sum of three strictly decreasing functions and has exactly one zero there. The sampled values $S(10^\circ,\dots,50^\circ)=32.7,\,7.99,\,3.32,\,1.59,\,0.668$ are a consistency check, not the proof. So the alternating hexagon is the only equilibrium in the whole two-ring family. Claim grade: derived (sign lemma off the equator; reflection argument and monotonicity on the equator). Falsifier: a $\delta\in(0,2\pi)$ with $G'(\delta)\ge0$ (explicit formula), or a twist $\phi\ne\pi/3$ with $S(\phi)=0$.

**Octahedron family with polarity permutations.** Up to the octahedral group there are two assignments of three positrinos to the six vertices $\pm\mathbf e_a$: (A) a face triangle $\{+x,+y,+z\}$ (the six poles G1), tangent magnitude $1$ at every member (wave 1), not an equilibrium; (B) an antipodal pair plus one vertex, $\{+x,-x,+y\}$ positive and $\{-y,+z,-z\}$ negative, with symmetry C$_{2v}$: the static tangent magnitudes are $0.7071$ at $\pm x$ and $\pm z$ and $0$ at $\pm y$ (direct evaluation), not an equilibrium. Claim grade: measured (explicit static sums at exact positions; the two values are $1/\sqrt2$ and $0$ to machine precision). Falsifier: a third orbit of assignments (there is none: the three positive vertices either contain an antipodal pair or not).

**C$_{3v}$ and C$_{2v}$ two-triangle placements, planar non-alternating hexagons, and everything else** are left to the numerical census below, with the analytic results above as its known controls (the hexagon must be found; the six poles and the octahedral class B must not).

### Companion (2026-10-11 01:01:33 UTC): local character of the hexagon by the constrained second variation

The constrained Hessian of $U$ on the product of spheres at an equilibrium is, per member block, $P_i\big[\nabla_i\nabla_iU-(\mathbf x_i\cdot\nabla_iU)\,I\big]P_i$ (the curvature term comes from the multiplier of the constraint $|\mathbf x_i|=1$, equal to $\mathbf x_i\cdot\nabla_iU$ at stationarity), with cross blocks $P_i\nabla_i\nabla_jU\,P_j$. Since $\mathbf A_i=-\nabla_iU$, this matrix is minus the Jacobian of the tangent static field with respect to tangent displacements followed by renormalization, which is the $12\times12$ matrix the instrument builds. At the alternating hexagon its eigenvalues (numerical, symmetric to $3\times10^{-10}$) are $-7.25$, $-4.528$ (twice), $-1.278$ (twice), $-0.673$, $0$ (three times), $+1.75$ (twice), $+3.577$, and they are exactly the negatives of the static mode sums of wave 4 and of item (b) below (in-plane $m=3,\pm2,\pm1,0$; out-of-plane $m=0,\pm1,\pm2,3$), as they must be, since a mode's static acceleration coefficient is minus its Hessian quadratic form. So the hexagon is a saddle of $U$ on the constraint manifold: three zero directions (rotations), three positive (the Family A latitude mode and the out-of-plane saddle pair) and six negative (all non-rigid in-plane directions and the polar shift). Claim grade: derived for the identification and the mode values, measured for the numerical eigenvalues (central differences, step $10^{-6}$). Falsifier: an eigenvalue of the displayed matrix not equal to minus a static mode sum. For any other equilibrium the local character would need the same computation; none was found.

## (b) Out-of-plane hexagon modes $m=0,\pm1,\pm2$: linearization

For vertical displacements $z_k(t)$ of the six hexagon members the wave-3 geometry applies verbatim: root-time shifts and transmitter-factor changes are second order (vertical displacement is perpendicular to every horizontal line of action), and the receiver's vertical acceleration is

$$
z_0''(t)=g\Big[a_0\,z_0(t)-\sum_{n=1}^{5}\frac{\sigma_n}{\tau_n^3}\,z_n(t-\tau_n)\Big],\qquad a_0=\sum_n\frac{\sigma_n\cos\theta_n}{\tau_n^3}=-\frac78-\frac{\sqrt3}9 ,
$$

the receiver term from its lift and the tilt of its vertical tangent, the delayed terms from each partner's own lift read at emission. Modes $z_k=e^{im\theta_k}e^{\Lambda t}$ give $\Lambda^2=g\big[a_0+\sum_ka_k^{(m)}e^{-\Lambda\tau_k}\big]$ with $b_k\equiv0$ and

$$
a_1^{(m)}=2\cos\tfrac{m\pi}3,\qquad a_2^{(m)}=-\frac{2}{3\sqrt3}\cos\tfrac{2m\pi}3,\qquad a_3^{(m)}=\frac18\cos m\pi .
$$
