## 2. Derivation of the own-antipode moving-source term and of the radial components (grade: derived; unadjudicated)

Path maps (wave 14, inherited): $\mathbf X_1(q)=(\cos q,\,-\sin q/\sqrt2,\,-\sin q/\sqrt2)$, $\mathbf T_1(q)=d\mathbf X_1/dq=(-\sin q,\,-\cos q/\sqrt2,\,-\cos q/\sqrt2)$; the antipode $E_1$ is at $\mathbf Y=-\mathbf X_1(q')$ with $q'=q(t-\tau)$ and velocity $\dot{\mathbf Y}=-v'\mathbf T_1(q')$, $v'=\dot q(t-\tau)$ (the inversion with polarity swap maps the common $q(t)$ onto the electrinos).

Separation vector (emission point to receiver): $\mathbf r=\mathbf X_1(q)+\mathbf X_1(q')=(\cos q+\cos q',\,-(\sin q+\sin q')/\sqrt2,\,-(\sin q+\sin q')/\sqrt2)$. Componentwise, $\mathbf X_1(q)\cdot\mathbf X_1(q')=\cos q\cos q'+\tfrac12\sin q\sin q'+\tfrac12\sin q\sin q'=\cos(q-q')$, so
$$r^2=2+2\cos(q-q')=4\cos^2\!\big(\tfrac{q-q'}{2}\big),\qquad r=2\cos\!\big(\tfrac{q-q'}{2}\big).$$
The chord depends on the **difference** $q-q'$, not on the sum: the two members of the antipodal pair ride the same rotation about the axis $\mathbf e_1\times(\mathbf e_2+\mathbf e_3)/\sqrt2$, so at equal arguments they stay exactly antipodal ($r=2$), and the delayed image lags by $q-q'\ge0$. At $q'=0$ this is the stationary chord $2\cos(q/2)$ of wave 14. (The sentence "chord $2\cos((q+q')/2)$, approaching" at the end of the meridional reference section 6 is therefore not reproduced here; it would be falsified by the componentwise product above at any $q'\ne0$.)

Transmitter factor: $\mathbf T_1(q')\cdot\mathbf X_1(q')=0$ and $\mathbf T_1(q')\cdot\mathbf X_1(q)=-\sin q'\cos q+\tfrac12\cos q'\sin q+\tfrac12\cos q'\sin q=\sin(q-q')$, hence $\dot{\mathbf Y}\cdot\mathbf r=-v'\sin(q-q')$ and
$$D_t=1-\dot{\mathbf Y}\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}=1+\frac{v'\sin(q-q')}{r}=1+v'\sin\!\big(\tfrac{q-q'}{2}\big).$$
For $q>q'$ and $v'>0$ the sign of $D_t-1$ is positive: the delayed antipode **recedes** along the line of sight (weight $1/D_t$ below one), like the positrino class and unlike the nearest-electrino class. At the reading instant ($q'=v'=0$) $D_t=1$.

Tangential hit on $P_1$ (unlike, attracting, $\mathbf A=-\hat{\mathbf r}/(r^2D_t)$): $\mathbf T_1(q)\cdot\mathbf r=\mathbf T_1(q)\cdot\mathbf X_1(q')=-\sin q\cos q'+\cos q\sin q'=-\sin(q-q')$, so
$$a_A(q,q',v')=-\frac{\mathbf T_1\cdot\mathbf r}{r^3D_t}=\frac{\sin(q-q')}{r^3D_t}=\frac{\sin(q-q')}{8\cos^3((q-q')/2)\,D_t}\ \ge 0,$$
which at $q'=v'=0$ is $\sin q/(8\cos^3(q/2))$, the wave-14 fixed antipode term $\sin q/r^3$ with $r=2\cos(q/2)$ (continuity at the reading instant). The lagging attracting image pulls $P_1$ forward along its own great circle.
