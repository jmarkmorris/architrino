## Assumptions to separate and test

Let $s_i(T)=|\mathbf v_i(T)|$, with positive target speed $s$. There are three distinct assertions:

1. All six speeds are equal at a given absolute time: $s_i(T)=s_j(T)$.
2. That common speed is constant in time: $s_i(T)=s$.
3. An energy label $E$ uniquely selects a branch and a pair $R(E),s(E)$.

The first assertion does not imply the second, and neither establishes the third. A permutation/spatial symmetry must preserve polarities, or combine with an admitted global polarity conjugation, and must preserve the entire relevant history and constraint. Positions symmetric at a single instant are insufficient for a delayed equation. A uniqueness argument must state its regular-root domain. A choreography that maps members by a time shift can give the same speed profile at shifted times without simultaneous equal speeds. The symmetry worker must distinguish each possibility rather than infer a conservation law from visual symmetry.

The [Energy chapter](../../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/energy.md#kinetic-energy-and-momentum-of-a-single-architrino) states that the acceleration law does not independently supply a kinetic-energy or momentum functional. Preserve $E\mapsto(R,s)$ as the operator's hypothesis; do not substitute $ms^2/2$, assume quantization, or assign arbitrary numerical energies. Until a compatible history-energy account is supplied, label cases by radius and speed and preserve the energy question as an explicit result of the campaign. Even a valid total-energy value need not uniquely identify geometry or history.

For a closed path of length $L$ traversed at constant speed, its traversal frequency is $f=s/L$. Only a once-traversed great circle gives $f=s/(2\pi R)$. The six-member configuration return period can differ from one member's traversal period; relative returns modulo rotation or permutation must be labeled separately. Irregular motion may have several spectral peaks without a single fundamental frequency. The quantity $s/R$ measures angular distance traveled per absolute time and alone does not determine a general scramble's recurrence frequency.

## Mathematical contract and initial derivations

Fix center $\mathbf C$ and radius $R>0$. For member $i$, define $\mathbf n_i=(\mathbf X_i-\mathbf C)/R$, tangent velocity $\mathbf v_i$, and canonical acceleration $\mathbf A_i$ evaluated on the declared constrained histories. The normal-constraint equation and outward-signed multiplier are

$$
\ddot{\mathbf X}_i=\mathbf A_i+\lambda_i\mathbf n_i,
\qquad
\lambda_i=-\frac{s_i^2}{R}-\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf A_i.
$$

Differentiating the fixed-radius condition twice derives the multiplier. Taking the scalar product with velocity gives

$$
\frac{d}{dT}\frac{s_i^2}{2}=\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf A_i.
$$

Consequently equal constant speed requires $\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf A_i=0$ for every member and every time. This is a kinematic identity, not an assumed kinetic-energy formula. Symmetry could make all six right-hand sides equal without making them zero. Normal support may remain nonzero on a valid constant-speed constrained solution; it is what the fixed-sphere experiment is designed to measure.

For $s_i=s>0$, let $\mathbf t_i=\mathbf v_i/s$. The additional along-path acceleration that would hold speed fixed is $\mu_i\mathbf t_i$, with $\mu_i=-\mathbf t_i\cdot\mathbf A_i$. This is a residual diagnostic only. For an arbitrary prescribed curve, even vanishing $\mu_i$ is insufficient: its sideways turning within the tangent plane must also match the equation. Every nominated trajectory must pass the full vector residual $\ddot{\mathbf X}_i-\mathbf A_i-\lambda_i\mathbf n_i$. The constraint classification therefore distinguishes radial support, along-path support and any remaining mismatch in surface turning.

There is an exact finite history bound for this experiment. If every source history and the receiver remain on this same fixed sphere, their spatial separation is at most $2R$. A causal root $S<T$ obeys $c_f(T-S)=|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(S)|$, hence

$$
0<T-S\le\frac{2R}{c_f}.
$$

Every partner and positive-delay self root therefore lies in that interval. This is a derived, self-reviewed geometric bound, not a numerical cutoff or a proof of finitely many roots. The root count, near-diagonal behavior, transversality, and any accumulation still need analysis. The bound requires the sphere condition on the entire past; if a preparation leaves the sphere earlier, it must supply its own reach bound. Multiple spheres or a translating center require a different bound. This simplification is one reason to study one fixed sphere first.

These initial derivations are inputs for independent reconstruction, not already independently accepted campaign findings.

## Measurements and interpretation

Every evaluated case records member positions and velocities, root inventory and unresolved intervals, minimum separation, normal/tangency errors, full constrained residual, six speed histories, simultaneous speed spread and common-speed drift. Record signed normal support $\lambda_i(T)$, along-path diagnostic $\mu_i(T)$, maxima, RMS values and dimensionless values $R\lambda_i/c_f^2$ and $R\mu_i/c_f^2$. Signed averages alone can hide large oscillatory corrections. Pattern descriptors include directional occupation, pair-separation histories, return defects, rotation/permutation conventions and frequency spectra with declared windows. Equal second moments do not establish uniform spherical occupation.
