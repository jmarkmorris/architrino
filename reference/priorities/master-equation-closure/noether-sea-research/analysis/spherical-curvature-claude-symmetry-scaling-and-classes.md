## Selected law and the quantities this report uses

Six architrinos, three positrinos and three electrinos, move on the sphere of radius $R$ centered at the origin. For member $i$ write $\mathbf X_i(T)$ for its position, $\mathbf v_i=\dot{\mathbf X}_i$ for its velocity, $\mathbf n_i=\mathbf X_i/R$ for the outward unit normal at its position and $P_i=I-\mathbf n_i\mathbf n_i^{\mathsf T}$ for the projector that removes the normal component of a vector. $\mathbf A_i$ is the complete canonical acceleration of the [Master Equation](../../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/master-equation.md#the-master-equation-canonical-form) at receiver $i$: the sum over every transmitter $j$ and every admitted causal root $T_t$ (an emission time with $|\mathbf X_i(T_r)-\mathbf X_j(T_t)|=c_f(T_r-T_t)$, $T_t<T_r$) of the per-hit acceleration $K\sigma_{ij}W_{ij}\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}/r_{ij}^2$, where $K=\kappa|q_iq_j|$ is the common per-pair coefficient, $\sigma_{ij}=+1$ for like polarities (repulsive) and $-1$ for unlike (attractive), $\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}$ points from the emission point to the receiver, $r_{ij}$ is that Euclidean chord, and $W_{ij}=c_f/|D_{t,ij}|$ with $D_{t,ij}=c_f-\mathbf v_j(T_t)\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}$ the transmitter-side factor. The selected surface-only motion is

$$
\ddot{\mathbf X}_i=P_i\mathbf A_i-\frac{|\mathbf v_i|^2}{R}\mathbf n_i .
$$

The first term is the tangent part of the interaction; the second is the turning acceleration a path confined to the sphere must have in Cartesian coordinates, and it is not retained radial interaction. The radial interaction component $(\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf A_i)\mathbf n_i$ is discarded. Dotting with $\mathbf v_i$ and using $\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf n_i=0$ gives the tangent-work identity $\tfrac{d}{dT}\tfrac12|\mathbf v_i|^2=\mathbf v_i\cdot P_i\mathbf A_i=\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf A_i$, so speed changes exactly when the interaction has a component along the velocity. Numerical instances use $c_f=1$ and $K=1$ for the fixed-coupling comparison.

### The surface-only law and the earlier normal-only constraint law generate the same Cartesian motion

The earlier closed campaigns used the normal-only constraint law $\ddot{\mathbf X}_i=\mathbf A_i+\lambda_i\mathbf n_i$ with $\lambda_i=-|\mathbf v_i|^2/R-\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf A_i$ (the [surface-constraint definition](../../equation-variants/surface-constraint/definition.md)). Substituting the support coefficient gives $\mathbf A_i-(\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf A_i)\mathbf n_i-(|\mathbf v_i|^2/R)\mathbf n_i=P_i\mathbf A_i-(|\mathbf v_i|^2/R)\mathbf n_i$, which is the selected surface-only equation term for term. The two laws therefore differ in bookkeeping only: the constraint law records the radial interaction and a support that cancels it, the surface-only law discards the radial interaction and records nothing. The vector $\ddot{\mathbf X}_i$, hence every generated position, velocity, speed, root chart and tangent work, is identical on any common ordinary acceleration chart. What does not transfer is the interpretation of $\lambda_i$ as a support quantity (its sign and size were reported as diagnostics in the earlier campaign and have no counterpart here), and any event or singular-chart treatment, which neither law supplies.

Claim grade: derived. The identity is the one-line substitution above. Falsifier: a state $(\mathbf X_i,\mathbf v_i,\mathbf A_i)$ with $\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf n_i=0$ at which $\mathbf A_i+\lambda_i\mathbf n_i\ne P_i\mathbf A_i-(|\mathbf v_i|^2/R)\mathbf n_i$; none can exist because the difference is identically zero. Consequence for the brief's deliverable 4: the tangent acceleration at release is the same quantity under both laws, and so is the whole acceleration; the earlier finite-window six-pole and hexagon results are mathematically applicable to this law as derived statements about the same ordinary-chart motion, although their claim grades, histories and windows remain exactly as recorded in those frozen files.

## Deliverable 1: nondimensionalization, dimensionless coupling and what "comparable across radii" means

### Dimensionless form

Picture the sphere shrunk to unit radius and the clock rescaled so that a wake crosses one unit of the shrunk length in one unit of time. Write $\mathbf X_i(T)=R\,\mathbf x_i(t)$ and $T=(R/c_f)\,t$, so $t=c_fT/R$ is time measured in wake-crossing times of the radius. A prime denotes $d/dt$. Then

$$
\mathbf v_i=c_f\,\mathbf x_i',\qquad \ddot{\mathbf X}_i=\frac{c_f^2}{R}\mathbf x_i'',\qquad \beta_i=\frac{|\mathbf v_i|}{c_f}=|\mathbf x_i'| .
$$

The causal root equation $|\mathbf X_i(T_r)-\mathbf X_j(T_t)|=c_f(T_r-T_t)$ becomes, after dividing by $R$,

$$
|\mathbf x_i(t_r)-\mathbf x_j(t_t)|=t_r-t_t ,
$$

so a physical delay $\tau=T_r-T_t$ corresponds to the dimensionless delay $u=c_f\tau/R$, and because a chord of the unit sphere is at most $2$, every admitted root has $u\in(0,2]$, that is $\tau\in(0,2R/c_f]$. The transmitter factor is $D_{t,ij}=c_f-\mathbf v_j\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}=c_f(1-\mathbf x_j'\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij})$, so the weight $W_{ij}=1/|1-\mathbf x_j'(t_t)\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}|$ is already dimensionless and depends only on the dimensionless history. The per-hit acceleration is $K\sigma_{ij}W_{ij}\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}/(R^2|\mathbf x_i-\mathbf x_j|^2)$. Define the dimensionless canonical sum

$$
\mathbf B_i=\sum_{j}\sum_{\text{roots}}\sigma_{ij}\,\frac{W_{ij}\,\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}}{|\mathbf x_i(t_r)-\mathbf x_j(t_t)|^2},
\qquad \mathbf A_i=\frac{K}{R^2}\mathbf B_i ,
$$

which is the same signed, weighted inverse-square root sum evaluated on the unit sphere with unit wake speed. Substituting into the selected law and dividing by $c_f^2/R$,

$$
\boxed{\;\mathbf x_i''=g\,P_i\mathbf B_i-|\mathbf x_i'|^2\,\mathbf x_i,\qquad g=\frac{K}{R\,c_f^2}\;}
$$

with $P_i=I-\mathbf x_i\mathbf x_i^{\mathsf T}$ on the unit sphere. The tangent-work identity becomes $\tfrac{d}{dt}\tfrac12|\mathbf x_i'|^2=g\,\mathbf x_i'\cdot\mathbf B_i$. In words: after rescaling, the geometric turning term has coefficient one, the delay structure is fixed by the dimensionless history alone, and the interaction enters through the single number $g$, the ratio of the interaction acceleration scale $K/R^2$ at radius-sized separation to the wake-kinematic acceleration scale $c_f^2/R$. The candidate parameter named in the plan is confirmed: $g=K/(Rc_f^2)$, and nothing else carries $K$ or $R$.

Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: a term in the selected law, after the substitution $\mathbf X=R\mathbf x$, $T=Rt/c_f$, that retains $R$ or $K$ other than through $g$; the displayed line-by-line substitution shows none.

### Comparable motion at fixed $K$ versus similarity, and the fixed-$K$ dilation obstruction

Two meanings of "the same motion at a different radius" must be kept apart.

**Similarity (mathematical comparison, $K$ scaled with $R$).** If $K$ is scaled so that $g$ is unchanged, $K'=aK$ when $R'=aR$ at fixed $c_f$, then the dimensionless problem is literally the same problem: the same $g$, the same dimensionless history, so the same dimensionless solution $\mathbf x_i(t)$ on the same dimensionless root chart. Physically $\mathbf X_i'(T)=a\mathbf X_i(T/a)$: positions dilate by $a$, times dilate by $a$, speeds are unchanged, accelerations divide by $a$, every frequency divides by $a$, so every frequency is proportional to curvature $\mathcal C=1/R$. This is the general scaling $K'=(a^3/b^2)K$ of the earlier frozen scaling note with the root-preserving choice $b=a$ forced by fixed $c_f$; I re-derived it above through the dimensionless form rather than reusing it. The plan requires this comparison to be labeled as similarity and kept separate from the physical comparison, and the atlas should present similarity rows only under that label.

**Fixed $K$ (the physical size comparison).** With $K=1$ and $c_f=1$ the family of radii $\{1/4,1/2,1,2,4,10\}$ is the family of couplings $g\in\{4,2,1,1/2,1/4,1/10\}$ at the same dimensionless history and the same $\beta$. "Comparable motion" then means: the same dimensionless initial data and history, compared across different $g$. It does not mean similar motion, and in general it is not.

**Obstruction (why a fixed-$K$ dilation of an exact solution fails).** Suppose $\mathbf X_i(T)$ is an exact solution on radius $R$ and define the dilated history $\mathbf Y_i(T)=a\mathbf X_i(T/a)$ on radius $aR$ at the same $K$ and $c_f$. Roots are preserved (the chord and the delay both scale by $a$), weights are unchanged, so $\mathbf A_i[\mathbf Y](T)=a^{-2}\mathbf A_i[\mathbf X](T/a)$, while $\ddot{\mathbf Y}_i(T)=a^{-1}\ddot{\mathbf X}_i(T/a)$ and $|\dot{\mathbf Y}_i|^2/(aR)=a^{-1}|\dot{\mathbf X}_i|^2/R$. The tangent residual of the selected law, $\mathbf F_i=P_i(\ddot{\mathbf X}_i-\mathbf A_i)$ (the normal part is satisfied identically by any tangent sphere path), transforms as

$$
\mathbf F_i[\mathbf Y]=\frac1a\mathbf F_i[\mathbf X]+\Big(\frac1a-\frac1{a^2}\Big)P_i\mathbf A_i[\mathbf X] .
$$

If $\mathbf F_i[\mathbf X]=0$, the dilated path solves the dilated problem if and only if $P_i\mathbf A_i=0$ for every member at every time, that is, only when the tangent interaction vanishes identically: each member is at rest or on a constant-speed great circle with zero tangent interaction. For a moving interacting six-member history this fails, so the fixed-$K$ radius comparison is a comparison of genuinely different dynamical problems indexed by $g$. In dimensionless terms the same fact reads: a fixed-$K$ dilation changes $g\to g/a$ while the root chart stays the same, so the equation itself changes.

Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: an exact moving solution with $P_i\mathbf A_i\ne0$ somewhere whose fixed-$K$ dilation is also an exact solution; the displayed residual identity forbids it, so such a case would require an error in the transformation of $\mathbf A_i$ or of $\ddot{\mathbf Y}_i$ above, both of which are one-line calculations.

### The dimensionless history convention that makes a preparation "the same" across radii

A preparation is "the same" across radii when it is one and the same function of the dimensionless variables: $\mathbf x_i(t)$ for $t\le0$ fixed, with $s=t$ the radius-scaled time of the brief. For the H1 family (stationary past joined to the release state along each member's own great circle in its release direction $\mathbf u_i$, a unit tangent at $\mathbf x_i(0)=\mathbf n_i$), the convention used here is

$$
\mathbf x_i(s)=\cos\theta(s)\,\mathbf n_i+\sin\theta(s)\,\mathbf u_i,\qquad
\theta(s)=\begin{cases}0,& s\le-\tfrac14,\\[2pt] \beta\,s\,(1+4s)^3,& -\tfrac14\le s\le0 .\end{cases}
$$

With $y=1+4s\in[0,1]$, $\theta=\beta(y-1)y^3/4$, $\theta'(s)=\beta(1+4s)^2(1+16s)=\beta y^2(4y-3)$ and $\theta''(s)=24\beta(1+4s)(1+8s)=24\beta y(2y-1)$. At $y=0$ all of $\theta,\theta',\theta''$ vanish, so the seam at $s=-1/4$ is $C^2$; $\theta'''(s)=24\beta(12+64s)$ jumps there (from $0$ to $-96\beta$), so the seam is not $C^3$. At release $\theta(0)=0$, $\theta'(0)=\beta$, so the dimensionless speed is $\beta$ and the physical speed is $c_f\beta=v$. On the preparation $\theta'$ ranges over $[-\beta/4,\beta]$ (extremes at $y=1/2$ and $y=1$) and $\theta$ over $[-27\beta/1024,0]$ (extreme at $y=3/4$); the preparation therefore never exceeds the release speed and displaces each member by less than $0.027\beta$ radians from its site. For $\beta=1/2$ this reduces to the frozen six-pole profile $\theta=\tfrac12s(1+4s)^3$, which I checked by substitution. The physical preparation duration is $R/(4c_f)$, so at fixed $K$ larger spheres have proportionally longer preparations, as the convention requires.

The point of the convention is this: with it, the fixed-$K$ radius sweep holds every dimensionless input fixed except $g$, and the similarity sweep holds everything fixed including $g$. A preparation specified instead by a fixed physical duration or a fixed physical displacement would change the dimensionless history with $R$ and mix two effects.

Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: $\theta(0)\ne0$, $\theta'(0)\ne\beta$, or a nonzero value of $\theta$, $\theta'$ or $\theta''$ at $s=-1/4$; each is a polynomial evaluation. A $C^2$ seam suffices for the ordinary root chart because the per-hit law reads transmitter position and velocity only, so the right-hand side of the equation is $C^1$ across the seam; this reasoning is the same as in the frozen first-incoming-preparation note and is re-derived here from the fixed-hit statement of the canonical law.

What each column means for the motion. The coupling $g$ is the acceleration the interaction supplies at radius-sized separation, measured in units of the acceleration $c_f^2/R$ that a wake-speed great circle would need; it is the only number that changes along a fixed-$K$ radius sweep. The delay range states that every causal root on the sphere lies within two radius-crossing times, so the history a receiver reads is never older than $2R/c_f$; compared with the kinematic circuit time $2\pi R/v$, the maximal delay is the fixed fraction $\beta/\pi$ of a circuit, independent of radius, so "how old the information is, in circuits" is a speed property, not a size property. The kinematic control $f_0$ is the frequency of a constant-speed great circle at the release speed; it is proportional to curvature at fixed speed and is an exact solution of the selected law only when the tangent interaction vanishes, so it is a control and not a prediction. The last column is the ratio of the interaction acceleration scale $K/R^2$ to the turning acceleration $v^2/R$ that the sphere already imposes; it equals $g/\beta^2$. Its operational meaning follows from two estimates: the speed change the interaction can produce in one kinematic circuit is of order $(K/R^2)(2\pi R/v)=2\pi c_f\beta\cdot g/\beta^2$ in units of $c_f$... written plainly, the fractional speed change per circuit is of order $2\pi g/\beta^2$; and the angular deflection of the path from its great circle per circuit, which is tangent acceleration perpendicular to the velocity divided by $v$ and integrated over a circuit, is also of order $2\pi g/\beta^2$. So $g/\beta^2$ is the single ordering parameter for how far one circuit carries the motion from free great-circle kinematics. When it is well below one (large sphere, fast members) the motion is geometry-dominated: nearly a free great circle over a circuit, with the interaction a slow drift of speed and heading. When it is of order one or larger (small sphere, slow members) the motion is interaction-dominated: the interaction can reverse or redirect a member within a fraction of a circuit and the kinematic frequency $f_0$ has no predictive content. The actual prefactors depend on geometry; for six poles the release tangent acceleration is exactly $|P\mathbf A|=K/R^2$ (next section), so the scale $K/R^2$ is attained without a geometric factor there.

Regime assignment at $K=1$ by this ratio: $R\in\{1/4,1/2\}$ interaction-dominated at every speed in the set; $R=1$ interaction-dominated at $v\le3/4$ and marginal ($g/\beta^2=1$) at $v=1$; $R=2$ marginal at $v\ge3/4$, interaction-dominated below; $R=4$ geometry-dominated at $v\ge3/4$, marginal at $v=1/2$, interaction-dominated at $v=1/4$; $R=10$ geometry-dominated at $v\ge1/2$ and marginal at $v=1/4$. ## Deliverable 3: symmetry classes of the complete histories

### Why a symmetry of the release state is a symmetry of the whole H1 or H2 history, and why it persists

A candidate symmetry is a triple $(Q,\varepsilon,\pi)$: an orthogonal matrix $Q$, a global polarity sign $\varepsilon=\pm1$ and a label permutation $\pi$, with $Q\mathbf X_i(T)=\mathbf X_{\pi(i)}(T)$ for all history times and $q_{\pi(i)}=\varepsilon q_i$. Under the H1 convention each member's history is the great-circle arc $\cos\theta(s)\mathbf n_i+\sin\theta(s)\mathbf u_i$ with one common profile $\theta$; an orthogonal $Q$ maps the arc of $i$ to the arc through $Q\mathbf n_i$ in direction $Q\mathbf u_i$. Hence $(Q,\varepsilon,\pi)$ preserves the whole H1 history exactly when it preserves the release positions, polarities and velocities, and the same holds for H2 (uniform great-circle motion at the release speed for all $s\le0$). This is why the instrument tests release states only.

Persistence: the canonical kernel is built from chords, delays, the transmitter factor $c_f-\mathbf v_j\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}$ and polarity products, each of which is invariant under a common orthogonal map of all positions and velocities and under a global polarity swap (which leaves every product $q_iq_j$ unchanged); the sphere centered at the origin and the projector $P_i$ are likewise covariant. So if $\mathbf X_i(T)$ solves the selected law with the given history, then $Q\mathbf X_{\pi^{-1}(i)}(T)$ solves it with the transformed history, which is the same history. On any interval where the ordinary-chart continuation is unique, the two solutions coincide, so the symmetry holds for the generated motion as long as that uniqueness holds. The group permutes labels; labels in one orbit have identical speed functions $|\mathbf v_i(T)|$, so a transitive group gives six equal speeds for as long as the continuation is unique. Equal does not mean constant: the common speed changes by the tangent work. A non-transitive group gives equality only within each orbit, and a trivial group gives no equality beyond what the release state happens to have.

Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: a term of the selected law not invariant under a common orthogonal map plus global polarity swap (none exists by inspection of the displayed kernel), or a continuation interval on which two solutions with the same history differ (a uniqueness failure, which the sub-wake method-of-steps argument excludes while every speed is below $c_f$).

## Deliverable 4: release tangent work and speed splitting for the six-pole common rotation V1

### When stationary-source formulas apply at release, and when the preparation's own motion enters

For an H1 history, every member is at rest for $T\le-R/(4c_f)$. At the release instant $T=0$ a receiver at its pole reads partner $j$ at the delay $\tau_j$ solving $|\mathbf X_i(0)-\mathbf X_j(-\tau_j)|=c_f\tau_j$. If $\tau_j\ge R/(4c_f)$ the source was at its stationary site at emission, so the root is $\tau_j=d_{ij}/c_f$ with $d_{ij}$ the chord between sites, $D_t=c_f$, $W=1$. This is self-consistent exactly when $d_{ij}\ge R/4$; for six poles the chords are $\sqrt2R$ (four partners) and $2R$ (the antipode), so every release root is a stationary-source root with delays $\sqrt2R/c_f$ and $2R/c_f$. Uniqueness: the whole H1 history has speed at most $c_f\beta$, and for $\beta<1$ the delay residual $\tau-|\mathbf X_i(0)-\mathbf X_j(-\tau)|/c_f$ is strictly increasing in $\tau$, so the stationary root is the only partner root and no positive-delay self root exists; for $\beta=1$ the preparation speed is below $c_f$ at every $s<0$ and equals $c_f$ only at the release instant, which still gives strict monotonicity for $\tau>0$ and chord strictly shorter than path for the member's own history, so the release census is complete at every speed in the set: five stationary partner roots per receiver, thirty directed roots, no self root. Hence the release field is exactly the stationary-source sum at every release speed in the set.

After release, partner $j$'s root leaves the stationary past when $T-\tau_j(T)>-R/(4c_f)$. Since the receiver has moved at most its path length $c_f\int_0^T\beta$, the chord to the fixed site is at least $\sqrt2R-c_f\beta_{\max}T$, so the stationary-source reduction remains exact at least while $T(1+\beta_{\max})\le(\sqrt2-\tfrac14)R/c_f$; with $\beta_{\max}\le1$ this is $T\le0.58\,R/c_f$, and for the frozen $R=10$, $v=1/2$ cell ($\beta_{\max}<0.53$ on its certified window) it is $T\le7.6$, consistent with that cell's certified unit interval. Falsifier: a chord below $R/4$ in the chosen geometry, or an H1 profile whose speed exceeds $c_f\beta$ inside the preparation.

### Symbolic tangent work in $g$ for V1

In dimensionless variables the tangent work is $\mathcal W_i(t)=g\,\mathbf x_i'\cdot\mathbf B_i=\tfrac{d}{dt}\tfrac12\beta_i^2$; the physical tangent work is $\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf A_i=(c_f^3/R)\,\mathcal W_i$. At release the six-pole field at a positrino is $\mathbf B=-\tfrac14\mathbf e_a-\mathbf c_a$ and the V1 velocity is $\beta\mathbf d_a$ with $\mathbf d_a\perp\mathbf c_a$ (because $\mathbf d_a=\mathbf w\times\mathbf e_a/|\cdot|$ is perpendicular to both $\mathbf w$ and $\mathbf e_a$, hence to their tangent combination $\mathbf c_a$), so

$$
\mathcal W_i(0)=0\quad\text{exactly, for all six members, at every }g\text{ and }\beta .
$$

Differentiating on the stationary-source window, $\mathcal W_i'=g(\mathbf x_i''\cdot\mathbf B_i+\mathbf x_i'\cdot D\mathbf B_i\,\mathbf x_i')$ with $\mathbf x_i''=gP\mathbf B_i-\beta^2\mathbf x_i$, $|P\mathbf B|^2=1$, $\mathbf x\cdot\mathbf B=-\tfrac14$, $\mathbf x'\cdot D\mathbf B\,\mathbf x'=-\beta^2/8$:

$$
\boxed{\;\mathcal W_i(t)=\Big(g^2+\frac{g\beta^2}{8}\Big)t+O(t^2),\qquad
\beta_i(t)=\beta+\frac12\Big(\frac{g^2}{\beta}+\frac{g\beta}{8}\Big)t^2+O(t^3)\;}
$$

for every one of the six members (the transitive $\mathbb Z_6$ symmetry makes them identical, and the closed form shows it directly). Physically $\tfrac{d}{dT}(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf A)|_0=(c_f^4/R^2)(g^2+g\beta^2/8)$ and $v''(0)=(c_f^3/R^2)(g^2/\beta+g\beta/8)$. Both coefficients are positive: the common rotation leaves the speed stationary at release and then raises it, with the $g^2$ term coming from the tangent acceleration doing work on the velocity it creates and the $g\beta^2/8$ term from the receiver's motion through the stationary partner field combined with the discarded-radial bookkeeping ($-\beta^2B_n=+\beta^2/4$) and the Jacobian ($-\beta^2/8$). No speed splitting occurs at any order on this window for V1: the symmetry forbids it.

Evaluations. Reference cell $R=1$, $v=1$, $K=1$ ($g=1$, $\beta=1$): $\mathcal W'(0)=9/8$, $\beta''(0)=9/8$, so $\beta(t)=1+\tfrac9{16}t^2+O(t^3)$; physical values identical since $R=c_f=1$. Frozen comparison cell $R=10$, $v=1/2$ ($g=1/10$, $\beta=1/2$): $\mathcal W'(0)=1/100+1/320=21/1600$, physical $\tfrac{d}{dT}(\mathbf v\cdot\mathbf A)|_0=21/160000$; $\beta''(0)=1/50+1/160=21/800$, physical $v''(0)=21/80000$, so $v(T)=\tfrac12+\tfrac{21}{160000}T^2+O(T^3)$. The last expression is the frozen six-pole reference's series obtained there by direct differentiation of the physical field; the agreement is between two independent derivations (dimensionless closed form here, physical Jacobian there). The instrument reproduces all four numbers to $10^{-12}$.

The brief's claim to verify, "the laws differ in the radial term only, so the tangent acceleration at release is the same quantity", is true and understated: as shown in the opening section the two laws produce the same $\ddot{\mathbf X}_i$ identically, so not only the release tangent acceleration but the entire generated motion, including this series, is common to both laws.

Claim grade: derived (closed forms); measured for the instrument confirmations. Falsifier: a nonzero release tangent work for V1 (requires $\mathbf d_a\cdot\mathbf c_a\ne0$, but $\mathbf d_x\cdot\mathbf c_x=(0,1,-1)\cdot(0,1,1)/2=0$); a value of $v''(0)$ at $R=10$, $v=1/2$ other than $21/80000$.

## Deliverable 5: which frequency statements are lawful

**Derived (kinematic controls).** The constant-speed great circle has period $P_0=2\pi R/v$ and frequency $f_0=v/(2\pi R)=c_f\beta/(2\pi R)$; at fixed speed it is proportional to curvature. It is an exact solution of the selected law only where the tangent interaction vanishes identically along it, which among the atlas families holds for $K=0$ and for the stationary hexagon (frequency $0$); for an interacting moving six-member history it is a control. The maximal delay $2R/c_f$ is the fraction $\beta/\pi$ of $P_0$ at every radius. Under similarity ($K\propto R$, fixed $g$, $\beta$ and dimensionless history) every lawful frequency of a lawful periodic motion scales as $f\propto c_f/R\propto\mathcal C$; this is the only sense in which "frequency proportional to curvature" is derived for interacting motion, and it is a statement about rescaled couplings, not about the fixed-$K$ sweep. At fixed $K$ the dimensionless period $p=c_fP/R$ of any lawful periodic motion is a function $p(g,\beta,\text{history})$, so $fR/c_f=1/p$ changes along the radius sweep unless $p$ happens to be independent of $g$; no such independence is derived.

**Diagnostic only.** A frequency read from a prescribed history (a rigid rotation at rate $\Omega$, an H2 uniform great-circle preparation, a scrambled closed curve) is an input. Its residual against the selected law, $\mathbf F_i=P_i(\ddot{\mathbf X}_i-\mathbf A_i)$ evaluated with the complete root chart of the prescribed history, is the diagnostic; a small residual over a finite window is a near-solution statement at that window's grade, never a solved frequency. The hexagon rigid-rotation condition $B_\phi(\omega)=0$ is of this kind: a zero of $B_\phi$ would make the prescribed rotation an exact solution, and only then would $f=\Omega/(2\pi)$ become a derived frequency of this law.

**Necessary conditions for a strict or relative periodic six-member history** under this law, with period $P$, none of which is asserted to be satisfiable:

1. Tangent work integrates to zero for each member over each period, $\int_T^{T+P}\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf A_i\,dT=0$, because speed returns; the speed may vary within the period and the six speeds need not be equal unless a transitive symmetry holds.
2. The root chart is periodic: for every receiver the multiset of (transmitter, delay, $D_t$) at $T$ and at $T+P$ coincide, including any self roots; counts cannot change across a period without a compensating change back, and every root must remain an admitted positive-separation root throughout.
3. The history read by the law is itself periodic: the equation at time $T$ reads sources on $[T-2R/c_f,T)$, so a motion can be periodic from $T_0$ on only if the sources on $[T_0-2R/c_f,T_0)$ are already the periodic ones. An H1 stationary past is therefore incompatible with strict periodicity starting at release; periodicity could begin at the earliest one maximal delay after the last non-periodic emission, and only after a transient that the law must generate.
4. Relative periodicity (return up to a rotation $Q\in SO(3)$ and a polarity-preserving label permutation) replaces each condition by its $Q$-conjugate: speeds return, the root chart returns up to relabeling, and the history on the trailing delay window is the $Q$-image of the earlier one; the sphere is $SO(3)$-symmetric, so $Q$ costs nothing geometrically, but $Q$ must commute with the polarity assignment under the permutation.

## Deliverable 6: the reference speed $v=c_f$ and what the sub-wake argument loses

No prohibition of super-wake motion is assumed; the law is stated for every speed. What the sub-wake argument supplies, and loses at $v\ge c_f$, is three things. First, partner-root uniqueness: for a history with speed bounded by $c_f\beta_{\max}<c_f$ the delay residual $h(\tau)=\tau-|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(T-\tau)|/c_f$ has $h'\ge1-\beta_{\max}>0$, so each partner has exactly one root; with speed at or above $c_f$ on part of the history $h$ need not be monotone and one partner can contribute several simultaneous hits. Second, self-root exclusion: a positive-delay self root needs the member's own chord over a delay window to equal $c_f$ times the delay, which the inequality chord $\le$ path $<c_f\tau$ forbids when the speed is below $c_f$ on the window; once the speed exceeds $c_f$ the member can outrun its own wake and self roots exist. Third, bounded weights: $W=c_f/|D_t|$ is bounded by $1/(1-\beta_{\max})$ below wake speed; at or above it $D_t$ can vanish (a source moving toward the receiver at exactly the wake speed along the line of action), where the ordinary chart ends. An all-root accounting for a cell at $v\ge c_f$ must therefore enumerate every partner root with its multiplicity (a scan without a uniqueness certificate), search for self roots over delays up to $2R/c_f$, record every $D_t$ sign change or zero, and report the chart as ended at any zero-separation or zero-$D_t$ event.

**At the release instant the reference cell is complete and regular.** For the H1 profile at $\beta=1$ the speed is below $c_f$ at every $s<0$ and equals $c_f$ only at $s=0$, so at $T=0$ the census is the five stationary partner roots with $D_t=c_f$ and no self root (the member's own chord is strictly shorter than its path on the curved sphere). The release field, tangent work and the second-order series of Deliverable 4 are exact at $T=0$.

**Immediately after release the reference cell has no ordinary-chart continuation (exact obstruction).** Take the reference cell G1, V1, H1, $R=K=c_f=1$, $v=1$, and suppose a continuation exists on an ordinary chart on $(0,\epsilon)$, meaning bounded acceleration (so continuous speed) and only positive-separation admitted roots. Either the speed stays at or below $c_f$ on $(0,\epsilon)$ or it exceeds $c_f$ at times accumulating at $0$. In the first case no self root exists (chord strictly less than path on a curved path, path at most $c_f\tau$) and the partner roots remain the stationary ones (their chords stay near $\sqrt2$ while a root from the moving part of a partner's history would need a chord below $\tau<t+\tfrac14$, impossible for small $t$), so the stationary-source reduction is exact and Deliverable 4 gives $\beta(t)=1+\tfrac9{16}t^2+o(t^2)>1$ for small $t>0$, a contradiction. In the second case, at any $t$ with $\beta(t)>1$ the member's chord to its position a short time $u$ earlier exceeds $u$ (chord $\approx\beta(t)u$), while for $u=t+w$ with emission inside the preparation the path length $t+O(\epsilon t)+w-12w^2+\dots$ falls below $u$ once $w\gtrsim\sqrt{\epsilon t/12}$ (the preparation speed is $1+24s+\dots$ just before release), so a self root exists with delay $u(t)\le t+w(t)\to0$ as $t\to0^+$. Its weight satisfies $W\ge c_f/(c_f+|\mathbf v_j|)\ge1/2$ near release and its magnitude is at least $K/(2r^2)=g/(2u^2)\to\infty$, contradicting bounded acceleration. Hence no ordinary-chart continuation exists from the release instant: the reference cell's law-generated motion begins with a self-hit onset of unbounded strength, which the ordinary-chart formula does not continue and for which no event or singular-chart rule is selected. This is the exact obstruction for cell A1 and equally for V3 and V4 (same $\beta'(0)=0$, $\beta''(0)>0$), for the G2 rotating hexagon at $\beta=1$ ($\Phi''(0)=0$, $\Phi'''(0)=gc\beta>0$), and for every member of V5, V6, G4 or G5 at $\beta=1$ whose release tangent work is nonnegative.

**Which $v=c_f$ cells are lawful.** The same argument shows that an upward crossing of wake speed by any member at any time, on any cell, is an ordinary-chart boundary (self-hit onset with delays tending to zero), because only the sign of the speed derivative at the crossing and the continuity of speed were used; the reference cell is the case where the crossing is at release. A $v=c_f$ release is therefore lawful on an ordinary chart exactly when every member's speed decreases at first order at release, so that the motion is strictly sub-wake for small $t>0$ and the sub-wake machinery (unique partner roots, no self roots, bounded weights) resumes immediately.
