## (2) Analysis only: the twisted hexagon ($50^\circ$, $g=1$) past its window end

Setting (wave 8): positrinos at $120k^\circ+\psi(t)$, electrinos at $50^\circ+120k^\circ-\psi(t)$, twist $\Phi=50^\circ-2\psi$; the symmetric family is exact for all time while the ordinary-chart continuation is unique (the order-12 group is preserved by the law), so the motion past the window is still one scalar function $\psi(t)$, now governed by a delay equation. Receiver $P_0$ at azimuth $\psi(t)$. Partner classes and their chords to the initial sites at release: $E_0$ at relative azimuth $50^\circ$ (chord $0.845$), $E_2$ at $290^\circ$ ($1.147$), $P_1,P_2$ at $\pm120^\circ$ ($\sqrt3$), $E_1$ at $170^\circ$ ($1.992$).

**Which roots read moving history, in what order.** A partner root reads post-release history when its emission time $t-\tau$ is positive, that is $\tau<t$; since $\tau$ equals the chord to the delayed partner position, the class with the smallest chord enters first. $E_0$ approaches the receiver (its relative azimuth is $\Phi$, decreasing), so its root enters first, at $T_w=0.679$ ($g=1$), where $\tau=t$ and the chord to the $E_0$ site equals the elapsed time. The others recede or hold their distance: $E_2$'s relative azimuth is $290^\circ-2\psi$, its chord $2\sin(35^\circ+\psi)$ increasing, so its root cannot read post-release history before $t\ge1.147$; $P_1$ and $P_2$ keep relative azimuth $\pm120^\circ$ with the receiver at equal times, and their delayed chord $2\sin\big((120^\circ+\psi(t-\tau)-\psi(t))/2\big)$ stays within a few per cent of $\sqrt3$ while $\psi$ varies by less than $10^\circ$ over a delay, so their roots enter near $t\approx\sqrt3$; $E_1$ at $170^\circ-2\psi$ has chord $2\sin(85^\circ-\psi)$, entering near $t\approx1.95$. Order: $E_0$ first (certain), then no further class before $t=1.147$ (derived from the receding chord of $E_2$ and the near-constant chords of the others, under the standing sub-wave assumption), then $E_2$ or the like pair depending on the actual $\psi$, then $E_1$.

**The root equation for the delayed azimuth.** For $t\in[T_w,T_w+\Delta]$ the $E_0$ root has emission time $t-\tau\in[0,T_w]$ (inside the window solution) with $\tau$ solving the chord condition

$$
\tau=2\sin\!\Big(\frac{\delta_0}{2}\Big),\qquad \delta_0=50^\circ-\psi(t)-\psi(t-\tau),
$$

the relative azimuth of the delayed $E_0$ image being $50^\circ-\psi(t-\tau)$ minus the receiver's present azimuth $\psi(t)$. The root is unique while the history is sub-wave: the residual $\tau-2\sin(\delta_0/2)$ has derivative $1-\cos(\delta_0/2)\,\psi'(t-\tau)\ge1-|\psi'|>0$ (the $E_0$ image moves at the receiver's own earlier speed, by the mirror symmetry).

**The transmitter factor from the delayed velocity.** $E_0$ moves clockwise at azimuthal rate $-\psi'(t-\tau)$ at emission; by the in-plane geometry of wave 4 ($\mathbf v\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}=-\omega_s\cos(\delta/2)$ for a source of azimuthal rate $\omega_s$ at relative azimuth $\delta$), $D_t=1-\psi'(t-\tau)\cos(\delta_0/2)$: an approaching source compresses, $D_t<1$, $W>1$.

**The right-hand side.** With the kernel $-\sigma G(\delta)$, $G(\delta)=\cos(\delta/2)/(4\sin^2(\delta/2))$, and the other four images still at their initial sites (relative azimuths $170^\circ-\psi$, $290^\circ-\psi$ for the electrinos, $120^\circ-\psi$, $240^\circ-\psi$ for the positrinos), the exact equation on $[T_w,T_w+\Delta]$ is

$$
\psi''(t)=g\left[\frac{G\big(50^\circ-\psi(t)-\psi(t-\tau)\big)}{1-\psi'(t-\tau)\cos\!\big(\tfrac{50^\circ-\psi(t)-\psi(t-\tau)}{2}\big)}+G(170^\circ-\psi)+G(290^\circ-\psi)-G(120^\circ-\psi)-G(240^\circ-\psi)\right],
$$

with $\tau=\tau(t)$ from the root equation; the delayed term reads $\psi(t)$, $\psi(t-\tau)$ and $\psi'(t-\tau)$ as the PI asked, the remaining terms read $\psi(t)$ only. At $t=T_w$ the delayed term equals the fixed-image term it replaces (the delayed image is at its site with zero velocity), so the right-hand side is continuous across the window end, and its first derivative is continuous too (the image's velocity is $O(t-T_w)$).

**What a method-of-steps continuation needs.** Enclosed input: the window solution $\psi$ and $\psi'$ on $[0,T_w]$ as interval functions (the wave-8/11 brackets give $\psi(t)$ at any $t$ to the envelope width, and $\psi'$ through the first integral), together with the root enclosure $\tau(t)$ obtained by interval bisection of the chord condition; then on each step the equation above is an ordinary differential equation in $\psi$ with the delayed quantities read from the enclosed input, so a certified continuation is an enclosure of that ODE (by an analytic bound on the right-hand side and its Lipschitz constant, not by an integrator). Allowed step: the delayed argument must stay inside the enclosed input, $t-\tau(t)\le T_w$, which with $\tau\ge\tau(T_w)=0.679$ decreasing slowly holds for $\Delta\le0.6$; the next root class must not enter, which holds for $t<1.147$, that is $\Delta\le0.468$; and the speed must stay below $c_f$: on the fixed-image reduction the speed would reach $1$ at $t=0.864$ ($\Delta=0.185$), and the delayed term with $W>1$ pulls harder than the fixed image, so unless the enclosure shows otherwise the wake crossing bounds the certified step to $\Delta\lesssim0.19$ at $g=1$ (the obstruction theorem then ends the ordinary chart). So the chord conditions allow $\Delta=0.468$ before the root classes change, but the sub-wave condition is the binding one at this coupling; at $g=1/10$, where the speed at the window end is $0.057$, the chord condition would bind instead. Claim grade: derived (the equation, the root condition, the transmitter factor, the entry order of $E_0$ and the $1.147$ bound); inferred for the step limit from the wake crossing (it uses the fixed-image reduction as a lower bound on the pull, which is justified by $W>1$ and the geometry but not enclosed here). Falsifier: a root of another class with emission time above $0$ before $t=1.147$ (impossible by the chord bounds while $\psi$ is monotone and sub-wave), or a value of $D_t$ above $1$ for the approaching $E_0$ image.
