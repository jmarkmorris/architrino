### A.2 Wake-speed surface root reference (coordination lines 40–51)

Codex's claim 1 (chord geometry and the transmitter-factor bound). Receiver at $\mathbf X_r$ and source emission point $\mathbf X_t=R\mathbf n_t$ on the same sphere, chord length $d>0$, $\hat{\mathbf r}=(\mathbf X_r-\mathbf X_t)/d$. Re-derivation: $\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\mathbf n_t=(\mathbf X_r\cdot\mathbf n_t-R)/d=R(\cos\gamma-1)/d$ with $\cos\gamma=\mathbf n_r\cdot\mathbf n_t$; since $d^2=2R^2(1-\cos\gamma)$, this equals $-d/(2R)$. Hence the tangent projection of $\hat{\mathbf r}$ at the source has length $\sqrt{1-d^2/(4R^2)}$. If the source velocity at the emission time is tangent there ($\mathbf V_t\cdot\mathbf n_t=0$, true for any history confined to the sphere) and $|\mathbf V_t|\le c_f$, then $\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\mathbf V_t=(P_t\hat{\mathbf r})\cdot\mathbf V_t\le c_f\sqrt{1-d^2/(4R^2)}$ by Cauchy–Schwarz, so

$$
c_f\Bigl(1-\sqrt{1-\tfrac{d^2}{4R^2}}\Bigr)\ \le\ D_t\ \le\ c_f\Bigl(1+\sqrt{1-\tfrac{d^2}{4R^2}}\Bigr),
$$

and the lower bound is strictly positive for $d>0$. Verdict: correct, with the upper bound added here. Consequence for the weight: $1/(1+\sqrt{1-d^2/4R^2})\le c_f/|D_t|\le1/(1-\sqrt{1-d^2/4R^2})$. At a root $d=c_f\tau$, so both bounds are functions of the delay alone. At the antipodal chord $d=2R$ the chord direction is exactly normal at the source, so $D_t=c_f$ and the weight is exactly $1$ whatever the tangent velocity.

Codex's claim 2 (monotone residual and one partner root). Re-derivation in the frozen reference's variables: $f(\tau)=\tau-|\mathbf X_r(T)-\mathbf X_t(T-\tau)|/c_f$ has, wherever the chord is positive and the history differentiable, $f'(\tau)=D_t/c_f\ge0$ under claim 1; the Lipschitz form of reference 3.2 covers the rest. (The excerpt does not state the sign convention of its "causal residual"; with residual $c_f(T_r-T_t)-r$ as a function of emission time the derivative is $-D_t\le0$, "nonincreasing in emission time", which is the same content.) At any root $\tau_*>0$ the chord is $d_*=c_f\tau_*>0$; the chord $d(\tau)$ is continuous in $\tau$ because positions are continuous, so on a neighbourhood of $\tau_*$ the bound of claim 1 gives $f'\ge\delta>0$ almost everywhere, with $\delta=1-\sqrt{1-d_*^2/(16R^2)}$ for instance on the sub-neighbourhood where $d\ge d_*/2$. Hence the root is isolated and, $f$ being nondecreasing on $[0,2R/c_f]$ with $f(0)<0$ (noncoincident) and $f(2R/c_f)\ge0$, the zero set is a single point: exactly one partner root. Verdict: correct. Sharpening: the strict-decrease-near-the-root step needs only continuity of positions, not of velocities, because the bound depends on the chord alone; so the argument survives velocity jumps (a $C^0$ seam) as long as speed stays at most $c_f$.

Codex's claim 3 (no positive-delay self root). Re-derivation: a self root needs $|\mathbf X(T)-\mathbf X(T-\tau)|=c_f\tau$ with $\tau>0$. The chord is at most the path length, which is $\int_{T-\tau}^T|\mathbf V|\le c_f\tau$; equality throughout needs speed $c_f$ almost everywhere and chord equal to path length, which holds only for a straight segment traversed monotonically; a straight segment of positive length does not lie on a sphere. Verdict: correct, and the argument needs only a rectifiable on-sphere path with speed at most $c_f$. It is stronger than reference 3.3's "chord shorter than arc at constant speed exactly $c_f$", which assumed constant speed.

What this certifies, precisely. For a reception time $T$ and a receiver $i$: if every transmitter's history on the retained window $[T-2R/c_f,T]$ lies on the sphere and has speed at most $c_f$ (equality included) throughout that window, then the root census at $T$ is exactly one root per noncoincident partner and no self root, and at each partner root with chord $d$ the transmitter weight lies in $[1/(1+\sqrt{1-d^2/4R^2}),\,1/(1-\sqrt{1-d^2/4R^2})]$. Reference 3.2–3.3 certified uniqueness only for $v_{\max}<c_f$ and listed $D_t=0$ folds as possible at $v=c_f$; on the sphere such a fold would need $|P_t\hat{\mathbf r}|=1$, that is $d=0$, which no noncoincident root has. So the Codex reference sharpens reference 3.3 to $v_{\max}\le c_f$ for on-sphere histories, and sharpens the common brief's statement that the sub-wake completeness argument "does not apply at or above wake speed": it does apply at wake speed on the sphere; it fails above wake speed. Reference 3.3 is not falsified, only conservative, and its super-wake statements (self root existence by the intermediate value theorem once $|\mathbf V_i(T)|>c_f$; possible multiple partner roots) stand.

What it does not certify. (a) It is a statement about the complete retained window, not the present state: a speed bound at $T$ alone certifies nothing. (b) The lower bound on $D_t$ vanishes as the chord tends to zero; without a proved positive minimum chord over the window there is no uniform weight bound, and a near-coincidence makes the weight large though finite. (c) Generated post-release histories: the selected law caps nothing, and reference 4.3 shows release states with positive tangent work at $v=c_f$ (V2 and copied-V2 positrinos, $d|\mathbf v|/dT=+K/R^2$), so speed exceeds $c_f$ immediately after release in those cells; from then on the certificate lapses for every reception time whose window contains a super-wake segment of the transmitter, and a self root of the super-wake member exists (reference 3.3). (d) Histories that leave the sphere, including numerically drifted ones, lose the tangency step of claim 1. (e) It certifies counts and weight bounds, not root locations or acceleration enclosures; those need the interval recipe of reference 3.4 or the stationary shortcut.

Claim grade: derived (each Codex claim re-derived above from sphere geometry and the canonical $D_t$; agreement of two separately authored derivations). Falsifier: an on-sphere history with speed at most $c_f$ on the retained window exhibiting two admitted roots for one ordered pair, a positive-delay self root, or a root with $D_t<c_f(1-\sqrt{1-d^2/4R^2})$.

### D.2 Section 1.4: the 41 percent figure

Codex is right that the local expansion $|\mathbf X|^2=R^2+|\mathbf v_0|^2T^2+O(T^3)$ does not by itself give a finite-time number for an interacting path. Correct scoping, with the remainder handled exactly: under $\mathbf X''=P\mathbf A$ (no normal term, $P$ removing the component along $\mathbf X$), $\tfrac{d^2}{dT^2}\tfrac12|\mathbf X|^2=|\mathbf v|^2+\mathbf X\cdot P\mathbf A=|\mathbf v|^2$, so with $\mathbf X(0)\cdot\mathbf v(0)=0$,

$$
|\mathbf X(T)|^2=R^2+2\int_0^T (T-t)\,|\mathbf v(t)|^2\,dt\ \ge\ R^2+v_{\min}^2T^2,\qquad v_{\min}=\min_{[0,T]}|\mathbf v| .
$$

So the departure is monotone and convex for every path, interacting or not, and the path never returns to the sphere while it moves. The figure "41 percent at $T=1$" ($|\mathbf X|=\sqrt2R$ at $R=1$, $|\mathbf v_0|=1$) is exact only for the zero-interaction case, where $\mathbf v$ is constant and the path is the straight line $\mathbf X_0+\mathbf v_0T$; for an interacting path the exact lower bound above replaces it, with $v_{\min}$ taken from the generated speed history (which the tangent work of reference 1.5 governs and which is not known in advance). The second-order departure statement of reference 1.4 stands unchanged.

Claim grade: derived (the integral identity is two integrations of an exact second derivative). Falsifier: a moving solution of $\mathbf X''=P\mathbf A$ with $|\mathbf X(T)|^2<R^2+v_{\min}^2T^2$ for some $T>0$.
