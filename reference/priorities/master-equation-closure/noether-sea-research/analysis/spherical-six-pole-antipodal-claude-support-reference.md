## 1. Own-antipode geometry (derived)

Paths (inherited, wave 14): $P_1$ at $\mathbf X_1(q)=\cos q\,\mathbf e_1-\sin q\,\mathbf u$ with $\mathbf u=(\mathbf e_2+\mathbf e_3)/\sqrt2$, unit tangent $\mathbf T_1(q)=-\sin q\,\mathbf e_1-\cos q\,\mathbf u$; the antipode $E_1$ at $-\mathbf X_1(q')$ with $q'=q(t-\tau)$ and velocity $-v'\mathbf T_1(q')$, $v'=\dot q(t-\tau)$. Both points are images of one rotation of the $(\mathbf e_1,\mathbf u)$ plane: $-\mathbf X_1(q')$ is the rotation by $q'$ of $-\mathbf e_1$ in the same sense in which $\mathbf X_1(q)$ is the rotation by $q$ of $\mathbf e_1$. Emitter-to-receiver vector $\mathbf r=\mathbf X_1(q)+\mathbf X_1(q')$. Componentwise, $\mathbf X_1(q)\cdot\mathbf X_1(q')=\cos q\cos q'+(\sin q\sin q')(\tfrac12+\tfrac12)=\cos(q-q')$, so $r^2=2+2\cos(q-q')=4\cos^2\tfrac{q-q'}2$ and the chord is $r=2\cos\tfrac{q-q'}2$. **The antipodal chord depends on the difference $q-q'$, not on the sum.** The expression "$2\cos((q+q')/2)$" is wrong for these path maps: with $q'=q$ the two points are exact antipodes and the chord must be the diameter $2$, which $2\cos\tfrac{q-q'}2$ gives and $2\cos\tfrac{q+q'}2$ does not. Root condition: $\tau=2\cos\tfrac{q(t)-q(t-\tau)}2$.

Transmitter factor. $\mathbf T_1(q')\cdot\mathbf X_1(q)=-\sin q'\cos q+\cos q'\sin q=\sin(q-q')$ and $\mathbf T_1(q')\cdot\mathbf X_1(q')=0$, so $\mathbf V\cdot\mathbf r=-v'\sin(q-q')$ and $\mathbf V\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}=-v'\sin(q-q')/(2\cos\tfrac{q-q'}2)=-v'\sin\tfrac{q-q'}2$. Hence $D_t=1-\mathbf V\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}=1+v'\sin\tfrac{q-q'}2$. For $q>q'$ and $v'>0$ this is **greater than one: the delayed antipode recedes along the line of sight** (weight $c_f/D_t$ below one), the same side of one as the like class and the opposite of the unlike meridional class. The meridional reference's "approaching" is therefore wrong for the own antipode. Consistency: with $h(\tau)=\tau-r$, $\partial_{q'}r=\sin\tfrac{q-q'}2$ and $dq'/d\tau=-v'$ give $dh/d\tau=1+v'\sin\tfrac{q-q'}2=D_t>0$, so the sub-wake root is unique while $q\ge q'$ and $v'\ge0$ (derived).

Tangential hit on $P_1$ (unlike, attracting, $\mathbf A=-\hat{\mathbf r}/(r^2D_t)$): $\mathbf T_1(q)\cdot\mathbf X_1(q')=-\sin q\cos q'+\cos q\sin q'=-\sin(q-q')$, so $a^{\rm ant}_{\rm tan}=\mathbf T_1\cdot\mathbf A=\sin(q-q')/(r^3D_t)=\dfrac{\sin\tfrac{q-q'}2}{4\cos^2\tfrac{q-q'}2\,D_t}$, positive (forward, along $+\mathbf T_1$, the direction of motion) for $q>q'$: the delayed image sits behind the exact antipode along the great circle, and the short arc to it runs forward. Radial hit, outward positive with $\mathbf n=\mathbf X_1(q)$: $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r=1+\cos(q-q')=r^2/2$, so $a^{\rm ant}_{\rm rad}=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A=-\dfrac1{2rD_t}=-\dfrac1{4\cos\tfrac{q-q'}2\,D_t}$, inward. Continuity: at $q'=v'=0$ these become $\sin\tfrac q2/(4\cos^2\tfrac q2)$ and $-1/(4\cos\tfrac q2)$ with chord $2\cos\tfrac q2$, which are exactly the fixed $E_1(-\mathbf e_1)$ contributions of the wave-14 field (checked numerically, section 5). Equal-angle limit $q'=q$: chord $2$, $D_t=1$ for every $v'$, $a_{\rm tan}=0$, $a_{\rm rad}=-1/4$ (the exact antipode exerts no tangential acceleration and the full inward pull $K/(2R)^2$).

## 2. Radial components for every source class (derived)

General identity on the unit sphere: for any emission point $\mathbf Y$ on the sphere, $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r=1-\mathbf X_1\cdot\mathbf Y$ and $r^2=2-2\mathbf X_1\cdot\mathbf Y$, so $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf r=r^2/2$ exactly. With the canonical hit $\mathbf A=\sigma_{\rm hit}\hat{\mathbf r}/(r^2D_t)$ ($\sigma_{\rm hit}=+1$ like, $-1$ unlike), **every per-hit radial component is $a_{\rm rad}=\sigma_{\rm hit}/(2rD_t)$**: like hits push outward, unlike hits pull inward, each with weight $1/(2rD_t)$. This holds for fixed and moving forms alike; only $r$ and $D_t$ change. Meridional classes (chords and $D_t$ as in the wave-17 header, re-derived componentwise in the instrument's direct vector route): like $P_2,P_3$ at $+\mathbf X_2(q')$, $r_L^2=2+\sqrt2\sin(q+q')-\sin q\sin q'$, $D_L=1+v'M/r_L$, $a_{\rm rad}=+1/(2r_LD_L)$ each; unlike $E_2,E_3$ at $-\mathbf X_2(q')$, $r_U^2=2-\sqrt2\sin(q+q')+\sin q\sin q'$, $D_U=1-v'M/r_U$, $a_{\rm rad}=-1/(2r_UD_U)$ each, with $M=\cos(q+q')/\sqrt2-\sin q\cos q'/2$; antipode as in section 1. Fixed forms: $q'=v'=0$, $D=1$, $r_L=\sqrt{2+\sqrt2\sin q}$, $r_U=\sqrt{2-\sqrt2\sin q}$, $r_A=2\cos\tfrac q2$.

Sum, with each class at its own delayed state: $a_{\rm rad}(t)=g\Big[\dfrac{2}{2r_LD_L}-\dfrac{2}{2r_UD_U}-\dfrac1{2r_AD_A}\Big]=g\Big[\dfrac1{r_LD_L}-\dfrac1{r_UD_U}-\dfrac1{2r_AD_A}\Big]$. Signed additional normal support, outward positive: $\hat\lambda=-\dot q^2-a_{\rm rad}$ (from $\mathbf n\cdot\ddot{\mathbf X}=-|\mathbf v|^2/R$ in physical units, $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A+\lambda=-|\mathbf v|^2/R$, made dimensionless by $c_f^2/R$); physical $\lambda=(c_f^2/R)\hat\lambda=\hat\lambda/10$. Release value ($q=0$, rest): $r_L=r_U=\sqrt2$, $r_A=2$, all $D=1$, so the four nearest images cancel radially and $a_{\rm rad}(0)=g(-1/4)=-1/40$ exactly, $\hat\lambda(0)=+1/40$, physical $\lambda(0)=1/400=K/(2R)^2$: at release the sphere must push outward by exactly the antipode's inward pull. Sign of $a_{\rm rad}$ on the admitted range (derived): for $0<q<\pi/2$, $r_L>r_U$ so the fixed like-minus-unlike pair is inward; the moving corrections ($D_L>1$ weakens the outward like term, $D_U<1$ strengthens the inward unlike term, $D_A>1$ only weakens the inward antipode term without changing its sign) keep $a_{\rm rad}<0$ wherever every $D_t>0$. So the canonical radial component is inward throughout; by itself this does **not** fix the sign of $\hat\lambda$, which is the competition $|a_{\rm rad}|-\dot q^2$.

## 3. Seam continuity (derived)

Each moving term reads its class's release emission ($s=0$) at the reading instant, where the delayed state is $q'=0$, $v'=0$ (rest for $s\le0$), so $r$ and $D_t=1$ coincide with the fixed form and every per-hit $a_{\rm rad}=\sigma_{\rm hit}/(2r)$ is continuous there; the seam of the antipode class is the instant where $\tau=2\cos\tfrac{q(t)}2$ first holds with $q'=0$, i.e. $2\cos\tfrac{q(t)}2-t=0$. Since $\dot q$ is continuous (the law is second order), $a_{\rm rad}$ and $\hat\lambda$ are continuous at every seam; their time derivatives jump because $d(q',v')/dt$ switches from zero to $(1-\dot\tau)(v',\ddot q')$ there (the same mechanism as the tangential seams of adjudications 19 to 21). Checked numerically for the antipode at $q=0.17$ (section 5, control 3).

## 4. Symmetry and a Noether-type identity (derived)

The order-12 group (cyclic axis permutation, the $\mathbf e_2\leftrightarrow\mathbf e_3$ mirror fixing $P_1$'s path, inversion with polarity swap) commutes with the law and with the radial projection $\mathbf n\cdot$ (it maps unit normals to unit normals and preserves chords, polarity products and $D_t$), so with one common $q(t)$ the sum $a_{\rm rad}$ and hence $\hat\lambda$ are identical for all six members; the per-class contributions permute among themselves (for $E_1$ the like class is $E_2,E_3$, the unlike class $P_2,P_3$, the antipode $P_1$) with the same numbers. Tangent-work identity: $\tfrac{d}{dt}\tfrac{\dot q^2}2=\dot q\,a_{\rm tan}$ with $a_{\rm tan}=g[\ldots]$ the selected tangential law; it constrains $\dot q^2$ (hence the bending term) but says nothing about $a_{\rm rad}$, which the selected law discards. On the fixed-image window it reduces to $\dot q^2=2gU(q)$, which makes $\hat\lambda$ a function of $q$ alone there, $\hat\lambda=g[-2U(q)-S_{\rm fix}(q)]$, used in section 6. No conservation law fixes the sign of $\hat\lambda$: it is a measured competition, not a Noether charge. The symmetry does give one exact identity, that the net radial component at $q=0$ of the four nearest images vanishes by the $r_L=r_U$ coincidence at release, which is why $\hat\lambda(0)$ is the antipode term alone.
