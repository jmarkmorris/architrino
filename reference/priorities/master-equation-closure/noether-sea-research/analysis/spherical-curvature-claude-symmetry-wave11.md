## Method: certified step envelopes and the bounds they give

On $[0,Q]$ along the receiver's great circle, split into $N=200$ sub-intervals; on each, a Lipschitz constant $L_k=\sum_j(1/d_{jk}^2+2/d_{jk}^3)$ from the per-image chord minima on that sub-interval and a $21$-point grid give certified bounds $f_{\mathrm{lb},k}\le f\le f_{\mathrm{ub},k}$ (wave 10's inequality). The step envelopes $W_{\rm lb}(q)=\sum_kf_{\mathrm{lb},k}\Delta q_k\le W(q)-W(0)\le W_{\rm ub}(q)$ then give, because the time integrand $1/\sqrt{2g(W-W(0))}$ is decreasing in $W$, two-sided bounds on the time to any position, each a closed-form sum over sub-intervals (on a sub-interval $W$ is linear in $q$ and $\int dq/\sqrt{2gW}=2(\sqrt{W_{\rm end}}-\sqrt{W_{\rm start}})/(f_k\sqrt{2g})$): $t_{\rm lb}(q)\le t(q)\le t_{\rm ub}(q)$. Three derived consequences:

1. **Wake-speed crossing.** $q_c$ is fixed by $W(q_c)-W(0)=1/(2g)$, so $q_c\in[q_{c,\rm lb},q_{c,\rm ub}]$ with $W_{\rm ub}(q_{c,\rm lb})=1/(2g)=W_{\rm lb}(q_{c,\rm ub})$, and $t_c\in[t_{\rm lb}(q_{c,\rm lb}),\ t_{\rm ub}(q_{c,\rm ub})]$. The upper end is the PI's "quadrature with the lower-bound field", applied up to the upper end of the position bracket.
2. **Window end, lower bound.** $q(T)\le q_{\rm ub}(T)$ (the inverse of $t_{\rm lb}$), and the running minimum $D(q)=\min_{q'\le q}d_{\min}(q')$ is non-increasing, so $d_{\min}(q(T))\ge D(q_{\rm ub}(T))$ and $T_w\ge T^*$ with $t_{\rm lb}(q^*)=D(q^*)$, $T^*=t_{\rm lb}(q^*)$. (The plain $d_{\min}$ is not monotone for the $30^\circ$ and $45^\circ$ antiprisms, where the nearest image changes along the path; the running minimum repairs the argument at the cost of a looser bound there.)
3. **Window end, upper bound, and "no crossing before the window end".** $q(T)\ge q_{\rm lb}(T)$ (inverse of $t_{\rm ub}$), and $E(q)=\max_{q'\in[q,Q]}d_{\min}(q')$ is non-increasing, so $T_w\le T^{**}$ with $t_{\rm ub}(q^{**})=E(q^{**})$. If moreover the speed bound $\sqrt{2gW_{\rm ub}(q_{\rm ub}(T^{**}))}$ is below $1$, no wake crossing occurs before the window end.

Controls recorded before the targets, all passed: the constant-field toy gives $t_{\rm lb}\le\sqrt{2q/(gf_0)}\le t_{\rm ub}$ with width below $10^{-7}$ and $q_{c,\rm ub}=1/(2gf_0)$ to $10^{-7}$; the stationary hexagon's envelope straddles zero at release; negative control: an envelope built from a polarity-flipped case violates the true $W$ bounds (violation $>10^{-3}$). Claim grade: derived for the three consequences (each is a monotonicity argument on displayed inequalities); the envelope numbers are measured evaluations of derived bounds. **Item 1 — sampled chord minima and floating error (companion to wave 10's certificates).** Wave 10's Lipschitz certificates, and revision 3 of this wave's envelopes, took the minimum chord to each image over the *grid* and used it as the minimum over the continuous sub-interval; those certificates are therefore conditional as written. Repair, in two separated parts. Theorem part: along a unit-rate great circle every chord $d_j(q)=|\mathbf x(q)-\mathbf s_j|$ is $1$-Lipschitz in $q$ ($|dd_j/dq|\le|\mathbf x'|=1$), so on a sub-interval sampled with spacing $h$ the continuous minimum is at least the grid minimum minus $h/2$; with these positive continuous lower bounds $d_j^*$, $L=\sum_j(1/d_j^{*2}+2/d_j^{*3})$ is a Lipschitz constant of $f$ on the sub-interval and $\min_{\rm grid}f-Lh/2\le f\le\max_{\rm grid}f+Lh/2$ holds between samples. Floating part, a stated model rather than a theorem: each grid value of $f$ is a sum of five terms, each bounded by $1/d_j^{*2}$ and computed in a few dozen double-precision operations; the evaluation error is modeled as $E_f=200\,\epsilon_{\rm mach}\sum_j1/d_j^{*2}$ ($\epsilon_{\rm mach}=2.2\times10^{-16}$, that is $200$ ulps of each term's magnitude bound), and every certified bound is $\min_{\rm grid}f-Lh/2-E_f$ and $\max_{\rm grid}f+Lh/2+E_f$. The same $1$-Lipschitz property makes the running minimum of $d_{\min}$ over the $2000$-point $q$-grid minus half its spacing a continuous lower bound, and the running maximum plus half the spacing a continuous upper bound, which items 2–3 use.
