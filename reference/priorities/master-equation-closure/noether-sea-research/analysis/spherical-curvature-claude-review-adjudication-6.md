## 1. Which equation is exact on the stationary-source window

Re-derivation. For $T$ inside the stationary-source window every admitted root of every member has emission time $T-\tau\le0$, so each source is read at its initial site, at rest, with $D_t=1$. The receiver $P_0$ at azimuth $\psi(T)$ is therefore accelerated by the static field of the five fixed initial sites (electrinos at $50^\circ,170^\circ,290^\circ$; positrinos at $120^\circ,240^\circ$), evaluated at the receiver's current position. With the per-hit azimuthal kernel of the frozen moving reference at zero speed, $\sigma$-signed $G(\delta)=\cos(\delta/2)/(4\sin^2(\delta/2))$ for a source at relative azimuth $\delta$ (positive toward an unlike source), the exact window equation is the autonomous one-degree-of-freedom ODE

$$
\psi''=g\,f(\psi),\qquad f(\psi)=\sum_{a\in\{50^\circ,170^\circ,290^\circ\}}G(a-\psi)-\sum_{a\in\{120^\circ,240^\circ\}}G(a-\psi),
$$

with $f(0)=S(50^\circ)=0.668065$; the like-partner terms cancel only at $\psi=0$. The quasi-static form with the current twist, $\psi''=gS(50^\circ-2\psi)$ (equivalently $\Phi''=-2gS(\Phi)$), which the review's frozen challenge reference displayed as the reduced equation, is WRONG on the window: it moves the images with the receiver, whereas the law reads them where they were. The subject is right. Ruling: fixed images, derived (the review re-derived it above; the review's own check reproduces $f(0)=0.668065$ and $f=dW/d\psi$ to $10^{-10}$ at five points). What survives of the frozen reference's D1 entry: the symmetry group and the one-parameter family (derived, unchanged); the release census and release acceleration $\pm S(50^\circ)$ (derived, unchanged, since the two equations coincide at $\psi=0$); the statement that the twist decreases from release (unchanged, now from $f>0$); the bootstrap window bound (re-justified below). Falsifier: a window root reading an emission with $s>0$ (by definition outside the window), or a like-partner azimuthal contribution that vanishes for $\psi\ne0$ (it does not: $G(120^\circ-\psi)+G(240^\circ-\psi)\ne0$ for $\psi\ne0$).

## 3. The window length

Criterion. For $T$ in the window the unique root of each partner (sub-wake, sphere certificate) is the chord from the receiver's current position to the partner's fixed site, which is a stationary-past root iff that chord is at least $T$; the first failure, by symmetry and by the monotone decrease of the nearest-image chord $2\sin((50^\circ-\psi)/2)$ (the next chords, to $E$ at $170^\circ$ and $P$ at $120^\circ$, stay above $1.5$), is the nearest electrino image, so the window end is exactly the first $T_w$ with $T_w=2\sin((50^\circ-\psi(T_w))/2)$: derived (the monotone root-residual argument gives that for $T<T_w$ every root reads $s\le0$ and at $T_w$ one root's emission reaches $s=0$). The subject's statement that this is a hard boundary and that nothing past it is derived from the reduction is correct. Values: $T_w=0.6791$ at $g=1$ (twist $29.40^\circ$, speed $0.619$; review check $0.67908$, $29.40^\circ$, $0.6187$) and $0.8242$ at $g=1/10$ (twist $47.34^\circ$; review check $0.82417$, $47.34^\circ$): measured (two instruments, direct integration at tolerance $10^{-12}$, agreeing to four digits); not certified, because no validated enclosure of $\psi(t)$ or of the quadrature was produced. Certified: the review's bootstrap bound $T_s\ge0.331$ (re-justified above), and the sub-wake census on the whole window (speed at most $0.62<c_f$ by the measured values, at most $0.53$ on the certified part). A validated version (interval quadrature of the first integral, or the review's Taylor-majorant method on this one-degree-of-freedom ODE) is the bounded step that would make $0.6791$ a certified window end. Falsifier: a $T_w$ not solving the displayed equation on an independent integration, or a root with emission $s>0$ before $T_w$.

## 4. The singular endpoint at twist zero

The subject's statement, that the coincidence of a positrino with its nearer electrino is a singular endpoint of the law because with sub-wake relative motion the pair's mutual root has delay at most $d/(1-\beta_{\max})\to0$ as the present separation $d\to0$ (chord to the partner's recent position at most $d$ plus path length $\beta\tau$, so $\tau\le d+\beta\tau$), its per-hit magnitude diverges as $1/d^2$, and the canonical law supplies no zero-separation or event rule: derived as a conditional statement about any approach to coincidence. What is not derived: that the actual motion approaches coincidence at all, since twist zero lies beyond the window ($t=0.959$ against $T_w=0.679$ at $g=1$; $3.03$ against $0.82$ at $g=1/10$); after $T_w$ the law is a delay equation on the symmetric family (the group keeps the motion in the family while the continuation is unique) whose continuation is open. The reduction's finite value at $\psi=25^\circ$ is a property of the fixed-image ODE, not of the law, as the subject says. Grade: derived (conditional endpoint statement), open (whether it is reached). Falsifier: a lawful continuation past $T_w$ on which the twist stops decreasing.
