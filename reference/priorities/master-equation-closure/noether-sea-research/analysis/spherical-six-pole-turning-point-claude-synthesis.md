## 5. Account of results

This section says in plain words what the campaign found. The exact numbers and grades are in section 3; nothing here adds a claim.

**The question.** Six architrinos released from rest at the poles of a sphere of radius 10 had been followed to time $t\approx4.6067$: the common speed was past its maximum and falling, and the extra normal support needed to hold them on the sphere was inward and still growing. The campaign asked which comes first: a path turn, where the motion stops and reverses, or a turning point of the support, where its growth stops.

**Neither was reached.** The motion is certified to $t\approx6.2267$ (physical time about $62.3$), and the path passes the quarter-circle point $q=\pi/2$ near $t\approx6.07$–$6.16$. The velocity stays positive, falling from about $0.38$ to about $0.27$–$0.30$ of the field speed, so the path has not turned; at that rate a turn is far beyond reach. The support stays inward the whole time, from about $-0.10$ to about $-0.12$ dimensionless.

**The support's growth slowed until its sign could no longer be decided.** Its rate of change is negative, meaning the inward support is still strengthening, on every whole step up to $t\approx5.57$, proved by two independent evaluations. So there is no support turning point there. Its rate is still negative at every step end up to about $5.67$ by all three evaluations, and up to about $5.81$ by the support's finer evaluation; those are end values only, so between $5.57$ and $5.81$ a brief positive excursion inside a step is not excluded and the question stays open there. After that, the uncertainty in the computed state is larger than the rate itself, so its sign is undecided. The rate rose steadily until about $5.06$, which is proved; after that it hovers just below zero. Whether it later peaks below zero or crosses zero is open. An earlier hint of a peak near $5.19$ did not survive checking.

**Why it hovers.** The unlike pair, and later the like pair, push backward along the path, which slows the motion. Slower motion needs less inward bending, so that effect works AGAINST further strengthening of the inward support. At the same time the discarded radial pull weakens and, near $t\approx5.67$–$5.72$, turns from inward to outward, which works TOWARD stronger inward support. The two effects nearly cancel. The outward turn of the radial pull is not a support event: once it points outward, both parts of the support requirement are inward.

**What stopped the run.** Twice the instrument's search window for a partner's emission time became too narrow. Both times it was widened under controls and the review admitted the change; the run finally stopped near $t\approx6.23$ when a partner's emission point left the controlled geometry box.
