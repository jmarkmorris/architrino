## 1. Correlated evaluation of the radial term, its rate and the support (own derivation)

**What is evaluated (derived).** $r = g\,[\,1/(r_L D_L) - 1/(r_U D_U) - 1/(2 r_A D_A)\,]$ (canonical radial acceleration with multiplicities 2, 2, 1 included once), $l = -w^2 - r$, $l' = -2wa - r'$, with $r'$ from the chain rule through the receiver state $(q, w)$ and each partner's source state $(q'_c, w'_c, a'_c)$ at its root $s_c$, $\dot s_c = (1 - \partial_q r_c\, w)/D_c$ (my frozen closed forms of the earlier campaigns; signed, no sign assumed). $a$ is the total tangent acceleration from the same source states. $l''$: by second-order jets as in my turning-point instrument, with the source acceleration rate from stored rate bounds (stored-rate premise).

**Dependent data (derived).** Suppose the continuation supplies every state and history quantity as a correlated representation in a common latent vector $\varepsilon \in E = [-1, 1]^n$: $x = x_c + J_x\,\varepsilon + R_x$, where $x_c$ is a centre, $J_x$ a sensitivity row (how $x$ moves with each latent parameter), and $R_x$ an interval remainder (truncation, rounding and every uncertainty NOT carried by $\varepsilon$, treated as independent per quantity). Each component of $\varepsilon$ must have a stated meaning (for example an anchor-state coordinate of the reconstruction, or an uncertain clock origin) and a stated joint domain; two quantities may share an $\varepsilon_j$ only if both are proved functions of that same latent quantity. A rectangular accepted box of $(q, w)$ means TWO independent parameters, never one. The history read by a partner at source time $s$ is $Q(s;\varepsilon) = Q_c(s) + J_Q(s)\varepsilon + R_Q(s)$ in the SAME $\varepsilon$ as the receiver (same release, same anchor), so receiver and source reads are correlated through $\varepsilon$, while the remainders stay independent.

**Evaluation rule I will use (derived): latent-parameter subdivision.** Cut $E$ into sub-boxes $E_k$ (and keep every remainder whole). On $E_k$ every input is enclosed by $x_c + J_x E_k + R_x$ evaluated in interval arithmetic, receiver and sources ON THE SAME $E_k$; each partner root is pinned on $E_k$ by the containment-checked contraction $S \leftarrow (t - r_c(q(E_k), Q(S; E_k))) \cap S$ (with the clock origin also a latent parameter if it is uncertain); then $r$, $a$, $l$, $l'$ are evaluated with the frozen closed forms. Because every true $\varepsilon$ lies in some $E_k$ and the remainders are kept whole, the hull over $k$ encloses the true values (validity), while each sub-box evaluation sees the cancellations between receiver and source dependence on $\varepsilon$ (gain). Conversion to boxes: the hull of the sub-box results, or of $x_c + J_x E + R_x$ for a single quantity, i.e. $x_c \pm \sum_j |J_{x,j}| + R_x$. A mean-value refinement on each $E_k$, $F(E_k) \subset F(\varepsilon_k) + \nabla_\varepsilon F(E_k)\,(E_k - \varepsilon_k)$, is optional; the subdivision rule needs no gradient through the delay roots and converges as the sub-boxes shrink. If only rectangular boxes are available (no latent representation), the rule reduces to my previous independent-box evaluation: no correlation is claimed.

**Intersections (derived).** If an old frozen record and a reconstructed object are BOTH proved to contain the true history at the same instants, their intersection is valid and may be used; otherwise not. An intersection that is empty is a falsifier of one of the two containment proofs, never a licence to choose one.

## 2. Signed geometry on a wider chart

**Chart (proposed).** Receiver $q \in [1.45, 2.00]$ (passing $\pi/2$), source $q' \in [0, 1.80]$, source signed velocity $w' \in [-0.10, 0.45]$, receiver $w \in [-0.10, 0.35]$. Basis (inferred): at $t = 6.2267$ $q \le 1.618$ and $w \le 0.302$; the unlike, like and antipode history ages near 1.3, 1.5 and 1.9 put the sources at most near $q' \approx 1.4$ by $t \approx 6.8$; the bounds leave margin and include sign changes of both velocities.

**Derived signs.** With the factorised forms $M = (\cos q\cos p/\sqrt2)[1 - \tan q(\tan p + 1/\sqrt2)]$ and $N = (\cos q\cos p/\sqrt2)[1 - \tan p(\tan q + 1/\sqrt2)]$ ($p = q'$): for $q \in (\arctan\sqrt2, \pi/2)$ and $p \in [0, \pi/2)$, $M < 0$ (bracket negative, prefactor positive); at $q = \pi/2$ exactly $M = -\sin p/\sqrt2 - \cos p/2 < 0$ and $N = -\sin p/\sqrt2 \le 0$ (zero only at $p = 0$); for $q \in (\pi/2, 2.0]$ the prefactor is negative and the bracket of $M$ is $1 + |\tan q|(\tan p + 1/\sqrt2) > 0$, so again $M < 0$. So $q = \pi/2$ is an ordinary point of the signed forms (no division by $\cos q$ is used in the actual closed forms, only in this factorisation). For $p > \pi/2$ (inside the proposed chart up to 1.80) the factorisation changes sign twice and I rely on the interval subdivision of the unfactorised forms (planned, section 3). $N$ for $q > \pi/2$: $-\cos q\sin p/2 > 0$ competes with $\cos(q+p)/\sqrt2 < 0$; sign to be decided by subdivision. Antipode: $N_A = \sin(q - q') > 0$ iff $q > q'$. Transmitter sides: with $M < 0$, $D_U - 1 = -w'_U M/r_U$ has the sign of $w'_U$, $D_L - 1$ the opposite sign of $w'_L$, $D_A - 1$ the sign of $w'_A$ while $q > q'$; after any source path turn ($w' < 0$) the sides swap; $D_c \ge 1 - |w'| > 0$ for sub-wake history, so the root map stays strictly monotone and each class keeps exactly one ordinary root and no self root, given strict positive chord floors (to be measured on the chart).
