1. **The argument is CORRECT (derived; checked step by step).** (a) On the unit sphere every chord is at most 2, so with $\tau_+=2+\epsilon$ the residual $h(\tau_+)=\tau_+-\rho\ge\epsilon>0$ for every admissible state, provided the source state at $t-\tau_+$ exists in covered history (stationary past or accepted records). (b) The chord is 1-Lipschitz in the source's arc length and each pole's arc speed is $|v|$ on the unit sphere, so $h'(\tau)\ge1-|v(t-\tau)|$ almost everywhere; if $\sup|v|<1$ over the WHOLE searched source interval and the path is continuous, $h$ is strictly increasing there and has at most one root (piecewise smoothness suffices). (c) With $\tau_->t_+-S_{max}$, every lower read $t-\tau_-$ lies strictly below the guaranteed lower end of coverage for every admissible $t$. (d) Then a proved strictly negative $h(\tau_-)$ over the whole current receiver box and time interval, together with (a), encloses exactly one ordinary root in $(\tau_-,\tau_+)$, which is the unique root on that interval by (b). (e) Refusal when $h(\tau_-)$ is not proved negative, or when $\tau_-\ge\tau_+$, is the right outcome; a zero-delay collision makes $h(\tau_-)>0$ and is refused, not hidden. Nothing in it uses a sign of $M$, $N$ or a tangent quotient, and it introduces no law or extrapolation. Two remarks, not defects: the bracket is only a search interval, the frozen step still has to produce a tight root enclosure inside it (more bisection steps, the same containment test); and $h$ is evaluated only at the two endpoints, each with the full uncertain time and receiver box.

2. **What an implementation must control before any target use (known and negative cases, recorded in that order).** KNOWN: (i) two stationary sphere points at a known chord $d\in(0,2]$: exact delay $d$, enclosed by a covered bracket below $d$ and $2+\epsilon$; (ii) agreement with the admitted v3b bracket at states where both apply (steps 274, 334 and 347 at least; consistency only); (iii) the failing like state at the step-406 end, which v3b refuses, is enclosed by the new bracket; (iv) the strict sub-wake bound computed as the hull of the certified speed boxes over the ENTIRE searched source interval, from the accepted records and the stationary past, with its lower margin $1-\sup|v|$ recorded per class and step. NEGATIVE: (v) a lower endpoint above a known root fails the strict-negative check; (vi) a lower delay reading into the possible but unguaranteed end of the last accepted step is refused; (vii) an artificial gap between retained records inside the searched interval is refused even though both endpoints are covered; (viii) an upper endpoint below a known root ($\tau_+<d$) fails the positive-residual check; (ix) an injected speed of 1 or more anywhere in the searched history refuses the uniqueness claim; (x) a changed transmitter sign is not accepted as a repair. Also required: the touched records of each endpoint read listed per class and step, and every appended own step of this campaign admitted through the checked append before it can be read.

3. **The simpler repair (only lowering the like bracket's lower end, for example to chord $-\,0.90$): EQUALLY ADMISSIBLE, under the same conditions.** Mathematically it is the same theorem with a different covered straddling interval: any interval on which $h$ is proved strictly increasing (sub-wake hull over the searched history), whose lower end gives a proved negative residual and whose upper end a proved positive residual, both reads inside guaranteed coverage, encloses the unique root. Lowering the lower delay moves its source read LATER, so its coverage condition is the binding one: $t_+-(\text{chord}-0.90)$ must stay below the guaranteed lower end of accepted history, which now includes this campaign's own appended steps. It needs the same known and negative cases (2 (ii), (iii), (iv), (v), (vi), (vii), (ix)), with the step-406 like state as the known case. Difference in robustness, not admissibility: a fixed chord offset can fail again as the roots drift (it has failed twice in this campaign), whereas the coverage-safe bracket does not need retuning. Either repair must be made under a new script version with controls first, and any target computed before them is provisional.

Ruling: the companion is ADMITTED as correct; both repairs are admissible once their controls precede use; my preference, for robustness only, is the coverage-safe bracket.
