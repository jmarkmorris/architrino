## (C) Linear spectrum about the stationary alternating hexagon

**Scope.** This is linear stability of a lawful equilibrium of the selected law (the stationary alternating equatorial hexagon with its stationary past: $P\mathbf A=0$ exactly by symmetry, so zero velocity is an exact solution). It is not a periodic orbit and not a six-body frequency. Dimensionless units $R=c_f=1$, $g=K/(Rc_f^2)$ the only parameter; delays $\tau=1,\sqrt3,2$ (chords) in units of $R/c_f$.

**Linearization (derived).** Member $i$ at $\mathbf u_i=(\cos\phi_i,\sin\phi_i,0)$, $\phi_i=i\pi/3$, polarity $q_i=(-1)^i$, tangent perturbation $\boldsymbol\xi_i(T)$ in the basis $\{\mathbf t_i=(-\sin\phi_i,\cos\phi_i,0),\mathbf e_z\}$ (12 degrees of freedom). For transmitter $j$ with unperturbed delay $\tau_{ij}=r_{ij}$: the root condition $|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(T-\tau)|=\tau$ gives the root shift $\delta\tau=\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}\cdot(\boldsymbol\xi_i(T)-\boldsymbol\xi_j(T-\tau_{ij}))$ at first order, but the shift enters $\mathbf X_j(T-\tau)$ and $\mathbf V_j(T-\tau)$ only through $\delta\tau\,\boldsymbol\xi_j'$, a product of two first-order quantities, because the unperturbed transmitter velocity vanishes; so the root-time shift contributes nothing at linear order about a stationary equilibrium. The transmitter factor contributes through $1/|D_t|\approx1+\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\boldsymbol\xi_j'(T-\tau_{ij})$ (velocity term). Expanding $\mathbf r/|\mathbf r|^3$ gives the per-hit linear kernel $\sigma_{ij}[(I-3\hat{\mathbf r}\hat{\mathbf r}^{\mathsf T})/r^3](\boldsymbol\xi_i(T)-\boldsymbol\xi_j(T-\tau_{ij}))+\sigma_{ij}(\hat{\mathbf r}\hat{\mathbf r}^{\mathsf T}/r^2)\boldsymbol\xi_j'(T-\tau_{ij})$. The projection also varies: with $\mathbf A_i^0=a_0\mathbf n_i$ purely radial at equilibrium ($a_0=\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A^0=-(5/4-1/\sqrt3)=-0.672650$ in units of $K/R^2$), $\delta(P_i)\mathbf A_i^0=-(a_0/R)\boldsymbol\xi_i$, a tangent push along the displacement from the inward radial part; the $-(|\mathbf v|^2/R)\mathbf n$ term is second order. Hence, with $\lambda$ the Laplace variable and $E_i$ the $3\times2$ tangent basis,
$$
\Delta(\lambda)=\lambda^2I_{12}-g\,D_{\mathrm{loc}}+g\sum_{j\ne i}e^{-\lambda\tau_{ij}}\big(M_{ij}-\lambda N_{ij}\big),\qquad
D_{\mathrm{loc},ii}=E_i^{\mathsf T}\Big[\sum_{j\ne i}\sigma_{ij}\tfrac{I-3\hat{\mathbf r}\hat{\mathbf r}^{\mathsf T}}{r^3}\Big]E_i-a_0I_2,
$$
$$
M_{ij}=E_i^{\mathsf T}\,\sigma_{ij}\tfrac{I-3\hat{\mathbf r}\hat{\mathbf r}^{\mathsf T}}{r^3}\,E_j,\qquad N_{ij}=E_i^{\mathsf T}\,\sigma_{ij}\tfrac{\hat{\mathbf r}\hat{\mathbf r}^{\mathsf T}}{r^2}\,E_j .
$$
The PI's form $\lambda^2I-\sum_j[M+\lambda N]e^{-\lambda\tau}$ omits the undelayed local block $D_{\mathrm{loc}}$ (the receiver's own displacement in the static kernel plus the projection variation); it is included here. In the rotating tangent frames the blocks depend only on $j-i$ (block-circulant, verified), and in-plane ($t$) and vertical ($z$) components decouple because every $\hat{\mathbf r}$ is in-plane (verified), so the Fourier modes $\boldsymbol\zeta_j=\hat{\boldsymbol\zeta}e^{ikj\pi/3}$, $k=0,\dots,5$ (the $D_6$ block-diagonalization, with the alternating polarity absorbed by $\sigma_{ij}=(-1)^{j-i}$), reduce $\det\Delta$ to the product of twelve scalar functions
$$
\Delta_k^{t}(\lambda)=\lambda^2-gD_t+g\sum_{m=1}^{5}\sigma_me^{ikm\pi/3-\lambda\tau_m}\big(c^M_m-\lambda c^N_m\big),\qquad
\Delta_k^{z}(\lambda)=\lambda^2-gD_z+g\sum_{m=1}^{5}\sigma_me^{ikm\pi/3-\lambda\tau_m}\,r_m^{-3},
$$
with $\sigma_m=(-1)^m$, $\tau_m=r_m=2\sin(m\pi/6)$, $c^M_m=[\mathbf t_0\cdot\mathbf t_m-3(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\mathbf t_0)(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\mathbf t_m)]/r_m^3$, $c^N_m=(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\mathbf t_0)(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\mathbf t_m)/r_m^2$, $D_t=\sum_m\sigma_m[1-3(\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\mathbf t_0)^2]/r_m^3-a_0=3.143875$ (the same number as the hexagon speed-curvature coefficient of wave-2 (b), as it must be: both are the tangential second derivative of the same static field), $D_z=\sum_m\sigma_m r_m^{-3}-a_0=-1.067450$; numerically $\sigma_mc^M_m=(-1.75,-0.240563,-0.125,-0.240563,-1.75)$, $\sigma_mc^N_m=(0.75,0.083333,0,0.083333,0.75)$, $\sigma_mr_m^{-3}=(-1,0.192450,-0.125,0.192450,-1)$ for $m=1..5$.
