## Selected law and notation

Six architrinos, three positrinos ($q=+1$ in units of $\epsilon$) and three electrinos ($q=-1$), on the sphere $|\mathbf X|=R$. With $\mathbf n_i=\mathbf X_i/R$ and $P_i=I-\mathbf n_i\mathbf n_i^{\mathsf T}$, the selected motion is $\ddot{\mathbf X}_i=P_i\mathbf A_i-(|\mathbf v_i|^2/R)\mathbf n_i$, where $\mathbf A_i$ is the complete canonical all-root acceleration on the actual histories with ambient Euclidean wake distance. The per-hit acceleration from transmitter $j$ emitting at $T_t$ and received at $T_r$ is $\mathbf A_{ij}=K\sigma_{ij}\,r_{ij}^{-2}\,(c_f/|D_t|)\,\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}$ with $\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}$ from emission point to receiver, $\sigma=+1$ for like polarities and $-1$ for unlike, $D_t=c_f-\mathbf V_j(T_t)\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_{ij}$, and $K=\kappa|q_iq_j|$ equal for every pair. A causal root is a delay $\tau=T_r-T_t>0$ with $|\mathbf X_i(T_r)-\mathbf X_j(T_r-\tau)|=c_f\tau$. Every numerical instance uses $c_f=1$, $K=1$. The radial interaction component $(\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf A_i)\mathbf n_i$ is discarded; it is recorded only as a diagnostic. Tangent work is $\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf A_i=\mathbf v_i\cdot P_i\mathbf A_i$ (equal because $\mathbf v_i$ is tangent) and gives the speed derivative $d|\mathbf v_i|/dT=\mathbf v_i\cdot\mathbf A_i/|\mathbf v_i|$. Dimensionless coupling candidate $g=K/(Rc_f^2)$; kinematic control $f_0=v/(2\pi R)$.

**Observation on the law (derived).** The displayed spherical reduction of the normal-only constrained equation in the [surface-constraint definition](../../equation-variants/surface-constraint/definition.md), $\ddot{\mathbf X}_i=(I-\mathbf n_i\mathbf n_i^{\mathsf T})\mathbf A_i-(|\mathbf V_i|^2/R)\mathbf n_i$, is term by term the selected equation above. The two descriptions differ only in bookkeeping: the constraint law reports a support $\mathbf C_i=-(\mathbf n_i\cdot\mathbf A_i+|\mathbf V_i|^2/R)\mathbf n_i$ that cancels the radial interaction, whereas the selected law discards that interaction outright. The motions coincide on any ordinary chart; the difference matters only for logged support and for any further speed-response clause a prior campaign may have selected in addition. Falsifier: a term in the constraint law's spherical reduction, or in a prior campaign's executed acceleration path, that is absent from $P_i\mathbf A_i-(|\mathbf v_i|^2/R)\mathbf n_i$.

## History families (derived)

Dimensionless time is $s=Tc_f/R$. Each member $i$ has release position $\mathbf n_{i0}$ (unit) and release unit tangent $\mathbf t_{i0}$ ($\mathbf n_{i0}\cdot\mathbf t_{i0}=0$) and moves on its own great circle $\mathbf X_i(T)=R\,[\cos\theta_i(s)\,\mathbf n_{i0}+\sin\theta_i(s)\,\mathbf t_{i0}]$, so $\mathbf V_i(T)=c_f\,\theta_i'(s)\,[-\sin\theta_i\,\mathbf n_{i0}+\cos\theta_i\,\mathbf t_{i0}]$ and speed $|\mathbf V_i|=c_f|\theta_i'(s)|$. Write $\nu=v/c_f$ for the release speed.

**H1 (stationary past, smooth preparation).** $\theta(s)=0$ for $s\le-1/4$ and $\theta(s)=\nu\,s\,(1+4s)^3$ for $-1/4\le s\le0$. With $u=1+4s\in[0,1]$: $\theta=\nu(u^4-u^3)/4$, $\theta'(s)=\nu\,u^2(4u-3)$, $\theta''(s)=4\nu(12u^2-6u)$, $\theta'''(s)=16\nu(24u-6)$. Checks: $\theta(0)=0$ and $\theta'(0)=\nu$ (correct release speed); at $u=0$ ($s=-1/4$) $\theta=\theta'=\theta''=0$ while $\theta'''$ jumps from $0$ to $-96\nu$, so the seam is exactly $C^2$ and not $C^3$. Inside the preparation the member first moves backward along its circle, reaching $\theta_{\min}=-27\nu/1024$ at $u=3/4$, and the speed profile $\nu|u^2(4u-3)|$ has backward extremum $\nu/4$ at $u=1/2$ and forward maximum $\nu$ attained only at release; hence $|\mathbf V_i(T)|<v$ strictly for $T<0$. The total preparation path length is $2\cdot27\nu R/1024\approx0.0527\,\nu R$. At release the prescribed angular acceleration is $\theta''(0)=24\nu$, which the selected law does not in general reproduce; the join to generated motion is $C^1$ (position and velocity), which is what delayed dynamics requires of a history. For $\nu=1/2$ this is the frozen six-pole preparation $\tfrac12 s(1+4s)^3$. Radius-scaled preparation duration is $R/(4c_f)$. A $C^2$ seam suffices for the root chart: the root function $f(\tau)=\tau-|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j(T-\tau)|/c_f$ is then $C^2$ in $\tau$ wherever $r_{ij}>0$, which is more than the $C^1$ needed for bracketing and bisection and for the monotonicity certificate below. Claim grade: derived. Falsifier: a direct evaluation of $\theta$, $\theta'$ or $\theta''$ at $s=-1/4$ or of $\theta'(0)$ differing from the stated values.

**H2 (moving preparation).** $\theta(s)=\nu s$ for all $s\le0$: uniform great-circle motion at the release speed, $C^\infty$, prescribed input. Its exact Cartesian acceleration is $-(v^2/R)\mathbf n_i$, so its tangent residual against the selected law is exactly $P_i\mathbf A_i$ (nothing in the prescribed path balances the tangent interaction).

**Prescribed post-release extension (diagnostic only).** Where a sample time $T>0$ is used, the path is extended by $\theta(s)=\nu s$ for $s>0$. This is a prescribed path, never motion; its residual against the selected law is $P_i\mathbf A_i$ and is reported as such.

**Root-completeness certificate.** $f'(\tau)=1-\hat{\mathbf r}\cdot\mathbf V_j(T-\tau)/c_f=D_t/c_f\ge1-v_{\max}/c_f$ where $v_{\max}$ bounds the transmitter speed over the scanned emission window. If $v_{\max}<c_f$ then $f$ is strictly increasing on $(0,\tau_{\max}]$, so each ordered pair has at most one root there, and because on a sphere every delay satisfies $c_f\tau=r\le2R$, scanning $(0,2R/c_f+\text{margin}]$ is exhaustive. For $\nu=1$ the uniform bound degenerates ($v_{\max}=c_f$ is attained at release); following the brief such cells are recorded as *uncertified* by this bound and the scan census is reported as measured only. (An inference, recorded separately below, is that for H1 the speed is strictly below $c_f$ at every emission time $T_t<0$, so $f'>0$ pointwise and uniqueness still holds; this is not used for certification.)

**Self roots (derived).** For any path on the sphere with speed at most $c_f$ on the emission window, no positive-delay self root exists: the chord $|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_i(T-\tau)|$ is strictly less than the arc length of a curved path, and the arc length is at most $c_f\tau$. This covers every prescribed history here (H1, H2 and the uniform extension) for $\nu\le1$. Numerically the self function has $f(0)=0$ and, at $\nu=1$, $f'(0)=f''(0)=0$ with $f(\tau)\approx\tau^3/(24R^2)$, which is below floating-point resolution for $\tau\lesssim10^{-4}R$; the first run bracketed a spurious sign change there. The instrument now starts the self scan at $\tau=10^{-3}R$, states the derived exclusion for $(0,10^{-3}R]$ in its output, and uses the scan above that as a consistency check only. Falsifier: a sphere path with speed $\le c_f$ and a chord equal to $c_f\tau$ for some $\tau>0$.

## Findings

**F1 (derived, independently reproduced).** For every H1 cell the release acceleration is the static inverse-square field of the release configuration: all five partner roots per receiver lie in the stationary past (smallest delay $\ge0.35R>R/4$), every $D_t=1$, no self root. Six poles, receiver at $+\mathbf e_1$: the two like neighbours contribute $+\tfrac{K}{2R^2}\tfrac{(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_2)}{\sqrt2}$ and $+\tfrac{K}{2R^2}\tfrac{(\mathbf e_1-\mathbf e_3)}{\sqrt2}$, the two unlike neighbours $-\tfrac{K}{2R^2}\tfrac{(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf e_2)}{\sqrt2}$ and $-\tfrac{K}{2R^2}\tfrac{(\mathbf e_1+\mathbf e_3)}{\sqrt2}$, the antipode $-\tfrac{K}{4R^2}\mathbf e_1$; hence $\mathbf A=-\tfrac{K}{\sqrt2R^2}(\mathbf e_2+\mathbf e_3)-\tfrac{K}{4R^2}\mathbf e_1$, so $|P\mathbf A|=K/R^2$ exactly, directed away from the positrino cap, and $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A=-K/(4R^2)$. Electrinos are the mirror image. Hexagon (G2), receiver at azimuth 0: $P\mathbf A=0$ by the two reflection symmetries, and $\mathbf n\cdot\mathbf A=-\tfrac{K}{R^2}\left(\tfrac54-\tfrac1{\sqrt3}\right)=-0.672650\,K/R^2$ (two unlike neighbours at chord $R$, two like at $\sqrt3R$, one unlike at $2R$). The instrument reproduces all of these to $10^{-15}$. Falsifier: an independent static-field evaluation at these positions differing from the closed forms.

**F2 (derived).** Tangent work at release on G1. ## Analytical method-of-steps statement for A1 (no integrator)

**First step is an autonomous static-field problem (derived).** Take A1 ($R=K=c_f=1$). Let $L_i(T)$ be member $i$'s path length after release. A root of a stationary source $j$ has delay $\tau=|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j^0|$ and lies in the stationary past iff $|\mathbf X_i(T)-\mathbf X_j^0|\ge T+\tfrac14$. While $L_i\le\tfrac34$, the triangle inequality gives neighbour chords $\ge\sqrt2-L_i$ and the antipodal chord $\ge2-L_i$, so $|\mathbf A_i|\le4/(\sqrt2-\tfrac34)^2+1/(2-\tfrac34)^2<9.71$, hence $|\mathbf v_i|\le1+9.71T$ and $L_i\le T+4.86T^2$. At $T=0.2$: $L_i\le0.394$ (consistent with $\le\tfrac34$), neighbour chords $\ge1.020\ge0.45=T+\tfrac14$, and any root with emission time in $(-\tfrac14,T)$ would need a separation below $T+\tfrac14=0.45$ while separations stay $\ge\sqrt2-2(0.394)-0.053=0.573$ (the $0.053$ is the preparation path length). Therefore on $0\le T\le0.2$ every partner root of every member lies in the stationary past, every $D_t=1$, and the selected law is exactly the decoupled autonomous system $\ddot{\mathbf X}_i=P_i\mathbf F(\mathbf X_i)-|\mathbf v_i|^2\mathbf n_i$ with $\mathbf F$ the static field of F1. This $T_s\ge0.2$ is a crude derived lower bound on the first step; the true first step ends when the leading root (delay about $1.41$ at release) reaches emission time $-\tfrac14$, which is later. Falsifier: a root of A1 with emission time above $-\tfrac14$ for some $T\le0.2$.

**Consequences on the first step.** The six members do not interact with each other's generated motion at all; each moves in the fixed static field of the other five release positions. The static field of the polarized octahedron is invariant under $C_3$ about $\hat{\boldsymbol\omega}$ and under inversion with polarity reversal, and the V1 and V2 release data are orbits of that group, so on the whole first step the six speeds of A1 and A4-V2 remain exactly equal (derived from uniqueness of the ODE solution; V3 and V4 are not orbits of that group and their equal release values are not shown to persist). The Taylor data at release are exact: $\mathbf X_i(T)=\mathbf X_i^0+\mathbf v_i^0T+\tfrac12\mathbf a_i^0T^2+O(T^3)$ with $\mathbf a_i^0=P\mathbf A-\mathbf n$ from F1, and $|\mathbf v_i|=1+\tfrac{9}{16}T^2+O(T^3)$ from F3.

**Self roots after release (inferred, leading order).** With $|\mathbf v|=1+\tfrac{9}{16}T^2$, the arc between emission $T_t\in[0,T)$ and reception $T$ exceeds the delay $\tau$ by $\approx\tfrac{3}{16}\tau(T^2+TT_t+T_t^2)\ge\tfrac3{16}\tau T^2$, while the sphere's chord deficit is $\approx\tau^3/24\le\tau T^2/24$; the arc excess dominates, so no positive-delay self root with emission after release appears at leading order in $T$. Self roots with emission in the stationary past are excluded on $[0,0.2]$ by the bound above: such a root needs chord $=\tau\ge T+\tfrac14$ while the chord is at most $L_i\le T+4.86T^2<T+\tfrac14$. Emissions inside the preparation window and higher orders after release are not covered. Falsifier: a self root of the first-step solution at some $T\le0.2$, which would require chord $=\tau$ exactly.

**Exact obstruction to a full enclosure.** A rigorous enclosure of the first-step solution on $[0,T_s]$ (an interval Taylor or Picard bound with a stated error) is attainable in principle because the step is an ordinary ODE with a smooth static field, but it was not built in this wave: the brief's time-stepping route was withdrawn and a validated enclosure method is a separate instrument requiring its own controls. Nothing physical obstructs it. The continuation beyond $T_s$ is a genuine delay problem (generated history enters the roots) and, for $v=c_f$, enters the super-wake regime immediately (F3), where root uniqueness must be re-established per root.
