## Families (derived) and method

As in wave 14: on the stationary-source window each member moves in the static field $\mathbf A=-\nabla\Phi$ of the five fixed images, along the unit-speed path $\mathbf X(q)$ that its release acceleration selects; $F=\mathbf T\cdot\mathbf A=-d\Phi/dq$, $\dot q^2=2gU$, $U=\Phi(0)-\Phi(q)$; wake crossing at $U(q_c)=1/(2g)$; window end $T^*=\min_j\text{chord}_j(q(T^*))$. New in this wave: the direction of motion is fixed by the sign of $F(0)$ on the family's path; the window-end residual uses the interval minimum over all five chords; and the first-cell bound accepts a release field that decreases as well as one that increases (if $F$ is monotone on $[0,u_1^2]$ the mean $U/q$ lies between $F(0)$ and $F(u_1^2)$, so $I$ lies between $I_0$ and $I_0/\sqrt{F(u_1^2)/F(0)}$). **Monotonicity of the residual (derived):** $d\,\text{chord}_j/dt=(\mathbf T\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_j)\,\dot q$ with $|\mathbf T\cdot\hat{\mathbf r}_j|\le1$, so on a sub-wake window ($\dot q<1$) every chord changes at a rate below $1$ in magnitude and $r(t)=t-\min_j\text{chord}_j$ is strictly increasing whatever the chord signs; the root is unique and the bracketing is rigorous wherever $\dot q<1$ up to the root. The script's cell-wise derivative check is therefore informational: it reports `False` for the antiprism cells, where the nearest image at release is a receding like partner, and for the six poles at $g=1/10$ (a tie at $q=0$), without affecting the certification; where the window end lies beyond the crossing it concerns the fixed-image problem only.
