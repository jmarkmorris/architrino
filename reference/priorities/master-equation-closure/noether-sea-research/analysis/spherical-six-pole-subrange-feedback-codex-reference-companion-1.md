# Local history inclusion: regularity and guaranteed coverage

Prepared2026-10-12 after reading the independently frozen support referencee39355ae… and review referencede88321d… plus review companionff91846d…. The [original coordinator reference](spherical-six-pole-subrange-feedback-codex-reference.md), frozen283c119c…, is preserved. This companion clarifies its hypotheses, not a new equation or a target result.

The integral argument needs the acceleration $q''$ to be absolutely continuous on the slab, with derivative in $J$ almost everywhere. Lipschitz acceleration with a bounded, piecewise continuous rate suffices. It does not require $q'''$ to exist at every reception time. A first reception of source release can change the one-sided acceleration rates while the acceleration remains continuous. If a retained slab straddles such a reception, both one-sided rates must be included in its certified $J$. The frozen review companion identifies the affected older records and requires proof or fallback to a separately certified whole-slab enclosure. The original inclusion formulas remain valid under this clarified hypothesis; a `certified` flag or endpoint replay alone does not establish the missing rate premise.

Guaranteed coverage is distinct from possible coverage. If the last accepted true end time is $E\in[E_-,E_+]$, a query's upper endpoint at most $E_-$ is safely within the accepted past for every admitted end time. A query in $(E_-,E_+]$ needs an explicit correlation argument with the same uncertain origin, or refusal; the existence of a candidate polynomial box is insufficient. The same issue applies to a point query at $E_+$ in an append control. Every target source query should record its margin to guaranteed coverage. A defect in a generic boundary refusal does not by itself invalidate earlier targets whose source queries stay well below $E_-$, but that separation must be verified row by row. Repair any boundary policy with known and negative controls before its repaired target use, preserving prior output and exact claim scope.

An interval hull at a source join preserves containment of the same continuous true history. It need not be a continuous function of query endpoints: different valid enclosure boxes can appear/disappear from the hull. Claims of smooth physical history and claims about continuity of an interval lookup are therefore separate. Derivative controls against numerical differences of prescribed histories are measured controls; their histories can be exact polynomials, but the differences are not themselves exact hand-known derivative values. The review's hand-derived cubic with a negative rate provides an additional exact case. All new target sign, rate and motion claims remain subject to the independently controlled lookup/remainder adjudication.
