## 1. Independent verification of the algebra (derived)

Kernel: the review's own antipode hit (reference section 2) is $\sin(q-q')/(r^3D_A)$ with $r=2\cos((q-q')/2)$; with $z=q-q'$, $\sin z=2\sin(z/2)\cos(z/2)$ and $r^3=8\cos^3(z/2)$ give $\sin z/(r^3D_A)=\sin(z/2)/(4\cos^2(z/2)D_A)=H(z)/D_A$, the coordinator's $H$. Derivative, by hand with $u=z/2$: $H=\tfrac14\sin u\sec^2u$, $dH/dz=\tfrac12\,dH/du=\tfrac18[\cos u\sec^2u+2\sin u\sec^2u\tan u]=\tfrac18[\sec u+2\sin^2u\sec^3u]$, the coordinator's formula; each term is positive wherever $\cos u>0$, i.e. on $0\le z<\pi$, and $H'(0)=1/8$. Domain: for $0\le p\le q<\pi$, $z=q-p\in[0,q]\subset[0,\pi)$, $H$ is increasing there, so $H(z)\le H(q)$ with equality only at $p=0$; for $v_s\ge0$ and $z\ge0$, $D_A=1+v_s\sin(z/2)\ge1$; hence $0\le H(z)/D_A\le H(q)$, strict for $p>0$. The theorem is correct as stated, including its limits ($p=v_s=0$: equality; $p=q$: zero). Its scope statement is correct: it compares two acceleration contributions at one receiver state and says nothing about trajectories, the sum of feedback corrections, or the support sign.
