## Independent reasoning

- **Achieved versus conditional:** assuming a substrate architecture is substantially correct does not provide a particular scalar mode, coupling, invariant measure or correspondence theorem. The former `Partially Recovered` rows supplied comparison targets but no named retained recovery derivation. The new label states only conditional compatibility after reinterpretation.
- **Noether versus trajectory identity:** for any differentiable `E(t)`, the quantity `E(t)+integral_0^t[-E'(s)]ds` equals `E(0)`. That identity holds even for arbitrarily chosen `E`; it supplies no independently specified physical charge or variational symmetry. This directly falsifies the stronger inference from trajectory compensation.
- **Measure propagation:** the deterministic flow `x'=1` transports a normalized point preparation at zero into a normalized point preparation at time `t`. The preparation remains a valid probability measure while not being invariant. Apparatus-preparation distributions therefore need not be stationary. This is an abstract counterexample, not a proposed architrino equation.
- **Statistical discrimination:** Bernoulli models with probabilities 0.2 and 0.8 assign the same support to outcomes zero and one. Observing one gives likelihood ratio four, despite being possible under both. Discrimination requires different probabilities, not an observation impossible for every competing history.
- **Conditioning:** a declared positive-probability selection event permits Bayes conditioning over unknown parameters; those parameters need not already have been dynamically derived. A measure selected afterward to force the desired posterior would be a different and unsupported procedure.
- **CHSH:** for deterministic responses bounded by one, the expression `A0(B0+B1)+A1(B0-B1)` has magnitude at most two; convex averaging under a normalized setting-independent hidden-variable measure retains that bound. Unbounded outputs or a setting-dependent sampling measure would not satisfy this proof’s premises.
- **Background validity:** a linearized operator describes perturbations of a declared reference solution. For example, `x'=1` has no fixed equilibrium at zero; treating its derivative there as an equilibrium stability spectrum would ignore the nonzero zeroth-order motion. The Jeans comparison must resolve its own homogeneous-background convention before attributing physical stability.
- **Entropy:** reversible permutation of a finite probability vector leaves its weighted entropy unchanged. Irreversibility or monotonic coarse-grained entropy requires additional dynamics, partition and boundary assumptions rather than following from the word entropy.

These derivations are independent of the chapter and introduce no standard-physics law as a substrate premise. No physical numerical wake-speed calculation or runtime experiment was needed.
