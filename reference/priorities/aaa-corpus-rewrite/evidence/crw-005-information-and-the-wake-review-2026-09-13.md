Free propagation changes radius by c_f times elapsed time and retains that measure; with boundary exports included it does not erase those labels. Taking a sum discards a decomposition: vectors (a,b) and (a+d,b-d) give the same total. No-scattering and invertibility of a limited readout are distinct statements.
2. Continuation versus finite information: even a constant real-valued history on a finite interval can contain an arbitrary real parameter; the interval length does not bound its exact bit description. A local existence theorem also states only its own interval, not global continuation or recovery of earlier unretained histories.
3. Canonical hit formula: at fixed nonzero separation and unit radial direction, the acceleration is proportional to sign(q_t q_r) times radial direction divided by r-squared and absolute(c_f-radial velocity). Transverse velocity does not enter this instantaneous expression. Holding the radial component fixed therefore preserves the hit while changing transverse data. Additional causal roots and full dynamic compatibility are expressly outside that kernel comparison.
4. Sign check: an opposite-polarity emitter ahead gives negative radial direction and negative polarity product, hence positive-axis acceleration. A same-polarity emitter behind gives positive radial direction and positive product. Absolute D_t in the weight keeps both conclusions when D_t is negative. Replacing it with a signed potential factor would be an incorrect acceleration rule.
5. Reach negative: normalized c_f=1, stationary emitter beginning at T=0, distance 2 and reception T=1 require emission T=-1, outside the admitted history. Continuous emission alone supplies no hit. Prior contact can also cease when the source/receiver geometry or admitted memory changes; a root-existence theorem must specify those domains.
6. A many-to-one map does not select weights on its fibers. Different probability measures can share the same support and readout ambiguity, so non-injectivity alone cannot specify a Born law or a numerical uncertainty product.
7. Infinite polarity arithmetic: infinitely many positive and negative identities do not define the difference of their divergent counts. A summation/density convention is needed; this does not alter permanence of each individual identity.
