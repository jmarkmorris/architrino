## Independent mathematical evidence

The following arguments are independent of the chapter's repaired wording. They establish identities or counterexamples under their declared assumptions, not physical assembly retention.

1. **Antipodal angular momentum.** With endpoints plus/minus d, d equals h times z plus rho times e, and derivative d equals rho times omega times z cross e, summing the two position-velocity cross products gives twice mu times omega times (rho squared times z minus h times rho times e). Averaging e over a complete nonzero-frequency cycle gives zero. Purely axial instantaneous total requires the sum of h times rho times each complex phase to vanish. The independent cross-product instrument first returned z for x cross y, then evaluated h=3, rho=2, omega=4, mu=1 at phase zero and returned J=(-48,0,32), with wake speed normalized to one. The old purely axial expression would give (0,0,32). This is prescribed geometry, not an EOM solution.
2. **Tilt inertia.** Two antipodal points have transverse-axis geometric inertia 2 mu times (h squared plus rho squared sine squared phase). The cycle average is mu times (2 h squared plus rho squared), matching the retained display. That geometric average alone does not establish a dynamical reduced inertia or gyroscopic spectrum.
3. **Compensating conservation.** For any sufficiently regular trajectory with constant mu, the derivative of X cross mu V is X cross mu A. Defining a wake account as minus the integral of that same derivative makes the total constant for every such trajectory. Its success therefore cannot distinguish an independent physical conservation law from its absence. The open-transaction formula must not reuse this compensator and then add the same mechanical increment again.
4. **Projection.** If I equals a dot L, the orthogonal decomposition is L=I a plus L-perpendicular. Substituting another unit vector n yields a dot L=I(a dot n), contradicting the definition whenever I is nonzero and a dot n differs from one. For a circular L=2 mu R squared omega n, the projected first variation includes both a dot n and I times a dot delta-n. The corrected aligned example remains algebraically valid.
5. **Canonical energy.** At fixed radius a quadratic rotational proxy has I=2 mu R squared omega and E=I squared/(4 mu R squared), so partial E/partial I=omega. In general E is not I omega. If radius changes while R omega is held constant, this proxy's energy remains constant; the parameter derivative cancels the action derivative. Consequently the four-substep energy relation remains conditional on its independently justified energy and parameter-work assumptions.
6. **Root events.** The local family g(x;s)=x squared minus s has no real roots for negative s and two simple roots for positive s, but a double root at s=0. The opposite derivative signs preserve signed degree. The implicit-function theorem keeps simple interior roots persistent; delay-boundary exit and the excluded zero-delay self endpoint are different events. This establishes no relation between two roots and two temporal action substeps.
7. **Rotation and exchange.** All half-integer irreducible spin representations acquire minus identity under a full rotation; the sign alone cannot select spin one half. An exchange path is defined on a many-object configuration space, so assigning its sign from a one-object path requires an additional mapping theorem. A finite integer parity sum is gauge invariant only when its component parities are well-defined and invariant.
8. **Time reversal and helicity.** The real matrix i sigma-y is [[0,1],[-1,0]], whose square is minus identity; composing it with complex conjugation gives the same square. On rays that overall sign is invisible. Reversing both spin and momentum preserves their scalar product and hence helicity; parity reverses momentum but preserves axial spin and flips helicity.
9. **Spherical harmonics.** The sum of Y(1,0) and Y(1,1) has angular-Laplacian eigenvalue two, but is not an Lz eigenfunction because the summands have different m. Choosing a simultaneous eigenbasis restores the retained chosen-axis relation.
10. **Measure and conditioning.** For a normalized uniform U on [0,1], the indicator U<p has probability p. Conditioning on U<1/2 changes that probability to min(2p,1), so an initially uniform detector coordinate need not remain uniform after record selection. Reusing one uniform U for two thresholds p=1/2 gives joint probability 1/2, not 1/4; sequential multiplication requires the correct conditional distribution. A measure concentrated on a threshold has nonzero boundary mass despite H(0)=0. An identity map on two points preserves every mixture, but a single orbit's time average need not equal a chosen nontrivial mixture; invariance alone is not ergodicity.
11. **Normalized delta.** For a simple root g(T_t)=0, integrating f times delta(g) over emission time yields f/abs(g-prime). Here g-prime is D_t; multiplying the distance-normalized delta integral by c_f yields the required c_f/abs(D_t). This is a symbolic normalization check; no non-unit wake-speed numerical run was made.
12. **Error and Bell bounds.** A sign computed from a first-order Q is reliable only when its magnitude exceeds a bound on the omitted remainder. In the Bell cell norm, each correlation has four outcome cells and CHSH has four correlations, giving the retained factor sixteen. The displayed product/singlet lower bound follows by triangle inequalities. These algebraic bounds do not supply a coupled dynamical law.

Each local identity is falsified by a counterexample satisfying its stated assumptions; the explicit examples above falsify the broader baseline claims. No mathematical or physical correctness claim is based solely on replaying a fixture or on a compensating integral.
