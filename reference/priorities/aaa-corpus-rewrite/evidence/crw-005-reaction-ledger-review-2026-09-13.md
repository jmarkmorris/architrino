## Separate mathematical references

These are analytic references, separate from agreement between editors. The full-document editorial pass is self-review.

For RL-08, take distinct global identities a and b of positive polarity. Input set {a} and output set {b} have identical polarity counts and admit the polarity-preserving bijection a to b. They do not route the same identity. Even on an unchanged set, a permutation of equal-polarity names does not document the corresponding continuous worldlines. Thus count equality is necessary but not sufficient; global identity preservation and explicit boundary participation supply the missing requirement. This uses the persistent-identity primitive in Architrino, not a standard-physics conservation import.

For RL-03 and RL-05, defining an unmeasured output to equal input minus every other output makes the residual identically zero for any chosen data. Such equality cannot independently test conservation. A disjoint account avoids counting a remnant both inside the final source and as an additional output; signed net changes also permit boundary influx without fictitious negative physical energy.

For RL-07, the difference between the two longitudinal projections is the projection of the balance defect up to sign. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its magnitude by the defect norm because the direction vector has norm one. Division by the positive action benchmark preserves the inequality. This proves only the projection bound, not two-valued helicity. An equal mixture of the two target helicity outcomes has mean zero, illustrating why the displayed two-valued condition must refer to an eigenstate or resolved outcome. Dividing numerator and depletion by one fixed reference action yields the dimensionless ratio in the chapter; clearing that reference action gives its stated dimensional equivalent.

For RL-02 and RL-10, subtracting the proposed scaffold counts gives minus twice the generation difference. Top and bottom both occupy generation III in this hypothesis, giving zero scaffold change. Their axial signed counts change from four positive units net to two negative units net, a decrease of six units; the compensating corridor/reservoir account has the opposite change. Reversing the axial exchange in neutron beta bookkeeping gives the opposite quark change. These are conditional integer identities and cannot identify actual emitted assemblies.

For RL-12, the variance of a difference is the sum of variances minus twice their covariance. The beam quadrature is valid only when the quoted statistical and systematic contributions can be treated independently; the two displayed residuals alone do not encode prediction errors or cross-method covariance.
