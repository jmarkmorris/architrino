## Independent analytic references

These are direct mathematical references independent of the chapter. Standard quantum algebra is used only as effective comparison mathematics.

- **Phase counterexample:** equal positive and equal opposite-sign two-component normalized states have the same squared coefficients, one half each, but opposite exchange-operator expectations. The independently written in-memory expectation function was first checked on the basis state with known expectation zero; only then the two equal-population states were checked, returning plus and minus one to rounding tolerance. Thus even correctly derived basin probabilities leave relative phase undetermined.
- **Normalization and measurement:** orthonormality makes the norm squared of the displayed expansion equal to the sum of squared coefficient magnitudes. Applying a rank-one basis projector retains its coefficient times that basis vector, giving the stated probability and conditioned state. A degenerate projector retains a subspace component instead, so the original single-vector statement was not general.
- **Invariant boundary:** the scalar autonomous equation with derivative equal to its state has solution equal to initial state times the exponential of elapsed time. A nonzero initial state never crosses zero. Uniqueness prevents crossing an invariant basin boundary under that same flow. A moving threshold equal to state minus time can pass a stationary trajectory without violating this fact.
- **Contact is not crossing:** a threshold evaluated along a trajectory as the square of time minus one contacts zero at time one but does not change sign. A positive threshold value decaying exponentially never reaches zero in finite time. These disprove both automatic triggering at first contact and a general finite-time guarantee.
- **Timing bound:** a positive initial threshold margin divided by an upper bound on its absolute rate supplies a lower travel-time bound. It gives no finite upper bound or guarantee of a crossing. A causal propagation distance likewise supplies a lower delay only if newly transmitted influence over that distance is required. No new numerical wake speed was instantiated.
- **Elapsed-time correction:** a qualifying event at clock instant thirteen after preparation at instant ten has elapsed duration three, not thirteen. Replacing the split formula's time set by positive elapsed increments and defining the corresponding instant enforces that difference independently of origin. The divisibility comparison needs its intermediate instant strictly before the declared terminal instant.
- **Measure ratio:** a basin measure divided by a positive calibration measure can equal a noninteger number. Its size does not prove that the basin contains an independently distinguishable record. Additivity can bound counts only with an established disjoint record partition and calibration extension.
- **Reduced memory:** two hidden histories can share one reduced state but give different subsequent records. Resetting the omitted history at an intermediate time then changes the reduced transition law in an entirely classical model. That discrepancy does not prove phase-sensitive quantum interference.
- **Decoherence:** a controlled unitary can correlate two target alternatives with different environment states. The target's reduced off-diagonal term is multiplied by the environment-state overlap; the combined pure state retains the coherence, and an inverse unitary can restore it. Loss of a reduced off-diagonal term is therefore neither selection of one outcome nor unconditional irreversibility.
