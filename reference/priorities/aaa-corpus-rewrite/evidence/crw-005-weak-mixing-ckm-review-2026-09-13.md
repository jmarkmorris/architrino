## Independent mathematics and counterexamples

Grade: derived algebra or scoped numerical confirmation of that algebra. Standard mixing matrices are effective comparison objects here, never substrate premises. The falsifier for each mathematical statement is an algebraic counterexample within its declared domain; numerical precision is evidence only for the evaluated case.

1. **Chiral normalization.** Inserting the projector equal to half of identity minus gamma-five into the conventional coupling gives a coefficient of the gauge coupling divided by twice the square root of two in front of the unhalved factor. The baseline coefficient was therefore twice the stated conventional amplitude.
2. **Overlap unitarity.** Expanding the inner product of two weak-basis vectors in a complete orthonormal mass basis gives the column/row orthogonality identities. Projection onto an incomplete subspace replaces the identity resolution with a projector and can reduce norms. The matrix whose nine entries equal one over the square root of three has all row and column squared norms equal to one; its Gram matrix has all entries equal to one and rank one. Thus normalization alone fails the full unitary condition.
3. **Independent rotation product.** A separately authored complex-matrix instrument, `.tmp/crw-005-weak-mixing/math.cjs`, first passed exact identity multiplication, identity determinant, and the product of one-plus-i with one-minus-i. It next passed the zero-angle three-rotation case, which must be the identity, before evaluating the chapter calibration. It forms three rotation matrices and multiplies them; it does not read the chapter's tabulated outputs. With sine inputs 0.225, 0.041, and 0.0037 and the positive cosine branch, it gives cosine 0.4010840108401084, phase 66.35403726327328 degrees, quartet triangle angle 66.3195379539612 degrees, quartet invariant 0.00003043902655694171, and top-down magnitude 0.008451047874980707. The maximum Gram-matrix residual is 2.220446049250313e-16. These support the inherited rounding, not a measured likelihood or a substrate derivation.
4. **Calibration and phase branch.** Multiplication gives the up-strange magnitude equal to the first sine times the smallest-angle cosine, and charm-bottom similarly. Exact calibration divides by that cosine. Reversing the phase conjugates the matrix, leaves every magnitude unchanged, and reverses the quartet invariant; the instrument checked all nine magnitudes and both signs. The cosine postulate thus fixes the magnitude of the CP invariant, not its sign.
5. **Transport reparameterization.** Taking negative logarithms yields nearest-edge parameters 1.491654876777717 and 3.1941832122778293, and penalty 0.9135843702764121. This transformation of three fitted inputs does not independently recover geometry. Nonnegative finite parameters restrict the sine/product ratio to the positive unit interval; some possible measured inputs would reject that restriction.
6. **Commutator identity.** In the up-diagonal basis, each off-diagonal commutator entry is the up-spectrum difference times the corresponding down-operator entry, and diagonal entries vanish. Expanding its determinant leaves the two oriented three-cycles. Their difference is twice i times the up-spectrum Vandermonde product times the imaginary part of the down-operator three-cycle. Expansion of that three-cycle in a unitary down eigenbasis gives the down-spectrum Vandermonde product times the stated quartet invariant. With the chapter's ascending spectral products and quartet orientation, the sign is positive. The instrument independently checked up eigenvalues (1,2,4), down eigenvalues (3,5,8), and the rotation-product matrix: direct determinant is approximately negative 3.47e-18 plus 0.010958049560499018 i; the spectral formula gives 0.010958049560499014 i. A repeated eigenvalue kills the spectral product even if an arbitrarily retained degenerate-basis matrix shows nonzero quartet invariant.
7. **Covariance expansion.** Differentiating the ratio gives the two negative denominator derivatives and the positive numerator derivative. Expanding the gradient–covariance quadratic form gives exactly the signs in the preserved display, including the positive covariance between the two denominator inputs. A central finite-difference check of all three derivatives agreed within 1e-7; an independently expanded non-diagonal covariance example matched the quadratic form at 0.000024385131478983524. These confirm the algebra, not any actual calibration covariance.
8. **Pull versus overlap.** Independent unit-standard-deviation estimates separated by 1.8 have overlapping one-standard-deviation intervals, but the difference pull is 1.8 divided by the square root of two, greater than one. For dependent estimates, the variance of their difference subtracts twice their covariance. Clipping out-of-domain draws changes probability mass and cannot preserve an unqualified coverage interpretation.
9. **Primitive inventory.** Two times six plus six equals eighteen, while nine plus three equals twelve; both twelve-site polarity differences have the desired magnitude of six elementary polarity units. The quark inventory changes from two positive/four negative to five positive/one negative, increasing charge by six units. Only an exchange ledger preserving individual positive and negative identities can implement that transaction. Charge difference cannot decide whether payload and cores overlap.

All new numerical calculations are dimensionless mixing/statistical arithmetic; no wake speed was instantiated. No computation sweeps a phenomenological knob and then claims an independently derived constitutive law.
