## Findings

- **MS-01 — medium, repaired: coordinate and ontology domain.** The identity matrix represents Euclidean geometry in a fixed orthonormal Cartesian chart. In spherical coordinates the same metric has the already displayed variable components; coordinate change does not create curvature. The four-dimensional metric exclusion now explicitly concerns native postulates, consistent with the guide's existing effective-metric layer, rather than denying the mathematical availability of comparison metrics.
- **MS-02 — medium, repaired: worldline regularity.** Absolute continuity of position gives velocity almost everywhere, not ordinary acceleration. The guide now requires locally absolutely continuous velocity for an almost-everywhere acceleration, stronger differentiability for pointwise formulas, and an explicit singular-event continuation. For example, absolute value of time is absolutely continuous but has no velocity at zero; still more general integrals of integrable functions need not have a differentiable velocity. [Master Equation](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/master-equation.md), lines 2478–2507, separately controls history spaces, compatibility, and existence hypotheses.
- **MS-03 — medium, repaired: shell distribution and smoothing.** Defined surface delta as unnormalized surface-area measure, making the two existing shell displays equal on positive-radius spheres. Spherical volume integration cancels the inverse-square factor and gives total signed q for the sharp shell. The radial Gaussian has nonzero weight outside the physical radial half-line, so finite-width shell mass is q multiplied by a factor strictly between zero and one. It also leaves a nonzero coefficient multiplying the inverse-square origin singularity. The guide no longer claims that radial width alone makes all calculus well defined. No new regularization prescription is selected.
- **MS-04 — medium, repaired: root and history domains.** Root playback requires positive separation and a differentiable simple root with nonzero transmitter derivative. Finite-width integration generally involves off-root history samples; a root-only DDE is a schematic sharp-root representation, not an automatic consequence of positive mollifier width. The overloaded schematic index now includes source and root labels. Infinite sums require convergence. Master Equation lines 260–297 give the exact delta coarea collapse; lines 2478–2507 identify additional compatible-history and continuation assumptions.
- **MS-05 — high, repaired: rotating projection derivatives.** A radial acceleration contribution does not imply a zero derivative of the transverse projection defined using a rotating line of action. The product rule adds velocity dotted with the derivative of the unit direction to the radial-component derivative. The valid displayed equations are retained explicitly for the selected hit in an axis frozen at the evaluation event; other hits and moving-basis derivatives are separated. Independent counterexample: constant Cartesian velocity (1,0) and unit axis (cos T,sin T) give zero acceleration but radial projection cos T, whose derivative is minus one at pi/2.
- **MS-06 — high, repaired: scalar-gradient availability.** Mollification alone does not establish a potential that reproduces the acceleration law. The conditional chart and independently verified gradient obligation now precede the unchanged energetic displays. [Energy](../../../../content/markdown/aaa/dynamics/energy.md#potential-energy-and-causal-wake-potential), lines 212–224, admits scalar representatives on regular receiver charts with fixed histories and controlled sums. Master Equation lines 5320–5355 separately records an action-scaffold residual and excludes a finite same-support local delta-jet repair; no generic scalar-action closure is inferred here.
- **MS-07 — high, repaired: time-dependent work.** The preserved work display now explicitly assumes a time-independent scalar representative and compatible kinetic bookkeeping. For general time dependence, the chain rule adds the integral of the partial time derivative of potential. Independent counterexample: potential equal to time at fixed position has zero gradient and zero work while its change is nonzero. A scalar-gradient relation is therefore not by itself total energy conservation.
