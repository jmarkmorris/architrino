## Independent mathematical checks and boundaries

For CT-04 and the observed order, take an exact error model $Y_h=Y+Ch^{p}g$ on a fixed grid, with $Y$ and $g$ smooth and $C$ a constant. The differences between successive rungs are $C(1-2^{-p})h^{p}g$ and $C(1-2^{-p})(h/2)^{p}g$, whose ratio is $2^{p}$, so the base-two logarithm of the ratio of the relative errors recovers $p$ up to the drift of the two denominators, which converge to $\|Y\|$. A Node calculation, after passing the known cases that the root-mean-square of a constant $2$ over four samples is $2$ and that $\log_2 8=3$, returned $p_{\mathrm{obs}}=0.988$ for $p=1$ and $1.999$ for $p=2$ with the normalized norm and zero floor. With unnormalized sums of squares on grids of $N$ and $2N$ samples and a fixed floor of $20$, the same model returned $0.747$ for $p=1$ and $1.751$ for $p=2$; the first value fails the chapter's $0.8$ requirement for a genuinely first-order instrument. The bias comes only from the floor's grid-dependent weight, which the normalized norm removes. This is a check of the estimator's definition, not of any run.

For CT-11, the hat matrix $H=X(X^{\top}X)^{-1}X^{\top}$ of a full-rank design with $N_{\mathrm{coef}}$ columns is an orthogonal projection onto a subspace of that dimension, and the trace of a projection equals its rank. The Node instrument first returned trace $1$ and maximum leverage $1/3$ for the constant design with three equations and one coefficient, the known answer, and then trace $5.000000000000$ for a random design with twelve equations and five coefficients, where $N_{\mathrm{coef}}/N_{\mathrm{eq}}=0.417$ and $R_{\mathrm{df}}=0.583$; that design's maximum leverage was $0.897$, which shows that the leverage bound is a separate condition not implied by the trace bound. Weighted least squares does not change the trace identity.

For CT-03, the Jensen–Shannon divergence with base-two logarithm of two distributions with disjoint support equals one, since each half-relative entropy against the mixture is $\log_2 2$. The Node instrument returned $0$ for identical atoms and $1$ for disjoint atoms as its known cases, then $1.000$ for three solved emission times $1,2,3$ against $1.001,2.001,3.001$ with no binning, $0.000$ with bin width $0.5$, and $1.000$ with bin width $0.0005$. The gate's verdict is therefore fixed by the discretization, which the repair requires to be declared before the run and no finer than the certified root-time error. This is a property of the divergence, not of any run.

For CT-02, with $F(T_t;T_r)=r-c_f\Delta$ the partial derivatives at a simple root are $\partial_{T_r}F=-D_r$ and $\partial_{T_t}F=D_t$, so a distance-gap width $\eta$ occupies reception-time width $\eta/\lvert D_r\rvert$ and emission-time width $\eta/\lvert D_t\rvert$ to first order where those derivatives are nonzero. These are the scales the repaired recipe states; the chapter now requires the step and history spacing to stay below them at each regulator rung. The argument is local and linear; a zero derivative needs higher-order event treatment, and no regulator-limit theorem is claimed.

For the existing jump residual, the numerator is a time difference and every denominator entry is a time: $\Delta T$ and $\Delta H_{\mathrm{hist}}$ directly, $\eta/c_f$ because $\eta$ is a distance width, and $\varepsilon_T$ by declaration. The provenance ratio $D_W$ divides a 1-Wasserstein distance between emission-time distributions, itself a time, by a time. Both are dimensionless as displayed, and both displays were preserved.
