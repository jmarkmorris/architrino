## Independent mathematical checks and boundaries

For ATI-04, the independent reference is the closed form derived in Binary Dynamics for the uniform circular chart: the principal self-delay equation $\delta_s=2s\sin(\delta_s/2)$ with $s=\|\mathbf V\|/c_f$, the threshold $s_0^\star=1$, and the expansion $\delta_s\sim\sqrt{24\mu}$ for $s=1+\mu$. The chapter's statement is the same equation written with $x=\delta_s/2$ as $\sin x/x=1/s$, which has a positive solution exactly when $1/s<1$, since $\sin x/x<1$ for $x>0$; the delay is $\Delta_{\text{self}}=R\delta_s/s$ because the arc traversed at speed $s$ in time $\Delta_{\text{self}}$ subtends angle $s\Delta_{\text{self}}/R$ and the chord it spans equals $c_f\Delta_{\text{self}}$. A Node bisection first returned the tabulated known case $x=1.895494$ for $\sin x/x=1/2$ and no root for $s=1$ and $s=0.9$, then gave $\Delta_{\text{self}}/R=0.15471$, $0.48336$, and $1.36179$ for $s=1.001$, $1.01$, and $1.1$, against the leading-order $2R\sqrt{6\mu}/s$ values $0.15476$, $0.48505$, and $1.40836$, relative errors $3.5\times10^{-4}$, $3.5\times10^{-3}$, and $3.4\times10^{-2}$. The expansion is therefore correct to leading order and degrades as expected away from the hinge.

For the fold-layer sentence, on the same chart the transmitter velocity at emission makes angle $\delta_s/2$ with the chord, so the transmitter-side factor of the newborn self root is $D_t=1-s\cos(\delta_s/2)$. Substituting $\delta_s^2\approx24\mu$ gives $D_t\approx2\mu$; the Node check returned $D_t=1.994\times10^{-2}$, $1.999\times10^{-3}$, and $2.000\times10^{-4}$ for $\mu=10^{-2}$, $10^{-3}$, and $10^{-4}$, with per-hit weight $c_f/\lvert D_t\rvert$ of $50$, $500$, and $5000$. The self root is therefore born at a fold with vanishing $D_t$ and singular weight, as the chapter now says. This agrees with the Master Equation's fold criterion $D_t=0$ and with the Binary Dynamics grazing-endpoint statement; it does not identify $\|\mathbf V\|=c_f$ with a caustic in general, which the Master Equation forbids, but only locates the fold of this one same-transmitter root on the circular chart.
