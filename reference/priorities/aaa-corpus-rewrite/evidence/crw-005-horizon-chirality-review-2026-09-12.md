## Checkable reasoning, grades, and falsifiers

**Crossing multiplicity — derived.** Take two oriented open strands with three selected positive crossings, with the over/under assignment chosen at each crossing. The event sum is three. A single unordered pair with a binary indicator contributes at most one, or at most two under an ordered-pair interpretation. Subdividing a strand must not change the count. The repair follows the live event definition. A pair-indexed convention that actually retains every distinct event and is subdivision-invariant would remove this finding, but the baseline supplied none.

**Topology and parity — derived limitations.** A projection can create or remove a kink crossing while the spatial curve remains uncut. Therefore a diagram writhe is not automatically a reconnection-only invariant. For a disjoint closed framed ribbon, linking can remain fixed while geometric writhe and twist exchange contribution; additional closure/framing hypotheses are essential. Separately, under spatial inversion, relative position and velocity both reverse, so their cross product is unchanged. A determinant built from three such axial vectors is parity even. These arguments do not exclude a parity-odd carrier using additional path/framing data. Reopening requires a defined invariant with its transformation and deformation domain, rather than a different mnemonic.

**Polarity conjugation — derived.** At fixed worldlines, C leaves positions and velocities fixed. If the relative vector is ordered by polarity, both that vector and its derivative reverse when the endpoints exchange, leaving their cross product unchanged. Consequently C alone supplies no contra-rotation. An explicit additional transformation and valid two-member history can resolve the photon-pair relation; changing the definition of C would be a separate canon decision.

**Conditional area — derived.** Write $a=R_\perp>0$, $c=R_\parallel=a/\gamma_f>0$. In the plane normal to $(\sin\vartheta,0,\cos\vartheta)$, choose orthonormal basis vectors $(0,1,0)$ and $(\cos\vartheta,0,-\sin\vartheta)$. Restricting the spheroid form gives diagonal coefficients $a^{-2}$ and $a^{-2}\cos^2\vartheta+c^{-2}\sin^2\vartheta$. The ellipse area is $\pi$ divided by the square root of their product, exactly the chapter formula. For $\gamma_f>1$, its maximum is at axial normals; at $\gamma_f=1$ all orientations tie. The area is unchanged by reversing any normal, so it ranks neither uniform nor mixed circulation signs.

The Taylor statement additionally expands in $(\gamma_f^2-1)\sin^2\vartheta$. Small angle alone does not control that parameter. In normalized units $c_f=1$, the diagnostic case $\gamma_f=100$, $\vartheta=0.02$ gives exact normalized area `0.44725533762196407` and quadratic approximation `-0.9998000000000002` by the Node arithmetic check. This falsifies use of the quadratic approximation uniformly near the singular limit. It does not falsify the exact formula or represent an evolved assembly. A different exact central-section determinant under the same positive-semiaxis assumptions would falsify the geometry derivation.

**Circulation and signs — derived.** Each of three nonzero signs has two choices, hence eight assignments. Simultaneous reversal of the viewing normal has no fixed nonzero sign tuple and groups them into four pairs. Coaxiality leaves all eight available. The unweighted net circulation can vanish even when every individual sign exists, for example axial magnitudes proportional to one, one, and minus two. Thus normalization requires a nonzero sum. These are kinematic statements, not momentum or spin conservation. A missed nonzero tuple falsifies the table count; a declared independently derived angular-momentum map is needed to promote the proxy.

**Conservation and conversion — derived limitations.** For the optional quadratic bookkeeping, differentiating $\mathbf L_{\mathrm{mech}}=\mu_{\mathrm{arch}}\sum_i\mathbf X_i\times\mathbf V_i$ gives $d\mathbf L_{\mathrm{mech}}/dT=\mu_{\mathrm{arch}}\sum_i\mathbf X_i\times\mathbf A_i$. Defining a wake term as the negative integral makes the total constant by definition, regardless of an independent conservation theorem. The coefficient is bookkeeping, not primitive mass. A compatible action or independent wake balance with boundary terms is the reopening evidence. Likewise, a fold multiplier proportional to $u^{-1/2}$ integrates to $2\sqrt{\delta}$ over $0<u<\delta$; amplitude divergence alone is not an infinite barrier. A discrete continuous sign is constant on a connected admissible path, but its applicability requires the actual sign and its continuity hypotheses. A retained conversion preserving all claimed invariance hypotheses falsifies that invariant or its application.

**Dynamical, population, and observer hypotheses — guessed.** Alignment, uniform-row preference, pro/anti population balance, horizon escape, exterior history recovery, spin, and weak exposure are not derived here. A stable mixed solution falsifies uniform-row exclusivity for the stated domain. A candidate failing complete acceleration or perturbation checks rejects that candidate, not every possible horizon construction. Two histories with distinct proposed labels but identical records falsify recovery of that label from that observation channel. A classified, persistent imbalanced sea would falsify a claimed universal balance rule. Those stronger claims need their own admissible histories and declared observers.
