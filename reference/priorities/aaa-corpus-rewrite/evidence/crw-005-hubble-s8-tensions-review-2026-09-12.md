## Mathematical witnesses and limits

All witnesses are derived conditional mathematics. They test implications in the old prose, not the existence, dynamics, or population of architrino assemblies. Normalized $c_f=1$ applies throughout; numerical quantities below are dimensionless or expressed in chosen reference units, not new cosmological measurements.

- W01: For $Z=AD+KD^2/2$, $Z'=A+KD$ whereas $Z/D=A+KD/2$. With $A=2$, $K=4$, $D=0.1$, these are $2.4$ and $2.2$, neither the local $A=2$.
- W02: A time-independent spatial transfer $Z=2D$ with $c_0=1$ has slope coefficient two. A constant effective scale has temporal logarithmic derivative zero. The definitions alone impose no equality.
- W03: The positive factorization gives $\ln\mathcal P=\ln(1+z)-\ln\Gamma_E+\ln\Gamma_R+\ln B+\ln D_v$. Values $(\Gamma_E,\Gamma_R,B,D_v,\mathcal P)=(2,1,4,1/2,3)$ give $1+z=3$. Multiplying both $B$ and $\mathcal P$ by seven leaves the redshift unchanged, demonstrating non-identifiability.
- W04: At fixed reception, $1+z=a_{\rm obs}/a(t_{\rm emit})$ gives $-d\ln(1+z)/dt_{\rm emit}=H(t_{\rm emit})$. With $dt_{\rm age}=A_{\rm age}dT_{\rm emit}$, the inferred age derivative is divided by $A_{\rm age}$. A rate $0.4$ and age factor two give $0.2$, absent an additional clock conversion.
- W05: At fixed dimensionless distance shape, supernova flux scales as $LH_0^2$. The replacement $H_0\mapsto sH_0$, $L\mapsto L/s^2$ leaves flux invariant. Likewise $D\mapsto sD$, $r_d\mapsto sr_d$ leaves BAO distance-to-ruler ratios unchanged. Neither observation supplies its missing absolute calibration alone.
- W06: For covariance $\mathbf C=\begin{pmatrix}1&\rho\\\rho&1\end{pmatrix}$, residuals $(1,1)$ and $(1,-1)$ give quadratic forms $2/(1+\rho)$ and $2/(1-\rho)$. At $\rho=1/2$, they are $4/3$ and four, versus two from the diagonal sum. At perfect duplication the covariance becomes singular.
- W07: Minimize $x^2+(y-1)^2+\lambda(x-y)^2$ for finite $\lambda\ge0$. The stationary solution is $x=\lambda/(1+2\lambda)$ and $y=(1+\lambda)/(1+2\lambda)$. The Hessian has positive eigenvalues two and $2+4\lambda$, so this is the unique minimum. The nonzero split is $1/(1+2\lambda)$; at $\lambda=1$ it is $1/3$.
- W08: A full-sky residual proportional to $n_z^2-1/3$ is quadrupolar and orthogonal to the dipole $n_z$ by parity. On equally weighted positive/negative coordinate-axis samples, the quadrupole values are $(2/3,2/3,-1/3,-1/3,-1/3,-1/3)$ and dipole values $(1,-1,0,0,0,0)$. Both variances are positive and correlation is zero. A constant second signal instead makes correlation undefined.
- W09: Choose $Z(D)=\ln(1+D)$, so $z=D$. Distances $d_L=D+bD^2$ have the same local first derivative and redshift curve but $q_{0,\rm eff}=1-2b$. For $b=0$ and one, the inferred quadratic coefficients are one and minus one. Both are positive-distance local maps. Redshift curvature alone cannot choose between them.
- W10: Two equally weighted contributions growing as $a$ and $a^2$ have total variance $(a^2+a^4)/2$. Their rms logarithmic growth rate is $(a^2+2a^4)/(a^2+a^4)$, which is $3/2$ at $a=1$, distinct from either mode's rates one and two. This is a counterexample to assigning one arbitrary mode's growth rate to an integrated variance, not a numerical cosmological spectrum.
- W11: In the explicit effective comparison $D_g''+bD_g'-2D_g=0$, the growing eigenmode exponent is $[-b+\sqrt{b^2+8}]/2$. Raising $b$ from one to two lowers that exponent from one to $\sqrt3-1$. This proves an eigenmode statement only; different initial-data mixtures, coefficient histories, exchange accounts, and physical derivation need their own analysis.
- W12: One common parameter $\theta$ gives residuals $\theta^2$ and $(\theta-1)^2$. Moving from zero to $1/2$ worsens the first from zero to $1/4$ but reduces the total from one to $1/2$. No history has split.
- W13: Choosing $\alpha_{\mathcal E}=a_\star(\mathcal E)/(c_0H_{\mathcal E})$ with all quantities positive makes every logarithmic ratio zero. At $a_\star=3$, $c_0=1$, $H=2$, this requires $\alpha=3/2$. Zero or negative $H$ does not belong to the logarithm's positive domain. Different thresholds can follow one law, for example $a_\star(\mathcal E)=a_0[1+n(\mathcal E)]$ for nonnegative class descriptor $n$ and fixed $a_0$.
- W14: Forward maps $\Pi_{\rm dist}(\theta)=2\theta$ and $\Pi_{\rm growth}(\theta)=\theta^2$ are compatible functions of one parameter even where their numerical outputs differ. Acceptance sets must be pulled back to the common parameter domain; equality of different output coordinates is not the criterion.
