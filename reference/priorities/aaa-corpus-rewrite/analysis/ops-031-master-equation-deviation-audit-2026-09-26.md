## What distinguishes a method from another law

An exact reformulation computes the same acceleration on its stated domain. The sharp causal delta integral and the complete causal-root sum are an example: for isolated simple roots, the delta integral evaluates the integrand at each root with the appropriate derivative weight. Rewriting that sum as an integral adds no physical wake thickness.

A support mask can also be an exact localization method. On a complete simple-root domain, multiplying the integrand by a function that equals one at every contributing root leaves the sharp delta integral unchanged. Suppressing an interval already proved root-free therefore removes no contribution. Conversely, excluding an unresolved positive-delay root does not establish equivalence. At finite smoothing width, even root-free regions can contribute, so the remaining integral or a bound on it must be included.

Numerical integration, interpolation, approximate root finding, and regularization can approximate the unchanged law. Their errors must be controlled for the claim being made. An auxiliary theorem may establish a property of a smoothed equation and still be useful mathematical exposition without establishing that property for the sharp equation. The transfer requires its own argument: convergence of acceleration on one supplied history is weaker than convergence of self-consistently evolved histories; regular-domain convergence need not continue through a singularity; existence of a limit need not establish uniqueness or independence from the approximation procedure.

No claim that a regulator is a physical parameter follows from its utility in a calculation. A proposed physical wake duration, an event rule selected by a softened model, or a fixed action transfer inferred only from an integer root count introduces an additional physical hypothesis. These are the relevant pullback candidates.
