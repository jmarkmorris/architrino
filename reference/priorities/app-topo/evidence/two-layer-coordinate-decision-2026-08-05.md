# Topo two-layer coordinate decision

**Status:** The beta-zero single-source **Source-local decades** checkpoint was visually accepted by the user on 2026-08-06. Its equal exponent spacing, labeling, heatmap relationship, source mask, and Purple/White presentation are now the approved reference behavior.

**Color decision:** The default heatmap must show honest raw magnitude. Each outward exponent step reduces the displayed contribution by a factor of ten: `e=0` is `1`, `e=-1` is `0.1`, `e=-2` is `0.01`, and `e=-3` is `0.001`. Contour lines preserve decade topology after the fill becomes faint. Any logarithmically enhanced color mode is optional, analytical, and explicitly labeled as display enhancement rather than physical magnitude.

## Purpose

Topo must distinguish two different mathematical objects that were previously being mixed together:

1. a portable, source-local field chart carried by each Architrino; and
2. a combined wake map evaluated in shared absolute space and time.

They may be related mathematically, but they are not the same visualization and must not be presented as one unlabeled coordinate system.

## Shared absolute space and time

Every Architrino has a prescribed history in common absolute space and time. Observation points, source positions, causal propagation, and comparisons between sources are evaluated in that shared world coordinate system.

For source `i`, let its prescribed path be `X_i(t)`. At an observation event `(x, T)`, the current causal construction finds the source event whose outward propagation reaches `x` at `T`. In the present prescribed-path model this is represented by a causal-delay solve of the form

`tau_i = |x - X_i(T - tau_i)| / c_f`.

The current displayed source contribution is then derived from that source event, with the established inverse-square form retained. This is a prescribed visualization contract, not an equation-of-motion or feedback result.

## Portable source-local chart

Each Architrino also carries its own local chart for expressing the strength distribution generated by that source.

The local radial display coordinate is indexed by integer wake-strength exponents. Equal changes of one exponent occupy equal radial display steps. For the outward, weaker-field sequence:

| Wake-strength exponent | Relative strength | Local display radius |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `-1` | `10^-1` | `1` |
| `-2` | `10^-2` | `2` |
| `-3` | `10^-3` | `3` |

Thus the source-local chart is logarithmic in wake strength and linear in the exponent label. It is not a claim that these radii are ordinary distances in absolute space.

The chart is anchored to its Architrino and travels with it. When a source moves, its local equal-strength surfaces may be shaped by its causal history and velocity. They must not automatically be treated as rigid circles translated through space.

## Combined absolute-space wake map

At a shared absolute-space observation point, each source contribution is calculated from that source's own history. The signed contributions are then combined:

`W_total(x, T) = sum_i W_i(x, T)`.

Contours of `W_total` belong to the combined absolute-space map. They are not the individual source-local decade rings. Cancellation, reinforcement, and zero sets appear only after the signed contributions are combined.

The combined map must remain in honest absolute-space coordinates. It must not silently inherit or merge the separate local radial coordinates of several sources.

## Required Topo views

Topo should expose these as two explicitly named views:

### Source-local decades

- Shows the portable exponent-radius chart for one selected source.
- Uses equal radial display steps for equal integer changes in the wake-strength exponent.
- Starts with stationary single-electrino and single-positrino scenarios, where the meaning is unambiguous.
- Labels the exponent sequence plainly, without duplicate zero labels, overlapping labels, or global-distance implications.
- Does not invent a multi-source or moving-source chart when the mapping has not yet been defined.

### Combined wake

- Shows the signed sum evaluated in shared absolute space and time.
- Preserves the existing causal source histories and raw field calculations.
- Shows combined contours, cancellations, and reinforcement as properties of the summed field.
- Clearly identifies itself as the combined absolute-space wake map.

The two coordinate meanings must never be mixed in one unlabeled canvas.

## Heatmap magnitude and analytical enhancement

The default heatmap communicates raw relative magnitude, not equal visual prominence per decade. A tenfold reduction in calculated wake magnitude must produce a tenfold reduction in the displayed contribution before ordinary color compositing with the selected neutral background.

This means the outer wake may become difficult to see quickly. That is intentional: users should understand that the inverse-square distributions appear as strongly localized peaks unless another source or observation point is sufficiently close.

Contour lines remain available to identify the integer decade surfaces even where the physical-magnitude fill is nearly invisible.

Topo may also offer an **Enhanced decade contrast** mode for analytical inspection. That mode must be visibly labeled as a display-only logarithmic enhancement, must not change raw values or contour locations, and must never be the default.

## Application-wide repair requirement

This decision is not a Topo-only presentation choice. Every Architrino application that calculates, transports, stores, or displays source wakes must use the same two-layer contract.

Before treating the repair as complete, the implementation must:

1. inventory every application and shared module that creates or consumes Architrino positions, source histories, wake samples, exponent levels, contours, or field values;
2. place the authoritative absolute-space and source-local definitions in one shared, versioned contract rather than duplicating formulas in individual apps;
3. preserve the authoritative solver and provenance of every value passed between apps;
4. migrate each affected app to the shared contract, removing incompatible local interpretations;
5. add cross-application fixtures proving that the same source history and observation event produce the same source contribution, exponent identity, polarity, and combined value everywhere;
6. expose the coordinate mode and completeness state in each relevant user interface so an app cannot silently present a source-local chart as an absolute-space map, or vice versa; and
7. keep an app fail-closed when a requested representation has not yet been defined, rather than inventing a convenient mapping.

The repair is not complete merely because Topo looks correct. It is complete only when all affected applications agree on the shared contract and their existing functions have been revalidated.

## Radius zero

The inverse-square source behavior is singular as radius approaches zero. This decision does not establish a physical core law or regularization.

For now, Topo may use a clearly identified display-only source mask so the chart remains finite and legible. That mask must not be described as resolving the physical behavior at the source. Any future core prescription requires its own explicit model and justification.

## Motion and feedback boundary

The current Topo scenarios use prescribed paths. They show how source history and velocity shape displayed wake contributions, but they do not yet calculate how those wakes change subsequent Architrino motion.

A future feedback model would require an explicit equation of motion that evaluates the combined field at each Architrino and advances its path. Until that exists, Topo must not claim self-consistent feedback, binding, stability, conservation, or physical acceptance.

## Immediate implementation checkpoint

The next visible checkpoint should:

1. add an accessible two-choice view control: **Source-local decades** and **Combined wake**;
2. implement the source-local exponent-radius chart for beta-zero single electrino and single positrino;
3. preserve the combined absolute-space view for every existing scenario;
4. show a clear unavailable state rather than inventing local charts for moving or multi-source scenarios; and
5. pause for visual review before expanding the model or running a long closure cycle.

This checkpoint is the reference implementation for visual judgment, not the end of the application-wide repair. After the checkpoint is accepted, the same canonical contract must be propagated through every affected app and shared interface.
