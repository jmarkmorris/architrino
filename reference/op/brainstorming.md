Retain an explicit no-change outcome when the existing treatment is adequate.

Discuss each set before applying changes to the reviewed chapters. Integrate accepted corrections and targeted explanatory rewrites while the reasoning is fresh, then check the resulting full documents and the effects on downstream claims. Review the associated priority and evidence owners when a chapter depends on them; do not require a second hierarchy-wide priority or operator-procedure rewrite as an entry condition. A contradiction that prevents interpretation of the next chapter is a reason to resolve that dependency before proceeding.

The recommendation is an inference from the recorded conversion coverage, remaining assurance work, current style scope, and the dependency of later chapters on Foundations and Dynamics. It should be revised if early sets reveal systematic unreadability that prevents substantive review, or repeated unresolved priority-owner contradictions. In those cases, perform a focused rewrite or owner reconciliation for the affected material before resuming. Assess the approach by the usefulness and checkability of its findings and the operator's ability to review each set, rather than by how many documents or words change.

#### Standards Conformance and Order of Work

Every workflow, prompt, and repository skill in this organizing effort must conform to the live [academic style guide](../../content/markdown/aaa/archie/academic-style-guide.md) and [operator explanation standard](operator-explanation-standard.md). Resolve shared authority conflicts first so downstream revisions use a consistent interpretation. Then work through one workflow family at a time: establish its owner and scope, implement accepted organization changes, bring its procedure and associated skill into conformance, and review both the written instructions and the behavior they produce. Every enumerated item needs an explicit disposition, including items that already conform; reviewing a sample cannot establish completion for the set.

Conformance has two distinct parts. The instructions themselves must explain their purpose, terms, reasoning, and boundaries clearly, while preserving their appropriate procedural form. They must also direct the agent to produce explanations and maintain working documents under the current operator standard. Refer to the standards rather than copying their rules into each skill. Preserve equations, evidence grades, review-only boundaries, commands, and historical records; a prose revision does not authorize a behavioral or claim-level change. A skill can remain concise when its routing purpose is fully explained.

Finish with a cross-workflow consistency review for naming, trigger overlap, conflicting output requirements, and omitted items. The proposed sequence treats conformance as part of completing each item, rather than as a separate editorial pass after implementation. Representative behavioral checks remain necessary because compliant prose alone does not demonstrate correct workflow selection or output.

Review representative requests against the intended workflow before and after any routing change. Include directory review, assessment without edits, integration of supplied feedback, verification of another agent's edits, theorem review, source mining, open-ended brainstorming, and mathematical preview. For each request, inspect the selected skill and live procedure, the permitted write scope, and the expected result. Ambiguous integration and exploration requests are especially useful for exposing overlapping triggers.

File and metadata checks can establish that definitions and links are valid; they do not establish that an agent selects the right workflow in practice. Behavioral review must confirm both correct selection and preservation of review-only boundaries. The organization assessment is an inference from the inspected definitions; repeated selection of an unintended procedure would overturn the assessment that the routing is sufficiently clear.
