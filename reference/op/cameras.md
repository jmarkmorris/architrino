    - What is DLOG-M?
        - D-Log, or DLOG-M in the context of DJI’s drones, is a type of color profile used when shooting video. It’s DJI’s implementation of a Log gamma curve, designed to capture as much tonal information as possible1.

          D-Log can give you more flexibility in your post-production by retaining a wider tonal range, allowing you more latitude to apply your color and style choices during editing1. However, shooting in Log can reduce image quality by trying to compress too much tonal information into a limited number of bits in the file1.

          To film in DLOG-M profile, you have to switch to H.2652. This might surprise some folks as they might not be able to even play the video on their PC, much less edit2. But there are ways around this, such as using proxies or proxy files in order to edit DLOG-M H.265 video in Premiere Pro2.

          In summary, D-Log is a low contrast video file designed to provide post-processing flexibility under conditions of high scene contrast3. It’s particularly useful when dealing with high dynamic range lighting situations1.

### Shutter Speed
- **A common rule of thumb in videography suggests that your shutter speed should be roughly double your frame rate for a cinematic look.**
- The relationship between the frame rate and the shutter speed forms the foundations of how your video looks.
- For example, if you’re shooting at 30 frames per second (fps), a shutter speed of 1/60th of a second is ideal.
- Frame rate is the number of individual frames that comprise each second of video, also known as FPS (frames per second).
- Shutter speed is the amount of time that each individual frame is exposed for.
- By controlling these two settings, you can achieve different visual effects in your video
