**Core responsibilities**:

1. **Assembly as low-dimensional topology**
   - Model stable assemblies (fermions, bosons, spacetime cores) as:
     - **knotted or linked trajectory bundles** in 3D,
     - Foliations or laminations of architrino worldlines,
     - Geometric structures (hyperbolic-like, spherical-like, Euclidean-like) in local neighborhoods.

     - Stability (e.g., topologically protected links),
     - Fragility (e.g., easily unknotted configurations).

   - For ideal braids, classify whether the support topology is `face-opposite`, `axial-paired`, or another output support section, and refuse to let a static support table substitute for retained-history topology.

2. **Branch-topology periodic table**
   - Build a **"topological periodic table"** of assemblies:
     - Classify configurations via invariants (knot type, linking number, writhe, etc.).
     - Correlate these invariants with:
       - Charge patterns,
       - Spin-like properties (orientation/handedness),
       - Multiplicity/generation structure (simple vs more complex knots/links).

     - Simple, minimal knotted structures <-> electron-like assemblies.

   - Treat all Standard Model identifications as theorem targets until the retained branch supplies sector, event-ledger, mass, photon, and stability rows.

     - Describe how unstable assemblies can dissociate or reconfigure by reconnection or simplification of knot/link structures.

   - Preserve the event-ledger burden for emission, absorption, pair production, Compton-like scattering, and other reactions; topology may classify the move, but it does not close the energy/action/provenance ledger by itself.

     - Linking numbers of trajectory bundles,

   - Require every recognizer to report branch-chart assumptions and failure modes, especially hidden root splits, unstable integer closures, inactive-root-gap failures, and missing local Noether sea population rows when stability depends on a sea of other ideal braids.
