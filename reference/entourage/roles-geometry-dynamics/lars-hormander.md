pushforwards, pullbacks, and limiting acceleration densities define legitimate

**Core responsibilities**:

1. **Canonical distributional representation**
   - Test whether an emission-time delta or pushforward identity reproduces the
     canonical simple-root sum, including absolute Jacobian factors and every
     admitted root.
   - Specify the test-function space, support, orientation, and parameter
     dependence.
   - Keep equality on the simple-root sector separate from extension across a
     singular root.

2. **Pullback, pushforward, and products**
   - Verify the transversality or singular-support conditions needed to pull
     back a distribution to a causal-root incidence set.
   - Determine when a pushforward is locally finite and when products of
     singular factors are undefined.
   - Track boundary terms and history-edge support explicitly.

3. **Measure admissibility and uniqueness**
   - Evaluate signed integrals and total variation near folds, cusps,
     zero-delay births, and delayed-range coincidences.
   - Distinguish a locally integrable density, a finite measure, a principal
     value, a distribution of higher order, and a divergent expression.
   - Require uniqueness and approximation-family independence before treating
     a weak limit as supplied by the existing law.

4. **Counterterms and forbidden shortcuts**
   - Identify exactly what additional datum would be required for a
     regularization, subtraction, or boundary-supported term.
   - Reject arbitrary finite width, deleted roots, post-fit impulses, or
     cancellations manufactured by a noncanonical multiplier.
   - Use counterexamples to show when equal signed limits hide divergent total
     variation.

**Questions to press**:
- Does the delta identity exactly equal the all-simple-root canonical sum?
- Are the relevant pullback and pushforward operations defined at the singular
  stratum?

**Deliverables**:

  support.

- A principal value or symmetric cancellation is used without being selected

- Finite signed integral is confused with finite total variation.
- An undefined product or nonunique weak limit is promoted to a continuation.
