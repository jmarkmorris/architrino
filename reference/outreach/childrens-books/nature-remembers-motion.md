# Nature Remembers Motion

Age band: 3-5

Format: illustrated picture book, 12 story spreads plus back matter

Core concept: motion leaves traces. A child can see before, after, return, repetition, and pattern before learning field language.

## Book Promise

This book teaches three lessons:

1. Motion leaves a visible trail.
2. Before and after order a path.
3. Repetition can make a pattern.

The parent/teacher layer should use the canonical terms `path`, `path-history`, `position`, `return path`, and `pattern`. The read-aloud layer should use ordinary language first: moved, stayed, before, after, near, far, again.

## Art Bible

Use case: illustration-story, scientific-educational.

Style: single-scene preschool picture-book pages, natural child and caregiver, white play spaces, black expressive linework, purple shadows, and red/blue/purple path geometry.

Recurring characters:

- **Nia:** a preschool child who notices trails, shadows, ripples, and repeated motion.
- **Mama:** a caregiver who names only what the child has already seen.

Recurring geometry:

- path-history appears as ordered dots;
- earlier source positions carry faint red/blue rings;
- purple overlap appears where histories cross;
- no named architrinos, no equations, no labels, no in-image text.

Image-generation rule: every prompt inherits the style-guide palette rule. Use only pure red, pure blue, red-blue purples, white, and black for non-human visual systems and $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ geometry. Keep scenery, clothing, and tools in white/purple/black unless a red or blue mark is explicitly part of the physics geometry. Human skin and hair may use natural tones. Do not include book text, captions, labels, equations, watermarks, or logos inside the illustration.

## Back Matter For Adults

### What The Child Just Learned

This book teaches path-history before terminology. The child has seen that:

- motion makes ordered positions;
- earlier places can still be visible later;
- rings can grow from an earlier event;
- repeated paths can make patterns.

### Classroom Activity Sequence

1. Drag a ribbon and point to the line.
2. Roll a ball and mark earlier places with dots.
3. Tap water and point to the fixed ring center.
4. Walk out and back along the same line.
5. Repeat the motion and look for a pattern.

### Image Prompt Reuse Notes

For production, keep ages 3-5 pages as one continuous illustration each. Use dots, trails, rings, and overlap; avoid arrows unless they are part of a physical toy in the scene.
