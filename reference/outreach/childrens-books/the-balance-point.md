# The Balance Point

Age band: 12-14

Format: illustrated science story, 18 story spreads plus back matter

Core concept: lawful systems can be sensitive. A small physical interaction near a threshold can choose one among several allowed paths without making nature random or magical.

## Book Promise

This book teaches three lessons:

1. A path can move through a potential landscape.
2. Small nudges near thresholds can choose among lawful outcomes.
3. Measurement is a physical interaction.

The parent/teacher layer should use the canonical terms `potential landscape`, `threshold`, `settling basin`, `measurement interaction`, `Noether sea`, and `deterministic multistability`. The story layer should use visible language first: slope, basin, ridge, balance, nudge, record, path.

## Art Bible

Use case: illustration-story, scientific-educational.

Style: early-teen tabletop experiments, restrained palette, natural students, white/purple room, black linework, and red/blue/purple potential contours integrated into the scene.

Recurring characters:

- **Mira:** now twelve, careful with measurements.
- **Tomas:** now fourteen, fascinated by sensitive outcomes.
- **Ari:** a steady questioner about whether uncertainty means no rule.
- **Dr. Sol:** teacher-caregiver, guiding threshold experiments.

Recurring geometry:

- potential landscapes appear as red/blue/purple contour fields;
- balance points appear as white centers inside purple threshold regions;
- converging paths approach the threshold;
- diverging red/blue paths leave toward different settling basins;
- measurement beams are physical purple-white interactions, not magic.

Image-generation rule: do not include book text, captions, labels, equations, or watermarks inside the illustration. Any labels should be added later by layout.

## Back Matter For Adults

### What The Child Just Learned

This book teaches threshold sensitivity without treating uncertainty as lawlessness. The child has seen that:

- paths move through a potential landscape;
- basins and ridges organize outcomes;
- converging paths can approach a threshold;
- tiny interactions near thresholds can send paths into different basins;
- measurement is a physical interaction that can leave a record.

### Canonical Term Map

| Story phrase | Canonical term | Adult note |
| --- | --- | --- |
| slope / table landscape | Potential landscape | A visual way to introduce response to potential. |
| basin | Settling basin | Region of paths that settle into the same outcome. |
| ridge / balance point | Threshold | Sensitive boundary between outcome regions. |
| tiny nudge | Perturbation / interaction | A physical change in state. |
| looking was not nothing | Measurement interaction | Observation has physical coupling. |

### Classroom Activity Sequence

1. Roll a bead into shallow bowls drawn on paper.
2. Draw two basins and a ridge between them.
3. Place the bead near the ridge and test small nudges.
4. Use converge and diverge with the drawn paths.
5. Shine a flashlight and ask what physical thing changed.
6. Separate observed paths from paths still only imagined.

### Image Prompt Reuse Notes

For production, keep every source PNG as one continuous tabletop scene. Do not use inset diagrams for alternate outcomes; show alternate paths faintly inside the same landscape.
