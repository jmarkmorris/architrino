# The Balance Point

Book: 12

Age interval: `11-12`

Series frame: **The Wonder of Nature and the Universe**

Wonder question: Why do paths bend toward some places?

Discovery sentence: A path can move through a landscape of hills, dips, ridges, and basins.

Text status: text-only manuscript draft; no image prompts in this pass.

## Book Promise

This book teaches:

1. A path moves through a potential landscape.
2. Slopes guide outcomes.
3. Basins and ridges matter.

Parent/teacher terms: `potential landscape`, `gradient`, `basin`, `ridge`, `equilibrium`.

## Read-Aloud Manuscript

### Cover

Read-aloud title:

> The Balance Point

### Spread 1: The Question

Read-aloud text:

> Electra watched the ball curve as it rolled.
>
> "Why does it bend toward that place?"

Editor note:

Curved motion becomes a question about the landscape.

### Spread 2: The Landscape

Read-aloud text:

> Poz set a little hill beside a little dip.
>
> "The path follows the landscape."

Editor note:

The hill and dip stand in for a potential landscape.

### Spread 3: Down The Slope

Read-aloud text:

> The ball rolled downhill.
>
> Its path changed without a hand touching it.

Editor note:

A slope can guide acceleration.

### Spread 4: Into The Basin

Read-aloud text:

> The ball circled the dip.
>
> Each turn was smaller.

Editor note:

A basin can draw motion inward.

### Spread 5: Out Of The Basin

Read-aloud text:

> Electra gave a stronger push.
>
> The ball climbed out.

Editor note:

Energy-like conditions affect escape.

### Spread 6: The Ridge

Read-aloud text:

> The ball reached a rounded ridge.
>
> One side led left.
>
> One side led right.

Editor note:

A ridge can separate outcomes.

### Spread 7: The Balance Point

Read-aloud text:

> At the top, the ball almost stopped.
>
> The smallest nudge mattered.

Editor note:

Balance can be sensitive.

### Spread 8: Map And Motion

Read-aloud text:

>

Editor note:

A map describes motion but is not the motion itself.

## Parent Note

The rolling setup is not the ontology. It is a controlled visual model for potential structure: local slope-like conditions guide path changes, while basins and ridges organize possible outcomes.
