# Book 2 Image-Generation Brief: Roll, Turn, Again

Use this brief after Book 1 review, before adding Book 2 back into `generation-manifest.json`.

## Master Style Prompt

Generate text-free 3:2 landscape source illustrations for **Roll, Turn, Again**, Book 2 of **The Wonder of Nature and the Universe**.

Match the Book 1 first-draft visual direction:

- Electra is a toddler girl with medium-brown skin, dark curly hair in two small puffs, and simple white-and-lavender play clothes.
- Poz is a toddler boy with light-brown skin, short straight dark hair, and simple white-and-purple play clothes.
- They are ordinary human children, not symbols, polarity figures, or architrinos.
- Use natural skin and hair tones only for people.
- Use only white, black, pure red, pure blue, and red-blue purples for balls, paths, clothing, play objects, shadows, hills, dips, floor details, and all non-human visual systems.
- Keep the art as a simple toddler board-book scene with generous white paper space, black expressive linework, soft purple shadows, and calm readable actions.
- Do not include arrows, labels, captions, readable text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, extra characters, tiled floors, grids, or busy backgrounds.
- Keep every red or blue ball clear, round, visible, and easy to point to.

## Source Images

### Cover

Electra and Poz sit on a clear white floor with a red ball and a blue ball nearby. A small rounded purple hill and a shallow purple dip sit along a simple floor route. One faint dotted purple path rolls forward, turns gently, and loops back for "again." The scene should introduce rolling, turning, and repeating without text.

### Spread 1: Ready

The floor is clear and ready. A red ball sits still near Electra and Poz. Keep the space open so the ball has a visible place to move. No tiled surface, no grid, no extra toys.

### Spread 2: Gentle Push

Electra gives the red ball one soft push with her hand. Poz watches nearby. Show the ball just beginning to move, with one or two very faint purple dots behind it.

### Spread 3: The Path

The red ball rolls along a smooth floor route. It does not hop. Show a simple dotted purple path behind the ball, with Electra and Poz watching from the side.

### Spread 4: The Hill

The red ball rolls up a small rounded purple hill. The ball is partway up the hill, slowing. Electra and Poz watch closely. Keep the hill low, safe, and board-book simple.

### Spread 5: The Turn

The floor route curves gently, and the red ball follows the curve. Show the path turning with soft purple dots. The scene should make the ball's turn easy to see without arrows.

### Spread 6: The Dip

The red ball rolls down into a shallow purple dip and circles gently inside it. Show a small curved dotted path around the dip. Keep the dip friendly, soft, and not like a hole.

### Spread 7: Stop

Poz catches the red ball with both hands at the end of the path. Electra smiles nearby. The dotted path stops at Poz's hands. The ball remains uncovered and visible.

### Spread 8: Blue Ball

Now the blue ball sits still at the start of the clear floor route. Electra and Poz look at it with a question-like expression. The old red-ball path can appear as a very faint purple memory in the background.

### Spread 9: Compare

Show the red ball's earlier path and the blue ball's newer path side by side on the white floor. The red path and blue path are both simple dotted purple curves, with the red and blue balls at different end places. Electra and Poz compare the two routes by looking, not by pointing at text.

### Spread 10: Again

Electra and Poz roll one ball again along the route. Show a calm repeating path: roll, turn, return. Use two soft dotted purple path traces, one older and one newer, with the red and blue balls nearby.

### Backmatter Activity

A clean text-free activity scene for adult-led play: Electra and Poz sit by a simple low purple ramp with one rounded hill and one shallow dip. A grown-up hand may be visible only as a small supporting hand placing the red ball at the start, but the hand must not cover the ball or become the main subject. The red and blue balls are both visible for repeating and comparing.
