# Book 3 Image-Generation Brief: Nature Remembers Motion

## Master Style Prompt

Generate text-free 3:2 landscape source illustrations for **Nature Remembers Motion**, Book 3 of **The Wonder of Nature and the Universe**.

Use the established early-series visual direction, with the approved **Nature Remembers Motion** exemplar as the trace/path-history reference:

- Electra is a toddler girl with medium-brown skin, dark curly hair in two small puffs, and simple white-and-lavender play clothes.
- Poz is a toddler boy with light-brown skin, short straight dark hair, and simple white-and-purple play clothes.
- They are ordinary human children, not symbols, polarity figures, or architrinos.
- Use natural skin and hair tones only for people.
- Use only white, black, pure red, pure blue, and red-blue purples for balls, ribbons, trails, water, sound rings, shadows, light patches, floor details, clothing, play objects, and all non-human visual systems.
- Keep the art as a simple older-toddler / first-preschool board-book scene with generous white paper space, black expressive linework, soft purple shadows, and calm readable actions.
- Treat `path-history` only as visible trace and ordered memory at this age. Do not draw architrinos, Noether sea, formal causal wakes, equations, labels, or detached diagrams.
- Do not include arrows, labels, captions, readable text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, extra characters, tiled floors, grids, or busy backgrounds.
- Keep every red or blue ball clear, round, visible, and easy to point to.
- Whenever a trace has order, show it with child-readable visual changes such as older/fainter purple marks and newer/fresher purple marks, not arrows or labels.

## Source Images

### Cover

Electra and Poz sit on a clear white floor with a red ball, a blue ball, and a purple ribbon trail nearby. A gentle purple trail curves from one earlier ball place to a later ball place, with faint older dots and fresher newer dots. A small set of pale purple ripple rings and a soft purple shadow patch can sit quietly in the scene so the cover introduces the idea that motion leaves readable traces. Keep the scene unified and calm, not a collage.

### Spread 1: The First Line

Electra pulls a simple purple ribbon across the white floor. The ribbon leaves a clear purple line behind it. Poz watches nearby. The line should read as a trace made by motion, not as a decorative stripe. No extra toys.

### Spread 2: The Ball Goes

Poz rolls the red ball away across the white floor. Electra watches. Show the ball moving from near the children toward a farther place with a simple faint purple trail behind it. Keep the red ball clear, round, visible, and uncovered.

### Spread 3: The Trail Stays

The red ball rests farther away, while the purple trail remains closer to Electra and Poz. The scene should make it obvious that the ball moved on but the trail stayed behind. Leave enough white space between the ball and the earlier trail so the contrast is readable.

### Spread 4: Before

Electra and Poz look at one continuous purple trail on the white floor. The first part of the trail is paler and softer; the next part is fresher and slightly deeper purple. The ordering should be visible without arrows, numbers, or labels.

### Spread 5: Near And Far

Show one purple path trace with some marks close together and some marks farther apart. Electra and Poz compare the spacing by looking at the trail. Keep the marks simple, rounded, and child-readable; do not make a grid, ruler, or measurement diagram.

### Spread 6: The Water Remembers

Electra gently taps a shallow white tray or small puddle of pale purple-blue water. Concentric purple-blue circles grow from the tap point. Poz watches nearby. Keep the water simple and flat, with the tap point and rings clear. Do not add naturalistic blue scenery or busy reflections.

### Spread 7: The Sound Remembers

Poz claps once while Electra listens a short distance away. Soft purple rings travel outward from Poz's hands toward Electra. The rings should read as a traveling trace of the clap, not musical notes. Do not use note symbols, sound-effect lettering, arrows, or labels.

### Spread 8: The Shadow Walks

Poz walks past a simple white/purple light patch on the floor or wall. A soft purple shadow moves with him, with one or two faint earlier shadow positions behind it. Electra watches. Keep the shadow readable as motion trace, not as an extra character.

### Spread 9: Back Again

Electra turns around and comes back along a path. A newer purple trail curves back and gently meets or crosses an older purple trail. The crossing should show return and earlier path memory without making a knot, grid, or maze.

### Spread 10: Follow It

Poz follows a clear purple trail on the white floor to find where something went. Electra watches from nearby. The trail leads the eye from the children toward the red ball or ribbon end. Keep the trace continuous and easy to follow without arrows.

### Spread 11: The World Shows Us

One calm continuous playroom scene gathers the trace examples without using panels: a purple ribbon line, a red-ball trail, pale water rings, soft sound rings, and a purple shadow patch are all visible at small scale around Electra and Poz. The children look at the traces together. Keep the scene open and simple, not a contact sheet or diagram collection.

### Spread 12: The World Remembered

Electra and Poz sit together on the white floor, looking at the quiet traces left behind: a purple ribbon line, a faint ball trail, soft water rings, and a gentle shadow mark. The red and blue balls sit nearby. The scene should feel like a calm final discovery: something moved, and the world kept a visible memory.

### Backmatter Activity

A clean text-free activity scene for adult-led play: Electra and Poz sit by a white play surface where a grown-up hand gently makes a simple purple ribbon trace or places the red ball at the start of a purple trail. The red and blue balls are both visible for repeating and comparing. A shallow white water tray with one small set of purple-blue rings may sit nearby, but it must not crowd the main ribbon/ball trace. Keep all traces physical and visible; do not use arrows, labels, symbols, musical notes, panels, or abstract diagrams.
