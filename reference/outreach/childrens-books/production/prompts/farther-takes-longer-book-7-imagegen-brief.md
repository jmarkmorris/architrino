# Book 7 Image-Generation Brief: Farther Takes Longer

Use this brief after Book 6 review, before adding Book 7 back into `generation-manifest.json`.

## Master Style Prompt

Generate text-free 3:2 landscape source illustrations for **Farther Takes Longer**, Book 7 of **The Wonder of Nature and the Universe**.

Match the Book 1 through Book 6 first-draft visual direction, with Book 7 focused on distance, direction, finite-speed travel, and the source-to-arrival line of action.

- Electra is a young child with medium-brown skin, dark curly hair in two small puffs, and simple white-and-lavender play clothes.
- Poz is a young child with light-brown skin, short straight dark hair, and simple white-and-purple play clothes.
- They are ordinary human children, not symbols, polarity figures, or architrinos.
- Use natural skin and hair tones only for people.
- Use only white, black, pure red, pure blue, and red-blue purples for bells, balls, rope, light source, paths, pulses, rings, shadows, floor details, play objects, clothing, and all non-human visual systems.
- Keep the art as a simple early-elementary board-book scene with generous white paper space, black expressive linework, soft purple shadows, and calm readable actions.
- Treat `distance`, `direction`, `field speed`, `source`, `arrival place`, and `line of action` as visible play geometry, not as formal diagrams. The child should see: one message begins here, crosses a shorter or longer path, and arrives there.
- Whenever near and far are compared, keep the setup physically comparable: same kind of bell, same kind of tap, same listener, but different distance.
- Show direction through source-to-arrival placement, gaze, posture, rope-wave travel, or a clean soft purple line of action. Do not use formal arrowheads unless a later human layout pass adds them deliberately.
- Keep a line of action as a soft physical-looking glow path or child-drawn line from source to arrival, not as a ruler, map line, grid, graph, or abstract coordinate axis.
- Keep every bell, ball, rope wave, light flash, source place, and arrival place clear, visible, and easy to point to.
- Do not draw architrinos, Noether sea, equations, labels, captions, readable text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, extra characters, tiled floors, grids, rulers, measurement marks, graphs, mazes, road maps, side-by-side panels, or detached diagrams.

## Source Images

### Cover

Electra and Poz sit in a simple white play space with one near bell and one far bell. Soft purple sound rings from the near bell reach Electra along a short path, while softer rings from the far bell cross a longer open gap. A clean purple source-to-arrival glow path hints at direction without arrowheads. The scene should invite the question "Why does farther take longer?" through distance and arrival timing alone, with no text, labels, numbers, rulers, or panels.

### Spread 1: Two Bells

Electra sets one small white-and-purple bell nearby while Poz places a matching bell far away across the open white floor. Electra looks between the two bells as if wondering which message will arrive first. Keep the near bell and far bell clearly separated by distance, with plenty of white space showing the longer gap.

### Spread 2: Same Tap

Show the near bell and far bell being tapped with the same kind of gentle action. The setup should make one variable clear: same tap, different distance. Keep both bells, Electra's listening place, and the different gaps visible in one continuous scene. Do not use labels, numbers, split panels, check marks, or measurement marks.

### Spread 3: Near First

The near bell's soft purple sound rings reach Electra first along a short path. Electra smiles or reacts quickly. The far bell remains visible in the distance but its message has not reached her yet. Keep the short path child-readable through spacing and rings, not through arrows or labels.

### Spread 4: Far Later

The far bell's soft purple sound rings cross a longer open white space before reaching Electra. Poz waits by the far bell, and Electra listens from the arrival place. The longer distance should be visible through the wide gap and extended ring path, not through a clock, numbers, ruler, or measurement diagram.

### Spread 5: The Rope Wave

Poz flicks one end of a soft white-and-purple rope while Electra watches the other end. A purple wave bump travels along the rope from Poz toward Electra. The rope should be a real play object on the white floor, and the wave should look like a traveling shape crossing the rope, not like an abstract graph.

### Spread 6: Not A Jump

The rope wave is partway along the rope, visibly between start and end. Show earlier/fainter purple wave positions behind it and the untouched rope ahead, so it is clear the wave did not jump to the end. Electra and Poz watch the wave crossing every place between. Avoid graphs, axes, arrows, and labels.

### Spread 7: Change Direction

Electra moves the arrival bell to the left side of the white play space. A soft purple sound path or ring sequence turns toward the new left arrival place. Poz remains at the source. The scene should show that moving the arrival place changes the direction of the message path, without using arrowheads, labels, or a road-map look.

### Spread 8: The Line

Poz draws or traces a clean soft purple line from the source start to the bell arrival place on a white play surface. Electra watches and points to the arrival bell. The line should read as a child-facing line of action: source to arrival with direction implied by the two endpoints, not a ruler, map, graph, or formal diagram.

### Spread 9: Faster Or Slower

Electra and Poz compare two visible travel examples in one calm play scene: a slow red ball rolling along a short purple path and a quick white-purple light flash or glow pulse crossing a nearby path. The scene should show that speed matters too, while staying grounded in play. Do not use lightning bolts, yellow light, labels, numbers, speedometers, or graph symbols.

### Spread 10: The Same Rule

Electra and Poz sit with several message examples arranged in one continuous white play scene: clap rings, ripple rings, a rope wave, and a rolling ball path. Each example should show a source, a traveling path, and a farther arrival taking longer. Keep the examples open and readable, not a contact sheet, split panel, or diagram collection.

### Spread 11: Predict The Arrival

Electra moves a bell farther away and looks toward the longer open gap. A soft purple path from source to arrival is visible but incomplete, suggesting the message will take longer. Poz watches from the source side. The scene should show prediction from the distance rule without thought bubbles, arrows, labels, numbers, or clocks.

### Spread 12: The Discovery

Electra and Poz sit together beside a simple source place, arrival place, and a clean purple source-to-arrival path. The path is visibly longer than a shorter nearby comparison path. The scene should feel complete: a message has a path, the path has a length, and farther takes longer. Keep the final image calm and uncluttered.

### Backmatter Activity 1

A clean text-free adult-led near/far bell activity: Electra listens at one side of a white play space while Poz and a small grown-up hand tap a near bell, then a far bell of the same kind. Use soft purple rings to show the near message traveling a short path and the far message crossing a longer open gap. Keep the grown-up hand small and secondary. Do not use arrows, labels, numbers, rulers, grids, panels, or abstract diagrams.

### Backmatter Activity 2

A clean text-free adult-led rope-wave activity: Electra and Poz hold or watch the ends of a soft white-and-purple rope while a small grown-up hand flicks one end. A purple wave bump travels along the rope, with older/fainter positions behind it and untouched rope ahead. Keep source end, traveling wave, and arrival end visible. Do not use arrows, labels, numbers, rulers, grids, panels, axes, or graph shapes.
