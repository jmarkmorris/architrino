# Book 6 Image-Generation Brief: The Message That Traveled

## Master Style Prompt

Generate text-free 3:2 landscape source illustrations for **The Message That Traveled**, Book 6 of **The Wonder of Nature and the Universe**.

Use the established series visual direction, with Book 6 focused on visible messages that begin in one place, travel through a medium, arrive later, and change what happens next.

- Electra is a young child with medium-brown skin, dark curly hair in two small puffs, and simple white-and-lavender play clothes.
- Poz is a young child with light-brown skin, short straight dark hair, and simple white-and-purple play clothes.
- They are ordinary human children, not symbols, polarity figures, or architrinos.
- Use natural skin and hair tones only for people.
- Use only white, black, pure red, pure blue, and red-blue purples for bells, balls, pebbles, leaves, water, boat, paths, rings, wakes, shadows, floor details, play objects, clothing, and all non-human visual systems.
- Keep the art as a simple kindergarten board-book scene with generous white paper space, black expressive linework, soft purple shadows, and calm readable actions.
- Treat `source`, `arrival`, `travel time`, `causal delay`, and `arrival object` as child-readable play actions, not as formal field diagrams. The visual story should be: something happens here, a visible message travels, and something there changes when the message arrives.
- Show messages as physical, age-readable traces: sound rings from a clap or bell, water ripples from a splash, a soft glow path between source and arrival, or a toy-boat wake. The cue must grow from the action in the scene, not float as detached decoration.
- Whenever a message travels, make the source place and arrival object both visible. The child should be able to point to where the message began and where it arrived.
- Keep delay visible through spacing, distance, or sequence cues inside one continuous scene. Do not use clocks as formal instruments except for Spread 1's simple tick-tock listening scene.
- Keep every red or blue ball, bell, leaf, pebble, or toy boat clear, visible, and easy to point to when it is the focal object.
- Do not draw architrinos, Noether sea, equations, labels, captions, readable text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, extra characters, tiled floors, grids, rulers, measurement marks, graphs, mazes, road maps, side-by-side panels, or detached diagrams.
- Do not use formal arrows. If direction must be visible, use gaze, posture, source-to-arrival placement, soft rings, or a gentle integrated glow path.

## Source Images

### Cover

Electra and Poz sit in a simple white play space with several message examples gathered calmly around them: soft purple sound rings from a clap or bell, pale purple-blue water ripples from a splash, a small toy boat wake, and one clear source-to-arrival glow path. The scene should show a message beginning in one place, traveling, and arriving somewhere else. Keep the children, source places, arrival objects, and traveling traces in one calm continuous scene, not a collage or diagram collection.

### Spread 1: Tick, Tock

Electra listens to a simple white-and-purple toy clock or rhythm toy nearby. Show two soft purple sound marks or pulses around the toy, one older/fainter and one newer/fresher, so the scene reads as tick before tock after. Keep the clock or rhythm toy simple and friendly, with no readable numbers, letters, or clock-face markings.

### Spread 2: The Clap

Poz claps across an open white yard or playroom space while Electra watches from farther away. Poz's hands are visibly together at the source place. Soft purple sound rings travel outward from Poz toward Electra, while Electra's eyes and posture show that she sees Poz first and hears the clap after. Keep the scene grounded in people and distance, not a formal wave diagram.

### Spread 3: Not Everywhere

Electra and Poz look at soft purple sound rings that are still traveling across the open white space after Poz's clap. The rings should occupy part of the space, with clear white space beyond them, so the message feels real but not everywhere at once. Keep Poz's clap source place visible and Electra's listening place visible.

### Spread 4: Where It Started

Poz stands by a simple white tree-like play object or purple-shadowed yard marker, clapping once. The sound rings begin at Poz's hands and clearly remember that source place. Electra watches from another place. The tree or marker should only help locate where the clap began; it must not become a busy outdoor scene.

### Spread 5: The Puddle

Electra drops a small purple pebble into a shallow white puddle or tray of pale purple-blue water. Concentric purple-blue rings run away from the splash point. Poz watches nearby. The splash source point must be clear, and the rings should be simple enough for a kindergarten reader to follow.

### Spread 6: The Ring Arrives

A small paper leaf floats on the pale purple-blue water. A purple-blue ripple ring reaches the leaf, and the leaf has just moved or tilted because the ring arrived. Electra and Poz watch the arrival. Make the source ripple path and the leaf arrival object readable without arrows, labels, or motion lines that look like text.

### Spread 7: Near

Poz taps a near bell or small white-and-purple chime close to Electra. A short set of soft purple sound rings reaches Electra quickly across a small distance. Electra listens with a pleased, immediate reaction. Keep the bell close, the path short, and the near distance obvious through composition.

### Spread 8: Far

Poz taps a far bell or small white-and-purple chime across a larger open white space. Electra waits and listens from farther away. Soft purple sound rings travel across the long gap, with more white space between Poz and Electra than in the near-bell spread. The scene should show that the far message takes longer without clocks, numbers, labels, or arrows.

### Spread 9: The Path Between

Electra points gently from the far bell source toward her ear while Poz watches. A soft purple glow path or sequence of sound rings connects the bell's source place to Electra's ear as the arrival place. The path should feel like the message crossed the space between them, not like a road, arrow, ruler, or formal diagram.

### Spread 10: The Boat Wake

Poz pushes a small white-and-purple toy boat across a shallow tray or smooth white play surface with pale purple-blue water. A little purple-blue wake opens behind the boat and reaches a small paper leaf. The leaf slides away when the wake touches it. Electra watches the traveling wake and the moved leaf. Keep the boat, wake, and leaf clear and simple.

### Spread 11: Which Message?

Electra and Poz sit with several message examples arranged in one calm play scene: a clap with soft sound rings, a bell with a short ring path, a ripple from a pebble splash, and a toy boat wake touching a paper leaf. Each example should show begin, travel, and arrive in miniature, but the page must remain one continuous scene, not a contact sheet or set of panels.

### Spread 12: The Discovery

Electra sits at the arrival place while a soft purple message is still on the way from a visible source place, such as Poz's clap, a bell, or a water ripple. Poz watches from the source side. The message should be clearly between source and arrival, showing that it is real before it reaches Electra. Keep the final scene calm, complete, and child-readable.

### Backmatter Activity 1

A clean text-free adult-led activity scene for sound delay: Electra and Poz sit apart in a white play space while a grown-up hand taps a small white-and-purple bell near Poz. Soft purple sound rings travel from the bell toward Electra. Keep the grown-up hand small and secondary. The source bell, travel path, and Electra's listening arrival place must all be visible. Do not use arrows, labels, numbers, rulers, grids, panels, or abstract diagrams.

### Backmatter Activity 2

A clean text-free adult-led activity scene for water-message arrival: Electra and Poz sit beside a shallow white tray of pale purple-blue water while a grown-up hand drops a small purple pebble into the water. A ripple travels outward and reaches a small paper leaf, moving it gently. Keep the grown-up hand small and secondary. The splash source, traveling ripple, and moved leaf arrival object must all be clear and visible. Do not use arrows, labels, numbers, rulers, grids, panels, or abstract diagrams.
