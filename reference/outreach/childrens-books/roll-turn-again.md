# Roll, Turn, Again

Book: 2

Age interval: `1-2`

Series frame: **The Wonder of Nature and the Universe**

Wonder question: Why does the ball go that way?

Discovery sentence: A rolling ball follows a path, and a turn changes the path.

Text status: text-only manuscript draft; no image prompts in this pass.

## Book Promise

This active toddler board book teaches:

1. Rolling follows a path.
2. A turn changes the path.
3. Repeating lets the child compare.

Parent/teacher terms: `path`, `turn`, `repeat`, `compare`.

## Read-Aloud Manuscript

### Cover

Read-aloud title:

> Roll, Turn, Again

### Spread 1: Ready

Read-aloud text:

> The white tiles are ready.
>
> The red ball waits.

Discovery:

A play surface gives the ball a place to move.

### Spread 2: Gentle Push

Read-aloud text:

> Mama gives one soft push.
>
> Roll, ball, roll.

Discovery:

Motion can begin from a push.

### Spread 3: The Path

Read-aloud text:

> The ball does not hop.
>
> It follows a path.

Discovery:

The route matters.

### Spread 4: The Hill

Read-aloud text:

> Up the little hill.
>
> Slow, slow, slow.

Discovery:

A surface can change motion.

### Spread 5: The Turn

Read-aloud text:

> The path turns.
>
> The ball turns too.

Discovery:

The ball follows the shape it meets.

### Spread 6: The Dip

Read-aloud text:

> Down in the little dip.
>
> Round and round.

Discovery:

A dip can hold motion for a moment.

### Spread 7: Stop

Read-aloud text:

> Papa catches the ball.
>
> Stop, little ball.

Discovery:

A hand can end the path.

### Spread 8: Blue Ball

Read-aloud text:

> Now the blue ball waits.
>
> Will it go the same way?

Discovery:

Trying again makes a question.

### Spread 9: Compare

Read-aloud text:

> Red went this way.
>
> Blue went that way.

Discovery:

Two paths can be compared.

### Spread 10: Again

Read-aloud text:

> Roll.
>
> Turn.
>
> Again.
>
> We learned the path.

Discovery:

Repeated play reveals the route.

## Parent Note

This book is supervised physical play. The tile surface is a story prop and future merchandise concept: a safe, modular rolling surface that gives the child visible contours without requiring adult physics language.
