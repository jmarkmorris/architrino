# Roll, Turn, Again

Book: 2

Age interval: `1-2`

Series frame: **The Wonder of Nature and the Universe**

Wonder question: Why does the ball go that way?

Discovery sentence: A rolling ball follows a path, and a turn changes the path.

Text status: text-only manuscript draft; no image prompts in this pass.

## Book Promise

This active toddler board book teaches:

1. Rolling follows a path.
2. A turn changes the path.
3. Repeating lets the child compare.

Parent/teacher terms: `path`, `turn`, `repeat`, `compare`.

## Read-Aloud Manuscript

### Cover

Read-aloud title:

> Roll, Turn, Again

### Spread 1: Ready

Read-aloud text:

> The floor is ready.
>
> The red ball is still.

Editor note:

A clear space gives the ball a place to move.

### Spread 2: Gentle Push

Read-aloud text:

> Electra gives one soft push.
>
> Roll, ball, roll.

Editor note:

Motion can begin from a push.

### Spread 3: The Path

Read-aloud text:

> The ball does not hop.
>
> It follows a path.

Editor note:

The route matters.

### Spread 4: The Hill

Read-aloud text:

> Up the little hill.
>
> Slow, slow, slow.

Editor note:

A small hill can change motion.

### Spread 5: The Turn

Read-aloud text:

> The path turns.
>
> The ball turns too.

Editor note:

The ball follows the shape it meets.

### Spread 6: The Dip

Read-aloud text:

> Down in the little dip.
>
> Round and round.

Editor note:

A dip can hold motion for a moment.

### Spread 7: Stop

Read-aloud text:

> Poz catches the ball.
>
> Stop, little ball.

Editor note:

A hand can end the path.

### Spread 8: Blue Ball

Read-aloud text:

> Now the blue ball is still.
>
> Will it go the same way?

Editor note:

Trying again makes a question.

### Spread 9: Compare

Read-aloud text:

> Red went this way.
>
> Blue went that way.

Editor note:

Two paths can be compared.

### Spread 10: Again

Read-aloud text:

> Roll.
>
> Turn.
>
> Again.
>
> We learned the path.

Editor note:

Repeated play reveals the route.

## Parent Note

This book is supervised physical play. Electra and Poz both try, notice, stop, and repeat. The ball, gentle pushes, hills, dips, stops, and repeat trials give the child visible changes without requiring adult physics language.
