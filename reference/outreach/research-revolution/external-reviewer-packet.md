# External Reviewer Packet: Research Revolution

## Review Target

Please review the research-revolution proposal as conditional institutional planning, not as a claim that the transition has already been validated. The proposal asks how funders, universities, laboratories, journals, and companies should respond if a candidate foundation-level implementation becomes technically serious enough to threaten inherited narratives while preserving much of inherited physics as benchmark machinery.

The useful review question is not "Do I accept the whole ontology now?" The useful question is "What technical object, evidence threshold, or failure condition would make each institutional action justified or unjustified?"

## Context To Assume

Assume the source memo is intentionally strong because it describes a possible research-system disruption. Strong policy language should not be read as proof of technical closure. Every disruptive action is meant to be conditional on threshold movement:

- `signal`
- `reviewable object`
- `benchmark pressure`
- `transition evidence`
- `institutional transition`

The reviewer should preserve that ladder. If a recommendation seems too aggressive, the best criticism is to name the missing threshold, not merely soften the prose.

## Compact Glossary

**False prior:** an assumption that became culturally or mathematically embedded before later evidence could test whether it was the right implementation basis.

**Benchmark machinery:** equations, data products, detector methods, approximations, and computations that remain useful even if their surrounding ontology is reinterpreted.

**Reviewable object:** a packet with primitives, update laws, equations or algorithms, assumptions, benchmark targets, and failure conditions.

**Translation key:** a map from candidate terminology into standard review language so skeptics can evaluate variables, equations, computations, residuals, and failure modes without accepting the ontology first.

**Archive-and-pivot:** preserve inherited results as baseline assets while redirecting authority, funding, and training toward the deeper implementation if the evidence justifies it.

**Requalification:** a structured process by which legacy experts renew authority by showing how their expertise maps into benchmark stewardship, translation, validation, teaching, or applied rebuild work.

**Assumption-risk register:** a portfolio document that identifies which funded work depends on observations, effective equations, computation tools, or narrative assumptions.

**Claim level:** the status label attached to a claim or service: exploratory, candidate, benchmark-pressure, transition-evidence, or institutional-transition.

## Known Burdens Already Acknowledged

Please do not spend the review only rediscovering these objections. They are already part of the proposal's burden structure. Useful review sharpens the missing object, threshold, or failure mode.

1. **Object formation.** A candidate implementation must be translated into variables, equations, algorithms, benchmark targets, and failure conditions.
2. **Recovery pressure.** GR, QM/QFT, Standard Model, thermodynamic, conservation-law, precision-test, and Lambda-CDM-era constraints must be recovered or clearly domain-labeled.
3. **Parameter discipline.** The candidate cannot win by adding hidden knobs domain by domain.
4. **Institutional proportionality.** Funding restrictions, requalification, facility cancellation, and authority reassignment require threshold movement and due process.
5. **Workforce protection.** Students, junior researchers, and useful legacy specialists need migration paths before programs are closed or redirected.
6. **Corporate boundary.** Private money can support tooling, education, simulation, and downstream applications, but it cannot certify scientific acceptance.
7. **Application gating.** Energy, materials, sensing, propulsion, manufacturing, and environmental claims remain downstream of mechanism, benchmark recovery, safety, and governance review.

## Requested Review Focus

Please prioritize these questions:

1. Does the packet keep institutional disruption conditional on evidence thresholds?
2. Which threshold definitions need sharper technical criteria?
3. Where does the proposal confuse benchmark preservation with narrative authority?
4. Which first reviewable object would most efficiently test the candidate implementation?
5. Which funding, workforce, or facility action is under-justified at its stated threshold?
6. What would make a rejection responsible: missing equation, missing benchmark, missing computation, missing failure condition, missing safety case, or missing governance control?
7. Does the proposal give skeptics enough authority to attack the candidate without allowing legacy institutions to veto object formation?

## Suggested Review Output

A useful review can be short if it is specific. Please use this structure:

1. **Strongest current contribution:** one paragraph.
2. **Most serious overreach:** identify the section and the threshold that is missing.
3. **Most valuable missing reviewable object:** name the primitives, equations, computations, or benchmark family that must appear next.
4. **One governance correction:** identify a funding, workforce, review, or facility safeguard that should be added or tightened.
5. **One exact sentence to add or revise:** propose wording if possible.
6. **Go/no-go threshold:** state what would move the proposal from current planning material to a serious intervention program.

## Review Posture

Treat the packet as a transition architecture. The right review posture is adversarial cooperation: reject premature claims, preserve the force of the false-prior risk where it is disciplined, and convert social objections into technical requests whenever possible.

## Architrino Corporation Role

Architrino Corporation should be reviewed as an implementation and support company, not as an acceptance authority. It may design applications, write specifications, build validation tools, create specialized AI models, provide training, support technical publications, run executive briefings, assess venture claims, and help institutions plan requalification or portfolio triage.

The reviewer should ask whether each proposed service is labeled at the correct claim level. Consulting, education, software tooling, due diligence, and AI support can be useful at candidate level. Application deployment, investment-grade technology claims, and institution-scale transition work require stronger benchmark, safety, and governance evidence.
