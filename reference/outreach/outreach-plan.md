# Outreach Plan

## Purpose

This plan treats outreach as the public communication layer for the open-source, zero-price $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ offer. The immediate goal is not mass persuasion. The immediate goal is to attract informed attention from people who can read, question, test, explain, or improve the architecture.

## Long-Term Accessibility Thesis

The long-term goal is broader than attracting the small population that can already work with GR, QM, or professional fundamental-physics literature. $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$ should test whether a more explicit level structure, visual causal geometry, simulations, children's books, and AI-assisted learning can let far more people understand how nature works from fundamentals.

The scale argument is motivational, not demographic precision. If only about `100,000` people worldwide can currently follow foundational physics deeply, then against a public measured in billions that is an order-of-magnitude signal of how narrow the current doorway is. Outreach should widen the doorway without weakening claim discipline.

AI is part of the accessibility strategy when it helps a learner animate hidden histories, run safe simulations, translate the same concept across ages, identify assumptions, and name claim levels. It should not make unfinished theory sound complete or replace physical play, reproducible checks, or careful criticism.

## Offer

The offer can be stated in one sentence:

> Inspect a public, open-source fundamental-physics architecture whose documents, simulations, validation ledgers, and open problems are available at zero price.

The offer should avoid implying that all derivations are complete. The stronger and more credible claim is that the architecture exposes its ontology, proof burdens, simulations, and failure points for public review.

## Return And Investment Method

Use a simple first-pass score before spending effort. Rank each channel-audience pair from `1` to `5`.

| Factor | Meaning | Example measurement |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Audience fit | The audience is likely to care about fundamental physics, open-source work, or public proof programs. | Percent of audience likely to click or read. |
| Conversion quality | The channel can produce useful action rather than only impressions. | Issues, comments, signups, pull requests, donations. |
| Message bandwidth | The channel can carry enough explanation without distortion. | Seconds, words, visuals, links, thread depth. |
| Trust context | The channel gives the project enough credibility to be considered seriously. | Host reputation, community norms, source transparency. |
| Labor cost | Time to prepare, post, moderate, and follow up. | Hours per asset or campaign. |
| Money cost | Direct cost to publish, print, advertise, or attend. | Dollars spent. |
| Reputation risk | Probability of attracting low-quality conflict or misunderstanding. | Moderation load, hostile responses, off-topic spread. |

Suggested formula:

$$
\mathrm{ROI}
=
\frac{
\mathrm{AudienceFit}
+\mathrm{ConversionQuality}
+\mathrm{MessageBandwidth}
+\mathrm{TrustContext}
}{
\mathrm{LaborCost}
+\mathrm{MoneyCost}
+\mathrm{ReputationRisk}
}.
$$

This is only a planning heuristic. Replace it with measured data once campaigns exist.

## Starter Hypotheses

- Highest early ROI likely comes from the project website, GitHub, GitHub Discussions, blog posts, email capture, and focused long-form video.
- Short-form video can produce discovery, but it needs a strong route into deeper material or it will generate weak attention.
- Physicist-facing outreach should lead with one narrow derivation, simulation, or closure burden, not the whole architecture.
- Open-source outreach should lead with concrete contribution paths: docs, validation, simulations, issues, and reproducible checks.
- Local posters and flyers may work best as QR-code gateways to a "start here" page rather than as standalone explanations.
- Print ads are probably low ROI until a high-performing message and landing page have already been validated elsewhere.

## Minimum Landing-Page Requirements

Every channel should route to a page that answers five questions quickly:

1. What is $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$?
2. What is the zero-price open-source offer?
3. What should a new reader inspect first?
4. How can a serious critic or contributor participate?
5. What claim level is being made: established result, working hypothesis, proof target, or open problem?
