# The Waves They Send

Book: 10

Age interval: `9-10`

Series frame: **The Wonder of Nature and the Universe**

Wonder question: What do the tiny sender-receivers send?

Discovery sentence: An architrino emits a traveling potential wave from an earlier position.

## Book Promise

This book teaches:

1. Emission creates a traveling potential wave.
2. Reception changes the next path.
3. Direction comes from the line of action.

Parent/teacher terms: `emission`, `potential wave`, `reception`, `line of action`, `field speed`.

## Read-Aloud Manuscript

### Cover

Read-aloud title:

> The Waves They Send

### Spread 1: The Old Place

Read-aloud text:

> The red architrino moved on.
>
> Electra drew a dot where it had been.

Editor note:

Emission is tied to prior position.

### Spread 2: A Wave Begins

Read-aloud text:

> From the old place,
>
> a wave began to travel.

Editor note:

A source can send after it has moved on.

### Spread 3: Not Sound, Not Water

Read-aloud text:

> "Is it sound?"
>
> Poz asked.
>
> Electra looked at the lab card.
>
> "It is a potential wave."

Editor note:

The analogy is limited; the canonical term is introduced.

### Spread 4: Field Speed

Read-aloud text:

> The wave had a speed.
>
> It did not fill the whole screen at once.

Editor note:

Potential waves propagate at finite field speed.

### Spread 5: Not There Yet

Read-aloud text:

> A blue architrino crossed the screen.
>
> The wave had not reached it yet.

Editor note:

Not-yet-arrived influences do not act now.

### Spread 6: Arrival

Read-aloud text:

> The wave met the blue path.
>
> The blue path bent.

Editor note:

Reception changes subsequent motion.

### Spread 7: The Line

Read-aloud text:

> Poz drew from the old dot to the blue path.
>
> "The bend has a direction."

Editor note:

The old-place-to-arrival relation gives a line of action.

### Spread 8: Old Source, New Arrival

Read-aloud text:

> The source was not there anymore.
>
> The old emission still arrived.

Editor note:

Present action depends on past source position.

### Spread 9: Red Source

Read-aloud text:

> A red source sent a red wave.
>
> The blue path bent when it arrived.

Editor note:

Source polarity belongs to the emitted influence.

### Spread 10: Blue Source

Read-aloud text:

> A blue source sent a blue wave.
>
> The next bend was different.

Editor note:

Polarity changes the response.

### Spread 11: Many Old Places

Read-aloud text:

> One moving architrino left many old places.
>
> Many waves were on the way.

Editor note:

Path-history becomes a set of possible emissions.

### Spread 12: Which Arrives Now?

Read-aloud text:

> Electra paused the model.
>
> "Which wave reaches the blue path now?"

Editor note:

Arrival selection becomes a central question.

### Spread 13: The Next Path

Read-aloud text:

> The blue point did not choose freely.
>
> Its next path answered the arrival.

Editor note:

Reception constrains next motion.

### Spread 14: The Discovery

Read-aloud text:

> The tiny senders send waves.
>
> The waves travel.
>
> The blue paths answer.

Editor note:

Potential-wave transmission is now part of the story.

## Parent Note

This book introduces `potential wave` carefully. It should not imply ordinary water or sound waves are the underlying physics; those examples only prepare finite-speed travel and arrival.
