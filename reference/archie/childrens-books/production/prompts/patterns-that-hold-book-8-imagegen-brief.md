# Book 8 Image-Generation Brief: Patterns That Hold

## Master Style Prompt

Generate text-free 3:2 landscape source illustrations for **Patterns That Hold**, Book 8 of **The Wonder of Nature and the Universe**.

Use the established series visual direction, with Book 8 focused on moving parts, timed messages, fit, stable patterns, and first assembly intuition.

- Electra is a young child with medium-brown skin, dark curly hair in two small puffs, and simple white-and-lavender play clothes.
- Poz is a young child with light-brown skin, short straight dark hair, and simple white-and-purple play clothes.
- They are ordinary human children, not symbols, polarity figures, or architrinos.
- Use natural skin and hair tones only for people.
- Use only white, black, pure red, pure blue, and red-blue purples for beads, strings, balls, tabletop pieces, paths, timing rings, glow cues, shadows, floor details, play objects, clothing, and all non-human visual systems.
- Keep the art as a simple early-elementary board-book scene with generous white paper space, black expressive linework, soft purple shadows, and calm readable actions.
- Treat `message`, `fit`, `stable pattern`, and `assembly` as visible play geometry, not as formal diagrams. The child should see: several moving parts can keep returning to a relation when their motions and arriving cues fit.
- Show stable pattern through repeated relation, not stillness: older/fainter purple traces, newer/fresher purple traces, bead positions, string angles, and simple timing rings may show the motion continuing.
- Use beads on strings, rolling balls, and soft tabletop play systems as physical toys. They must not look like atoms, planets, faces, social groups, magic charms, or detached technical apparatus.
- When spacing changes, make only one spacing change clear while the other parts stay comparable. The visual question should be what happens to the pattern when one part is moved.
- Show messages fitting through integrated purple cues: a soft ring, short glow path, or timing pulse that travels from one moving part to another at the moment the next part moves. Do not use formal arrowheads unless a later human layout pass adds them deliberately.
- Keep every bead, string, ball, repeated path, spacing change, and stable pattern clear, visible, and easy to point to.
- Do not draw explicit architrinos, Noether sea, equations, labels, captions, readable text, letters, numbers, logos, watermarks, extra characters, tiled floors, grids, rulers, measurement marks, graphs, mazes, road maps, side-by-side panels, or detached diagrams.
