# Draft Letter: DESI DR2, Source History, And Neutrino-Mass Inference

Primary arXiv page for contact lookup: [arXiv:2504.20338, "Positive neutrino masses with DESI DR2 via matter conversion to dark energy"](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.20338)

Dear Professor Ahlen, Professor Tarle, Professor Croker, Professor Farrah, and collaborators,

I am writing because your DESI DR2 analysis appears to isolate a point that is easy to miss when the discussion is reduced to the phrase "dark energy model." The important issue is not only whether one phenomenological model fits better than another. It is that late-time cosmological inference changes when the matter budget is allowed to have source history rather than being treated as a closed, passively diluted inventory. From the perspective of Architrino Assembly Architecture ($\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$), that is the part of the work with the broadest conceptual value.

As I understand the argument, the matter-conversion model uses the star-formation-rate density as a physical source history for a dark-energy-like component. That changes the inference problem. The observed baryon abundance, the late matter density, supernova distances, BAO, CMB information, local ladder information, and neutrino-mass constraints are no longer independent story fragments. They are coupled through a bookkeeping choice: what matter is assumed to survive as late gravitating matter, what matter is converted into another effective sector, and where that conversion appears in the expansion and perturbation history.

The neutrino result is especially important in that light. In standard inference, cosmological limits on $\sum m_\nu$ are often described as if they were only a clean free-streaming measurement. But they are also model-dependent constraints inside a late-time matter-accounting pipeline. If a portion of matter is moved from the clustering budget into an effective dark-energy budget according to a source history, then the allowed neutrino mass changes because the model has altered the background and perturbation covariance structure. The claim does not need to be that neutrino physics itself has changed. The claim can be more modest and more powerful: the inferred neutrino mass depends on how the late matter ledger is closed.

In compact form, the paper writes the dark-energy production rate as

$$
\frac{\d \rho_{\rm DE}}{\d a} = \frac{\Xi \psi}{H a^4},
$$

where $\psi(a)$ carries the star-formation history and $\Xi$ sets the conversion strength. Whether this exact expression is the final form is less important than the discipline it imposes. It replaces a free dark-energy curve with a matter-linked source term. That makes the model more vulnerable to falsification, because the source history is constrained by astrophysical data, and it makes the model conceptually sharper, because the dark-energy-like component is no longer just a label attached to residual expansion.

Our framework treats this as an implementation clue rather than as a completed ontology. We ask what physical process would make a source-history term appear in the coarse-grained cosmological equations. If the answer is compact-object coupling, baryon conversion, finite-region boundary accounting, or another late-time matter process, the same structural requirement remains: the model has to state what is removed from the matter ledger, what receives it, what remains gravitationally active, and how the perturbations respond. Without that perturbation-level accounting, a source-history model risks becoming a background-only repair. With it, the model becomes a serious physical alternative to treating $w(z)$ as an unconstrained descriptive function.

This is also why I think the neutrino-mass discussion should be framed carefully. The strongest claim is not that positive neutrino masses are "recovered" by adding freedom. The stronger claim is that the conventional tension partly reflects an over-constrained matter ledger: one that projects early-universe abundance forward while suppressing late-universe conversion channels. Once a physically motivated source term is admitted, the inference space changes in a way that can restore compatibility with laboratory-motivated positive mass expectations. That is a sharper and more defensible statement than a broad claim that the dark-energy model solves neutrino mass.

The main implementation challenge is to prevent the source term from becoming silent bookkeeping. If matter is converted, then some observable sector should know. The background expansion sees it; the growth of structure should see it; lensing should see it; cluster abundance and void statistics may see it; and the baryon-cycle history should constrain it. In other words, the source term must be carried through the full cosmological inference chain, not only through the distance-redshift relation. The more your model exposes those cross-checks, the more difficult it becomes to dismiss as a flexible fit.

The question I would most like to see answered explicitly is this: can the matter-conversion model be written as a coupled energy-transfer ledger with background and perturbation equations side by side? In such a form, the reader could see the early baryon abundance, late surviving matter, converted component, neutrino free-streaming contribution, and expansion response in one accounting system. That would make the proposal easier to compare with standard $\Lambda$CDM, $w_0w_a$ parameterizations, interacting dark-energy models, and compact-object coupling models without forcing the debate to stay at the level of labels.

For $\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}\mathbb{A}$, the compatible insight is this: formal state-space or density labels are not automatically the physical implementation. A component called "dark energy" may be a residual category hiding a source-history ledger. A constraint called "cosmological neutrino mass" may depend on how matter bookkeeping, clustering, and conversion are modeled. Your paper seems valuable because it puts that dependence into a concrete data-analysis setting rather than leaving it as a philosophical objection.

If you have a preferred technical note or derivation where the source term is carried through perturbations and lensing observables, I would be grateful for the pointer. That seems to be the place where the model can move from an interesting background reconstruction into a fully reviewable physical alternative.
