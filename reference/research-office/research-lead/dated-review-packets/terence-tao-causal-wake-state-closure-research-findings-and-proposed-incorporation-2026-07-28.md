## 3. Foundational findings

### 3.1 Minimal-state theorem or counterexample

Define two directions to be reception-equivalent when no admissible future reception can distinguish their local output or their locally updated continuation:

\[
\boldsymbol\omega_1\sim\boldsymbol\omega_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\mathcal O_{\boldsymbol\omega_1}(S,H)
=
\mathcal O_{\boldsymbol\omega_2}(S,H)
\ \text{for every admissible present state \(S\) and future history \(H\)}.
\]

Plainly: two directions may share one state coordinate only if every allowed future experiment treats them identically.

**Direction-separation theorem.** Assume:

1. the evolution is Markovian in the proposed present state;
2. receptions at disjoint surface locations may occur independently;
3. a local reception can change the output or later continuation at one location without necessarily changing another;
4. future receiver behavior is determined by the present state.

Then any sufficient present state must separate the quotient \(S^2/{\sim}\). In particular, one scalar shared by the whole emitted surface is insufficient whenever two directions are not reception-equivalent.

Proof skeleton: take two histories with identical transmitter history and identical global emission scalar, but apply an admissible update at \(\boldsymbol\omega_1\) in only one history. Independent reception permits a later receiver at \(\boldsymbol\omega_1\) or \(\boldsymbol\omega_2\) to distinguish the histories. A Markov state that still identifies the two histories cannot determine both future outcomes, contradicting sufficiency.

Plainly: if one part of the surface can be changed without changing another part, the state must remember which part changed.

This proves directional distinguishability, not three independent primitive coordinates. A strictly smaller representational counterexample is one latent scalar field \(u(T_t,\boldsymbol\omega)\) with predeclared outputs

\[
m=f(u),\qquad e=g(u),\qquad
\boldsymbol\pi=h(u)\boldsymbol\omega .
\]

Plainly: one stored number per direction can determine all three advertised outputs if a constitutive relation ties them together.

That counterexample does not establish that such a constitutive relation is physically correct or conservation-compatible. It establishes only that independence of \((m,e,\boldsymbol\pi)\) has not been proved. Without continuity or locality assumptions on the encoding, even component-count arguments are meaningless because several real values can be encoded pathologically into one real value. A genuine coordinate-minimality theorem would need a regular encoding assumption and an independently reachable open set of output values.

There is a further state-class issue. If a reception is exactly localized at one pair \((T_t,\boldsymbol\omega_{\mathsf h})\), then ordinary \(L^1\) density fields cannot record a finite account transfer through a pointwise change. The account state must instead support atoms, finite patches, or nonlocal redistribution.

**Grade:** derived conditional theorem; inferred operational quotient.
**Falsifier:** exhibit a Markov present state that identifies two independently updatable, reception-distinguishable directions yet uniquely predicts both future receptions. A regular one-variable constitutive encoding already falsifies any unconditional claim that the three outputs must be independent coordinates.

### 3.2 Coincident-birth asymptotic audit

For nonnegative measurable \(M\), the weakest necessary-and-sufficient condition is exactly

\[
M\in L^1\!\left((0,L),T^{-3}dT\right),
\qquad\text{equivalently}\qquad
\int_0^L M(T)T^{-3}\,dT<\infty .
\]

Plainly: the suppressed acceleration has finite total effect exactly when its weighted area is finite.

The bound

\[
M(T)\le C T^{2+\delta}
\quad\text{near \(T=0\), for some \(\delta>0\)}
\]

Plainly: this more-than-quadratic pointwise decay guarantees convergence, because the remaining integrand is bounded by \(CT^{-1+\delta}\).

It is not necessary. Let \(T_n=2^{-n}\), put \(M(T)=T_n^2\) on intervals of width \(T_n^3\) centered near \(T_n\), and set \(M=0\) elsewhere. The weighted contribution of the \(n\)-th spike is of order \(T_n^2\), so the integral converges, but \(M(T_n)/T_n^{2+\delta}=T_n^{-\delta}\) diverges for every \(\delta>0\).

Plainly: very narrow quadratic-height spikes can have finite total area even though no uniform more-than-quadratic bound holds.

For the natural monotonicity orientation—\(M\) nondecreasing as \(T\) moves away from birth—weighted integrability implies

\[
M(T)=o(T^2)\qquad(T\downarrow0).
\]

Plainly: a monotone maturity cannot repeatedly hide large values in narrow spikes, so it must be smaller than quadratic pointwise.

That condition is still not sufficient. For example,

\[
M(T)=\frac{T^2}{\log^2(eL/T)}
\]

Plainly: this monotone function is not bounded by any \(T^{2+\delta}\), yet its weighted integral converges because \(\int dT/[T\log^2(eL/T)]\) is finite.

An exact monotone characterization is obtained on dyadic shells. With \(t_n=2^{-n}L\), weighted integrability is equivalent, up to fixed multiplicative constants and an index shift, to

\[
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{M(t_n)}{t_n^2}<\infty .
\]

Plainly: for monotone maturity, convergence is decided by summing its quadratic-normalized values over successively halved time scales.

If “monotone” instead means nonincreasing as \(T\) increases, nonnegativity and integrability force \(M\) to vanish near birth; otherwise its positive lower tail makes the integral diverge.

For a regularly varying law

\[
M(T)=T^\alpha L(T),
\]

Plainly: \(\alpha\) is the leading power and \(L\) changes more slowly than any fixed power.

The classification is:

- \(\alpha>2\): convergence, under the usual eventual positivity and local boundedness of \(L\);
- \(\alpha<2\): divergence;
- \(\alpha=2\): the borderline condition is exactly \(\int_0^L L(T)\,dT/T<\infty\).

For a pure power \(M(T)\sim CT^\alpha\), \(C>0\), the condition is simply \(\alpha>2\).

If \(M\) is analytic in \(T\) and not identically zero, write

\[
M(T)=a_kT^k+O(T^{k+1}),\qquad a_k\ne0.
\]

Plainly: \(k\) is the first nonzero Taylor order.

Weighted integrability holds exactly when \(k>2\). Thus the first admissible integer order is \(k=3\), but analyticity does not require a cubic term: quartic and higher leading orders are also admissible.

The change to \(z=|D_t|/c_f\) preserves the same exponent only if \(z\) and \(T\) are asymptotically comparable, for example

\[
z(T)=aT(1+o(1)),\qquad 0<a<\infty ,
\]

Plainly: near birth, \(z\) must shrink at the same linear rate as \(T\); defining \(z\) alone does not prove this.

More generally, if \(z(T)\sim aT^\beta\) and \(M(z)\sim Cz^k\), then integrability requires

\[
\beta k>2.
\]

Plainly: a nonlinear relation between the root derivative and time changes the maturity exponent required for finite impulse.

Therefore “analytic in \(z\) implies cubic first” is valid only after proving \(\beta=1\). For general \(\beta\), the first analytic order is the least integer strictly greater than \(2/\beta\).

**Grade:** derived.
**Falsifier:** direct evaluation of the weighted integral contradicting any stated convergence classification, or a certified birth asymptotic showing that \(z/T\) does not stay between positive finite bounds.

### 3.3 Nonuniqueness and the missing scale

Assume, solely for an explicit control, that \(z=aT\) near birth with \(a>0\), and introduce a transition scale \(z_0>0\), equivalently \(\tau=z_0/a\). Two admissible effective coupling laws are

\[
M_3(z)=
\begin{cases}
(z/z_0)^3,&0\le z<z_0,\\
1,&z\ge z_0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
M_4(z)=
\begin{cases}
(z/z_0)^4,&0\le z<z_0,\\
1,&z\ge z_0.
\end{cases}
\]

Plainly: both switch off sufficiently fast at birth and exactly recover the regular value after the same declared threshold, but one grows cubically and the other quartically.

For a scalar singular coefficient \(A\,T^{-3}\) with constant \(A\ne0\), their birth-region impulses are

\[
I_3=A\int_0^\tau\frac{(T/\tau)^3}{T^3}\,dT
=\frac{A}{\tau^2},
\qquad
I_4=A\int_0^\tau\frac{(T/\tau)^4}{T^3}\,dT
=\frac{A}{2\tau^2}.
\]

Plainly: both impulses are finite, but they differ by a factor of two, so finite integrability does not determine a unique outgoing velocity or history.

Smooth versions can be produced by joining either near-zero law to \(1\) with a smooth transition. Even requiring any finite number of matching derivatives at \(z_0\) does not restore uniqueness: a sufficiently small smooth perturbation supported strictly inside \((0,z_0)\) preserves positivity, endpoint matching, and weighted integrability while changing the impulse.

There is also a consistency distinction. The stated free law transports \(m\) unchanged along characteristics:

\[
(\partial_T+c_f\partial_R)m=0.
\]

Plainly: a stored value \(m\) cannot mature from zero to one during free propagation under this equation.

Thus the displayed \(M(z)\) laws can only be:

- reception-time output functions computed from \(z\) and an invariant stored state;
- solutions of a new sourced transport law;
- or emission labels whose apparent change comes from sampling different elements.

Treating \(m\) simultaneously as freely constant and as an age-evolving maturity is not closed.

The minimal extra selector is not another endpoint condition. It is a predeclared native constitutive generator, including its scale and initial data, such as a causal semigroup generator for the stored state plus a reception output map. Local Lipschitz regularity of that generator would provide mathematical uniqueness; it would not derive the generator itself.

**No-go statement.** Nonnegativity, finite birth impulse, recovery of \(M=1\) on a regular domain, and any finite collection of endpoint matching conditions leave an infinite family of maturity laws with different impulses. No unique transition follows from those conditions alone.

**Grade:** derived nonuniqueness; the two laws are inverse constructions used only as counterexamples.
**Falsifier:** derive, from an allowed supplied primitive, an additional functional equation whose unique solution satisfies all boundary and integrability requirements without introducing a free scale or fitted datum.

### 3.4 Conservation compatibility

Let

\[
\mathbf p(\mathbf v)=
\begin{cases}
P(\|\mathbf v\|)\mathbf v/\|\mathbf v\|,&\mathbf v\ne0,\\
\mathbf0,&\mathbf v=0,
\end{cases}
\qquad
k(\mathbf v)=K(\|\mathbf v\|).
\]

Plainly: these are the most general rotationally covariant single-particle motion accounts already assumed by the packet.

For a continuous receiver segment with acceleration \(\mathbf a\) and speed \(s>0\),

\[
\dot k
=
K'(s)\hat{\mathbf v}\cdot\mathbf a,
\qquad
\dot{\mathbf p}
=
\left[
P'(s)\hat{\mathbf v}\hat{\mathbf v}^{\mathsf T}
+
\frac{P(s)}{s}
\left(I-\hat{\mathbf v}\hat{\mathbf v}^{\mathsf T}\right)
\right]\mathbf a .
\]

Plainly: energy-account change reads the acceleration component along velocity, while momentum-account change can have both parallel and transverse pieces.

At an instantaneous reception with continuous position and velocity jump \(\mathbf I=\Delta\mathbf V_r\), local conservation requires

\[
\Delta E_{\mathcal W}
=
-\bigl[K(\|\mathbf V_r+\mathbf I\|)-K(\|\mathbf V_r\|)\bigr]-\Phi_E,
\]

Plainly: the wake and boundary energy change must cancel the chosen motion-energy change.

\[
\Delta\mathbf P_{\mathcal W}
=
-\bigl[\mathbf p(\mathbf V_r+\mathbf I)-\mathbf p(\mathbf V_r)\bigr]
-\boldsymbol\Phi_P,
\]

Plainly: the wake and boundary momentum change must cancel the chosen motion-momentum change.

\[
\Delta\mathbf J_{\mathcal W}
=
-\mathbf X_r\times
\bigl[\mathbf p(\mathbf V_r+\mathbf I)-\mathbf p(\mathbf V_r)\bigr]
-\boldsymbol\Phi_J .
\]

Plainly: because position does not jump, the receiver’s instantaneous orbital-angular-account change is position crossed with its momentum-account change.

These are compatibility identities, not selection principles. They determine required account changes only after \(K\), \(P\), the support of the wake update, and the boundary convention have already been fixed.

A sharper obstruction follows from the measure used in the packet. If \(e\) and \(\boldsymbol\pi\) are ordinary integrable densities and a reception changes them only at one coordinate \(a=(T_t,\boldsymbol\omega_{\mathsf h})\), then

\[
\int \Delta e\,dT_t\,d\boldsymbol\omega=0,
\qquad
\int \Delta\boldsymbol\pi\,dT_t\,d\boldsymbol\omega=\mathbf0 .
\]

Plainly: changing an ordinary function at one point does not change its integral, so it cannot balance a finite receiver update.

A local finite transfer therefore requires an atomic update such as

\[
\Delta\mu_E=\alpha\,\delta_a,
\qquad
\Delta\boldsymbol\mu_P=\boldsymbol\beta\,\delta_a,
\]

Plainly: the wake accounts must be measures with a finite lump at the reception coordinate, unless the reception acts over a finite patch or emits a nonlocal response.

For a strictly local atomic update at the intersection point \(\mathbf Y(a)=\mathbf X_r\), zero local boundary flux gives

\[
\alpha=-\Delta k,
\qquad
\boldsymbol\beta=-\Delta\mathbf p .
\]

Plainly: the atom carries the exact scalar and vector account changes required at that event.

Free propagation of the momentum atom changes its orbital angular account at rate

\[
\frac{d}{dT}
\left(\mathbf Y\times\boldsymbol\beta\right)
=
c_f\,\boldsymbol\omega\times\boldsymbol\beta .
\]

Plainly: a freely moving momentum atom preserves orbital angular account only when its vector account is parallel to its propagation direction, unless another angular account compensates it.

The regular impulse is radial, so \(\mathbf I=\lambda\boldsymbol\omega\). Requiring \(\Delta\mathbf p\parallel\boldsymbol\omega\) for every incoming \(\mathbf V\), every direction \(\boldsymbol\omega\), and every admissible \(\lambda\) forces

\[
\mathbf p(\mathbf V)=a\mathbf V,
\qquad
P(s)=a s
\]

for one constant account coefficient \(a\).

Plainly: if every local radial reception must hand its momentum account to a radially propagating wake atom without an intrinsic angular account, the motion momentum map must be linear in velocity.

Proof skeleton: write \(\mathbf p(\mathbf v)=g(\|\mathbf v\|)\mathbf v\). Resolve \(\mathbf v\) into components parallel and perpendicular to \(\boldsymbol\omega\). The transverse component of \(\mathbf p(\mathbf v+\lambda\boldsymbol\omega)-\mathbf p(\mathbf v)\) is \([g(\|\mathbf v+\lambda\boldsymbol\omega\|)-g(\|\mathbf v\|)]\mathbf v_\perp\). Vanishing for arbitrary \(\mathbf v_\perp,\lambda,\boldsymbol\omega\) forces \(g\) to be constant.

This coefficient is an account scale, not primitive physical mass. The result does not select its value or select \(K\).

Euclidean rotations and translations justify scalar dependence on speed, vector covariance, and orbital position-cross-momentum form. They do not determine the functions \(K\) and \(P\). Absolute-time translation also does not create a conserved scalar without a supplied action or constitutive balance law. An action or equivalent native pairing is still required to relate \(K\), \(P\), acceleration, emission, and reception.

Post hoc cancellation is vacuous because for any chosen differentiable \(K\) and \(P\), one may define wake increments by the negatives above. Choices such as \(K(s)=0\), \(K(s)=s^4\), or any other regular scalar produce different “conserved” ledgers for the same motion. Agreement then reports only how the ledger was defined.

**Grade:** derived compatibility identities; derived conditional point-event no-go; derived conditional linear-\(P\) theorem.
**Falsifier:** a finite local point update of ordinary densities that changes their stated integrals; or a nonlinear rotationally covariant \(P\) whose arbitrary radial increments are always radial and whose transported local wake momentum preserves angular account without extra angular support.

### 3.5 Trilemma adjudication

Split the wake state into a dynamically visible part \(W^{\mathrm d}\), read by later acceleration, and a passive account part \(W^{\mathrm p}\), never read by acceleration.

Let the universal regular law be \(A=F(\mathcal H_t,\text{current root geometry})\), with no dependence on earlier receptions. Suppose a reception changes \(W^{\mathrm d}\) by a map \(U\), and suppose the visibility is nontrivial: some admissible later reception has different acceleration when it samples \(W^{\mathrm d}\) and \(U(W^{\mathrm d})\). Independent reception then produces two histories with identical transmitter history and current root geometry but different earlier receptions. The later accelerations differ, contradicting universality.

Plainly: a reception-updated state cannot both affect later acceleration and remain absent from a law claimed to depend only on transmitter history.

Therefore one of the following must hold:

- the regular law is enlarged to read reception history or visible wake state;
- the reception update lies in the kernel of all later acceleration observations;
- or no dynamically visible reception update occurs.

A passive coordinate can provide a mathematical counterexample to an overbroad trilemma. If \(K\), \(P\), and a reception rule are predeclared, a passive account can be updated by fixed formulas that balance them while the active regular law remains unchanged. This does not contradict the precise theorem because the updated coordinate is not dynamically visible. It also does not derive that account’s interpretation or boundedness.

For repeated receptions, a passive scalar account obeying

\[
b_n=b_0-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Delta K_j
\]

Plainly: its value is the initial capacity minus the cumulative receiver energy-account gains.

It is bounded below for all admissible histories only if those partial sums are uniformly bounded above or a replenishment/boundary rule is supplied. If \(\Delta K_j\ge\varepsilon>0\) infinitely often, then \(b_n\to-\infty\). An active wake component similarly needs an invariant bounded state set or a predeclared Lyapunov/capacity estimate; mere local update existence does not prevent blow-up or unbounded ledger drift.

**Grade:** derived visibility theorem; inferred terminology split.
**Falsifier:** two histories with identical transmitter history and root geometry but different visible wake states that nonetheless produce the same acceleration for every allowed future reception, while visibility remains operationally nontrivial.

### 3.6 Positive-emission-capacity obstruction

Let \(Q_E(T)\ge0\) be the total energy-account emission rate into retained wake state, before reception and boundary loss. On a finite window \([a,b]\),

\[
E_{\mathcal W}(b)-E_{\mathcal W}(a)
=
\int_a^b Q_E(T)\,dT
+\mathcal R_E[a,b]
-\Phi_E[a,b],
\]

Plainly: wake energy changes by emitted capacity plus reception transfers minus what crosses the retained boundary, using the declared sign convention.

If \(Q_E>0\) almost everywhere, the emission contribution is positive, but need not have a positive lower bound. If \(Q_E\ge q_0>0\), it is at least \(q_0(b-a)\). On an infinite window, positive emission causes unbounded cumulative wake energy exactly when

\[
\int_0^\infty Q_E(T)\,dT=\infty .
\]

Plainly: “positive forever” alone does not imply divergence; an integrable positive emission rate can have finite total capacity.

For an isolated global accounting domain with no outgoing boundary flux and no compensating reception return, conservation requires a debit of the same cumulative amount from another declared account. If that funding account is lower bounded, nonintegrable positive emission is impossible indefinitely.

The logically distinct escape routes within the allowed primitives are:

- emission stops or its rate is integrable — new emission constitutive data;
- emission draws from a finite predeclared capacity and therefore eventually weakens or stops — new capacity and depletion data;
- receptions causally replenish emission capacity — new reception-transfer data and no guarantee for an isolated transmitter;
- a retained finite window exports energy through explicit boundary flux — required bookkeeping for the window, but not removal of global energy;
- emission produces signed account contributions whose total is controlled — new sign and pairing data, and incompatible with an assumption of strictly positive wake energy if applied to the same account;
- the scalar is denied a physical energy interpretation — logically possible, but then it cannot establish physical energy closure;
- another passive reservoir is added — new state and constitutive data, not a derivation from the current primitives.

Allowing the account to run to \(-\infty\) is an algebraic escape only; it fails lower boundedness.

**Grade:** derived finite- and infinite-window account statements.
**Falsifier:** a lower-bounded, globally conserved account with nonintegrable strictly positive isolated emission and no debit, return, or boundary term.

### 3.7 Well-posedness architecture

A defensible local existence-and-uniqueness theorem would require at least:

- histories in a specified complete state space, for example \(C^1\) retained trajectories with absolutely continuous velocities;
- wake coupling in a space with defined directional traces, and wake accounts as finite Radon measures if receptions are point-local;
- free propagation as a strongly continuous causal transport or push-forward semigroup;
- locally integrable emission data and explicit retained-boundary traces;
- simple roots uniformly separated from \(D_t=0\) on regular charts;
- a certified one-sided normal form at each admitted root birth, with unique branch selection;
- separation of event times, or a deterministic commuting/priority rule for simultaneous events;
- no event accumulation in finite time unless a separate Zeno continuation theorem is proved;
- reception and emission maps locally Lipschitz on an invariant bounded set;
- acceleration and its state-Lipschitz modulus locally integrable through the singular birth;
- causal dependence only on retained history and present wake state;
- explicit behavior when required history has exited the retained domain.

Plainly: existence needs finite accumulated acceleration; uniqueness additionally needs small changes in the present state to cause integrably controlled changes in future acceleration.

At a coincident birth, finite impulse alone is insufficient for uniqueness. A useful sufficient condition is

\[
\int_0^\varepsilon
\left(
\|A(T,S)\|
+
\operatorname{Lip}_S A(T,\cdot)
\right)dT
<\infty .
\]

Plainly: both the acceleration and its sensitivity to the state must have finite area near birth.

Under these assumptions, one may solve by causal method of steps: transport the wake and integrate the history-dependent motion between events, apply the unique jump map at each event, and continue until the first boundary of the certified chart. This would prove only local mathematical well-posedness of the declared constitutive system.

Exact point updates make ordinary function spaces unsuitable for the account variables; point evaluation also is not defined on a generic \(L^1\) equivalence class. The state must either use continuous directional fields with finite-patch updates, or measure-valued accounts with a declared atomic reception rule.

**Grade:** inferred theorem architecture, based on standard mathematical sufficiency logic but not proved for an unseen concrete map.
**Falsifier:** a supplied map meeting these hypotheses that nevertheless admits two continuations from the same state, or a missing hypothesis exposed by a concrete finite-time failure.

## 4. Strongest derived result

**Conditional point-reception conservation theorem.** Assume:

1. a finite reception occurs at one emission coordinate;
2. wake accounts are updated only locally at that coordinate;
3. positions are continuous through reception;
4. the wake carries no intrinsic angular account;
5. the momentum account freely propagates with the intercepted surface direction;
6. regular reception impulses are radial;
7. closure is required for arbitrary non-circular incoming velocities.

Then:

- ordinary \(L^1(dT_t\,d\boldsymbol\omega)\) wake densities cannot balance a finite receiver-account jump;
- the local account must be atomic, patch-supported, or nonlocally redistributed;
- under the atomic strictly local option, angular compatibility for all receptions forces \(P(s)=a s\), with \(a\) an undetermined account coefficient.

**Claim grade:** derived, conditional mathematical theorem.
**Scope:** representation and local account compatibility only; it establishes neither physical momentum nor a conserved realized branch.
**Operator-checkable falsifier:** provide a concrete predeclared local reception map satisfying all seven assumptions with ordinary densities and a finite integral change, or with nonlinear \(P\) and no compensating angular support, then directly evaluate the energy, momentum, and angular totals through reception and subsequent free propagation.

### 2. Exact \(K\)-\(P\) compatibility

\[

\qquad
\mathbf p(\mathbf V)=P(s)\mathbf u,

\[

=

\]
Therefore
\[
\dot K=\mathbf V\cdot\dot{\mathbf p}
\quad\text{for arbitrary }\mathbf A
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
K'(s)=sP'(s).
\]

Plainly: arbitrary acceleration lets the common velocity projection be varied, forcing the two speed derivatives to agree coefficient by coefficient.

\[

Plainly: the current Energy assumptions do not by themselves guarantee a finite momentum account at rest.

### Finite-increment affine rigidity with an explicit richness hypothesis

Let $D\subseteq\mathbb R^3$ be a connected open velocity domain and let

\parallel

$$

$$

&=
\lambda_{uw}(\mathbf w-\mathbf u),\\
\mathbf p(\mathbf w)-\mathbf p(\mathbf v)
&=
\lambda_{vw}(\mathbf w-\mathbf v),\\
\mathbf p(\mathbf v)-\mathbf p(\mathbf u)
&=
\lambda_{uv}(\mathbf v-\mathbf u).
\end{aligned}
$$

Plainly: each edge of the velocity triangle has an account increment parallel

\lambda_{uw}
=
\lambda_{vw}
=
\lambda_{uv}.

Plainly: a closed non-collinear triangle cannot support three different
parallel scaling factors while preserving additivity of the account map.

$$
\mathbf p(\mathbf V)=a\mathbf V.
$$

Plainly: after the account is normalized at rest, its vector value is a single
