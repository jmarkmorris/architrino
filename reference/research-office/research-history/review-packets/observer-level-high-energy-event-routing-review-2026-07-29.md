## Research Findings

### 1. Event classes need a carrier-satisfiability equivalence relation

The existing event taxonomy is routed mainly by object and event-family labels. The review proposes a stricter operational equivalence: two event records belong to one event class only when one declared carrier target, using only predeclared parameter variation, can satisfy both records within their declared uncertainty. Visual similarity, a shared object label, a similar far-field readout, or a similar waveform does not establish that equivalence.

This proposal is directly useful as a classification rule because it exposes what could falsify a proposed grouping. It does not show that any current event families are equivalent, and it does not supply a carrier target.

- Claim grade: proposed operational definition.
- Falsifier: a declared carrier target and allowed parameter domain fail one member's uncertainty-aware residual, require a private parameter or source-window change for one member, or cannot share the stated record structure.

Plainly: two events may share a class only if the same kind of proposed explanation can cover both without adding a private adjustment for one of them.

### 2. A routable single-event record needs explicit observer and bookkeeping fields

The review proposes that a routable event record distinguish:

- source window and relevant source history;
- observer benchmark, observable map, and validity regime;
- channel-resolved event ledger;
- remnant and surrounding-medium update;
- uncertainty, including cross-channel covariance where channels share errors or inference assumptions;
- owning priority or equation route;
- and a no-hidden-retune witness.

Most of these fields already have partial homes in the High-Energy Astrophysics carrier shell. The directly supported change is to make source history, benchmark validity, and covariance explicit, while clarifying that the existing event ledger is channel resolved and includes remnant and medium updates.

- Claim grade: proposed record-sufficiency criterion.
- Falsifier: an owner demonstrates that one of these fields is redundant because it is a declared functional of the remaining same-record fields, or that the proposed record still permits a hidden source-window or parameter change.

Plainly: the record should show what happened, what was measured, how uncertain the linked measurements are, what remained afterward, where the record goes next, and whether the explanation changed its settings midway.

### 3. Observer pressure should be detachable from substrate premises

The review proposes a detachability test. An observation is legitimate observer-level benchmark pressure only when:

1. it enters through a declared observable map;
2. that map is tested by a residual capable of failing; and
3. removing the observation does not change the substrate premises used to construct the candidate record.

This is a level-separation discipline, not a proof that a particular observable map is correct. It prevents an observer benchmark from being silently installed as an architrino-level law or mechanism.

- Claim grade: proposed operational test.
- Falsifier: removing the observation changes a substrate premise, construction rule, native state definition, or parameter admissibility rule; alternatively, the declared residual cannot reject a mismatching candidate.

Plainly: the observation may test the theory, but it may not secretly write the theory's starting rules.

### 4. Similar readouts do not collapse distinct source histories

The review supports four conservative separations already compatible with the owner packets:

- compact stars and black holes remain distinct until a declared carrier-satisfiability argument joins them;
- black-hole/black-hole, neutron-star/neutron-star, and mixed mergers remain distinct unless one target covers each full record within uncertainty;
- waveform similarity alone does not establish merger-class equivalence;
- and release-channel similarity does not establish source-window equivalence.

- Claim grade: inferred routing discipline, consistent with the current owner boundaries.
- Falsifier: one declared carrier target with allowed parameter variation passes the complete source-history, channel-ledger, remnant/medium, covariance-aware observer, and no-hidden-retune records for the proposed merged class.

Plainly: the same-looking output can come from different histories, so the output alone cannot decide that the histories belong to one class.

### 5. EQ-07A is the smallest safe first trial, but only at observer-routing grade

The supplied review recommends EQ-07A compact-star support because its quasi-static support comparisons provide a comparatively limited channel record and already named observer benchmark rows. The smallest safe step is a record-shape trial that routes one compact-star support window to EQ-07A and records uncertainty, covariance, owner, and detachability fields.

The trial must stop before naming a native compact-region carrier. It does not establish compact-region retention, radial stability, a dense-matter branch, physical realization, accepted evidence, or a score change. EQ-07A's first blocker remains `missing_accepted_compact_region_carrier`.

- Claim grade: proposed implementation order.
- Falsifier: the selected compact-star record cannot isolate a source window and observer benchmark regime without importing a native premise, or another existing owner already holds a smaller complete record with fewer unresolved channel dependencies.

Plainly: begin by organizing one compact-star observation record well enough to test later. Do not treat that organization as the missing native explanation.

### 6. Population statistics are not single-event rows

Population rates, distributions, selection effects, and population-level covariance require either a separately owned ensemble record or an explicit out-of-scope disposition. They should not be silently added to one event record.

- Claim grade: proposed scope boundary.
- Falsifier: an existing owner supplies an explicit, lossless map showing that the population statistic is a declared functional of the single-event records and selection model without adding ensemble-level assumptions.

Plainly: one event record describes one event; population claims need their own accounting unless an existing owner already proves the reduction.
